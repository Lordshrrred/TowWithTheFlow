Use the calculator above to get a realistic range before you agree to a tow. It's built from typical national pricing patterns — not a quote from any specific company — so treat the range as a sanity check, not a guarantee.

## How this estimate works

The calculator adds up a base hook-up fee, a per-mile charge for the distance you enter, and any extra fees for vehicle type or situation (like needing a flatbed or winch). Night, weekend, and holiday calls get a timing multiplier since most companies charge more outside normal business hours. If you add storage days, those are billed separately on top of the tow itself.

None of this is submitted anywhere — every calculation happens in your browser.

## What actually moves the price

- **Distance** is usually the single biggest factor after the base fee — most companies charge a flat hook-up fee plus a per-mile rate.
- **Vehicle type** matters because heavier or larger vehicles (RVs, trailers, dually trucks) need bigger tow trucks and more time to secure safely.
- **Timing** — a tow at 2 a.m. or on a holiday almost always costs more than the same tow at 2 p.m. on a Tuesday.
- **Situation** — a car that rolls onto a flatbed in two minutes costs a lot less to move than one that's stuck in a ditch and needs winching, or one involved in an accident.

## Common Questions

**Q: How accurate is this tow cost estimate?**
A: It's a planning estimate, not a quote. Real prices vary by company, region, and exact conditions. Use the range to know roughly what to expect and to spot a price that seems unusually high before you agree to it.

**Q: Why do tow prices vary so much between companies?**
A: Hook-up fees, per-mile rates, and after-hours surcharges are set by each towing company individually — there's no fixed national rate. Distance, vehicle size, time of day, and whether winching or flatbed equipment is needed all change the total.

**Q: Does my car insurance cover towing?**
A: Some policies include roadside assistance or towing coverage, and some credit cards or auto club memberships do too. Check your policy or call your insurer before you pay out of pocket — see the related guide below on insurance deductibles and towing.

**Q: Will I be charged for storage if my car gets towed?**
A: Only if the vehicle is impounded or left at a storage lot rather than taken directly to a shop or your chosen address. Storage fees usually accrue per day starting the same day or the next, which is why the calculator includes an optional storage-days field.

**Q: What if the actual bill is higher than this estimate?**
A: Ask for an itemized breakdown before you pay. Compare each line item — hook-up, mileage, after-hours fee, winching, storage — against what you were told when the truck was dispatched. A tow company should be able to explain every charge.
