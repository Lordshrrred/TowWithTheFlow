A car breakdown in Phoenix during summer is not just an inconvenience. When it is 112 degrees outside and your car dies on the I-10 or Loop 202, you have maybe 10 minutes before heat becomes a medical problem. Get safe first, then worry about the tow bill.

> **Quick Answer:** Towing in Phoenix during summer typically runs $75 to $125 for a hookup fee plus $3 to $7 per mile. A 10-mile tow to a shop will cost $105 to $195 on average. Summer heat does not automatically trigger a surcharge, but after-hours calls and freeway responses add $25 to $75. Using roadside assistance through insurance can cut your out-of-pocket cost to zero.

## What To Do

1. **Get off the road immediately.** Pull completely onto the shoulder or into the nearest parking lot. Do not stop in a travel lane or on a narrow shoulder. Turn on hazard lights the moment you feel trouble.

2. **Get out of the car if it is overheating or smoking.** In extreme Phoenix heat, a hot engine in an enclosed car can create dangerous fumes. Step away from traffic and find shade. Do not stand behind the vehicle.

3. **Call for a tow before you call a mechanic.** If the car will not move safely, you need a tow truck confirmed first. Then call your shop so they know it is coming.

4. **Use your insurance roadside assistance first.** Check your policy app or card before you call a random tow company. Most major insurers cover at least one tow per incident, and some cover the full distance to a shop. If you have Progressive, GEICO, State Farm, or similar coverage, call that number first. You can review [what Progressive actually reimburses for towing](/progressive-insurance-towing-reimbursement-amount/) before you call so you know your limit.

5. **If you have no roadside coverage, call a local Phoenix tow company directly.** Avoid the first result that auto-populates on Google Maps. Those are often national dispatch brokers who mark up the rate and add extra mileage. Search "tow truck Phoenix AZ" and call a company with a local phone number and real reviews.

6. **Ask for the total price before you agree.** Get the hookup fee and the per-mile rate confirmed verbally. On a 15-mile tow at $5/mile with a $95 hookup, you are looking at $170. That should not surprise you when the driver arrives.

7. **Stay hydrated and stay in the shade.** If you are on a Phoenix freeway shoulder in summer, call 911 if you are feeling dizzy, nauseous, or disoriented. Heat stroke sets in fast at these temperatures. The tow can wait. Your health cannot.

## Stay Safe

- **Do not sit in a parked car in direct sun** while waiting for a tow. Interior temps can exceed 150 degrees within minutes.
- **Carry water in your car year-round** if you drive in Phoenix. A 20-minute wait becomes dangerous without it.
- **Wear light clothing and use sunscreen** if you need to be outside near your vehicle.
- **Call 911 if anyone in the car is showing heat illness symptoms.** EMS response takes priority over the tow truck.
- **Keep your hazard lights running** until the tow truck arrives, even if you think the battery is weak.
- **Do not accept a tow from an unmarked vehicle** that stops unsolicited. Licensed tow operators in Arizona are required to display company name and licensing.

If you are unsure whether your insurance covers this tow or you will be paying out of pocket, check [whether your insurance deductible applies to towing costs](/car-insurance-deductible-applies-to-towing-cost/) so you are not caught off guard at billing time.

## Common Questions

**Q: How long will I have to wait for a tow truck in Phoenix on a hot summer day?**
A: On a mild day, most Phoenix tow companies arrive within 30 to 45 minutes. During heat advisories or peak summer afternoons, wait times can stretch to 90 minutes or more because call volume spikes. If you have insurance roadside assistance, their dispatch networks sometimes have shorter estimated arrival windows than local direct calls.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow company directly or use a service like AAA?**
A: AAA membership typically covers the full tow up to a set mileage limit, which can save you $100 or more on a single call compared to paying out of pocket. Calling a company directly gives you more control over which shop your car goes to, but you pay the full rate. If you have any roadside coverage through insurance or a membership, use that first before spending your own money.

**Q: Does Phoenix have any rules about how much a tow company can charge?**
A: Arizona does not cap towing rates statewide, so prices vary between companies. Licensed operators are required to disclose their rates on request, which is why confirming the hookup fee and per-mile rate before the truck is dispatched protects you from surprises at drop-off.
