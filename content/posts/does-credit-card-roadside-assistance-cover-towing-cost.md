> **Quick Answer:** Most credit cards with roadside assistance do cover towing, but coverage varies sharply by card. Some cards pay the full tow cost as a free benefit. Others work as dispatch services only, meaning they find you a truck but charge your card for the actual bill. Check your card's benefits guide before you assume you're covered.

## Does My Credit Card Actually Pay for the Tow or Just Call Someone?

That depends entirely on whether your card offers roadside assistance as a true benefit or as a dispatch service. These are two different things, and the distinction matters a lot when you're stranded.

A true benefit means the card covers the tow cost up to a dollar limit, usually at no charge to you at the moment of service. Visa Signature and World Mastercard cards, for example, often include this as a paid-up perk, with no per-use fee. Dispatch-only service means the card's network calls a truck for you, but you pay the provider directly when they arrive. Cards that bundle in "24/7 roadside assistance" as a generic perk are frequently dispatch-only, especially mid-tier cards.

The only way to know which type you have is to pull up your card's benefits guide, not the front page of your bank's site, but the actual Visa/Mastercard/Amex benefits document. It will say either "no charge to you" or "you agree to pay the service provider."

## How Much Will a Credit Card Roadside Benefit Actually Cover?

Cards that pay directly typically cap coverage at $50 to $100 per service call, and most limit you to four or five calls per year. A local tow under five miles often falls within that window. A longer tow does not.

A flatbed tow for a broken-down car runs roughly $75 to $125 for the first few miles, then $3 to $7 per mile after that. If your car dies on the interstate 40 miles from the nearest shop, a $100 card benefit leaves you with a significant balance to pay. [Understanding what a tow actually costs for longer distances](/how-much-to-tow-car-100-miles-interstate/) helps you plan for that gap before the driver hands you the bill.

Premium cards, specifically cards with annual fees above $400 like the Chase Sapphire Reserve or Amex Platinum, tend to have more robust roadside programs, but even those have per-incident caps. Read the fine print on your specific card, not a generic description of the card tier.

## What Happens If My Credit Card Coverage Isn't Enough?

You pay the difference at the scene or on your card when billed by the provider. Most tow operators accept credit cards on the spot.

Before you call your card's roadside number, consider whether you have other coverage stacked behind it. Your auto insurance policy may include roadside or towing coverage, and some policies specifically cover breakdowns unrelated to accidents. [Your auto insurance may already cover the tow](/does-insurance-cover-towing-if-car-breaks-down-not-accident/), which could eliminate the gap entirely. If you have both, the insurance coverage often has higher limits and covers longer tows.

## Is Credit Card Roadside Assistance Worth Using Over AAA or My Insurance?

For a short, local tow within your card's coverage cap, credit card roadside assistance is often the best call because you pay nothing out of pocket in the moment. For anything beyond that, insurance or AAA usually provides better dollar-for-dollar coverage.

AAA's Classic membership covers up to 5 miles per tow, while Plus and Premier tiers extend that to 100 and 200 miles respectively. [A side-by-side look at AAA membership cost versus paying for a tow](/how-much-does-aaa-membership-cost-compared-to-paying-for-tow/) breaks down when the math favors each option. Credit cards fill a real gap for drivers who don't have AAA or whose insurance doesn't include roadside, but they're not a replacement for a purpose-built roadside plan if you drive frequently or in remote areas.

One specific tip: if you use a dispatch-only card service and the tow cost comes in high, you can dispute the charge as unreasonable after the fact through your card issuer. Keep a photo of the mileage, your location, and any quote the driver gives you before the tow starts.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does my Visa credit card cover towing automatically?**
A: Visa Signature and Visa Infinite cards typically include roadside assistance as a covered benefit with no per-use fee, up to a set dollar limit per call. Standard Visa cards usually offer dispatch only, meaning you pay the tow company directly. Check your specific card's benefits guide to confirm which you have.

**Q: How do I actually use my credit card roadside assistance?**
A: Call the number on the back of your card and tell them you need roadside assistance. They route you to a dispatch network, which sends a provider to your location. If it's a true benefit, no payment is due at the scene up to your limit. If it's dispatch-only, have your card ready to pay the driver.

**Q: Will my credit card roadside assistance cover a long-distance tow?**
A: Most cards cap towing coverage at $50 to $100 per incident, which covers short local tows but not long ones. If you need a tow of 20 miles or more, expect to pay the difference between your card's cap and the actual bill.

**Q: Can I use my credit card roadside benefit more than once?**
A: Yes, but most programs limit you to four or five uses per year per account. After that, you either pay full price through the dispatch network or need a different coverage source.

**Q: What if the tow truck arrives and charges more than my card covers?**
A: You pay the balance directly to the driver, usually by card or cash. Get an itemized receipt, note the exact mileage, and contact your card issuer if the charge seems inflated. Some issuers will assist with disputes on roadside services billed through their network.
