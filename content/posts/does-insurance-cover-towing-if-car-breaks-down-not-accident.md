> **Quick Answer:** Standard liability insurance does not cover breakdown towing. You need either a roadside assistance add-on or comprehensive coverage with towing included. Check your policy's declarations page for "roadside assistance," "emergency road service," or "towing and labor." If you have AAA, that covers it regardless of your auto policy. Without one of these, you are paying out of pocket.

## Does My Regular Car Insurance Pay for a Breakdown Tow?

Not unless you added roadside assistance or towing and labor coverage to your policy. Basic liability only covers damage you cause to other people and their property. Collision covers your car after a crash. Neither touches a mechanical breakdown. Comprehensive covers things like theft, fire, and weather damage, but towing is a separate line item even there. The coverage you want is called "roadside assistance," "emergency road service," or "towing and labor," depending on your insurer.

To know for certain, pull up your declarations page right now. Look for one of those three phrases. If it is not listed, you do not have it.

## Which Insurance Add-Ons Actually Cover a Breakdown Tow?

Roadside assistance riders are the most common route. Most major insurers offer them: GEICO, State Farm, Progressive, USAA, Liberty Mutual, and others. The cost is usually a few dollars a month added to your premium. What you get in return varies more than people expect.

Typical breakdown towing coverage through an insurer will pay for a tow to the nearest qualified repair facility, often with a dollar cap or a mileage cap. Progressive, for example, caps towing reimbursement per incident. [Progressive's towing coverage limit per incident](/progressive-insurance-towing-coverage-limit-per-incident/) explains exactly where that ceiling sits and what happens when the bill goes over it.

USAA's roadside assistance works similarly, with its own caps and limits on how many times per year you can use it. If you are a USAA member, check [USAA roadside assistance towing coverage limits](/usaa-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-limit/) before you assume you are fully covered on a long haul.

If the tow is covered, the insurer typically reimburses you after the fact, or dispatches a provider directly. Either way, save your receipt.

## What If I Have AAA Instead of an Insurance Add-On?

AAA handles breakdown towing independently of your auto insurance policy. Your membership level determines how many miles are covered per tow. Classic membership covers up to 5 miles. Plus covers 100 miles. Premier covers 200 miles. Beyond your covered miles, you pay a per-mile rate.

For most local breakdowns, Classic is enough. For a highway breakdown far from home, it may not be. [AAA towing coverage miles per year](/aaa-towing-coverage-miles-per-year-limit/) breaks down exactly how the annual mileage limits work and when you can hit the ceiling without realizing it.

AAA does not file anything with your auto insurer. It is a separate membership service. Using it will not affect your insurance premium or claims history, which is one reason many drivers keep both.

## Should I Call My Insurer or a Tow Truck First?

Call your insurer or roadside assistance provider first if you have coverage. They either dispatch a contracted provider directly or give you a reimbursement authorization number to use when you call a tow company yourself. If you hire a tow company without authorization first, some insurers will deny reimbursement or reduce it to their contracted rate, which may be lower than what the tow company actually charged you.

If you are in a genuinely unsafe situation, call 911 or get clear of the vehicle first. Then worry about the tow. ## Common Questions

**Q: Does comprehensive insurance cover towing for a breakdown?**
A: Not automatically. Comprehensive covers damage from non-collision events like fire, theft, or weather. Towing after a mechanical breakdown requires a separate roadside assistance or towing and labor add-on, even if you have comprehensive.

**Q: Will using my roadside assistance for a breakdown raise my insurance rates?**
A: Generally no. Roadside assistance claims are typically not counted as at-fault incidents and do not trigger a rate increase with most major insurers. Confirm this with your specific carrier, as policies vary.

**Q: What if my car breaks down and I have no coverage at all?**
A: You will pay out of pocket. Call a local tow company directly, or use a service like Urgent.ly or Honk, which let you pay per use without a membership. Prices are comparable to what AAA charges above your mileage limit.

**Q: Does GEICO roadside assistance cover a breakdown tow?**
A: Yes, if you added the roadside assistance option to your GEICO policy. It covers towing to the nearest repair facility, lockout service, fuel delivery, and flat tire changes. The coverage is per incident and has a dollar limit.

**Q: Can I add roadside assistance to my policy after my car already broke down?**
A: No. You cannot add coverage mid-breakdown and then use it immediately. Insurers treat that as a pre-existing incident. Add it before you need it, the same way you would not buy flood insurance while your basement is flooding.
