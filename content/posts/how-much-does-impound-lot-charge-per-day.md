> **Quick Answer:** Most impound lots charge roughly $20 to $100 per day for storage, depending on the city, the lot, and your vehicle's size. That's on top of the tow fee and an administrative release fee. Costs add up fast, so the single best move is to retrieve your car within 24 hours, before another day's storage charge starts.

## How Much Does an Impound Lot Charge Per Day?

Expect roughly $20 to $100 per day in storage, with $30 to $60 being common in many metro areas. Rates vary by city, by whether the lot is city-run or private, and by what you drive.

Daily storage is only one piece of the bill. A typical impound total includes:

- **Tow fee:** often $100 to $300, higher for flatbeds, heavy trucks, or after-hours pickups
- **Daily storage:** the per-day charge above, sometimes billed per calendar day instead of per 24 hours
- **Release or administrative fee:** a flat fee, often $25 to $150, for processing paperwork
- **Gate or after-hours fee:** some lots charge extra to release a car outside business hours

Three days at $50 a day plus a $200 tow and a $75 release fee puts you around $425. A week turns it into a four-figure problem.

## Why Do Impound Fees Vary So Much?

Fees vary because cities and states set their own rate caps, and private lots charge whatever local rules allow. Some municipalities publish a maximum daily storage rate. Others leave it to the lot.

What pushes your daily rate up:

- **Vehicle size.** A box truck or RV costs more to store than a sedan.
- **Lot type.** Police-ordered impounds often follow city rate schedules. Private-property tows follow different rules, which is covered in [private property towing rules and who is responsible](/private-property-towing-rules-who-is-responsible/).
- **Calendar-day billing.** If a lot bills by calendar day, a car towed at 11:30 PM can owe two days by morning. Ask how they count a day before you assume anything.
- **Why the car was impounded.** A police hold after an accident, an arrest, or unpaid tickets can add fines, hold fees, or require a release authorization before you can pay anything.

## What Do I Need to Get My Car Out of Impound?

Bring a valid driver's license, proof you own or are authorized to take the car, and payment. Many lots want proof of current insurance and registration too.

Call the lot before you go. Ask these exact questions:

1. What is the total owed right now, itemized?
2. What forms of payment do you accept? Some lots are cash only or add a card surcharge.
3. What are your pickup hours, and is there an after-hours fee?
4. Do I need a release form from the police or the towing authority first?
5. When does the next day's storage start?

If the car was towed after a crash or arrest, the lot may refuse release until police clear the hold. Paying storage while waiting on that paperwork is common, so get the release moving the same day.

## How Can I Keep the Daily Charges From Piling Up?

Get there fast and get your documents in order.

 Every day you wait adds another storage charge, and lots can eventually sell a vehicle at lien auction if fees go unpaid.

Practical ways to cut the bill:

- **Go within 24 hours.** One day of storage beats three.
- **Check your coverage.** If the car was towed after a covered accident, some insurance policies reimburse towing and storage. See [who pays for towing after an accident that wasn't your fault](/who-pays-for-towing-after-a-car-accident-not-my-fault/).
- **Ask what's negotiable.** Release and admin fees are usually fixed, but you can ask whether the lot offers any hardship option. Our guide on [whether you can negotiate tow truck price](/can-you-negotiate-tow-truck-price/) covers what actually works.
- **Don't abandon it.** Walking away doesn't end the debt. Fees keep accruing, and unpaid fees can lead to a lien sale and collections.

If you can't afford the full amount right away, call the lot and ask about their policy before storage runs up. Some will hold the car a short time if you contact them early.

## What If I Think the Impound Charges Are Wrong?

Pay to get the car out first if you need it, then dispute the charges. Most lots won't release a vehicle while you argue, and storage keeps running either way.

Get an itemized receipt. Photograph your car and the lot's posted rate sign before you drive off. Check the daily rate against any posted or published schedule for that city. If the tow was improper, such as a car towed from a legal spot, you may be able to recover the money.

## Common Questions

**Q: Do impound lots charge for the day I pick up my car?**
A: Often yes. Many lots bill by calendar day, so any part of a day counts as a full day. Ask whether they charge per 24 hours or per calendar day.

**Q: Can an impound lot keep my car if I can't pay?**
A: Yes. The lot has a lien on the vehicle until fees are paid. If they stay unpaid long enough, the lot can sell the car at auction under state law.

**Q: Can someone else pick up my car from impound?**
A: Usually, with written authorization, a copy of your ID, and the ownership and insurance documents. Policies differ by lot, so call first.

**Q: Are impound fees covered by insurance?**
A: Sometimes. If the tow followed a covered accident or loss, your policy may reimburse towing and storage. Standard coverage rarely pays for parking or ticket-related impounds.

**Q: How long do I have before my car is sold from impound?**
A: It varies by state, often a few weeks to a couple of months after the lot sends legal notice. Don't wait. Call the lot and your local police non-emergency line to confirm your deadline.
