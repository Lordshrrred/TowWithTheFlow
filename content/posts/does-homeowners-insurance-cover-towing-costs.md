> **Quick Answer:** Homeowners insurance does not cover towing costs. It covers your house and personal property inside it, not your vehicle or anything that happens to it on the road. Towing coverage comes from your auto insurance policy (usually as a roadside assistance add-on), a membership like AAA, or your credit card. Check those three sources first.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover My Car at All?

No. Homeowners insurance and your vehicle are almost entirely separate worlds. Your homeowners policy covers the structure of your home, your personal belongings inside it, and liability for injuries that happen on your property. Your car parked in the driveway is specifically excluded from most standard homeowners policies, including HO-3 and HO-5 forms, which are the two most common policy types sold in the United States.

The one narrow exception some people confuse things with: personal property stolen from inside your car (a laptop, camera gear, a bag) may be covered under the personal property portion of your homeowners or renters policy, subject to your deductible. But the car itself, a breakdown, a dead battery, a blown tire, and any resulting tow? Not covered.

## So What Insurance Actually Covers Towing?

Your auto insurance policy can cover towing, but only if you have the right add-on. The coverage to look for is called roadside assistance or emergency road service. It's a separate endorsement, typically $5 to $15 per month depending on your insurer and state. Without it, even a comprehensive and collision policy won't pay for a tow unless the tow is directly tied to a covered accident.

If your car breaks down without any collision involved, the path to coverage is roadside assistance, not comprehensive. [Does liability insurance cover towing costs](/does-liability-insurance-cover-towing-costs/) breaks down the other common policy types and what they actually pay.

Major insurers handle this differently. GEICO, Progressive, State Farm, USAA, and Liberty Mutual all offer roadside assistance add-ons with varying reimbursement caps, typically $50 to $100 per tow. If you have GEICO, check [how much GEICO roadside assistance actually pays](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-how-much-will-they-pay/) before you assume you're fully covered.

## What If I Don't Have Roadside Assistance on My Auto Policy?

You still have options. Check these three in order:

1. **AAA membership.** Covers towing up to a set number of miles per year depending on your membership tier. Classic members get up to 5 miles per tow; Plus and Premier members get significantly more. ## Will My Homeowners Insurance Ever Play Any Role Here?

Rarely, and only in very specific situations. If your vehicle is damaged by a fire that started in your garage and spread to your car, the car damage is still handled by your auto insurance (specifically, comprehensive coverage). Your homeowners policy covers the garage structure, not the vehicle inside it.

The only scenario where a homeowners claim might touch your vehicle indirectly: if a tree from your property falls on a neighbor's car, your homeowners liability coverage could respond to their claim. Your own car, still handled by your auto policy.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does renters insurance cover towing costs?**
A: No. Renters insurance covers your personal belongings and personal liability, not your vehicle or anything related to it. Towing coverage has to come from your auto policy or a separate roadside membership.

**Q: Can I add towing coverage to my auto insurance policy today, even if my car is already broken down?**
A: No. You cannot add coverage after a loss has already occurred. Insurers call this "post-loss binding" and it's not allowed. Add roadside assistance to your policy now, before you need it, or use AAA or a credit card for the current situation.

**Q: What's the cheapest way to get towing coverage going forward?**
A: Adding roadside assistance to an existing auto policy is usually the cheapest route, often $5 to $10 per month. AAA Classic membership runs around $60 to $70 per year. Both are cheaper than a single out-of-pocket tow in most cities.

**Q: If I got into an accident, does my auto insurance cover the tow?**
A: It depends on your policy. If you have collision coverage, the tow from an accident scene is usually covered as part of the claim. Without roadside assistance or collision, you may be paying yourself. [Does comprehensive insurance cover towing after an accident](/does-comprehensive-insurance-cover-towing-after-accident/) explains how the coverage layers work.

**Q: Does GAP insurance cover towing?**
A: No. GAP insurance only covers the difference between what you owe on your car loan and what your insurer pays out after a total loss. It has nothing to do with towing or roadside service.
