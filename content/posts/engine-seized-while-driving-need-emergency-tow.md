> **Quick Answer:** Pull over immediately and shut the engine off if it hasn't already died. Do not try to restart it. A seized engine means internal metal parts have stopped moving, either from oil starvation, overheating, or a thrown rod. The car cannot be driven. You need a flatbed tow truck, not a wheel-lift, because dragging a seized drivetrain on its wheels can damage the transmission or axle. Call for a tow now.

## What Exactly Happens When an Engine Seizes While You're Driving?

The engine locks up because internal components, usually the pistons or crankshaft bearings, have fused or jammed from friction, heat, or lack of lubrication. You'll typically hear a loud knock or bang, feel the engine lose power suddenly, and then it either stalls or you kill it before it tears itself apart further. In a hydrolocked engine, water enters the cylinders and the pistons physically cannot compress it, so they stop. In an oil-starved seizure, metal grinds on metal until something welds itself in place or shatters.

Either way, the engine is not restarting on the side of the road. This is not a jump-start situation.

## What Do I Do the Moment My Engine Seizes?

Signal immediately and get to the shoulder or the nearest exit. If the engine stalled on its own, you still have one good pump of the brake pedal and some steering assist before the power systems go dead. Use both. Coast as far off the travel lane as possible.

Once stopped: turn on your hazard lights, put the car in park, and do not turn the key again. Cranking a seized engine can snap the starter motor or crack the block. If you have emergency triangles or flares, set them out behind the car. Stay inside with your seatbelt on if you're on a high-speed road. Read through the safety steps at [broken down on the highway at night](/broken-down-on-highway-at-night-safety-tips-what-to-do/) before you get out if traffic is moving fast.

Call 911 if you're in a dangerous position, then call a tow truck or your roadside assistance provider.

## Does My Insurance Cover the Tow After an Engine Seizure?

It depends on your policy. Roadside assistance add-ons through GEICO, State Farm, USAA, and AAA typically cover towing to the nearest repair facility regardless of why the car broke down. Mechanical breakdown coverage is a separate product and not common. The tow itself is usually covered if you have roadside. The engine repair is almost never covered under a standard auto policy unless it was caused by a covered collision or a manufacturer defect under warranty.

Check your insurance app right now before you call a tow company. Knowing your coverage saves you from paying $200 out of pocket when your insurer would have sent a truck for free. See [does GEICO cover towing after breakdown](/does-geico-cover-towing-after-breakdown/) or [does USAA cover emergency roadside towing costs](/does-usaa-cover-emergency-roadside-towing-costs/) for specifics by insurer.

## Can the Engine Be Saved, or Is It a Total Loss?

It depends on what caused the seizure and how fast you shut it down. A mild seizure from oil starvation caught early, where bearings are scored but the block is intact, might be repairable with a rebuild. A thrown connecting rod that punched through the engine block is a total loss. A hydrolocked engine that was shut off immediately sometimes comes out fine after the water is removed and injectors are inspected.

Do not let anyone sell you a diagnosis on the side of the road. Get it to a shop, have the oil checked for metal shavings, and ask for a borescope inspection if the shop has one. That tells you immediately whether the cylinder walls are intact. Your tow destination matters here: bring it to a shop that does engine work, not just an oil change franchise.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive even a short distance with a seized engine?**
A: No. A seized engine either will not turn over at all or will destroy itself further if it does. Even a quarter mile can turn a repairable seizure into a full block replacement.

**Q: Will a regular tow truck work or do I specifically need a flatbed?**
A: Request a flatbed. If your drivetrain is seized and the wheels cannot roll freely, dragging them on a wheel-lift can damage the transmission, CV axles, or differential. A flatbed carries all four wheels off the ground.

**Q: How do I know if my engine seized from low oil vs. overheating?**
A: Check the oil dipstick and the temperature gauge history. If the oil is empty or looks like dark sludge, oil starvation is likely. If the temperature gauge was pegged in the red before the seizure, overheating is the culprit. Both cause the same end result, but the cause matters for the repair estimate.

**Q: Should I have the car towed to a dealer or an independent shop?**
A: If your car is under manufacturer warranty or powertrain warranty, go to the dealer first. Otherwise, an independent shop that specializes in engine rebuilds will usually charge less and diagnose faster. Call ahead before the tow truck arrives so the shop knows to expect the car.

**Q: What information should I give the tow company when I call?**
A: Your exact location (mile marker, exit number, cross street, or GPS coordinates from your phone), the year, make, model, and drivetrain type (front-wheel, rear-wheel, or all-wheel drive), and the fact that you need a flatbed because the engine is seized. AWD and 4WD vehicles especially require flatbed transport.
