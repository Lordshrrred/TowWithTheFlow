> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in Boston runs $75 to $175 for the first 5 miles, with most drivers paying $100 to $150 for a standard wheel-lift truck in the city. Flatbeds cost more, usually $125 to $200 for the same distance. On I-93, the Mass Pike (I-90), or the Southeast Expressway, expect towing fees to climb fast once per-mile charges kick in, typically $3 to $6 per mile after the base hook-up fee.

## What Does It Actually Cost to Get Towed in Boston Right Now?

A hook-up fee of $75 to $125 is your baseline, and that covers the truck showing up and loading your car. After that, per-mile charges of $3 to $6 apply to every mile beyond the first 5. A tow from Dorchester to a shop in the South End might run $90 to $110. A tow from a breakdown on I-93 near the Braintree split all the way up to a dealer in Burlington could easily hit $200 to $275.

Flatbed towing, which you need for all-wheel-drive vehicles, lowered cars, and most luxury or sports cars, runs a flat $25 to $50 more than a wheel-lift on the same route. If you are not sure whether your car needs a flatbed, [this breakdown will help you decide](/how-do-i-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/).

## Why Does Boston Cost More Than the National Average?

Dense urban traffic, strict city parking rules, and high operator costs in Massachusetts all drive prices up. The Big Dig tunnels on I-93, the O'Neill Tunnel, and the Ted Williams Tunnel all create bottlenecks that add time to every tow. Time is money for the driver behind the wheel of that truck. Night and weekend rates add another $25 to $50 on top of standard pricing, and most shops in Boston apply them from 6 p.m. to 8 a.m.

Storage fees are also a real concern here. Boston impound lots charge $35 to $60 per day. If your car gets towed after an accident on the Southeast Expressway on a Friday night, and you cannot reach your insurance adjuster until Monday, you are looking at two or three days of storage stacking up before you even deal with the repair bill.

## Does Insurance Cover This, or Am I Paying Out of Pocket?

If you have roadside assistance through your auto insurance policy, it likely covers a tow up to a set dollar amount or mileage limit, but read the fine print. Many Massachusetts policies cap towing reimbursement at $50 to $100, which covers a short local tow but leaves you short on anything heading out to the suburbs. AAA members in Massachusetts get towing coverage up to 5 miles on the Classic tier and up to 100 miles on the Premier tier.

If you are paying cash with no coverage, get a price confirmed before the truck hooks up. Reputable companies will give you a quote. ## What If I Break Down on the Mass Pike or I-93?

Call 911 first if you are in a live lane. If you are safely on the shoulder, you can call a private tow company directly. Massachusetts State Police patrol major highways including I-90 and I-93, and they can dispatch a tow through their contracted rotation system. That rotation tow will often cost more than calling a private company yourself, frequently $150 to $250 for a short haul, because those contracts prioritize availability over price.

If you break down near the I-93/I-90 interchange (the South Bay area), or on the elevated section near Chinatown, getting a tow truck into position can take 30 to 60 minutes in peak hours because of access restrictions. Stay in your car with the seatbelt on if traffic is moving around you. Hazard lights on, wheels turned toward the guardrail if you are on a shoulder. ## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a flatbed tow cost in Boston?**
A: A flatbed tow in Boston runs $125 to $200 for a local haul under 5 miles. Per-mile charges after that are $4 to $7, slightly higher than wheel-lift rates because of the equipment involved.

**Q: Is towing in Boston more expensive at night?**
A: Yes. Most Boston-area tow operators charge a night rate after 6 p.m. and on weekends, adding $25 to $50 to the base hook-up fee. Holiday rates can be even higher.

**Q: Can I negotiate the tow price in Boston?**
A: You can ask, but do not expect much movement on the base hook-up fee. Where you have some leverage is asking whether they will waive the first mile or two if you are going to their affiliated shop for repairs. Some operators will discount the tow if it leads to a repair job.

**Q: What happens if my car gets towed by the city of Boston?**
A: Boston city tows, typically for parking violations or blocking a fire lane, carry a tow fee set by the city plus daily storage charges. You pick up the vehicle at the towing company the city contracts with and pay before they release the car. The city's tow fee is separate from any parking ticket you also owe.

**Q: How do I find a reputable tow company in Boston without getting overcharged?**
A: Call two or three companies and ask for a quote including the hook-up fee and per-mile rate before anyone rolls a truck. Check that the company is licensed in Massachusetts. If your insurer has a preferred provider list, start there, those companies have agreed to controlled rates.
