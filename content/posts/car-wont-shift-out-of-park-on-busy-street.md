> **Quick Answer:** Press the brake pedal hard, then try the shifter again. If it won't move, use the shift lock release: a small slot or cap near the shifter that lets you push the lever out of park with a key or screwdriver. Turn on your hazards first. A dead battery or a failed brake light switch causes most stuck-in-park cases. If the release doesn't work, call for a tow.

## What Do I Do First When My Car Won't Come Out of Park on a Busy Street?

Turn your hazard lights on immediately, keep your foot on the brake, and stay buckled in. Your car is stopped, so your first job is making sure other drivers see it.

Then run through this quick sequence:

1. Keep the engine running, or turn the key to "ON" if it's off.
2. Press the brake pedal firmly with your whole foot. A light tap won't release the lock.
3. Wiggle the shifter gently while pressing the button on the side (if yours has one).
4. Try again after turning the steering wheel slightly. Sometimes a loaded wheel puts pressure on the park pawl.

If you're sitting in a live lane, don't get out unless you can reach a sidewalk safely. If traffic is dodging around you, call 911 and tell them you're blocking a lane. That brings a patrol car with lights, which protects you better than hazards alone.

## How Do I Use the Shift Lock Release to Get Out of Park?

The shift lock release is a manual override, usually a small rectangular slot or a pop-off cap right beside the shifter. Pry the cap off with a key or flat screwdriver, push the key or screwdriver into the slot, hold it down, and press the brake while shifting to neutral.

Details that matter:

- Some cars hide it under a plastic trim piece on the console. Check your owner's manual or search your exact make and model.
- Honda, Toyota, Ford, and Chevy all use some version of this. The location varies.
- Put the car in neutral, not drive, so you can push it or get it towed.
- Once in neutral, the release only gets you moving. It does not fix the underlying problem.

If you don't have a manual in the glovebox, most owner's manuals are available as PDFs on the manufacturer's website. Pull it up on your phone.

## Why Won't My Car Shift Out of Park in the First Place?

The most common causes are a weak or dead battery, a failed brake light switch, or a worn shift interlock solenoid. All three stop the electronic lock from releasing.

Here's how to narrow it down fast:

- **Brake lights don't work when you press the pedal:** Have a bystander check, or look at the reflection in a shop window. A bad brake light switch is a cheap part, often under $30, but it keeps the interlock locked.
- **Dash is dim, clicking, or dead:** Your battery is the problem. See [car won't start with a clicking sound](/car-wont-start-clicking-sound-need-tow-or-jump-start/) for the jump-or-tow decision.
- **Parked on a steep slope:** The weight of the car is pressing hard on the park pawl. Rolling slightly or shifting weight sometimes frees it.

## Should I Push the Car Out of the Way or Wait for a Tow?

If you've gotten it into neutral and the street is clear enough, you can roll it to safety. Otherwise, stay put with hazards on and wait for help. Pushing a car in live traffic gets people hurt.

Only push if all of these are true:

- You have a second person to steer and brake while you push.
- The path is flat or downhill to a safe spot, like a side street or parking lane.
- You can see traffic in both directions and nobody is closing fast.

Never push alone on a busy street while someone sits in the driver's seat unless they are actively on the brake. If anything is uncertain, wait. Tow trucks are used to this call. If the wait stretches long, see [what to do if your tow truck is taking too long](/tow-truck-taking-too-long-what-are-my-options-roadside/).

Tell the dispatcher your car is stuck in park and blocking traffic. Ask for a flatbed. Because the transmission is locked, a flatbed avoids dragging the drive wheels. A wheel-lift tow with the car stuck in park can damage the transmission.

## Is It Safe to Drive After I Get It Out of Park?

If a battery jump or brake switch fix solved it, you can drive carefully to a shop. If you had to use the shift lock release to get moving, expect it to happen again.

The release bypasses the safety interlock, which exists to stop the car from lurching when you start it. Driving on the bypass means the next failure could come at a bad time. Get the brake light switch, battery, or shift interlock checked soon.

## Common Questions

**Q: Why won't my car come out of park even when I press the brake?**
A: Usually the brake light switch has failed or the battery is too weak to power the shift interlock solenoid. Check if your brake lights turn on, and look for a dim dashboard.

**Q: Can I damage my transmission by forcing the shifter?**
A: Yes. Never force it. Use the shift lock release slot instead of yanking the lever. Forcing it can snap the shift cable or bend the linkage.

**Q: How much does it cost to fix a car stuck in park?**
A: A brake light switch is often $20 to $60 for the part plus shop labor. A shift interlock solenoid or shift cable repair can cost more. A dead battery replacement is the cheapest common fix.

**Q: Can I drive with the shift lock release in use?**
A: Technically yes, but the safety interlock is bypassed, so the car can be started in gear. Fix the root cause rather than relying on the override.

**Q: Will AAA or my insurance cover a tow if my car is stuck in park?**
A: Most roadside plans cover a tow for a disabled vehicle, including one that won't shift. Check your plan's tow distance limit before the driver arrives.
