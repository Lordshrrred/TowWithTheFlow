> **Quick Answer:** If your transmission is slipping on the highway, get off at the next exit and stop. A mild, occasional slip with normal fluid might get you a short distance on surface streets. Hard slips, no power, a burning smell, or a flashing check engine light mean tow it. Every mile on a slipping transmission can turn a repair into a full rebuild.

## Should I Keep Driving or Pull Over Right Now?

Pull over at the next safe exit or shoulder. A slipping transmission can lose drive without warning, and you do not want that to happen in the left lane at 70 mph.

Signs you are slipping: the engine revs climb but the car does not speed up, the RPM jumps between gear changes, or the car lurches into gear. Slipping means the clutch packs inside the transmission are not gripping, and that slip creates heat. Heat is what destroys a transmission.

Get to the right shoulder or an exit ramp. Turn on your hazards, put it in park, and let it sit. Do not keep testing it "just to see."

## Can I Make It Home on a Slipping Transmission?

Only if the slip is mild, the car still pulls normally most of the time, and home is a few miles away on surface streets. Anything else is a tow.

Run this quick check once you are safely stopped and the engine has idled a few minutes:

- **Pull the transmission dipstick** (most older cars have one, many newer ones do not). With the engine running and the car in park, healthy fluid is bright red or pink. Brown or black fluid that smells burnt means the clutches are already cooked.
- **Low fluid** is the one cheap cause. If it is low, a quart or two of the correct fluid for your car can sometimes restore normal shifting. Check the owner's manual for the exact type. Wrong fluid causes its own damage.
- **Shift into Drive and Reverse** in a parking lot. If it takes several seconds to engage or will not engage, do not drive it.

If you cannot confirm the problem is low fluid, assume it is internal and tow it. Highway speeds make it worse, because the transmission works hardest in the upper gears and under load.

## When Is Driving a Slipping Transmission a Bad Idea?

Never drive it if any of these apply: the slip happens every shift, there is a burning smell, the check engine or transmission temperature light is on, or the car goes into limp mode. Limp mode locks the car into one gear to protect itself. Take that warning seriously.

Reasons to stop:

- **Heat compounds the damage.** Slipping burns clutch material, which contaminates the fluid, which causes more slipping.
- **You can lose all drive.** A failing transmission can drop out of gear on a ramp or in traffic.
- **Cost jumps fast.** Catching it early can mean a fluid service or a solenoid. Driving it home can mean a rebuild or replacement, often several thousand dollars on most vehicles.

## Does It Need a Flatbed
![tow truck loading car](/images/transmission-slipping-on-highway-can-i-make-it-home-or-tow/mid.jpg)

 Tow?

Yes, for most cars. A flatbed lifts all four wheels off the ground so the transmission never turns, and that is the safest way to move a damaged one.

This matters most for all-wheel-drive vehicles, where dragging on two wheels can damage the drivetrain even with a healthy transmission. A front-wheel-drive car with a bad automatic can sometimes be pulled on a wheel-lift with the drive wheels raised, but ask the driver which they are using. Tell dispatch up front: "automatic transmission slipping, wheels need to come off the ground." Not sure which truck you need? Use this [flatbed tow checklist](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/).

If it is a weekend or late night, rates run higher. See [what emergency towing costs on weekends](/how-much-does-emergency-towing-cost-on-weekends/) before you agree to a price. Ask for the total, including hookup and per-mile charges, before the truck leaves.

## What Do I Do While I Wait for the Tow?

Stay safe first. On a highway shoulder, get everyone out on the passenger side and well away from traffic, behind the guardrail if there is one. Wait outside the vehicle, not inside it.

Call your insurer or roadside plan first, since towing is often already covered. Have your location ready from a mile marker or the nearest exit number. If help is slow, [how long you can wait for a tow truck on the highway](/how-long-can-you-wait-for-a-tow-truck-on-highway/) covers when to escalate. Do not restart the engine to "check if it fixed itself."

## Common Questions

**Q: How do I know if my transmission is slipping or something else is wrong?**
A: Slipping feels like the engine revving high without the car accelerating to match. A misfire or bad fuel delivery feels like shaking or stumbling, not a rev-without-speed feeling. A flashing check engine light with shaking points to the engine instead.

**Q: Will low transmission fluid cause slipping?**
A: Yes, low fluid is the most common cheap cause. Low fluid means low hydraulic pressure, so the clutches cannot grip. Topping off with the correct fluid can help, but a leak means you still have to fix the source.

**Q: Can I drive a slipping transmission a short distance to a shop?**
A: Only if the slip is mild and the shop is very close on slow roads. Highway speeds and longer trips raise the heat fast and risk leaving you stranded or ruining the transmission.

**Q: Is a slipping transmission covered by roadside assistance?**
A: Roadside plans usually cover the tow to a shop, up to the plan's mileage limit. They do not cover the repair itself. Check your tow distance cap before you pick a shop that is far away.

**Q: Can a slipping transmission be fixed without replacing it?**
A: Sometimes. A fluid and filter service, a bad solenoid, or a faulty sensor can fix mild slipping. Burnt fluid and worn clutch packs usually mean a rebuild or replacement.
