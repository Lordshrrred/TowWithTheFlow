> **Quick Answer:** Pull completely off the road if you can, turn on your hazards, and stay inside the car. In a Denver blizzard, the car is your shelter. Call for a tow immediately because wait times spike during storms. If you're on I-70, I-25, or C-470, expect delays of 60 to 90 minutes or longer. Keep the exhaust pipe clear of snow if you run the heat.

## What Do I Do the Second My Car Dies in a Denver Blizzard?

Get off the road as far as possible, hazard lights on, and stay in the vehicle. A dead car on the shoulder of I-25 near the 6th Avenue interchange or on I-70 heading into the foothills puts you in real danger from passing vehicles that can't see clearly through blowing snow. If you can coast to an exit, do it. Exits along Federal Boulevard, Colorado Boulevard, or Havana Street give you gas stations and shelter within walking distance if conditions get truly dangerous.

Once stopped:

- Set your parking brake.
- Keep interior lights on so you're visible.
- Crack a window slightly if you're running the engine for heat. Carbon monoxide buildup is a real risk when snow packs around the exhaust pipe.
- Get out briefly to check that the tailpipe isn't buried in snow, then get back in.
- Place road flares or reflective triangles behind the car if you have them.

Do not stand outside the vehicle on a highway shoulder in a blizzard. Stay buckled if you're in a travel lane.

## Who Do I Call for a Tow During a Denver Blizzard?

Call a tow company immediately, then call 911 if you're in a dangerous position on the road. During active Denver blizzards, tow trucks are dispatched from multiple yards, but demand overwhelms supply fast. If you have AAA, use the app. If you don't, search for 24-hour towing companies serving Denver metro and call two or three simultaneously. Tell each dispatcher your exact location including mile marker if you're on a highway.

For response time expectations during a snow emergency, [tow truck response time in Denver]((/tow-truck-response-time-denver-colorado/) can stretch well past an hour. The [tow truck cost during a Denver snow emergency](/tow-truck-denver-co/) typically runs $100 to $175 for a local pull, but blizzard surcharges are common. You also have the option of dialing 511 for Colorado road conditions and CDOT updates while you wait.

Do not try to walk to safety on I-70 between C-470 and Genesee during a mountain blizzard. That stretch has virtually no shoulder and minimal lighting.

## Is It Safe to Stay in My Car During a Denver Winter Storm?

Yes, staying in the car is almost always safer than walking in blizzard conditions. The exception is if your exhaust is buried and you can smell fumes. The car shields you from windchill, which in a Denver blizzard can push effective temperatures below negative 20 degrees Fahrenheit, and it makes you visible to rescuers. Keep the engine running in short cycles: 10 minutes on, 10 minutes off, checking the tailpipe each time.

If you have any emergency supplies in the car, now is the time to use them. A mylar emergency blanket, extra gloves, or even a floor mat over your legs buys significant warmth if fuel runs low. Denver can see snowfall rates of 3 to 5 inches per hour during a bomb cyclone event, so don't assume conditions will clear quickly.

## What If My Car Won't Start Again After the Blizzard Passes?

A car that dies in a blizzard and won't restart often has one of three issues: a battery that failed under the cold load (lead-acid batteries lose roughly half their cranking power at 0 degrees Fahrenheit), a fuel system problem like a frozen fuel line in older vehicles, or a flooded engine from repeated failed start attempts. If you hear one click and nothing else, that points squarely to the battery or starter. See [car won't start, clicks once and nothing](/car-wont-start-clicks-once-and-nothing-else/) for what that symptom means mechanically.

If the car cranks but won't fire, stop cranking after three attempts. Continued attempts drain the battery further and can flood the engine. At that point, you need a tow to a shop regardless. Check with your insurance provider about roadside assistance reimbursement before you pay out of pocket.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I get towed off I-70 during a Colorado blizzard closure?**
A: Yes. CDOT closures don't prevent tow trucks from operating. Call a private tow company. If CDOT has closed the highway and your car is blocking a lane, Colorado State Patrol may dispatch a tow on their rotation, but you still pay.

**Q: Should I call 911 if my car breaks down in a Denver blizzard?**
A: Call 911 if you're in an active travel lane, injured, or your exhaust is buried and you can't safely clear it. For a car safely on the shoulder, call a tow company first. 911 dispatch will send a trooper when they can, but it won't speed up the tow.

**Q: Will my insurance cover a tow during a Denver blizzard?**
A: It depends on your policy. Comprehensive or roadside assistance add-ons typically cover it. Liability-only policies do not. Call your insurer while you wait. Most have 24-hour claims lines and can authorize towing directly.

**Q: What should I keep in my car for a Denver winter emergency?**
A: At minimum: a mylar emergency blanket, a small shovel, cat litter or sand for traction, jumper cables or a jump starter pack, a flashlight, and a phone charger. A full gas tank before any winter mountain drive is not optional.

**Q: How much will a tow cost during a Denver blizzard?**
A: A local tow within the metro runs $100 to $200 during a snow event, sometimes more with a blizzard surcharge. Tows from the foothills or I-70 mountain corridor into Denver can run $300 or higher depending on distance and road conditions.
