> **Quick Answer:** Toll road towing costs $150-400 for basic service, plus $50-150 toll road surcharges. You pay the tow truck driver directly, then collect tolls separately from the toll authority. Emergency lane positioning adds $25-75 to base rates due to safety risks and traffic control requirements.

## What To Do

1. **Get out of traffic immediately.** Pull as far right as possible into the emergency lane. Turn on hazard lights and set parking brake. If your vehicle dies in a live lane and you cannot move it, stay inside with your seatbelt on until help arrives.

2. **Exit through the passenger side.** Never exit toward traffic. Move behind the concrete barrier or as far from your vehicle as safely possible. On narrow toll road shoulders, that barrier is often only a few feet away, so stay tight against it.

3. **Call 911 first if you're in active traffic lanes.** Highway patrol will secure the scene before towing arrives. They may also dispatch their preferred tow company. In some states, like Florida and Texas, highway patrol has contracted towers on rotation for exactly these situations, which can cut your wait time significantly.

4. **Note your exact mile marker.** Toll road dispatchers need precise location for billing and routing. Look for green mile markers along the roadside. If you cannot find one, note the nearest on-ramp or interchange name, since toll road operators can cross-reference those against their camera system to locate you.

5. **Ask about toll charges upfront.** The tow truck cannot pay tolls for your vehicle. You handle this separately with the toll authority after service. Most authorities will mail a bill to the registered owner based on license plate reads from overhead gantries along the route.

6. **Request flatbed service.** Emergency lane positioning makes wheel-lift towing dangerous. Flatbed costs $25-50 more but protects your transmission and other vehicles. All-wheel-drive and four-wheel-drive vehicles should never be wheel-lifted anyway, since doing so can destroy the transfer case.

## What It Might Cost

**Base towing rates:** $125-300 depending on distance and time of day. [Construction zones may require police escort](/towing-cost-construction-zone-police-required/), adding $75-150 to your bill. Peak congestion hours, typically 7-9 AM and 4-7 PM on weekdays, sometimes carry a demand surcharge of $20-40 because the tow truck itself has to navigate heavy traffic to reach you.

**Toll road surcharges:** $50-150 for emergency lane service. This covers traffic control, extended setup time, and increased insurance liability. Some companies charge a flat emergency access fee; others build it into a higher hourly labor rate. Ask which method they use before you agree, so you are not surprised by a line item that doubles the base quote.

**Additional fees:**
- After hours (6 PM - 6 AM): $25-75
- Weekend service: $15-50
- Flatbed upgrade: $25-50
- Mileage beyond 5-10 miles: $3-7 per mile

**Toll collection:** You pay tolls separately. Most toll authorities bill your license plate for the tow truck's passage. Expect $15-45 depending on distance traveled. If you have a transponder like E-ZPass or SunPass in the vehicle, your account may be charged automatically during the tow. Check your account history a few days later and dispute any charges for exits or ramps you did not actually use.

Your insurance roadside coverage applies to toll roads the same as regular highways. [Check if your policy covers the full amount](/towing-cost-from-interstate-to-dealership/) or just a portion. Many standard roadside plans cap reimbursement at $100-125, which may not fully cover a toll road call with surcharges. Premium roadside add-ons from AAA or your insurer often cover $200-300.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I just wait in my car on the toll road shoulder until a friend picks me up?**
A: Not a good idea. Most toll road authorities treat stopped vehicles as active hazards and will dispatch a patrol truck to move you within 15-30 minutes regardless. You will likely end up being towed anyway, often to an impound lot of their choosing rather than your preferred shop.

**Q: Will my roadside assistance plan cover the toll road surcharge specifically?**
A: It depends on the plan. Basic coverage through your insurer or a credit card usually reimburses a flat dollar amount and does not itemize surcharges separately, so it may cover the whole bill or leave you with a gap. AAA Premier and similar premium tiers tend to be more flexible. Call your provider before the truck leaves to get a confirmation number.

**Q: Does the tow truck driver handle the toll booths, or do I need to pay those on the spot?**
A: The driver handles the physical passage through any toll points along the tow route. You are not expected to hand over cash at the booth. However, you are responsible for the charges afterward, either through a plate-based bill from the toll authority or a charge to your transponder account.

## Stay Safe

- Never attempt repairs in the emergency lane
- Keep hazard lights on until tow truck arrives
- Stay behind barriers or far from your vehicle
- Have exact mile marker ready for dispatcher
- Confirm tow destination before truck leaves
- Take photos of your vehicle's position for insurance

Most toll authorities work directly with towing companies for emergency lane calls. The tow truck knows which exit to use and how to handle toll booth procedures. Your job is staying safe until help arrives.
