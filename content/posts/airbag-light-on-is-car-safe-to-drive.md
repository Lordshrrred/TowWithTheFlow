> **Quick Answer:** The car will usually drive fine with the airbag light on, but the airbag system is likely disabled, meaning it may not deploy in a crash. Brakes, steering, and engine are not affected. Drive carefully to a shop within a day or two. If the light came on after an accident, or comes with other warning lights or odd behavior, have it inspected before driving further.

## Is It Safe to Drive With the Airbag Light On?

Mechanically, yes. Practically, you're driving without your airbag protection until it's fixed. The airbag light (often labeled SRS, for Supplemental Restraint System) doesn't touch your brakes, steering, or engine. The car will drive normally.

What the light tells you is that the SRS computer found a fault and likely shut the system down. In a crash, the airbags and seat belt pretensioners may not fire. The seat belts still work as regular belts, so wear them every time.

Short trip home or to a shop is reasonable. A months-long commute with the light on is not. You're skipping a major safety system, and you also can't count on it for passengers or kids in the front seat.

## What Causes the Airbag Light to Come On?

The most common cause is a bad connection or failed sensor, not a failing airbag. Typical culprits:

- **Clockspring failure.** This coiled ribbon behind the steering wheel carries the signal to the driver airbag. It wears out from years of turning, and a common sign is a dead horn or steering wheel buttons along with the light.
- **Seat belt buckle sensor.** Dirt, coins, or a worn connector can trip it.
- **Passenger occupancy sensor.** The mat in the front passenger seat weighs whoever sits there. A bad mat, or a heavy object on the seat, can trigger the light.
- **Loose under-seat connector.** The yellow plugs under the front seats get kicked, bumped, or corroded. Cleaning or reseating a connector sometimes clears it, but airbag wiring is not a casual DIY job.
- **Low battery voltage.** A weak or recently replaced battery can set the light, and some cars need the code cleared afterward.
- **Crash sensor or SRS module fault.** Less common, more expensive.

Don't pull connectors or poke around under the seats yourself. Airbag circuits can deploy if handled wrong.

## What If the Airbag Light Came On After an Accident?

Treat it as a different situation and have the car inspected before you keep driving it. After a collision, the light can mean sensors or the module were damaged, or that an airbag deployed and the system needs repair. A deployed airbag is not something to drive around on.

Look for a deployed bag, a damaged dash or steering wheel cover, or crash damage near the front sensors. If the car is otherwise drivable, a short trip to a shop is possible, but if the front end is crushed or fluids are leaking, don't drive it. ## When Should I Stop Driving and Call a Tow Truck?

Stop and tow the car if the airbag light comes with other symptoms. A light by itself is a get-it-checked problem. A light plus any of these means the car needs to stay put:

- Brake pedal feels soft or goes to the floor. See [what to do if the brake pedal goes to the floor](/brake-pedal-went-to-floor-while-driving-what-to-do/).
- Steering feels loose, heavy, or makes noise. A failing clockspring alone won't do this, but combined damage can.
- Multiple warning lights at once (ABS, traction control, check engine).
- Burning smell, smoke, or fluid leaking under the car.
- Visible damage from a recent impact.

If you do need a tow, a flatbed is the safer choice for a car with unknown damage. Here's [how to know if your car needs a flatbed](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/).

## How Much Does It Cost to Fix an Airbag Light?

Expect anywhere from nothing to over $1,000, depending on the cause. Roughly:

| Problem | Typical cost range |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic scan (reads SRS codes) | $50 to $150 |
| Seat belt buckle or connector fix | $100 to $300 |
| Passenger occupancy sensor | $200 to $600 |
| Clockspring replacement | $200 to $600 |
| SRS control module | $400 to $1,200+ |

Many shops need an SRS-capable scan tool, since a basic OBD-II reader won't read airbag codes. Ask for one before paying for parts. Some auto parts stores will read engine codes for free, but not always SRS codes.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will my airbags deploy if the airbag light is on?**
A: Often not. The light means the system found a fault, and most cars disable the airbags until it's fixed. Assume you don't have airbag protection and wear your seat belt every trip.

**Q: Can I pass inspection with the airbag light on?**
A: It depends on your state. Some states fail a car for an active airbag light, and others don't check it. Even where it passes, it's still a safety defect worth fixing.

**Q: Can a low or dead battery cause the airbag light?**
A: Yes. Low voltage or a recent battery change can set the light. Sometimes it clears on its own, but if it stays on, get the SRS codes read.

**Q: Can I reset the airbag light myself?**
A: Disconnecting the battery rarely clears it, and it doesn't fix the cause. The light will return. A shop with an SRS scanner can read the code and clear it after the repair.

**Q: Is the airbag light covered by insurance or warranty?**
A: Normal insurance doesn't cover wear-related failures, but a collision claim can include airbag repair. Check your warranty, since many cover SRS components for longer than the basic warranty period.
