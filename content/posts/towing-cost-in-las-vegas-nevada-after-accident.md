> **Quick Answer:** Towing after an accident in Las Vegas typically costs $125 to $400 for a local tow, plus daily storage of roughly $40 to $100 if the car sits in a yard. Heavy damage, a wrecker recovery off the roadway, or an after-hours call pushes it higher. If another driver was at fault, their insurance should reimburse you. Keep every receipt.

## How Much Will a Tow Cost After a Wreck in Las Vegas?

Expect $125 to $400 for a standard accident tow within the Las Vegas valley. That covers the hookup and the first several miles. Extra charges stack on from there.

- **Hookup and base fee:** $75 to $175, often higher at night or on weekends.
- **Mileage:** $4 to $8 per mile past the included distance.
- **Winching or recovery:** $100 to $300 extra if the car is off the road, nose-down in a drainage channel, or wedged against a barrier.
- **Storage:** Yards typically charge daily once the car is parked, and some start the clock the same day.

Prices vary by company, and I can't give you a guaranteed number for your tow. Ask for the full total before the hook goes on. For baseline pricing without the accident extras, see [how much a tow truck costs in Las Vegas](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-in-las-vegas-nevada/).

## Who Pays for the Tow If the Other Driver Hit Me?

The at-fault driver's insurance is responsible, but you usually pay first and get reimbursed. Tow companies want payment before releasing the car, and the other insurer may take days or weeks to process a claim.

Get the other driver's insurance information at the scene. Photograph their card and plate. Then pay the tow, keep the itemized receipt, and submit it with your claim.

If you caused the crash, your own policy may cover the tow under collision coverage or a roadside rider. Details differ by policy, so read yours. This is the same question covered in [does my car insurance cover towing if I cause the accident](/does-my-car-insurance-cover-towing-if-i-cause-the-accident/).

## What Happens If Police Order the Tow on I-15 or the Strip?

If the car blocks traffic or can't be driven, officers will call a rotation tow, and you generally don't get to pick the company. That's common on I-15 near the Spaghetti Bowl interchange, on US-95 through downtown, and on Las Vegas Boulevard where a wreck can jam traffic fast.

Rotation tows aren't automatically more expensive, but you're in a bad spot to negotiate. Do these things:

1. Ask the driver for the total cost and storage location before they leave the scene.
2. Take photos of the car's damage and its position before it's hooked.
3. Get the company name, truck number, and a written receipt.
4. Ask where the car is going and what the daily storage rate is.

## Can I Pick My Own Tow Company After an Accident?

Yes, unless police have ordered a specific tow because the vehicle is a hazard. If the car is safely off the road and you're not in danger, you can call your own insurer's roadside line, AAA, or a company you trust.

Waiting costs you nothing on a quiet shoulder, but never wait on a live lane. If you're stuck on the interstate, read [how long you can wait for a tow truck on the highway](/how-long-can-you-wait-for-a-tow-truck-on-highway/) first.

Be wary of tow trucks that show up unsolicited. Some drivers monitor accident scenes and pitch hard. Never sign a blank authorization form, and never let one hook your car before you've heard the total price.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Hit With Storage Fees?

Move the car out of the tow yard within a day, because storage is the fee that quietly doubles your bill. Pick it up fast or have your insurer arrange a transfer to a body shop.

Call the yard the next morning and ask three things: the daily rate, when it started, and what hours they release vehicles. Many yards charge a release fee on top of storage, and after-hours pickup can cost extra.

If you think you were overcharged, you have options. Compare the invoice to what you were quoted, and see [how to dispute a towing charge](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-if-overcharged/). Nevada regulates towing, so unlawful charges can be challenged.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does my insurance pay for towing after an accident in Las Vegas?**
A: Often yes, if you carry collision coverage or a roadside rider. Liability-only policies typically don't cover your own tow. Call your insurer from the scene to confirm.

**Q: How much is storage at a Las Vegas tow yard?**
A: Daily storage commonly runs about $40 to $100, depending on the yard and vehicle size. Ask for the exact rate and start time in writing.

**Q: Can I get my belongings out of a towed car?**
A: Generally yes. Yards usually let you retrieve personal items with ID, though some limit hours. Get medications, documents, and car seats out right away.

**Q: What if the car is totaled?**
A: Your insurer typically pays the tow and storage to the yard or a salvage facility as part of the claim. Tell your adjuster immediately so storage doesn't pile up.

**Q: Should I take the first tow truck that shows up?**
A: No. Unless police ordered it, ask the total price first. If it feels wrong, call your insurer or AAA instead.
