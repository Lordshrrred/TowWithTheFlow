> **Quick Answer:** A stolen catalytic converter can prevent your car from starting because of a broken oxygen sensor wire or a completely severed exhaust pipe disrupting engine management. Do not attempt to drive it. Call the police first to get a report number, then call a tow truck. You need that police report for insurance. The car goes straight to a shop, not your driveway.

## Why Won't My Car Start If the Catalytic Converter Was Stolen?

Your car won't start because the thieves almost certainly cut the oxygen sensor wiring harness when they cut the converter off the exhaust pipe. Modern engine management systems rely on the upstream and downstream O2 sensors to calculate fuel trim. With that signal severed or throwing a fault, some vehicles will crank but not fire, and others will start briefly then stall.

There is also a less common scenario: the exhaust pipe was cut so close to the engine that the gap creates a massive vacuum or pressure leak, and the ECU throws enough fault codes to prevent a clean start. Either way, the car is not drivable. Do not try to limp it anywhere.

## What Do I Do Right Now, in the Next 10 Minutes?

Call 911 or your local non-emergency line immediately and report the theft. Get a case number before you do anything else, because your insurance company will require it to process a comprehensive claim. Most catalytic converter theft is covered under comprehensive, not collision, so your deductible applies but it will not count against you as an at-fault claim.

After the police call, call a tow truck. The vehicle needs to go directly to an exhaust shop or dealership, not your house. Storing it at home just delays the repair and may complicate an insurance inspection if an adjuster needs to see it at the shop. If you have [roadside assistance through your insurer, check what they actually cover](/does-state-farm-roadside-assistance-cover-towing/) before you call a tow independently, since many policies include a free tow.

## How Much Is This Going to Cost Me?

Catalytic converter replacement is expensive. For common targets like a Toyota Prius (the most frequently stolen converter in the United States), a genuine OEM converter runs $1,500 to $2,500 in parts alone, plus labor. Honda Elements, Ford F-250 diesels, and Chevy Silverados are also high on the theft list, with replacement costs ranging from $800 to over $3,000 depending on the vehicle.

Beyond the converter itself, you will likely pay for:

- Replacement O2 sensors: $50 to $200 per sensor, and there are usually two
- Exhaust pipe repair or section replacement: $100 to $400 depending on how much was cut
- ECU diagnostic scan to clear fault codes: $75 to $150
- Tow to the shop: roughly $75 to $150 for a local haul

## Is It Safe to Be Around the Car Right Now?

Yes, but stay alert. Converter theft usually happens in parking lots, driveways, and on the street overnight. In most cases the thieves are long gone, but if you discovered it while the theft was in progress, do not confront anyone. Get distance and call 911.

Once you confirm the car is just sitting with a missing converter, there is no fire risk or fluid leak hazard from the missing part itself. The exhaust system is open to atmosphere, which is fine while the car is not running. Just do not start it and let it idle with an open exhaust near an enclosed space, because raw exhaust gases can enter the cabin without the proper routing in place.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive my car with a stolen catalytic converter if it does start?**
A: No. Even if it cranks, driving without a catalytic converter is illegal in every U.S. state, produces dangerously loud exhaust, and risks engine damage from the disrupted backpressure and O2 sensor faults throwing the fuel mixture off.

**Q: Will insurance cover a stolen catalytic converter?**
A: Yes, if you have comprehensive coverage. Theft falls under comprehensive, not collision. You will pay your deductible and the rest is covered up to the vehicle's actual cash value. Call your insurer before authorizing repairs.

**Q: How long does it take to replace a stolen catalytic converter?**
A: Most shops can complete the job in one to three days if the part is in stock. OEM converters for high-theft vehicles like the Prius are sometimes on backorder, which can stretch the timeline to one to two weeks.

**Q: Do I need a police report even if I already know it was stolen?**
A: Yes. Without a police report number, your insurance company will not process the claim. File the report the same day, and ask the officer for the case number before they leave.

**Q: Can a tow truck handle my car if the exhaust is damaged underneath?**
A: Yes. A flatbed tow is the cleanest option since nothing is dragging underneath and the car rides on the bed. A wheel-lift tow also works fine. Just tell the dispatcher the converter was cut off so they know the undercarriage has open exhaust pipes.
