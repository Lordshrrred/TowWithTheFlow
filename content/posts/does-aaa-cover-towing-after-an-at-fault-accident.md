> **Quick Answer:** Yes. AAA treats a collision like any other disablement, and fault doesn't change eligibility. Your membership tier sets the free tow distance: Classic covers a short tow, while Plus and Premier cover much longer ones. AAA pays only for the tow itself. Collision repairs, storage fees, and police-ordered impound charges are not covered. Call AAA from a safe spot, not from the travel lane.

## Does AAA Actually Care Who Caused the Accident?

No. AAA roadside service is a membership benefit, not an insurance claim, so fault is irrelevant. If your car can't be driven after a collision, it qualifies as a disablement the same way a dead alternator would.

What matters is your membership, not your driving record. You also don't need to report anything to AAA about who hit whom. The dispatcher asks where you are, what happened to the car, and whether it's drivable. Nothing more.

One catch: your membership has to be active and the service has to be requested for a vehicle you're covered on. AAA membership follows the person, not the car, but confirm your tier before you promise a destination to anyone. ## What Do I Do Right After an At-Fault Crash Before Calling AAA?

Get everyone safe and call 911 first if anyone is hurt or the road is blocked. A tow call is the second call, never the first.

1. Turn on your hazards. If the car runs and it's a minor collision on a highway, move it to the shoulder.
2. Check for injuries. Don't move anyone who complains of neck or back pain.
3. Smell for fuel and look under the car for leaking fluid. If you see or smell fuel, get away from the vehicle.
4. Photograph both cars, the plates, the damage, and the surrounding road before anything is moved.
5. Exchange insurance information. Admit nothing about fault at the scene. Describe what happened to police factually.
6. Then call AAA or use the AAA app, which sends your GPS location automatically.

If police are on scene and ordering the car cleared, they may call a rotation tow company. You are generally allowed to decline that tow and request your own provider, but if police say the car must move immediately, a rotation tow may be the faster safe option. Ask the officer. Don't argue on a live highway.

## How Far Will AAA Tow My Car After a Collision?

The distance depends on your tier, and it's a hard cap, not a suggestion. Classic membership covers a short tow, and Plus and Premier extend that substantially. Exact mileage figures change by region and club, so check your member card or the AAA app before relying on a number.

Past your included miles, you pay the towing company's per-mile rate for the extra distance. A practical tip: tell the dispatcher your destination before the truck arrives, and pick a shop within your free range. Towing to a body shop 40 miles away can turn a free tow into a real bill.

## What Won't AAA Pay for After an At-Fault Accident?

AAA pays for the tow and nothing else. Everything downstream is on you or your insurer.

- **Repairs.** Collision repair is your auto policy's job, subject to your deductible.
- **Storage fees.** If the car sits at an impound yard or tow lot, daily fees pile up fast. Move it quickly.
- **Police impound and release fees.** If the car was impounded by order, AAA does not cover the release charge.
- **Winching or recovery.** Pulling a car out of a ditch, off an embankment, or from a rollover can fall outside standard tow coverage, depending on your plan and club.
- **Damage to other vehicles.** That's your liability coverage.

The cleanest move is to use AAA for the tow, then call your insurer the same day to start the claim. Your insurer may also cover towing as part of collision coverage, which is worth knowing if you've blown past your AAA mileage. ## Will Calling AAA Raise My Insurance Rates?

No. A roadside call goes to AAA, not to your insurance company, and it doesn't appear on your driving record or claim history. Your rates can change because of the at-fault accident itself if you file a claim, but the tow call has nothing to do with it.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does AAA tow me if my car is totaled?**
A: Yes, as long as it's a covered vehicle and a covered disablement. AAA will tow it to your chosen destination within your mileage limit. Expect to pay for any extra miles beyond that limit.

**Q: Can I use AAA if the police already called a tow truck?**
A: Often yes, but only if the officer allows it. If the car is blocking traffic and police insist on their rotation tow, accept it, then submit the receipt to your insurer. You may be charged by that company directly.

**Q: Does AAA tow a car with airbags deployed?**
A: Yes. Deployed airbags don't change eligibility. Let the dispatcher know so the driver brings the right equipment, since a heavily damaged car may need a flatbed. Our guide on [airbag light on after an accident](/airbag-light-on-after-accident-can-i-still-drive-car/) covers when the car shouldn't be driven at all.

**Q: Do I need to be the one driving the car for AAA to cover it?**
A: Not always. AAA coverage follows the member, so you generally need to be in or with the vehicle. See [Does AAA Cover Towing When Someone Else Is Driving Your Car?](/does-aaa-cover-towing-for-someone-else-driving-my-car/) for the specifics.

**Q: What if I'm not a AAA member yet and I'm stuck at the scene?**
A: You can usually sign up by phone or app and request service, but many clubs add a waiting period or a fee for immediate-need joins. If that's not an option, you'll pay out of pocket.
