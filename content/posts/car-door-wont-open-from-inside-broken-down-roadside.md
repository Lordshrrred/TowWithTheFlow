> **Quick Answer:** Pull the interior door handle firmly and hold it while pushing the door outward with your shoulder. If that fails, try the child lock switch on the door edge, roll down the window and use the exterior handle, or climb out through another door. If you're on a highway shoulder, exit toward the guardrail side, away from traffic. Do not stay trapped in the car longer than necessary.

## How Do I Get Out If My Car Door Won't Open From the Inside?

Roll down the window first. Reach outside and use the exterior door handle to open the door from that side. This bypasses the interior latch entirely and works most of the time when the interior release cable has snapped or the latch mechanism has seized.

If the window won't roll down either, move to another door. Rear doors on most sedans and SUVs operate independently from the front. Get out through whichever door opens, then assess the situation from outside the vehicle.

If all doors are stuck, which is rare but happens after a collision that bends the frame, your last option is the window. A glass break tool or a sharp, heavy object aimed at a lower corner of the window will shatter tempered glass. Carry a seatbelt cutter/glass breaker on your keychain for exactly this situation. Laminated windshields will not shatter the same way. Side and rear windows will.

## What Actually Breaks and Causes a Door to Not Open From Inside?

The interior release cable is the most common failure. It's a thin steel cable that runs from the interior door handle to the latch mechanism. It can snap from age, corrosion, or being yanked too hard. When it breaks, the handle moves but nothing happens at the latch.

The second most likely culprit is the child safety lock. It's a small slider or toggle on the rear door edge, usually labeled with a padlock icon. If someone accidentally engaged it, the interior handle is physically disconnected from the latch. You cannot open that rear door from inside no matter how hard you pull. Check this before assuming anything is broken.

Other causes include a bent door from a previous minor collision (the latch can't disengage because the door is binding against the frame), a corroded or frozen latch pawl, or a broken interior handle itself where the plastic lever snaps off at the pivot point.

## Is It Safe to Stay in the Car While I Wait for Help on the Side of the Road?

On a busy highway shoulder, staying in the car is actually more dangerous than standing well behind the guardrail. A significant number of roadside fatalities involve vehicles struck from behind while stopped on the shoulder, often at high-speed roads like I-25, I-10, or I-95 where traffic is fast and shoulder space is tight.

Get out of the car first, then call for help. If you can only exit through the passenger side toward the travel lane, wait for a gap in traffic, exit quickly, and move immediately behind the guardrail or as far off the roadway as possible. Do not stand behind your car.

Once you're safely away from the vehicle, place your hazard lights on if they still work, and if you have emergency triangles or flares, deploy them behind the car. ## What Should I Tell the Tow Truck or Roadside Service When I Call?

Tell them the door is stuck so they know what they're dealing with when they arrive. A good technician can often pop a stuck latch with a slim jim or a flathead screwdriver through the door jamb without full disassembly. If the child lock is the issue, they can access the door edge from outside and toggle it off in under a minute.

## Can I Fix the Door Myself on the Roadside?

Probably not fully, but you can often get it functional enough to get to a shop. If the interior handle broke but the door opened once using the exterior handle, tie the interior handle or jam something in the latch to keep the door from fully latching again. That way you can get in and out until the repair is made. Do not drive with a door that cannot latch at all. It will swing open.

If the child lock was the issue and you toggled it off, the door works normally. No repair needed.

A snapped interior release cable is not a roadside fix without parts. The door card (interior panel) needs to come off, the old cable needs to be unclipped from the handle and latch, and a new one installed. That's a shop job. Budget roughly $80 to $200 at a shop depending on the vehicle, with most of that being labor since the part itself is usually under $30.

## Common Questions

**Q: My rear door won't open from inside but the front doors are fine. What's wrong?**
A: Check the child safety lock first. It's on the rear door edge, a small slider that disables the interior release. Flip it to the unlocked position from outside and the interior handle will work again. This accounts for the majority of "stuck rear door" calls.

**Q: Can a stuck door lock mechanism cause the door to not open from inside?**
A: Yes. If the door is locked and the lock actuator or linkage is broken, the latch stays in the locked position even when you pull the handle. Try unlocking manually with the physical key cylinder in the door edge, or use the central lock switch and see if you can hear the actuator move.

**Q: My door was fine before the accident. Now it won't open at all from inside or outside. Is it ruined?**
A: A collision often bends the door or the B-pillar just enough that the latch bolt can't retract from the striker plate. A body shop can sometimes realign the door, but if the frame is bent, the door may need replacement. Do not force it repeatedly. You can damage the latch permanently.

**Q: Is it safe to drive with a car door that won't open from the inside?**
A: Only if the door latches completely and you can exit from other doors. It is not safe to drive with a door that won't latch shut. If the door can be opened from outside and stays fully closed while driving, you can make a short trip to a shop, but passengers in that door should know how to exit through another door in an emergency.

**Q: Will roadside assistance help with a stuck door if the car itself isn't broken down?**
A: Some plans cover it under lockout service, especially if accessing the interior handle is the issue. Call your provider and describe it as a lockout-type situation. AAA technicians in particular carry tools to manipulate door latches. If you're paying out of pocket, a mobile locksmith is usually faster and cheaper than a tow for this specific problem.
