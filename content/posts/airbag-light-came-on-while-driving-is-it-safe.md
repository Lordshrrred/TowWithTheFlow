> **Quick Answer:** Yes, the car is mechanically safe to drive, but it is not fully safe in a crash. A lit airbag (SRS) light means the system found a fault and may have disabled your airbags. You don't need to pull over or call a tow. Drive carefully, avoid highways if you can, and get it scanned within a few days.

## Is It Safe to Keep Driving With the Airbag Light On?

Yes, the car will drive normally, but your airbags may not deploy in a collision. The airbag light is a separate system from your engine, brakes, and steering. It won't cause a breakdown or loss of control.

The risk is what happens in a crash. When the SRS (Supplemental Restraint System) computer detects a fault, it often shuts the system down as a precaution. That means no frontal airbags, and possibly no seatbelt pretensioners either. Wear your seatbelt every time, keep following distance generous, and skip the aggressive highway driving until it's checked.

The one exception: if the light came on right after a collision, even a minor one, stop driving and have it inspected. A damaged sensor or a partially deployed component is a different problem.

## Why Did the Airbag Light Come On?

The most common cause is a faulty sensor or wiring connection, not a failing airbag itself. The usual culprits:

- **Seat belt buckle switch:** the connector gets dirty or worn, especially on the driver's side.
- **Clockspring:** a coiled ribbon cable behind the steering wheel that carries airbag signals. When it wears out, you'll often see the light plus a horn or steering wheel button that stops working.
- **Passenger occupancy sensor:** a mat in the passenger seat that detects weight. Heavy items, wet seats, or a failing mat can trigger the light.
- **Under-seat connectors:** the yellow wiring plugs under the front seats get bumped when seats are adjusted or things are shoved underneath.
- **Low battery voltage:** a weak battery or a dying alternator can set the SRS fault. If your battery light is also on, read [battery light came on while driving: should I pull over?](/battery-light-came-on-while-driving-should-i-pull-over/) and check the charging system first.
- **Crash sensor or control module failure:** less common, and more expensive.

## What Should I Do Right Now With the Light On?

Stay calm, keep driving to a safe place, and check the easy things first. The light is not an emergency stop.

1. Buckle everyone in. Seatbelts are your primary protection now.
2. Check whether other warning lights came on at the same time. A battery, engine, or brake light alongside the airbag light changes the priority. See [engine light came on highway: is it safe to keep driving?](/engine-light-came-on-highway-is-it-safe-to-keep-driving/) if the check engine light is also lit.
3. Turn the car off, wait 30 seconds, and restart. A one-time glitch can clear itself. If the light stays on, it's a real fault.
4. Remove heavy items from the passenger seat and check that the seat isn't soaked.
5. Don't poke around the airbag wiring yourself.

If the light goes out after a restart and stays out, still get the car scanned. Intermittent faults come back.

## How Much Does It Cost to Fix an Airbag Light?

Expect roughly $100 to $150 for diagnosis alone, and the repair itself ranges widely depending on the cause. A shop with an SRS-capable scan tool reads the stored fault code. A basic parts-store scanner often can't read airbag codes, so don't assume a free scan will tell you much.

Rough ranges by cause:

- **Loose connector or seat belt buckle sensor:** often under $200.
- **Passenger occupancy sensor:** commonly $300 to $800.
- **Clockspring:** typically $200 to $600 depending on the vehicle.
- **Airbag control module:** can run over $1,000.

Ask for the stored code in writing and a repair estimate before work begins.

## Will the Airbag Light Cause Me to Fail Inspection?

In states with safety inspections, an illuminated airbag light is a fail in many cases. Rules vary by state, so check with your local inspection station. In states without inspections, there's no legal penalty for driving with the light on, but there are real consequences after a crash: an insurance company or lawyer may ask whether you knew about the warning.

If the light came on during a trip and you're far from home, you can usually drive to your destination. Just don't let it sit for weeks. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can the airbag light go off on its own?**
A: Yes, if the cause was a temporary glitch like a loose connector or a voltage dip. But the fault code stays stored in the module, and the light often returns. Get it scanned even if it clears.

**Q: Will my airbags still deploy with the light on?**
A: Possibly not. Many systems disable the airbags when a fault is detected, and some disable only part of the system. You can't know which without a scan, so assume they won't work.

**Q: Can a bad battery cause the airbag light to come on?**
A: Yes. Low voltage can trigger an SRS fault, and some cars store a code after a jump start or battery replacement. If the light appeared right after battery work, a shop can often clear it once voltage is stable.

**Q: Is it safe to put a child in the front seat with the airbag light on?**
A: No. Children belong in the back seat regardless, and with a fault in the passenger occupancy system, the airbag may deploy when it shouldn't or fail when it should.

**Q: Can I reset the airbag light myself?**
A: A reset only clears the light, not the fault. If the problem is real, it returns. Resetting also hides a system that may not protect you, so find the cause first.
