> **Quick Answer:** Tow it. A snapped serpentine belt kills your alternator output and, on most cars, your power steering. On many engines it also drives the water pump, meaning your engine will overheat within minutes of running. Do not start the car again to "just get to an exit." Call a tow and wait safely off the road.

## What Just Happened to My Car and Why Did It Stop?

Your serpentine belt drives most of the engine accessories at once: the alternator, power steering pump, air conditioning compressor, and on many engines the water pump. When it snaps, all of those accessories stop working immediately. The alternator can no longer charge the battery, so if you kept driving, the engine would die within a few minutes once the battery drained. Some drivers also feel the steering go heavy or the temperature gauge spike right before the car stalls.

The belt itself fails for a few reasons: age and hardening (most belts are rated for 60,000 to 100,000 miles), a seized accessory pulley that locks up and burns through the belt, or a tensioner that lost its spring pressure and let the belt slip and fray. Look at the belt fragments if you can see them safely from outside the car. If the belt is shredded rather than cleanly snapped, a frozen pulley or tensioner is likely the cause, and that part will need to be replaced along with the belt.

## Is It Safe to Sit on the Highway Shoulder While I Wait?

Get as far off the travel lanes as possible, turn on your hazard lights, and stay out of the car or stand behind a guardrail. A highway shoulder is one of the most dangerous places to be on foot, especially if visibility is poor. Set up emergency triangles or flares at least 200 feet behind your car to warn traffic. For a detailed breakdown of which warning device works better in different conditions, see [Emergency Triangle vs Flares: Which Is Safer on the Highway?](/emergency-triangle-vs-flares-which-is-safer-on-highway/)

Do not attempt to inspect the belt in a live travel lane. If you are in a tunnel or a location where you cannot safely exit the car, call 911 first.

## Why Can't I Just Restart and Limp to the Next Exit?

Because the water pump on most 4-cylinder and many V6 engines is belt-driven. The moment you start the engine without the serpentine belt, coolant stops circulating. You will overheat the engine in two to five minutes of idling, sometimes faster under load. Overheating a modern aluminum engine can warp the cylinder head, crack the block, or blow the head gasket. A blown head gasket repair runs $1,500 to $3,000 or more. A serpentine belt replacement at a shop runs $75 to $200 in parts and labor. The math is not close.

If your specific engine uses a separate timing belt to drive the water pump (some Honda and Subaru engines do), the cooling system may still work briefly. But you still have no alternator output, so you are running entirely on battery reserve. Most car batteries provide 10 to 20 minutes of engine-on runtime without the alternator, depending on electrical load. That is not enough margin to gamble on. Tow it. For a related situation where overheating has already done damage, see [Car Engine Seized Up on Highway: What to Do Right Now](/car-engine-seized-up-on-highway-what-to-do/).

## How Do I Know If the Belt Failure Caused Other Damage?

A clean break with the car shut off quickly usually means no downstream damage. The risk climbs if you drove any distance after hearing a squeal, smell burning rubber, or saw the temperature gauge rise before you stopped. Tell the mechanic exactly what you heard and saw in the minutes before and after the belt snapped. That sequence tells them where to look.

Check your battery warning light situation too. If the alternator was under-performing before the belt failed, you may also need a battery test. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive with a broken serpentine belt even for a mile?**
A: No. If your water pump is belt-driven, you will overheat the engine within minutes. Even if it is not, you have no alternator, and the car will stall when the battery drains. One mile is enough to cause thousands in engine damage.

**Q: How long does it take to replace a serpentine belt at a shop?**
A: Usually 30 to 60 minutes on most cars. It is a straightforward job. If the tensioner or an idler pulley also needs replacement, add another 15 to 30 minutes.

**Q: What does a snapped serpentine belt sound like before it breaks?**
A: You may hear a loud squeal, a slapping noise under the hood, or a sudden silence from the AC and power steering. Some belts go with no warning at all. If you heard squealing for days before this, a worn tensioner or failing pulley bearing was likely the cause.

**Q: Will my insurance cover the tow for a serpentine belt failure?**
A: Only if you have roadside assistance on your policy. Standard liability or collision coverage does not cover mechanical breakdowns. Call your insurer before calling a tow company to confirm what is covered.

**Q: Can the serpentine belt be replaced on the side of the road?**
A: Technically yes, if someone brings the correct belt and you have basic tools. In practice, it is risky roadside work on a live shoulder, and you need to confirm the belt routing and check for a damaged tensioner before restarting. Most people are better off towing to a shop.
