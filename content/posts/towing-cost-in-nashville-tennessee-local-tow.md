> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in Nashville typically costs $75 to $150 for the first five miles, plus $3 to $5 per mile after that. Flatbed service, after-hours calls, and breakdowns on I-40 or I-65 near downtown can push that higher. Most private tows run $100 to $200 total. If your insurance has roadside assistance, you may owe nothing out of pocket.

## What Does a Local Tow Actually Cost in Nashville?

A standard hookup and short haul inside Nashville city limits runs $75 to $150 for the first five miles, with per-mile rates of $3 to $5 beyond that. So if you break down on I-65 near the Wedgewood Avenue exit and need a tow to a shop in Berry Hill, you're looking at a $90 to $120 bill at most. A longer haul, say from the I-440 and I-40 interchange (the "Spaghetti Junction" area) out to a dealership in Brentwood, adds mileage charges and can land closer to $175 to $225.

Flatbed service costs more than a wheel-lift tow. If you drive a newer car with all-wheel drive, a leased vehicle, or a low-clearance sports car, most reputable shops will insist on a flatbed. That typically adds $25 to $50 to your base rate.

For a deeper breakdown of per-mile charges specific to Nashville, see [Towing Cost in Nashville Tennessee Per Mile Rate](/towing-cost-nashville-tennessee-per-mile-rate/).

## What Makes Nashville Towing More Expensive Than I Expected?

Three things drive costs up fast: location, time of day, and traffic. Nashville's core highways, I-40, I-65, and I-24, are all high-volume corridors where tow trucks sometimes charge a premium for the difficulty of accessing your vehicle safely. A breakdown in an active travel lane on I-24 near Murfreesboro Road requires more time to set up safely than a stall in a parking lot on Nolensville Pike.

After-hours and weekend calls also cost more. Expect a $25 to $50 surcharge for tows between roughly 9 PM and 6 AM, or on Sundays. If your car dies on Broadway on a Saturday night, you are paying after-hours rates.

Long waits are a separate issue. During peak hours on I-65 near the Armory Drive interchange in the Antioch area, response times can stretch to 45 to 60 minutes. That wait does not affect your bill, but it affects your safety. Get your hazard lights on, stay inside with your seatbelt on if you are on an active highway, and get off the roadway if you can.

## Does My Insurance Cover a Local Tow in Nashville?

Usually yes, if you have roadside assistance on your policy. Comprehensive auto policies from State Farm, Allstate, USAA, and others typically include towing reimbursement or direct dispatch. The coverage limit varies. State Farm roadside commonly covers tows up to a fixed dollar amount, and if the bill exceeds that, you pay the difference. Check your declarations page for the exact cap before you call a tow truck.

AAA members get a set number of free tows per year based on membership tier. Classic membership covers up to five miles free per tow. If your shop is six miles away, you pay the overage. It is worth knowing your tier before you assume you are fully covered.

## What Should I Do Right Now If I'm Stuck on a Nashville Highway?

Pull completely off the travel lane if at all possible. On I-40 through downtown or on I-65 near the Trinity Lane exit, the shoulders are narrow and fast-moving traffic is unforgiving. Get to an exit ramp or a surface street if you can make it there safely without driving on a flat or overheating further.

Once stopped, hazard lights on immediately. If you are on a bridge or in a tunnel with no shoulder, stay in your vehicle with your seatbelt fastened. Do not stand behind your car.

Call 911 if you are blocking a lane or if you feel unsafe. Tennessee Highway Patrol and Metro Nashville Police can contact TDOT HELP trucks, which patrol major Nashville interstates and can push your car to a safe spot at no charge. Then call your tow service or roadside assistance provider.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a tow truck cost in Nashville for a short local trip?**
A: Most local tows inside Nashville run $75 to $150 for the first five miles. Beyond that, add $3 to $5 per mile. Flatbed service adds another $25 to $50.

**Q: Do Nashville tow companies charge more at night?**
A: Yes. After-hours calls, generally 9 PM to 6 AM, typically carry a $25 to $50 surcharge. Weekend rates, especially Saturday night in the entertainment district, often trigger the same premium.

**Q: Can I negotiate with a tow truck driver in Nashville?**
A: Rarely on the street. Rates are usually set by the company, and if they were dispatched by police after an accident, the rate may be on a city-approved schedule. Your best leverage is calling multiple companies before committing if you have time and safety permits.

**Q: What is a TDOT HELP truck and is it really free?**
A: TDOT HELP trucks are state-funded highway emergency response vehicles that patrol I-40, I-65, I-24, and I-440 in Nashville. They can push your vehicle to a safe location and provide minor assistance like a gallon of gas or a tire change at no cost to you. They do not tow you to a shop.

**Q: How do I know if a Nashville tow company is legit and not overcharging?**
A: Ask for the rate breakdown before they hook up your vehicle: base hookup fee, per-mile rate, and any after-hours or flatbed surcharges. A reputable company gives you that information without hesitation. If they refuse to quote before hooking up, call someone else.
