> **Quick Answer:** Pull completely off the road, turn on your hazard lights, and stay in the car with your seatbelt on if you're on a highway. Find your exact location using your phone's map app, then call roadside assistance or search "tow truck near me" for local options. Do not let a random tow truck that shows up unsolicited take your car anywhere until you know the destination and the price.

## What Do I Do in the First Five Minutes After Breaking Down?

Get the car off the road and get your hazards on. That is the whole job for the first two minutes. If you can limp to an exit, a parking lot, or a wide shoulder, do it. If the car dies in a lane, push it if it's safe and you have help, otherwise stay inside with your seatbelt buckled until emergency responders arrive.

Once you're stopped safely, pin your location. Open Google Maps or Apple Maps and drop a pin on your current position. Note the nearest cross street, highway exit number, or mile marker. You will need this to give dispatchers an exact location. On an interstate, look for green mile marker signs on the right shoulder. These are the fastest way to tell a tow dispatcher exactly where you are.

If you're on a highway and cannot safely exit the vehicle, call 911. They can dispatch a patrol unit to protect you while you wait.

## How Do I Find a Trustworthy Tow Truck in a City I Don't Know?

Search Google Maps for "tow truck" or "roadside assistance" with your current location active. Read the reviews quickly. Look for companies with at least 20 reviews and a rating above 4.0. A company with 200 reviews and a 4.3 rating is almost always safer than one with 3 reviews and a 5.0.

Confirm the price before the truck hooks up your car. Ask the dispatcher for a total estimate, not just a per-mile rate. A standard local tow in most U.S. cities runs $75 to $150 for the first few miles, with a per-mile charge after that. If a company quotes you $400 for a 3-mile tow with no explanation, hang up and call someone else. If you have roadside assistance through your insurance, credit card, or AAA, call them first. They dispatch a vetted local provider and handle the billing directly. See [how much roadside assistance costs without a membership](/how-much-does-emergency-roadside-assistance-cost-without-membership/) if you need to pay out of pocket.

Watch out for "bandit tow trucks," which are trucks that cruise breakdown scenes unsolicited and pressure drivers into signing paperwork fast. You are never required to use a tow company that shows up on its own.

## Where Should I Tell Them to Tow My Car?

To the nearest reputable repair shop that can actually fix your problem, not just the closest one. Search "auto repair near me" in Google Maps and check reviews the same way you checked the tow company. Look for shops that list your car's make as a specialty. A transmission shop is not the right place for a coolant leak.

Call the shop before the tow truck arrives. Confirm they are open, that they work on your vehicle, and that they have capacity to look at it today or give you a realistic timeline. Many shops in busy metro areas are booked out several days. If that's the case, ask them to recommend a shop with faster availability. Most will tell you straight.

If your car overheated before it died, read up on [what to do when a car overheats and won't start](/car-wont-start-after-overheating-do-i-need-tow/) before you authorize any repairs, because the damage assessment matters before you commit money.

It depends on what you have, but many drivers have more coverage than they realize. Check your insurance card right now for a roadside assistance phone number. Your credit card may also have roadside coverage built in, particularly Visa Signature and certain Chase and Amex cards.

## Common Questions

**Q: Should I stay in my car or get out after breaking down on a highway?**
A: Stay in the car with your seatbelt on if you're on a high-speed highway. The shoulder is one of the most dangerous places for a person on foot. Only exit the vehicle if there's a fire, smoke, or immediate danger inside the car.

**Q: What if a tow truck shows up that I didn't call?**
A: You are not obligated to use them. Ask for their company name, license plate, and a written price quote before signing anything. If they pressure you or refuse to give a price upfront, walk away and call a dispatcher yourself.

**Q: Can I authorize repairs over the phone if I can't be at the shop in person?**
A: Yes, but get a written estimate texted or emailed to you first. Do not approve work verbally without a written record. Ask the shop to send photos of the problem if possible before you say yes to anything expensive.

**Q: How do I find out if the repair shop is reputable when I have no local knowledge?**
A: Google reviews, volume of reviews, and ASE certification signs are your fastest filters. You can also call your car's dealership network in that city. A Ford dealer service department, for example, will work on your Ford even if it's not where you bought it.

**Q: What if I can't afford the tow or the repair right now?**
A: Tell the tow company upfront and ask about payment options. For repairs, ask the shop if they accept payment plans or financing through services like Synchrony or Snap Finance.
