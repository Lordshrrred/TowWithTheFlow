> **Quick Answer:** A typical tow in Phoenix runs about $75 to $175 for the hookup, plus roughly $3 to $7 per mile after the first few miles. Flatbeds, nights, weekends, and freeway pickups push the price higher. Get the total quoted before the driver hooks up, and ask whether the mileage rate starts at your pickup spot or at their yard.

## How Much Does a Tow Cost in Phoenix After a Breakdown?

Expect $75 to $175 for the base hookup in the Phoenix metro, plus mileage that usually lands between $3 and $7 per mile. A 10-mile tow from a Tempe side street to a shop in Mesa will often total $125 to $250 depending on the truck and the time of day.

Prices vary by company, so treat these as ballpark ranges, not fixed rates. What moves the number:

- **Truck type:** Flatbeds cost more than wheel-lift trucks.
- **Time:** Nights, weekends, and holidays add a surcharge.
- **Location:** Freeway pickups and hard-to-reach spots cost more.
- **Distance:** Mileage often starts the moment the driver leaves their yard, not when they reach you.

## What Should I Do First If My Car Dies on a Phoenix Freeway?

Get the car off the travel lanes if you can, turn on your hazards, and call for help from outside the vehicle if it's safe. If you can't safely exit the car, stay buckled in and call 911.

Phoenix freeways are unforgiving. Narrow shoulders on stretches of the I-10 through the Deck Park Tunnel area and the Loop 202 near the Broadway Curve construction zones leave little room between you and 65 mph traffic. Shoulder width can change quickly, so don't assume you have space.

Do this in order:

1. Coast toward the right shoulder, as far from the lane as possible.
2. Hazards on, wheels turned away from traffic.
3. Call your roadside provider or a tow company. Tell them your exact freeway, direction, and nearest exit or mile marker.
4. Stay clear of the roadway. Standing behind a guardrail beats sitting in a car on a thin shoulder.

Arizona's 511 service and the ADOT traffic app can help you confirm your location and nearby closures. On freeways, authorized towing is often coordinated through state or highway patrol, so a company you pick yourself may not be allowed to reach you quickly.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged on a Phoenix Tow?

Ask for the full out-the-door price before the truck is hooked up, and get it in a text or on paper if possible. Most overcharging happens when drivers quote a low base rate and then add fees at the end.

Ask these questions every time:

- What is the total, including hookup, mileage, and any fees?
- Does mileage start at my location or at your yard?
- Is this a flatbed or a wheel-lift? (All-wheel drive and low-clearance cars generally need a flatbed.)
- Are there storage fees if the car sits at your lot overnight?

Be cautious with unsolicited tow trucks that show up at a breakdown or accident scene without being called. Verify the company name on the truck matches your dispatch. ## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow truck in Phoenix per mile?**
A: Mileage typically runs about $3 to $7 per mile in the Phoenix area, added on top of a base hookup fee. Some companies include the first few miles in the hookup price, so ask before you agree.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive than a wheel-lift in Phoenix?**
A: Usually, yes. Flatbeds generally cost more, but they're the safer choice for all-wheel-drive vehicles, low cars, and anything with transmission or drivetrain damage. See [flatbed vs wheel lift costs in Phoenix](/towing-cost-phoenix-arizona-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/).

**Q: Does AAA cover towing in Phoenix?**
A: AAA membership includes towing up to a set mileage limit that depends on your plan tier. Check your plan's coverage limit before you call, because going past it means paying the extra mileage yourself.

**Q: Can I choose which shop my car gets towed to?**
A: Yes, in most breakdown situations you choose the destination. Pick a shop that's open and expecting your car, and confirm drop-off options if you're arriving after hours.

**Q: What if I can't pay for the tow right away?**
A: Ask the company what payment methods they accept before they hook up. Most take cards, and some take mobile payment. Don't agree to a tow you can't pay for, because the car can be held until you do.
