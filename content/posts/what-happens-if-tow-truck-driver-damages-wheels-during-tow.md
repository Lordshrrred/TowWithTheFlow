> **Quick Answer:** If a tow driver damages your wheels, the tow company is responsible for the repair. Photograph the damage before the truck leaves, note it on the paperwork, and don't sign anything saying the vehicle was "received in good condition." Then file a claim with the company's garage liability insurance. If they refuse, your own insurer or a small claims court is the next step.

## Who Pays When a Tow Driver Damages My Wheels?

The tow company pays. Licensed operators carry garage liability and garagekeepers insurance specifically to cover damage to vehicles in their care, and wheels are one of the most common claims.

Your own collision coverage can also pay, but using it may mean a deductible and a claim on your record. Let the tow company's insurer handle it first, and keep your insurer as the backup.

One exception: damage that existed before the tow. A curb-rashed alloy from last month is not their problem, which is why before-and-after documentation decides these disputes.

## What Should I Do Right Now If I See the Damage?

Document everything before the truck leaves the scene or before you sign the release at the yard. Once you sign and drive away, proving the damage happened during the tow gets much harder.

1. **Photograph every wheel from several angles**, including the ones that look fine. Get close-ups of scratches, cracks, and bent lips on the rim.
2. **Get wide shots** showing the wheel, the tow equipment, and the truck's name and plate in the frame if possible.
3. **Tell the driver out loud and write it down.** Have them note the damage on the invoice or work order. If they refuse, write "wheel damage noted at drop-off" yourself, with the date and time.
4. **Don't sign a release** stating the vehicle was received in good condition or that you waive claims.
5. **Get the company's name, the driver's name, the truck number, and the invoice.** You need all four for a claim.

If you took photos when the car was loaded, even a quick phone shot, you now have a before-and-after. That is the strongest evidence you can have.

## How Do Tow Trucks Actually Damage Wheels?

Most wheel damage comes from the equipment, not bad luck. Knowing the cause helps you argue the claim and avoid a repeat.

- **Wheel-lift tows.** The metal yokes hook under the tires and can scrape or chip a wheel lip, especially on low-profile tires with expensive alloy rims.
- **Flatbed straps and chains.** Chains or hooks run across or through a wheel instead of nylon straps over the tire. This gouges spokes and bends lips.
- **Dollies and skates.** Used on AWD or locked-up cars, they can gouge the rim if wedged in wrong.
- **Loading mistakes.** Dragging a car up a steep flatbed angle scrapes wheels and undercarriage.

If your car needed a flatbed and got hooked to a wheel-lift, that is a bigger deal. AWD vehicles and cars with low clearance are the usual examples.

## How Do I Get the Tow Company to Pay for Repairs?

 a repair estimate within a few days. Phone calls get forgotten. Emails and certified letters create a record.

1. **Get an estimate** from a wheel repair shop or dealer. Cosmetic alloy refinishing is often cheaper than replacement, but a cracked or bent wheel usually must be replaced for safety.
2. **Email the tow company** the photos, invoice number, date, and estimate. Ask for their insurance carrier's claim contact.
3. **File with their insurer** if they give you one. Garage liability adjusters handle this every week.
4. **Follow up in writing** if you hear nothing in 7 to 10 days.

If they deny it or ghost you, call your own insurer and ask about a collision claim. They may pursue the tow company later through subrogation. You can also report the company to your state's regulator, since many states license towers and take complaints. ## Is a Damaged Wheel Safe to Drive On?

Not if it is cracked or bent. A bent rim can leak air slowly or fail under load, and a cracked alloy can let go at highway speed.

Surface scratches and curb-style scuffs are cosmetic. Anything that changes the shape of the wheel is not. Watch for a vibration at 50 to 70 mph, a tire that keeps losing pressure, or visible wobble when the wheel spins. Any of these means a shop checks it before you drive further. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can a tow company refuse to pay for wheel damage?**
A: They can deny it, but if you documented the damage and it clearly happened during the tow, they are legally on the hook. A denial pushes you to their insurer, your insurer, or small claims court.

**Q: Should I let the tow driver unload my car if I already see damage?**
A: Yes, but photograph everything first and have the damage written on the paperwork. Refusing delivery can create storage fees and complicate the claim.

**Q: What if I didn't notice the wheel damage until the next day?**
A: You can still file. Photograph it immediately, send the tow company written notice, and explain when you found it. The claim is weaker without same-day proof, so move fast.

**Q: Does my own insurance cover wheel damage caused by a tow truck?**
A: If you carry collision coverage, usually yes, minus your deductible. Your insurer may recover the money from the tow company afterward.

**Q: Can I go to small claims court over damaged wheels?**
A: Yes. Bring photos, the invoice, your repair estimate, and your written communication with the company. Small claims limits vary by state but cover most wheel repair costs.
