> **Quick Answer:** Yes, State Farm roadside assistance covers towing, but it is not automatic. You need to have either the Roadside Assistance add-on on your auto policy or a Select Service membership. Coverage typically pays to tow your car to the nearest qualified repair shop. Dollar limits and mileage caps depend on your specific policy, so pull up your declarations page or call State Farm directly before assuming you are fully covered.

## Does State Farm Roadside Assistance Actually Cover Towing?

Yes, towing is covered under State Farm's roadside assistance, but only if you have the coverage added to your policy. It does not come standard with basic liability or even comprehensive and collision coverage. You have to opt in, either through the Roadside Assistance add-on on your auto policy or through State Farm's Emergency Road Service feature, depending on how your policy was written.

The coverage pays for a tow to the nearest repair facility that can handle your vehicle's problem. That matters. "Nearest" is the operative word: if you want the car towed across town to your preferred shop, State Farm may only cover part of that distance and bill you for the rest.

For the full picture on what State Farm specifically caps and how those limits work in practice, see [State Farm Roadside Assistance Towing Coverage Limit](/state-farm-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-limit/).

## How Do I Actually Use State Farm Roadside Assistance When I'm Stuck?

Call State Farm's roadside assistance line or use the State Farm mobile app to request help. The app is faster if you have signal: it locates you automatically, shows estimated arrival time, and tracks the truck. If you are calling, have your policy number ready.

A few things to do while you wait:

- Get fully off the road if possible. If you are on a highway shoulder, exit the vehicle from the passenger side and stand behind a guardrail.
- Turn on hazard lights immediately.
- Set out warning triangles or flares if you have them. If you are not sure which to use, [this breakdown of emergency triangles vs. flares](/emergency-triangle-vs-flares-which-is-safer-on-highway/) is worth a quick read.
- Do not accept help from a tow truck that pulls up unsolicited before your dispatched driver arrives. Predatory towing is a real problem, especially near busy interchanges.

State Farm dispatches through a network of contracted towers. You do not get to pick the company, but you can ask the dispatcher which shop the driver will take you to.

## What Does State Farm Roadside Assistance Not Cover?

State Farm roadside assistance does not cover everything that can go wrong. The coverage is designed for emergency situations, not routine repairs or certain predictable failures.

## Is State Farm Roadside Assistance Worth It Compared to Other Options?

State Farm's add-on typically runs between $5 and $10 per year per vehicle, which makes it one of the cheapest ways to have towing coverage. That cost is hard to beat. A single tow without any coverage can run $75 to $150 for a short local haul, and significantly more on a highway or after hours.

The tradeoff is flexibility. AAA, for example, lets you choose your shop and covers longer tow distances. State Farm dispatches through their own network and controls where you go. If you have a preferred mechanic, that can be a real frustration.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does State Farm roadside assistance cover towing if my car won't start?**
A: Yes, a no-start situation is a covered breakdown. State Farm will dispatch a tow truck or attempt a jump start first. If the battery jump does not work, the vehicle gets towed to the nearest shop at no additional cost up to your policy limit.

**Q: How many roadside assistance calls does State Farm allow per year?**
A: Most State Farm roadside assistance policies allow up to three service calls per vehicle per policy period. Going over that limit means you pay out of pocket for the next call. Check your declarations page for your exact number.

**Q: Does State Farm roadside assistance cover towing for a flat tire if I have no spare?**
A: Yes. If you have no spare or the tire cannot be safely changed, State Farm will tow the vehicle to the nearest shop. They will also attempt a tire change first if you do have a usable spare.

**Q: Will State Farm roadside assistance cover towing if my car breaks down out of state?**
A: Yes, coverage applies nationwide. The benefit follows the vehicle listed on your policy regardless of what state you are in when the breakdown happens.

**Q: What if the tow costs more than State Farm's coverage limit?**
A: You pay the difference directly to the tow company. This comes up most often when the nearest qualified shop is far away or when you request a specific shop outside the dispatch network. Ask the dispatcher upfront what the policy maximum is so you are not surprised at the drop-off.
