> **Quick Answer:** Yes. Nearly every roadside plan covers a dead battery jumpstart, including AAA, insurance add-ons, and most credit card programs. The service call is usually included at no extra charge. What's often not covered is the battery itself. If the jump fails, the driver will test the battery and may offer a replacement for a fee, or tow you to a shop.

## Does Roadside Assistance Actually Cover a Dead Battery Jumpstart?

Yes, a jumpstart is one of the most basic services roadside plans include. AAA, insurer add-ons from companies like Progressive and GEICO, and most manufacturer roadside programs cover it as a standard service call.

The details vary. Some plans cap the number of service calls per year, commonly three to four. Others only cover calls for the vehicle listed on the policy. If you're unsure whether your plan counts, read [how car insurance handles roadside assistance when you add it](/does-car-insurance-cover-roadside-assistance-if-i-add-it/) before you need it.

Check the app or card in your wallet. You'll often see "battery service" listed right on it.

## What Do I Do Right Now If My Battery Is Dead?

Confirm it's the battery, then call your provider while you stay safe. A dead battery usually looks like this: clicking sound when you turn the key, dim or flickering dash lights, or complete silence with no lights at all.

1. Turn off everything: headlights, radio, A/C, phone chargers.
2. Put your hazards on if you're anywhere near traffic.
3. Call your roadside provider or open its app. Have your location, plate number, and policy or membership number ready.
4. Ask for the estimated arrival time and write it down.
5. Wait outside the vehicle and away from traffic if you're in a lot with moving cars.

If the car won't start after rain, it may not be the battery at all. See [car won't start after rain: dead battery or something else](/car-wont-start-after-rain-dead-battery-or-something-else/). If you're stuck in a lot, [who can help with a dead battery in a parking lot](/car-battery-dead-in-parking-lot-who-can-help/) covers your options.

## What Won't Roadside Assistance Cover on a Dead Battery?

The jump is covered. A new battery usually isn't. Providers typically don't pay for the replacement battery, installation labor, or any repair work the diagnosis turns up.

AAA is the notable exception in many areas. Its battery service trucks can test your battery on the spot and sell and install a new one right there. You pay for the battery, but the service call itself stays covered. Pricing varies by battery type and region, so ask for a quote before agreeing.

Also not covered: jumps for vehicles not tied to your plan, and calls beyond your annual limit. If someone else is driving your car, check [whether AAA covers towing when someone else is driving your car](/does-aaa-cover-towing-for-someone-else-driving-my-car/), since the same membership rules often apply to battery calls.

## What If the Jumpstart Doesn't Work?

If the car starts and dies again quickly, or

 won't start at all after a jump, the problem is probably not just a drained battery. Two common causes:

- **Failed alternator.** The battery gets a charge from the jump, then drains because the alternator isn't recharging it. Dimming headlights while running is the tell. See [alternator died while driving: symptoms and what happens next](/alternator-died-while-driving-symptoms-what-happens-next/).
- **Bad starter or corroded terminals.** A white or green crust on the battery posts blocks current. A driver can sometimes clean it on the spot.

One specific tip: after a successful jump, don't shut the engine off right away. Drive at least 20 to 30 minutes, ideally on a road at steady speed, so the alternator can recharge the battery. Short trips and idling won't do it.

If the driver can't get it running, ask for a tow to a shop. Your plan's tow mileage limit now matters, so ask the dispatcher before the truck loads you up.

## Should I Pay for a Jumpstart Instead of Using Roadside Assistance?

If you have coverage, use it. If you don't, a private jumpstart typically costs less than a tow but varies by area, time of day, and how long you wait. Weekend and night calls usually cost more.

Without a plan, you can also try a neighbor, a portable jump starter if you keep one in the trunk, or an auto parts store that tests batteries for free. A portable lithium jump pack is a cheap piece of insurance and works without another car.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is a jumpstart free with AAA?**
A: Yes, for members, a battery jumpstart is included as part of your membership service calls. You only pay if you buy a replacement battery from the driver.

**Q: Does car insurance cover a jumpstart?**
A: Only if you added roadside assistance to your policy. Standard liability or collision coverage does not include jumpstarts. Check your declarations page for a roadside or towing endorsement.

**Q: How long does a roadside jumpstart take?**
A: The jump itself takes a few minutes. The wait for the driver is the variable, anywhere from 20 minutes to over an hour depending on demand, weather, and location.

**Q: Does my credit card cover a dead battery jump?**
A: Some premium cards include roadside dispatch, though many charge you per call and reimburse later. See [whether your credit card covers roadside assistance towing](/does-my-credit-card-cover-roadside-assistance-towing/) for how those programs work.

**Q: Can I jump my own car if I have cables?**
A: Yes, if you have a working donor car or a portable jump pack. Connect positive to positive, then the donor's negative to bare metal on your car, not the dead battery's negative post. If you're unsure, call for help instead.
