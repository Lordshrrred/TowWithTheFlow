> **Quick Answer:** If your car is submerged or partially submerged after flooding, do not attempt to start it. Call 911 first if anyone is still inside or in danger. Once you are safe, call a tow company to extract the vehicle, then call your insurance company to open a flood claim. Do not try to drive it out or start the engine. Water in the cylinders will destroy the engine instantly.

## Is It Safe to Approach a Flooded Car to Retrieve It?

Only if the water has fully receded and there is no current. Moving floodwater as shallow as six inches can knock an adult off their feet. A foot of standing water is enough to float most passenger cars. If water is still flowing around the vehicle, stay back and wait for it to stop. Your car is replaceable. Once conditions are safe, you can approach to assess damage and retrieve documents or valuables, but do not attempt to start the engine under any circumstances.

## Who Do I Call First When My Car Is Submerged?

Call 911 first if there is any person trapped in or near the vehicle, or if the car is blocking a road and creating a hazard. Once immediate safety is handled, your next call is to a tow company with flood extraction experience. Not every tow truck can safely recover a vehicle that has been partially or fully submerged. You need a flatbed, because a wheel-lift tow on a flooded car can allow water to continue sloshing through the drivetrain and interior during transport, making damage worse. After you have towing arranged, call your insurance company to open a claim before the car is moved if at all possible, since adjusters often need to document the vehicle in place.

If you have roadside assistance through AAA or your insurance policy, call that line too. Some policies cover flood extraction, though coverage limits vary. Check [what State Farm roadside assistance actually covers](/does-state-farm-roadside-assistance-cover-towing/) before assuming your plan pays for the full tow.

## Will My Insurance Cover a Submerged Car?

Your insurance covers flood damage only if you carry comprehensive coverage. Liability-only policies do not cover flood damage at all. Comprehensive is the portion that handles acts of nature: flooding, hail, fire, falling objects. If you have it, file the claim immediately and do not authorize any repair work before an adjuster inspects the car. The insurer may total the vehicle depending on the depth of submersion and how long it sat in water. A car that sat submerged for several hours with water reaching the dashboard or higher is almost always declared a total loss because water infiltrates the ECU (the engine control unit), the airbag modules, the transmission, and the interior foam, all of which are expensive to remediate properly.

For a full look at what you might pay out of pocket if coverage is limited, see [how much does a tow truck cost without insurance](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-without-insurance/).

## What Damage Does Submersion Actually Cause, and Does It Matter for Recovery Decisions?

Yes, it matters significantly. If the water line stayed below the bottom of the doors, the engine and electrical systems may have escaped the worst of it. If water reached the seat cushions or dashboard, assume the electrical system is compromised. If the car was fully submerged, you are almost certainly looking at a total loss. The specific damage mechanic techs watch for:

hydrolocked cylinders (water entered the combustion chamber, engine cannot turn over), saturated wiring harnesses, a water-contaminated transmission, and mold already beginning in the carpet and headliner. Starting a hydrolocked engine bends or breaks connecting rods immediately, turning a potentially repairable situation into a scrap heap. That is why leaving the key alone is the single most important thing you can do.

## What Happens After the Tow Truck Gets There?

The tow operator will assess whether the vehicle is stable enough to winch or roll onto the flatbed. If it has sunk into soft ground or is partially embedded in debris, recovery gets more complex and more expensive. A basic flood recovery tow in most metro areas runs $150 to $300. Add winching fees if the vehicle is off-road or stuck in mud, and you can reach $400 to $600 or higher before transport. Once at the shop or impound lot, do not let anyone attempt to start the car until a mechanic has drained the cylinders, checked the oil for water contamination (it will look milky or gray), and confirmed the electrical system is safe. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can a flooded car be saved or is it always totaled?**
A: It depends on water depth and soak time. A car that took on water only at floor level for a short time can often be saved with thorough drying and electrical inspection. One that sat submerged past the dashboard for hours is almost always a total loss due to ECU damage, hydrolocking risk, and mold.

**Q: How long do I have to file a flood claim with my insurance?**
A: Most insurers want you to report the loss promptly, ideally within 24 to 48 hours. Check your specific policy, but waiting several days can complicate the claim. Call your insurer as soon as the vehicle is in a safe location.

**Q: Should I try to start my car to see if it still runs after a flood?**
A: No. If water entered the air intake or cylinders and you attempt to crank the engine, you will hydrolock it. Hydrolocking bends or snaps connecting rods and typically destroys the engine beyond economical repair. Wait for a mechanic to inspect it first.

**Q: Who is responsible for towing costs if flooding was caused by a city drainage failure?**
A: Possibly the municipality, but this requires filing a claim against the city and proving negligence, which is a slow legal process. Pay the tow out of pocket to get your car secured, then pursue reimbursement separately. Do not wait on a city claim before moving the vehicle.

**Q: Does my comprehensive insurance also cover a rental car while my flooded vehicle is being assessed?**
A: Only if you added rental reimbursement coverage to your policy. This is a separate optional add-on. Check your declarations page or call your insurer directly to confirm before renting.
