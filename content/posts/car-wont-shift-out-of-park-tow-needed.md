> **Quick Answer:** Your car won't shift out of park because of a failed brake-transmission shift interlock (BTSI), a dead battery, a broken brake light switch, or a damaged shift cable. Before calling a tow, try pressing the brake pedal firmly and locating the manual shift lock override slot near your shifter. If none of that works, you need a tow. Do not force the shifter.

## Why Won't My Car Shift Out of Park?

The most common culprit is the brake-transmission shift interlock, a safety system that prevents shifting unless the brake pedal sends a signal to a solenoid in the shifter assembly. If that solenoid fails, the signal never arrives and the car stays locked in park no matter how hard you push. A dead or weak battery causes the same result because the solenoid needs power to release. Less often, the brake light switch (the small switch on the brake pedal arm itself) fails and kills the signal upstream. A stretched or broken shift cable, common on higher-mileage automatics, is a mechanical failure that no solenoid override will fix.

## What Can I Try Before Calling a Tow?

Use the manual shift lock override before you call anyone. On most vehicles, it is a small slot or cap near the base of the shifter, sometimes labeled "Shift Lock Release" and covered by a plastic plug you pop out with a coin or flathead screwdriver. Insert a key, screwdriver, or the override tool from your owner's manual, press it down, then press the brake pedal and move the shifter. This bypasses the BTSI solenoid electrically and lets you shift manually.

If that does not work, check whether your brake lights are on when someone presses the pedal. No brake lights means a dead brake light switch, which is a $15-30 part but requires diagnosis and replacement before the car will shift normally again. Also try jump-starting the battery if it is dead or borderline. A healthy 12-volt battery is required for the interlock to function. If the car starts and you still cannot shift, the problem is mechanical, and forcing the shifter at that point risks snapping the shift cable entirely.

## Is It Safe to Drive After Using the Manual Override?

Using the override once to move the car out of a dangerous spot is fine. Do not rely on it as a daily workaround. The BTSI exists to prevent the car from rolling when you move the shifter without intending to. Driving regularly without a functioning interlock means one lapse in brake-pedal pressure during shifting could result in an unintended gear change. Get the solenoid or brake light switch repaired at a shop before you drive normally again. If you used the override to shift into drive and now need to park, the car may lock in park again when you turn it off. Plan your stop carefully.

## What Does It Cost to Fix a Car That Won't Shift Out of Park?

A brake light switch replacement runs $75 to $150 at most shops, including parts and labor. The BTSI solenoid itself costs $30 to $80 for the part, but labor to access and replace it ranges from $100 to $200 depending on how buried it is in the center console. If your shift cable is broken, expect $150 to $300 for parts and labor. None of these are cheap-out repairs. If the car will not move at all, you will also need a tow on top of the repair bill. ## When Do I Just Call a Tow Truck?

Call a tow if the manual override does not release the shifter, if you hear or feel a snap in the shifter when trying to move it (broken cable), or if the car is in a location where leaving it creates a safety hazard. A car stuck in a parking garage, on a hill, or blocking traffic cannot wait for a mobile mechanic appointment. In those cases, get it on a flatbed and let a shop diagnose it properly. If you are weighing whether your roadside assistance plan covers this, check your policy's language around "mechanical disablement." Most plans including AAA and State Farm do cover a tow in this situation. ## Common Questions

**Q: Is there a manual shift lock override on every automatic car?**
A: Most automatic transmission vehicles built after the mid-1990s have one, but the location varies. Check near the base of the shifter or consult your owner's manual under "shift lock release." Some older vehicles and a few newer push-button-shift designs handle it differently.

**Q: Can a dead battery cause a car to get stuck in park?**
A: Yes. The brake-transmission shift interlock solenoid needs 12-volt power to release. A dead or very weak battery cuts that power and the shifter locks. Jump-start the battery first before assuming the solenoid itself is bad.

**Q: What if I can shift into neutral but not drive?**
A: If you can get to neutral, you can at least roll the car to a safer spot with the engine on. This points more toward a shift cable or shifter mechanism issue than a BTSI problem. Do not drive it. Shift to neutral, release the parking brake carefully, and push or roll it to a safe location before calling a tow.

**Q: Will insurance or roadside assistance pay for the tow if my car is stuck in park?**
A: In most cases, yes. Roadside assistance plans treat a stuck shifter as a mechanical disablement, which is a covered reason for towing. Confirm with your provider. If you do not have roadside coverage, [check whether your auto insurance covers towing](/does-insurance-cover-towing-if-car-is-not-driveable/) for non-accident breakdowns.

**Q: Can I damage my transmission by forcing the shifter out of park?**
A: Yes. Forcing a stuck shifter can snap the shift cable, crack the shift mechanism housing, or damage the park pawl inside the transmission itself. The manual override is the right tool for this. Brute force is not.
