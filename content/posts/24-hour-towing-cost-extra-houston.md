> **Quick Answer:** Houston 24-hour towing typically costs $50-100 extra for after-hours service. Base towing runs $75-125, so expect $125-225 total for nights, weekends, and holidays. Distance and vehicle type affect the final price.

## What To Do

1. **Call multiple tow companies** to compare after-hours rates. Prices vary significantly between operators. Three calls takes five minutes and can save you $60 or more.

2. **Ask for the total cost upfront** including base rate, after-hours fee, and mileage charges. Don't accept vague estimates. A reputable company will give you a firm number before the truck rolls.

3. **Check if your insurance covers towing** through roadside assistance. Many policies include 24-hour service at no extra cost. Pull up your insurance app or call your carrier before paying out of pocket.

4. **Verify the company is licensed** with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation before agreeing to service. You can check TDLR's online license lookup in under two minutes.

5. **Get the driver's name and truck number** when they arrive. Legitimate operators always provide this information. If someone refuses, send them away and call another company.

6. **Pay only after your vehicle is delivered** to the destination. Never pay upfront for towing services. This protects you if the driver attempts to change the rate mid-job, a practice known as predatory towing that does happen in Houston.

## What It Might Cost

**Standard Houston towing rates:**
- Daytime towing: $75-125 base rate
- After-hours surcharge: $50-100 extra
- Mileage: $3-5 per mile beyond first 5-10 miles
- Heavy vehicles (trucks, SUVs): Additional $25-50

**Total 24-hour towing cost: $125-225**

Weekend and holiday rates often match after-hours pricing. Some companies charge flat rates for short distances, others use mileage from pickup. If you are being towed more than 20 miles, that mileage charge adds up fast. A 25-mile tow with a $4 per mile rate adds $60-80 on top of your base and surcharge, pushing the total toward $275 or higher for a large vehicle.

Flatbed towing costs more than wheel-lift. If you drive an all-wheel-drive or four-wheel-drive vehicle, always request a flatbed. Using a wheel-lift on an AWD vehicle can damage the transfer case and turn a $150 tow into a $1,500 repair.

**Insurance typically covers:**
- AAA: Up to 4 service calls per year, with towing included up to a set mileage limit depending on your membership tier (Classic covers 5 miles, Plus covers 100 miles)
- Auto insurance roadside: Usually 24-hour coverage included, but filing a claim can sometimes affect your rate, so check with your agent first
- Credit card benefits: Check your card's emergency services, as Visa Signature, certain Chase cards, and American Express cards often include roadside dispatch at no added cost

## Common Questions

**Q: Why is towing so much more expensive late at night in Houston?**
A: After-hours surcharges cover the operator's overtime, reduced crew availability, and increased liability during low-visibility conditions. Most companies treat anything after 8 or 9 PM and before 6 AM as off-hours, and weekends often carry the same premium even in daylight.

**Q: Can a Houston tow company change the price after they hook up my car?**
A: Legally, no. Texas requires tow operators to provide a written or verbal estimate before work begins, and they cannot increase the agreed rate once your vehicle is loaded. If a driver tries to raise the price at delivery, document everything, refuse to pay the inflated amount, and file a complaint with TDLR.

**Q: Does my deductible apply if I use my auto insurance for roadside towing?**
A: Roadside assistance coverage is usually a separate add-on with no deductible, so most policyholders pay nothing out of pocket for covered tows. However, if you file it under comprehensive instead, your deductible does apply. Call your insurer before the tow and ask specifically which coverage applies.

## Stay Safe

- Stay in your vehicle if on a busy road until help arrives
- Turn on hazard lights and use flares or reflective triangles
- Never accept rides from unlicensed tow operators
- Keep your doors locked until the legitimate tow truck arrives
- Take photos of your vehicle before it's loaded for transport
- Ask for the destination address and estimated arrival time
