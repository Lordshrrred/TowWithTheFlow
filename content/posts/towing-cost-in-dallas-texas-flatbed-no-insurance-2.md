> **Quick Answer:** A flatbed tow in Dallas with no insurance runs $95 to $175 for a short local haul (first 5 miles), then $3 to $5 per mile after that. On major corridors like I-35E or LBJ Freeway, after-hours calls and highway service fees can push that base rate to $200 or more. Pay out of pocket and get a written quote before the truck hooks up.

## How Much Does a Flatbed Tow Cost in Dallas If I Have No Insurance?

Without insurance, you are paying cash, and the rate depends on distance, time of day, and where you broke down. A local flatbed tow inside Dallas city limits, say from Deep Ellum to a shop in Uptown, will cost $100 to $150 total. A longer haul from the Stemmons Freeway interchange near I-35E and I-30 out to Garland or Irving adds $15 to $25 per additional mile.

Flatbeds cost more than wheel-lift trucks because the driver has to load your entire car onto a platform. That extra equipment and time justifies the higher base rate. For AWD vehicles, vehicles with low ground clearance, or anything that cannot roll, a flatbed is not optional. It is the only safe method, and a good shop will tell you the same.

## Why Is the Price Higher on Certain Dallas Highways?

Location drives cost more than most people expect. If you break down on the LBJ Freeway (I-635), the Dallas North Tollway, or on I-30 near the High Five interchange, tow companies often charge a highway service fee ranging from $25 to $50 on top of the base rate. That fee covers the driver's time sitting in traffic and the added risk of a highway pickup.

The Mixmaster, where I-35E and I-30 converge downtown, is one of the highest-cost breakdown zones in the city. Access is complicated, merge traffic is constant, and drivers charge accordingly. Breaking down on the DNT between LBJ and the Bush Turnpike (SH-190) can generate similar fees because of toll-road access logistics. Know this going in so you are not surprised when the invoice arrives.

## What Should I Do the Moment My Car Stops Moving?

Get off the travel lane immediately. If you can, coast to an exit ramp or a frontage road. If you are stuck on I-35E or I-635 with no shoulder room, turn on your hazards, stay in the car with your seatbelt on, and call 911 if traffic is actively passing close to your door. Do not stand behind or beside the vehicle on a highway.

Once you are as safe as possible, call at least two towing companies and get a verbal quote from each before you commit. Ask specifically: base rate, per-mile rate, and whether there is a highway or after-hours surcharge. Get the company name and driver's name when you call. If the driver shows up and quotes a number higher than what dispatch told you, you have grounds to push back or call someone else.

## What If This Happens After Midnight or on a Weekend?

After-hours and weekend rates in Dallas are real and significant. Most tow companies apply a surcharge between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. and on weekends. That surcharge typically runs $25 to $75 on top of the standard flatbed rate. A tow that costs $120 at 2 p.m. on Tuesday can run $185 at 1 a.m. on Saturday.

The after-hours premium is most aggressive in outlying areas like Mesquite, Duncanville, and far north Dallas near SH-121. Fewer drivers are working those zones late, so the ones who are available charge more. You can sometimes avoid the worst of it by calling a 24-hour dispatch center rather than a smaller owner-operator who sets their own hours. ## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average flatbed tow cost in Dallas with no insurance?**
A: Expect $100 to $175 for a local tow within Dallas city limits. Add $3 to $5 per mile for anything over 5 miles, plus potential surcharges if you are on a major highway or calling after hours.

**Q: Do I have to use a flatbed, or can I get a cheaper wheel-lift tow?**
A: If your car is AWD, has low clearance, or cannot roll freely, a flatbed is not optional. For a standard two-wheel-drive sedan that rolls, a wheel-lift is cheaper and fine for short local tows. Ask the dispatcher what your car qualifies for.

**Q: Will a Dallas tow company take a credit card if I have no insurance?**
A: Most will. "No insurance" does not mean cash only. You are simply paying out of pocket rather than filing a claim. Credit cards are accepted by most licensed tow operators in Dallas. Confirm payment method before the truck dispatches.

**Q: How do I avoid getting scammed by a predatory tow truck in Dallas?**
A: Get a written or texted quote before hookup, confirm the company name and license plate when the truck arrives, and never let a driver who showed up uninvited at your breakdown hook up your car without your explicit agreement. Unsolicited tow trucks at accident scenes are a known issue.

**Q: How long will I wait for a flatbed tow in Dallas during rush hour?**
A: On I-35E, I-635, or US-75 during peak hours (7 to 9 a.m. and 4 to 7 p.m.), wait times of 45 to 90 minutes are common. Calling during off-peak hours cuts that to 20 to 40 minutes for most parts of the city.
