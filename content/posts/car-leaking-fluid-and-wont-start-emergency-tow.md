> **Quick Answer:** Stop cranking the engine and call for a tow. A car that leaks fluid and won't start can have a dead fuel, oil, coolant, or brake system, and trying again can destroy the engine or start a fire. Look at the puddle color, keep everyone away from it, and tell the dispatcher what you see. Do not drive it, even a short distance.

## Should I Try Starting the Car Again If It's Leaking Fluid?

No. Every extra crank risks turning a repairable problem into a dead engine or a fire.

If the leak is oil and the engine is nearly empty, cranking grinds metal on metal. If it's fuel, the pump keeps pushing gasoline onto a hot exhaust or battery terminal. If it's coolant, a cracked line or failed water pump may already have cooked the engine.

Do this in order:

1. Turn the key off and take it out of the ignition.
2. Set the parking brake and turn on your hazards.
3. Get everyone out and well away from the car, on the side away from traffic.
4. If you smell raw gasoline, stay at least 50 feet back and don't touch anything electrical, including your phone flashlight near the puddle.
5. Call for a tow.

If you see smoke or flames, skip the tow call. Call 911 first. See [smoke coming from under the hood](/smoke-coming-from-under-hood-while-driving-what-to-do/).

## What Does the Color of the Fluid Tell Me?

Color tells you how bad it is and what to tell the dispatcher. Slide a piece of white paper or cardboard under the puddle if you can do it safely.

- **Green, orange, or pink, sweet smell:** Coolant. Likely a burst hose, bad water pump, or radiator crack. See [radiator hose burst](/radiator-hose-burst-while-driving-can-i-drive-to-shop/).
- **Brown or black, slick, burnt smell:** Engine oil. A failed oil filter, drain plug, or pan gasket can empty the crankcase fast.
- **Clear to light amber, strong gasoline smell:** Fuel. A cracked fuel line or leaking injector. This is the fire risk, so keep your distance.
- **Red or reddish-brown, thin:** Transmission fluid or power steering fluid, depending on where it's pooling.
- **Clear or slightly yellow, slippery, near a wheel:** Brake fluid. If the pedal feels soft or sinks, do not move the car at all.
- **Clear and watery, under the cabin:** Usually just A/C condensation. Harmless, and not why your car won't start.

Photograph the puddle and the spot under the car before you call. It helps the shop later.

## When Does a Fluid Leak Mean I Need an Emergency Tow Instead of a Jump?

Any active leak plus a no-start means tow, not jump. A jump start fixes a weak battery, and a leak plus a no-start almost never points at the battery.

If the car clicks but doesn't turn over and there's no puddle, that's a battery or starter problem. See [car won't start clicking sound](/car-wont-start-clicking-sound-need-tow-or-jump-start/). If it cranks but never fires and you see fluid, assume the leak caused it until a mechanic proves otherwise.

One tell worth knowing: an oil leak that dropped the level low can trigger the oil pressure light before the engine quits. ## What Should I Tell the Tow Dispatcher?

Give them the fluid type, whether the car turns over, and exactly where you are. That lets them send the right truck the first time.

Say these things:

- "It's leaking [color] fluid and won't start."
- "I need a flatbed." A leaking transmission or a car with a seized engine should not be dragged on its drive wheels.
- Your exact location: highway and mile marker, nearest exit, or cross streets.
- If the brakes feel soft, tell them. The driver needs to know before loading.

If you're on a highway shoulder, stay out of the vehicle and behind the guardrail when possible. See [car died on highway shoulder](/car-died-on-highway-shoulder-safe-to-wait-for-tow-truck/) for how long to wait and where to stand.

## How Much Will the Tow and the Repair Cost?

A basic local tow usually runs a flat hookup fee plus a per-mile charge, and a flatbed costs more than a hook-and-chain truck. Emergency or after-hours calls add to that. Prices vary a lot by city, so see our local breakdowns such as [towing cost in Atlanta per mile](/towing-cost-in-atlanta-georgia-per-mile/) or [how much a tow truck costs in Houston](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-in-houston-texas/) for realistic numbers.

Before you pay out of pocket, check your insurance or a membership. Many policies cover towing, but there are limits on distance and number of uses. See [GEICO roadside assistance](/geico-roadside-assistance-how-many-times-per-year/) as an example of how those caps work.

Repair cost depends entirely on the source. A loose drain plug or a clamp is cheap. A cracked fuel line is moderate. A seized engine from lost oil is the expensive one, which is why not cranking matters.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is it safe to drive my car a short distance if it's leaking fluid and won't start?**
A: It won't start, so you can't drive it, and you shouldn't try rolling it or pushing it into traffic. Leave it parked, hazards on, and call a tow.

**Q: Can I just add oil or coolant and try again?**
A: Only if you've found a small, obvious cause and the engine isn't overheated or seized. If it's still leaking, you're just pouring fluid onto the ground. If you smell fuel, don't try anything. Tow it.

**Q: What if the fluid is clear and I can't tell what it is?**
A: Smell it and feel it with a paper towel, not bare skin. Watery with no odor is likely A/C condensation. Slippery near a wheel is brake fluid. Gasoline smells unmistakable. When unsure, treat it as serious and tow.

**Q: Will a tow truck driver refuse my car because it's leaking?**
A: Rarely. They tow leaking cars all the time, but tell them upfront so they bring the right equipment. A fuel leak may require extra precautions.

**Q: Does my insurance cover the tow if the leak caused the breakdown?**
A: Roadside coverage usually covers the tow regardless of cause, up to your plan's distance and use limits. The repair itself is typically not covered unless you have a mechanical breakdown policy. Check your policy before you call.
