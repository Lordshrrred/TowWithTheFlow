> **Quick Answer:** Call 911 first if anyone is hurt or if the car is a traffic hazard. If everyone is safe and the car is off the road, call your roadside assistance provider or a tow truck directly. Do not try to drive out of a ditch on ice. You need a tow with a winch, not a push. Keep your hazard lights on and stay inside the car until help arrives.

## Who Do I Call First After Sliding Into a Ditch on Ice?

Call 911 if there are injuries, if your car is blocking traffic, or if you're in a dangerous position like on a highway shoulder or partially in a travel lane. If everyone is fine and the car is fully off the road, skip 911 and call your roadside assistance provider directly. Police don't retrieve cars from ditches. That's a tow truck's job.

The call order for a non-injury ditch situation:

1. **Roadside assistance** through your insurance, AAA, or a standalone policy
2. **A local tow company** if you don't have roadside coverage
3. **911** only if you feel unsafe, someone is injured, or the car is creating a hazard for other drivers

Your state's 511 traffic information line can give you road condition updates if you're deciding whether it's safe to wait outside the vehicle.

## Can I Just Get Pushed Out, or Do I Actually Need a Tow?

You almost certainly need a tow with a winch. A car buried in a ditch on ice has no traction and often has one or more wheels off the ground. Pushing or rocking it out makes the situation worse, and it risks damaging your axles, bumper, or undercarriage. A flatbed or a wrecker with a cable winch is the right tool. Winch-out service is different from a standard tow and some policies cover it separately. Confirm with your provider before they dispatch.

If the ditch is shallow and the car is barely off the road, a tow truck driver may still opt to winch rather than hook up for a full tow. Either way, you're calling the same number. For more on what your coverage actually pays, see [does comprehensive insurance cover towing after an accident](/does-comprehensive-insurance-cover-towing-after-accident/).

## How Do I Stay Safe While I Wait for Help?

Stay inside the car if you're off the road and not in immediate danger. A car in a ditch is visible and provides protection. Standing outside on an icy road puts you at serious risk from other sliding vehicles.

Turn on your hazard lights immediately. If you have road flares or reflective triangles in your trunk and can access them safely without stepping into traffic, place them 50 to 100 feet behind your vehicle. Keep the engine running for heat if the exhaust pipe is clear of snow or dirt. A blocked exhaust pipe causes carbon monoxide buildup inside the cabin, which is fatal. Check it before you idle.

If visibility is poor and you're on a highway, call 911 even without injuries. Whiteout conditions and icy roads mean other drivers may not see you until it's too late. Read more on [how to stay safe waiting for a tow truck on the highway](/how-to-stay-safe-waiting-for-tow-truck-on-highway/).

## Does My Insurance Cover a Ditch Tow After Sliding on Ice?

It depends on what coverage you carry. Roadside assistance added to your auto policy almost always covers a winch-out and tow. Comprehensive coverage handles damage to your car from the slide itself, like a bent wheel, damaged suspension, or cracked bumper. Liability alone covers nothing for your own vehicle.

If you hit something on the way into the ditch, like a guardrail or a fence post, that's a collision claim, not comprehensive. Check with your insurer before authorizing any repairs at the shop. Take photos of the car's position and any damage before the tow truck moves anything. That documentation protects you if the insurance company questions the circumstances later.

## Common Questions

**Q: Should I call 911 or a tow truck first after sliding on ice?**
A: If no one is hurt and the car is fully off the road, call a tow truck or your roadside assistance provider first. Call 911 if there are injuries, the car is blocking traffic, or you feel unsafe where you are.

**Q: How long will it take for a tow truck to reach me on an icy road?**
A: During a winter storm, expect 60 to 120 minutes or longer. Tow companies get slammed with calls all at once when roads ice over. Call immediately after the incident rather than waiting to see if conditions improve.

**Q: Will my car drive normally after being pulled out of a ditch?**
A: Not always. Even a low-speed slide can bend a wheel, damage a tie rod, or knock the alignment out. Have the car inspected before driving it at highway speeds, especially if it pulled to one side or felt loose on the way to the shop.

**Q: What if I can't get cell service after sliding into a ditch?**
A: Turn on your hazard lights, stay in the car, and wait for a passing motorist to stop or call for you. If you must leave the car, move well away from the road and stay visible. Carrying a roadside emergency kit with a whistle and bright-colored cloth helps if you're in a rural area with low traffic.

**Q: Is a winch-out covered by AAA if I slid off the road?**
A: Yes. AAA covers winch-out service as part of standard membership when your car is stuck off the road. It counts as one of your annual service calls. The tow that follows, if needed, also applies toward your annual tow benefit.
