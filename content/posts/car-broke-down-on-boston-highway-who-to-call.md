> **Quick Answer:** If you're in immediate danger or blocking a lane, call 911 first. Otherwise, call your roadside provider (AAA, your insurer, or your car maker's assistance line), then a local tow company if you have none. Stay in the car with your seatbelt on if you're on a narrow shoulder, or behind the guardrail if you can get there safely.

## Who Do I Call First When My Car Dies on a Boston Highway?

Call 911 first if you're stopped in a travel lane, on a blind curve, or anywhere another driver could hit you. Otherwise, call your roadside assistance provider first.

Boston highways are tight. The Mass Pike (I-90), I-93 through the city, the Southeast Expressway, Route 128/I-95, and Storrow Drive all have short or nonexistent shoulders in stretches. A disabled car in those spots is a real hazard, and State Police can send a cruiser to block traffic behind you. Tell the dispatcher your direction of travel, the nearest exit or mile marker, and any landmark you can see.

If you're safely off the road, work down this list:

1. **Your roadside plan.** AAA, your auto insurer's roadside coverage, or your car maker's assistance line (many new cars include it free for a few years). Check the app or the sticker on your window.
2. **A local tow company.** Only if you have no coverage or the plan can't reach you quickly.
3. **A family member or friend.** For a ride, not a repair.

## What Should I Do While I Wait on I-93 or the Pike?

Get your car as far right as you can, turn on your hazards, and stay put with your seatbelt on unless the shoulder is wide enough to stand behind a guardrail.

Standing next to your car on a Boston highway is more dangerous than sitting in it. Traffic on I-93 near the Zakim Bridge or the Pike near the Allston-Brighton toll area moves fast and bunches up unpredictably.

Do this in order:

- Turn the wheels away from traffic so a rear impact doesn't push you into the lane.
- Hazards on, parking brake set, car in park.
- If it's dark, turn on the interior light so you're visible.
- Use your phone's map to confirm your exact location, and read the nearest overhead exit sign or green mile marker to dispatch.
- Skip flares and warning triangles unless you can place them without leaving a safe spot. Walking out into traffic to set one is not worth it.

If you smell fuel, see smoke, or the car is on fire, get out on the passenger side, move well away from the road, and call 911.

## Can I Call a Tow Company Directly, and How Do I Avoid Getting Burned?

Yes, but on a Boston highway you often can't pick your own tow. If State Police are involved, they may rotate in a tow operator from their approved list, and you generally have the right to request a different company if you speak up early.

If you do call yourself, be careful with anyone who just stops and offers help. Unsolicited tow trucks that show up fast are a known problem on busy urban highways. Before anyone hooks up your car:

- Ask for the company name, a quoted price, and where they're taking the car.

## How Long Will It Take for Help to Arrive?

Plan on 30 to 90 minutes in most cases, longer during rush hour or bad weather. Highway calls take longer than street calls because the truck has to reach you through the same traffic you're stuck in.

Response times stretch during the morning and evening commutes on I-93 and I-90, and during snow or heavy rain. If your plan's estimate is much longer than that, ask for a callback time. If you're stuck far past the quoted window, [here are your options when a tow truck is taking too long](/tow-truck-taking-too-long-what-are-my-options-roadside/), and [how long you can reasonably wait on a highway](/how-long-can-you-wait-for-a-tow-truck-on-highway/).

Weather changes the math on price too. [Towing after a storm in Boston](/towing-cost-in-boston-massachusetts-after-storm/) often costs more because demand spikes.

## Should I Get My Own Ride Instead of Waiting?

Only if waiting becomes unsafe. A Boston highway shoulder in a Nor'easter, extreme cold, or heavy snow is not a place to sit for two hours with a dead engine and no heat.

If you have kids, elderly passengers, or anyone medically fragile, tell the 911 dispatcher so they prioritize you. If you can't wait, ask the tow driver whether your passengers can ride in the cab. Most operators allow it when they have room.

Rideshare drivers can't legally pick up on a highway shoulder, so don't count on an Uber or Lyft to reach you where you are. Once the tow truck gets you off the highway to a safe lot or shop, then a rideshare makes sense.

## Common Questions

**Q: Should I call 911 or a tow company first on a Boston highway?**
A: Call 911 if you're in a travel lane, on a narrow shoulder, or in any danger. If you're safely stopped, call your roadside assistance provider first.

**Q: What is the number for towing on the Mass Pike?**
A: There's no single public number to memorize. Call 911 if you're in danger, or use your roadside provider's number from your insurance card or app. State Police may also arrange a tow for you if they respond.

**Q: Can State Police tow my car from the highway?**
A: They don't tow it themselves, but they can call a rotation tow operator to clear a disabled vehicle. You can usually ask to use your own provider instead if you act quickly.

**Q: Is it safe to walk to the next exit on I-93 or the Pike?**
A: No. Highway walking is dangerous and often illegal. Stay in or behind your car unless fire or an immediate hazard forces you out.

**Q: How much will a highway tow in Boston cost?**
A: Expect a base hookup fee plus a per-mile charge, and more during storms or nights. See the [Boston towing cost breakdown](/towing-cost-boston-massachusetts/) for current ranges.
