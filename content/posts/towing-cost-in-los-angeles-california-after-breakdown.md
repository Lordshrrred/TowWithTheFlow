> **Quick Answer:** After a breakdown in Los Angeles, expect to pay roughly $100 to $200 for a local tow of a few miles, plus about $4 to $8 for each additional mile. Rush hour, nights, weekends, and flatbeds push the price up. Get the total quoted before the truck is hooked up, and ask where the car is going and what the storage fee is.

## How Much Does a Tow Cost in Los Angeles After a Breakdown?

A typical Los Angeles tow runs $100 to $200 for the hookup and the first few miles, with extra mileage on top. Exact rates vary by company, so treat these as ranges, not promises.

- **Hookup fee:** the base charge for getting the car on the truck. Often $75 to $150.
- **Per-mile rate:** commonly $4 to $8 after the included miles.
- **Flatbed:** often $25 to $75 more than a hook-and-chain tow, and necessary for AWD, low-clearance, and most newer cars.
- **After hours:** nights, weekends, and holidays can add a surcharge.

Cross-town tows get expensive fast. Hauling a car from the 405 near LAX to a shop in the San Fernando Valley can run several hundred dollars. For a closer look at mileage math, see [towing cost per mile in Los Angeles](/towing-cost-per-mile-los-angeles/).

## Why Is Towing Pricier on LA Freeways?

Freeway tows cost more because of traffic, risk, and time. A driver stuck on the 10 near the 405 interchange at 5 p.m. burns an hour to cover a few miles, and the price reflects it.

Rush-hour surcharges are common on the 405, 10, 101, 110, and 5 corridors. Working on a narrow shoulder is also riskier for the driver, which some companies price in. Our breakdown of [towing cost in Los Angeles during rush hour traffic](/towing-cost-los-angeles-rush-hour-traffic/) covers how much extra to expect.

If you're on a freeway right now, getting safe matters more than saving money. Read [what to do when your car breaks down on a Los Angeles freeway](/car-broke-down-on-los-angeles-freeway-what-to-do/) first.

## Who Sends the Tow Truck, and Does It Change the Price?

On a freeway, the tow often comes from the California Highway Patrol rotation, and that changes your options. If you call 911 or CHP for a stalled car on a state highway, CHP may dispatch a rotation tow company assigned to that stretch. Rotation tows have regulated rates, but you generally can't shop around.

If you call a company yourself, or use AAA or your insurance roadside coverage, you pick the truck and negotiate the price. Be careful about trucks that pull up unrequested. Unsolicited "helpful" tow drivers near a breakdown are a known problem, and they may quote high or vague numbers once your car is hooked up.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged on a Tow?

Get a total price in writing, by text if possible, before the driver touches your car. Then ask these questions:

1. What is the total including hookup, mileage, and any surcharges?
2. Is that a flatbed, and does my car need one?
3. Where exactly is it going, and what are the storage fees if I can't get there tonight?
4. Do you take card, and will I get an itemized receipt?

Take photos of the car, the odometer, and the truck's license and company name before it leaves. If the quote suddenly changes at the destination, you have a record.

Tow to a shop you've confirmed is open, or to your home. Dropping a car at a random yard after hours can mean storage charges that start the next morning. If you're uninsured and watching every dollar, [towing cost in Los Angeles with no insurance](/towing-cost-in-los-angeles-california-no-insurance/) lays out what to expect.

## Can My Insurance or AAA Cover the Tow?

Often yes, and you should check before paying out of pocket. AAA membership usually includes a tow of a set distance, and the level you bought decides how many miles are free. Many auto policies add roadside coverage for a small monthly fee, and some credit cards include it too.

Call your insurer or the number on your membership card, and confirm the free mileage before agreeing to anything. If the destination is farther than your coverage, you pay the overage, so choose a shop within range when you can.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a flatbed tow cost in Los Angeles?**
A: Expect roughly $25 to $75 more than a standard tow, so a short local flatbed commonly lands around $125 to $250. Ask for the total quote before loading.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow company myself or wait for CHP?**
A: Calling yourself lets you compare prices, but CHP rotation tows have regulated rates and arrive without you searching. On a busy freeway, safety and speed usually matter more than the savings.

**Q: Can I be charged storage fees right after a tow?**
A: Yes. Tow yards typically charge daily storage starting the next day, sometimes sooner. Pick up the car or move it to a shop quickly, and ask about the daily rate upfront.

**Q: Do tow companies in LA charge more at night?**
A: Many do. After-hours, weekend, and holiday surcharges are common, so ask whether the quote includes them.

**Q: What if the driver demands cash only?**
A: Ask for the quote and a receipt in writing first. If you can't verify the company, think twice, and if you feel unsafe, call 911.
