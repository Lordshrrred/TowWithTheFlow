> **Quick Answer:** In Phoenix, a wheel lift tow typically runs $75 to $150 for a short local haul, while a flatbed usually adds $25 to $75 on top. Flatbed is the safer choice for all-wheel drive, low cars, EVs, and anything with a transmission problem. If your car is front-wheel drive and mechanically sound, a wheel lift is fine and cheaper.

## What Is the Difference Between Flatbed and Wheel Lift Towing?

A flatbed carries your whole car on a flat deck with all four wheels off the ground. A wheel lift hooks under two wheels and drags the other two on the pavement.

Wheel lifts are common in Phoenix because the trucks are cheaper to run and faster for quick pickups. The catch: the two wheels still touching asphalt are spinning, and that matters depending on your drivetrain. A flatbed has none of that risk, which is why most shops now send one by default for anything unusual.

## Which Costs More in Phoenix, Flatbed or Wheel Lift?

Flatbed costs more, usually by $25 to $75 on a local tow. Expect a hookup fee of roughly $75 to $125 plus a per-mile charge after the first few miles, with the flatbed carrying the higher rate on both.

Rough Phoenix ranges for a short local tow:

| Tow type | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Wheel lift, first few miles | $75 to $150 |
| Flatbed, first few miles | $100 to $200 |
| Extra mileage | Varies by company, ask for the per-mile rate |

These are ballpark figures, not quotes. Rates climb at night, on weekends, and on freeways like the I-10 or Loop 101 where the driver has to work next to fast traffic. ## Does My Car Need a Flatbed?

Yes, if it is all-wheel drive, four-wheel drive, an EV, a low-clearance car, or has a transmission or drivetrain failure. Anything with a damaged axle or wheel also needs a flatbed.

The reasons are mechanical, not sales talk:

- **AWD and 4WD:** Dragging two wheels forces the center differential or transfer case to spin against the stationary pair. That can wreck it. Subarus, Audis, and most newer crossovers fall here.
- **EVs and hybrids:** Spinning wheels can push power back through the motor. Tesla and others say flatbed only in the owner's manual.
- **Transmission trouble:** If your car is slipping or won't go into gear, wheels dragging can finish off what is left. See our guide on [automatic transmissions that won't go into gear](/car-wont-go-into-gear-automatic-transmission-emergency/).
- **Low front bumpers:** Sports cars and lowered cars scrape on a wheel lift's cradle.

Check the owner's manual towing section before you call. It will say "flatbed only" if that applies.

A wheel lift is fine for a front-wheel-drive or rear-wheel-drive car with a healthy drivetrain, hauled a short distance. Think a dead battery or flat tire on a Civic or Camry, towed a few miles to a shop in Tempe or Mesa.

For rear-wheel-drive cars, the driver should lift the rear wheels, so the driven wheels are off the ground. Ask which end is going up. For front-wheel drive, the front goes up. A good driver knows this. If yours hesitates, request a flatbed.

## What Should I Tell the Dispatcher to Avoid a Wrong Truck or Surprise Bill?

Tell them your make, model, and drivetrain, then ask for a total price before they dispatch. Say "I need a flatbed" if your car falls in the categories above, because dispatchers often send whatever truck is closest.

Ask these on the phone:

1. What is the hookup fee and the per-mile rate?
2. Is that the total to my destination, including any freeway or after-hours charge?
3. Is the truck a flatbed?

If you broke down on a freeway shoulder, such as the I-17 near the Black Canyon curve or the I-10 through the Broadway Curve, stay out of the car on the passenger side away from traffic if you can do so safely. ## Common Questions

**Q: Is flatbed towing always better than wheel lift?**
A: For protecting your car, yes, since no wheels touch the road. But it costs more, and for a healthy front-wheel-drive car on a short tow, a wheel lift does the job fine.

**Q: Can a wheel lift tow damage my transmission?**
A: It can if your car is AWD, 4WD, or already has transmission trouble, because the wheels left on the ground keep spinning drivetrain parts. Front-wheel-drive cars towed by the front wheels with a healthy drivetrain are generally fine.

**Q: How do I know if my car is flatbed only?**
A: Check the towing section of your owner's manual. It states "flatbed only" for AWD, 4WD, many EVs, and some automatics. If you cannot find it, ask for a flatbed to be safe.

**Q: Will the tow company charge extra if I ask for a flatbed?**
A: Often yes, a modest bump on the hookup fee or per-mile rate. Ask for the total price including the flatbed before the truck rolls so there is no surprise at drop-off.

**Q: Can I ride in the tow truck with my car?**
A: Usually yes, in the cab, space permitting. Ask the dispatcher when you call. Most drivers allow one or two passengers, but it is their call.
