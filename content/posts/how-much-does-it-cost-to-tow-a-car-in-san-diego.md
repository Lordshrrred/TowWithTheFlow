Towing a car in San Diego is not cheap, and the price swings fast depending on where you broke down, how far the shop is, and what kind of truck shows up.

> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in San Diego runs $75 to $150 for the first 5 miles, with most companies charging $4 to $7 per mile after that. A breakdown on the I-5 near downtown going to a Mission Valley shop will land around $100 to $130. Long-distance hauls, flatbed trucks for AWD vehicles, and after-hours calls push that number to $200 to $400 or more. No insurance adds another layer of out-of-pocket cost.

## What Does a Tow Truck Actually Cost in San Diego Right Now?

Most San Diego tow calls cost between $85 and $175 for a standard local tow under 10 miles. The hook-up fee (the flat charge just to dispatch and hook your car) runs $50 to $100 on its own, and then the per-mile rate stacks on top.

## Does It Cost More to Get Towed on the I-5 or I-8 Than Side Streets?

Yes. Freeway breakdowns in San Diego almost always cost more. CHP has primary control of tow assignments on freeways like the I-5, I-8, I-15, and SR-163, and they rotate through a list of registered tow providers. You do not get to pick the cheapest one. Those CHP-dispatched companies often charge at the higher end of the rate scale.

If you break down on the I-8 near the College Avenue interchange or on the I-15 near Miramar Road, expect that hook-up fee to be closer to $85 to $100 before the mileage even starts. If you can safely pull off onto a side street or a parking lot before calling a tow, you regain the ability to call any private company and negotiate.

## What If I Don't Have Insurance or Roadside Coverage?

You pay out of pocket, and San Diego is not forgiving. Without AAA or insurance roadside coverage, a standard local tow in areas like Hillcrest, North Park, or Chula Vista will cost $100 to $175 cash or card. Some independent operators in the area will negotiate slightly, especially for cash payment, but do not count on a big discount.

## Flatbed vs. Wheel-Lift: Does My Car Require One or the Other?

Your car may require a flatbed and you may not have a choice. AWD vehicles, low-clearance sports cars, and vehicles with drivetrain damage all need a flatbed. Wheel-lift towing on an AWD car like a Subaru Outback or an Audi Quattro can destroy the transfer case.

In San Diego, a wheel-lift tow runs about $75 to $130 for a local job. A flatbed on the same route adds $25 to $75. If the tow company shows up with a wheel-lift and you have AWD, tell them before they hook up. A good operator will know to send the right truck. If you're not sure which setup your car needs, [this flatbed vs. wheel-lift guide for San Diego](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/) walks through it clearly.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a tow truck cost in San Diego at night or on a weekend?**
A: After-hours and weekend tows in San Diego typically add $50 to $100 to the base rate. A job that costs $120 on a Tuesday afternoon can run $180 to $220 on a Saturday night.

**Q: Can I choose my own tow company if CHP dispatches the tow on I-5?**
A: No, not immediately. When CHP calls in a tow on a California freeway, they use a rotation system. You can request your preferred company, but CHP may not wait if traffic is a hazard. Once the scene is cleared, you can transfer the car to another shop at your own cost.

**Q: How much does it cost to tow a car from San Diego to Los Angeles?**
A: A long-distance tow from San Diego to the L.A. area (roughly 120 miles) typically runs $300 to $500 depending on destination, truck type, and the company. Get a flat-rate quote upfront rather than a per-mile estimate for anything over 50 miles.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover the tow in San Diego?**
A: Only if you have roadside assistance added to your policy. Basic liability or even full coverage does not automatically include towing. Check your declarations page or call your insurer before assuming you're covered.

**Q: Is it cheaper to use AAA for a tow in San Diego?**
A: If you're already a member, yes. AAA covers a free tow up to 5 miles with basic membership, and up to 100 miles with Premier membership. The annual cost of a Plus or Premier membership is often less than a single out-of-pocket tow, so it pays for itself fast in San Diego's traffic-heavy environment.
