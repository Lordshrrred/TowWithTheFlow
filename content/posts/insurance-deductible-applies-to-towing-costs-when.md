> **Quick Answer:** Your insurance deductible applies to towing costs when the tow is part of a covered claim (like after an accident or comprehensive damage). It does NOT apply to roadside assistance towing, which is usually a separate no-deductible benefit with dollar limits.

## What To Do

1. **Check if you have roadside assistance coverage** - This covers breakdowns, dead batteries, flat tires. No deductible, but limited to $100-200 per incident typically.

2. **Determine if your situation involves a covered claim** - Accident damage, theft recovery, vandalism, weather damage all trigger your deductible.

3. **Call your insurance company immediately** - They'll tell you which coverage applies and authorize the tow to approved facilities.

4. **Ask about direct billing** - Many insurers pay the tow truck directly to avoid out-of-pocket costs.

5. **Keep all receipts** - Whether it's roadside assistance or a claim, document everything for reimbursement.

6. **Know your policy limits** - Roadside assistance often covers only the nearest qualified repair shop, not your preferred mechanic across town.

Most roadside assistance plans cap mileage at 5 to 15 miles per incident. If your nearest in-network shop is 20 miles out, you pay the overage yourself, often at $3 to $7 per mile. That can add $35 or more to what you thought was a free tow. Know that number before the truck starts rolling.

Also worth noting: using roadside assistance usually does NOT count as filing a claim. It will not raise your rates. Using comprehensive or collision coverage for an accident tow might, depending on your insurer and state. That distinction matters when you are deciding which coverage to invoke.

## When Deductible Applies vs When It Doesn't

**Deductible APPLIES:**
- Towing after an accident you're claiming
- Moving your car after comprehensive damage (hail, flood, vandalism)
- Towing from theft recovery location
- Any tow connected to a claim you're filing

**Deductible DOES NOT apply:**
- Dead battery jumpstart and tow to shop
- Flat tire service and tow
- Lockout service
- Running out of gas
- Mechanical breakdown towing (if you have roadside coverage)

One edge case that trips people up: if your car breaks down because of an accident, even a minor fender bender, and you file a claim for the damage, the tow gets bundled into that claim. Your deductible kicks in on the whole thing, including the tow. If the tow costs $150 and your deductible is $500, you pay the full $150 yourself because the tow cost does not exceed the deductible. Insurance only starts paying after you have hit that deductible threshold.

Another edge case: flood damage. If rising water stalls your engine and you need a tow, that falls under comprehensive coverage. Your comprehensive deductible applies, which on many policies is different from your collision deductible. Plenty of drivers carry a $100 comprehensive deductible and a $500 collision deductible. Check which one you actually have before assuming.

## What It Might Cost

Roadside assistance towing: Usually free up to policy limits ($100-200)
Claim-related towing: You pay deductible ($250-1000 typical), insurance covers rest
Out-of-pocket towing: $75-200 for local, $3-7 per mile for long distance

If you end up paying out of pocket, a local tow in a metro area typically runs $75 to $125 for the first 5 miles, then $3 to $7 per additional mile. Rural areas and highway tows from remote locations push toward the high end. After-hours, weekend, and holiday calls usually add a $25 to $75 surcharge on top of that. If your car needs a flatbed because it is all-wheel drive, a low-clearance sports car, or has transmission damage, expect another $20 to $50 added to the bill.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will filing a roadside assistance tow raise my insurance rates?**
A: In almost all cases, no. Roadside assistance is treated as a service benefit, not a claim against your liability or collision coverage. Using it does not trigger a rate review.

**Q: What if the tow truck arrives before I reach my insurer?**
A: Pay out of pocket and save every receipt. Your insurer can reimburse you after the fact, but they need documentation. Get the tow company's name, invoice number, and itemized receipt before the driver leaves.

**Q: Does AAA roadside assistance work the same way as insurance roadside coverage?**
A: The end result is similar, no deductible and a mileage cap per call, but AAA is a separate membership program, not insurance. It does not interact with your auto policy at all, so using it will never affect your premiums or claims history.

## Stay Safe

- Never assume coverage - always call your insurer first
- Get pre-authorization before agreeing to expensive tows
- Understand your roadside assistance has mileage limits
- Some policies require using their preferred tow companies
- Emergency towing at night or weekends costs more
