> **Quick Answer:** When an alternator dies, your car runs on battery power alone until the battery drains, usually 15 to 30 minutes, sometimes less. Signs include a battery or ALT warning light, dimming headlights, a weak radio, and a flickering dash. Turn off every accessory, get off the road safely, and plan on a tow. Once the battery is flat, the engine will stall and won't restart.

## What Are the Symptoms of an Alternator Dying While Driving?

The most common symptom is a battery-shaped or "ALT" or "GEN" warning light on the dash. That light means the charging system has stopped charging, not that the battery is simply old.

Other signs usually show up together:

- Headlights and dash lights dim, then flicker, especially at idle
- Power windows move slowly
- Radio cuts out or the screen resets
- Speedometer or gauges act erratically
- A burning rubber smell (slipping serpentine belt) or a hot-wire smell from the alternator itself
- A whining or grinding noise from the front of the engine, often a failing alternator bearing
- Power steering feels heavy if the belt that drives the alternator has snapped, since that belt often drives other components too

If you see the battery light, assume the alternator is dead until proven otherwise. Don't wait to see if it goes away.

## How Long Can I Keep Driving After the Alternator Dies?

Expect roughly 15 to 30 minutes on a healthy battery, and far less if the battery is old, the weather is cold, or the car has lots of electrical load. A modern car with a big touchscreen, heated seats, and electric power steering can die in under 10 minutes.

Your engine doesn't run on the alternator. It runs on fuel and spark. But the spark, the fuel pump, and the engine computer all pull from the battery once the alternator stops feeding it. When voltage drops too low, the fuel pump slows, the ignition weakens, and the engine stalls. Then the car will not restart, because the starter needs more current than a drained battery can give.

Your move: shut off the A/C, heated seats, rear defroster, stereo, and phone chargers. Leave only the headlights if it's dark. Drive to the nearest safe place, not your destination.

## What Should I Do Right Now If My Alternator Quits on the Highway?

Get to the right shoulder or the nearest exit before the engine dies, because a stall in a live lane is the real danger here. If you lose power steering or brake assist, you can still steer and brake. They just take far more muscle. Don't panic and stomp.

1. Turn off all non-essential electrical loads immediately.
2. Turn on your hazards only if you've stopped or are slowing a lot. They draw power too.
3. Signal early and move to the right shoulder or an exit ramp. Parking lots and gas stations are better than the shoulder.
4. Once stopped, put it in park, set the brake, and get everyone out on the side away from traffic if you're on a highway shoulder.
5. Call for help.

If you're stuck on a narrow shoulder, a bridge, or in a live lane, call 911 first. A jump-start will get you moving, but it won't fix the problem. If the alternator is dead, the jump only restores the battery's charge temporarily, and the car will die again within minutes to a few miles once the jump energy is used up.

Jump-starting also carries risk: a dying alternator can put out erratic voltage and damage the battery, engine computer, or other electronics. If you're dealing with a no-start, our guide on [a dead battery in a parking lot](/car-battery-dead-in-parking-lot-who-can-help/) covers who can help.

A quick test: if the car starts fine after a jump but dies as soon as the jumper cables come off, the alternator is the culprit.

## Should I Tow It or Get a Mobile Mechanic?

Tow it unless you're within a mile or two of a repair shop and the battery is still holding. Driving on a failing alternator risks stalling in traffic, and a dead battery also means no hazards, no brake lights, and no power steering.

Alternator replacement typically runs about $400 to $900 installed, depending on the vehicle. Some cars are easy, and others bury the alternator behind other components and push labor higher. A tow adds to that, so check what your coverage includes. If you're covered, [here's how far insurance will tow your car](/how-far-will-insurance-tow-my-car-after-a-breakdown/) after a breakdown. If money is tight, see [your options if you have no money for a tow truck](/car-broke-down-no-money-for-tow-truck-what-are-my-options/).

Tell the dispatcher it's a charging system failure and that the car may not start. Ask whether they carry a flatbed. A dead alternator with a drained battery often means the car can't be put in neutral on some electronic shifters, and a flatbed avoids that problem.

## Common Questions

**Q: How do I know if it's the alternator or the battery?**
A: If the car starts with a jump but dies shortly after the cables come off, it's the alternator. If it won't hold a charge overnight but runs fine while driving, it's likely the battery. A shop can test both in minutes.

**Q: Can a bad alternator drain a new battery?**
A: Yes. A failed alternator stops recharging the battery, so even a brand-new one will run flat quickly. A bad diode in the alternator can also drain the battery overnight while parked.

**Q: Will my car run without an alternator?**
A: Briefly. It runs off battery power until the voltage drops too low, usually 15 to 30 minutes, then stalls and won't restart.

**Q: Is it safe to drive with the battery light on?**
A: Only to the nearest safe stopping point. The battery light means no charging, so you're on borrowed time and could lose power steering assist, lights, and engine power without further warning.

**Q: What if the battery light and the temperature gauge both go up?**
A: Pull over immediately. A snapped serpentine belt drives the alternator, water pump, and often the power steering, so you lose charging and engine cooling at once. See [overheating on the freeway](/car-overheating-on-freeway-should-i-pull-over-or-keep-driving/) for what to do next.
