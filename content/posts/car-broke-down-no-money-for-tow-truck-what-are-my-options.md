> **Quick Answer:** Get safe first, then check for towing you already have: insurance roadside add-on, credit card benefits, manufacturer assistance, or AAA through a family member. If none apply, call tow companies, ask about payment plans, and request the shortest possible tow to a nearby shop. Never leave the car on a highway shoulder to avoid the cost. That gets cars towed at your expense anyway.

## What Should I Do First If I Can't Afford a Tow?

Get yourself and your passengers safe before anything else, because money problems can be solved from a safe spot and not from a shoulder. Pull as far off the road as possible, turn on your hazards, and if you're on a highway, get out on the passenger side and stand well behind the guardrail. If you're stuck in traffic lanes or feel unsafe, call 911. Calling for safety is not the same as calling for a tow bill.

Once you're safe, don't call the first tow company you find. The first number you dial sets your price. Spend ten minutes checking the free options below first.

## What Free Towing Might I Already Have?

You may already have towing coverage you've never used. Check these in this order:

- **Your auto insurance app or policy.** Many policies include roadside assistance that you never noticed. Others let you add it after the fact on some policies, so read [whether car insurance covers roadside assistance if you add it](/does-car-insurance-cover-roadside-assistance-if-i-add-it/). Know the limits too: [Progressive's towing coverage limit](/progressive-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-limit/) is a good example of coverage that caps at a certain distance or dollar amount.
- **Your credit card.** Several Visa, Mastercard, and Amex cards include roadside dispatch or reimbursement. Flip the card over or log in to the benefits page.
- **Manufacturer roadside assistance.** Many newer cars come with a complimentary period, often tied to the factory warranty. Look in your glovebox manual or the owner's app.
- **Someone else's AAA.** AAA membership often follows the member, not the car, so a parent, spouse, or friend with a membership may be able to request the tow for you. The details matter, so see [whether AAA covers towing if the car isn't registered in your name](/does-aaa-cover-towing-if-car-is-not-registered-in-my-name/).
- **Ride-share driver programs.** If you drive for Uber or Lyft, check [what roadside help those platforms offer](/does-lyft-or-uber-offer-roadside-assistance-to-drivers/).

If a coverage hotline gives you a maximum tow distance, use that limit strategically. A covered tow to a shop three miles away beats a free tow that stops short of anywhere useful.

## How Can I Lower the Cost If I Have to Pay?

Ask for the shortest tow that solves the problem, and say your budget out loud. A basic local tow commonly runs a flat hook-up fee plus a per-mile rate, so every mile you skip comes straight off the bill. Quotes for a flatbed versus a wheel-lift tow also differ, and not every car needs a flatbed, which you can check with [this flatbed checklist](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/).

1. **Call three companies, not one.** Ask for the total price, not the hook-up fee. Get it in a text or email if you can.

## What Are My Non-Towing Options?

Sometimes the cheapest tow is no tow. A dead battery, a blown tire, or an empty tank are fixable on the spot, and a jump start or a spare swap costs far less than a flatbed. A friend with jumper cables is worth more than any app.

If the car is in a parking lot or a quiet side street and not blocking anything, it can sit there for a short time while you sort out money. Check the signage first, since lots with posted rules will tow you within hours. Never leave a car on a highway shoulder or a no-parking street hoping to deal with it later. It will be towed, and impound lot fees stack up daily.

Look into local help too. Some cities and counties run assistance programs for low-income drivers, and community organizations, churches, and nonprofit auto programs sometimes cover emergency repairs or towing. Dialing 211 connects you to local social services by region. It won't dispatch a truck, but they can point you to funding that exists near you.

## What Happens If I Just Leave the Car or Can't Pay at All?

Leaving the car on public property gets it towed and impounded, which costs far more than a normal tow. Impound lots charge a release fee plus a daily storage fee, and the longer you wait, the bigger the bill gets. After a set period, the lot can sell the car to recover its fees.

If you truly can't pay, call the tow company or impound lot and tell them immediately. Some will set a payment plan or reduce storage fees if you act fast. Silence costs you the most. If the car is worth little, ask what a junk-car buyer would pay. Many offer free towing when they buy a non-running car, which turns the vehicle into cash instead of a bill.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I get a free tow if I don't have insurance?**
A: Rarely from a tow company directly, but check your credit card benefits, manufacturer warranty roadside program, and any family member's AAA membership. Local assistance programs through 211 may also help.

**Q: Will a tow company tow my car if I can't pay up front?**
A: Some will, especially small independent operators, but you have to ask before they load the car. Get the payment terms in writing, because the company can hold the car at their lot until paid.

**Q: Can I tow my own car with a rope or chain?**
A: It is risky and illegal in some states, and it can wreck your power brakes and power steering without the engine running. If you try it, use a proper tow strap and a second driver, and only for very short distances.

**Q: How much does a basic tow cost?**
A: Most local tows run a flat hook-up fee plus a per-mile charge, and prices vary by city, time of day, and truck type. Compare your own city's rates using the Nashville, Charlotte, or Houston guides on this site, or get three written quotes.

**Q: What if a junkyard will tow it for free?**
A: Many salvage buyers offer free towing if you sell them the car, which can turn a dead car into cash. Get a quote first, and make sure the title is in your name.
