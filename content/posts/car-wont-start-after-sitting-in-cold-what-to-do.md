> **Quick Answer:** Cold weather kills batteries and thickens engine oil, both of which make starting harder or impossible. Try a jump start first. If the engine cranks slowly or clicks, you almost certainly have a weak battery. If it cranks normally but won't fire, the problem is likely fuel delivery or a flooded engine. Either way, do not keep grinding the starter or you will make things worse.

## Why Won't My Car Start After Sitting in the Cold?

The most common reason is a battery that was already marginal and finally gave out when the temperature dropped. A car battery loses roughly half its cranking power at 0°F compared to 80°F, and a battery that starts fine in September can fail completely in January. The second most common reason is thickened engine oil, especially in older vehicles running the wrong viscosity for winter. A distant third is fuel system issues: water in the fuel line freezing in the line, or a weak fuel pump that loses prime after sitting overnight.

Listen when you turn the key. A rapid clicking sound points directly to a dead or nearly dead battery. A slow, labored crank points to a weak battery or thick oil. Complete silence usually means a dead battery, a blown fuse, or a failed starter solenoid. A normal crank with no start points away from the battery entirely. That distinction matters before you spend money.

## What Do I Do First When My Car Won't Start in the Cold?

Try a jump start. It is the fastest way to confirm whether the battery is the problem and often fixes it completely. Connect positive to positive, negative to negative, and ground the final negative clamp to a bare metal bracket on the dead car, not to the battery terminal itself. Let the running car idle for two full minutes before you attempt to start the cold car. If it starts, drive it for at least 30 minutes to recharge the battery. Do not just idle in a parking lot.

If a jump start does not work after two attempts, stop. Continuing to crank a dead battery or a flooded engine causes real damage. At that point you are looking at a tow or a mobile battery service, not a roadside fix.

If your car is in a parking garage, the process is the same but finding another vehicle to jump from can be harder. Check [Car Won't Start in Parking Garage: What to Do Right Now](/car-wont-start-in-parking-garage-what-to-do/) for that specific scenario.

## How Do I Know If It's the Battery or Something Else?

If the jump start works and the car dies again within a day or two, the battery is failing and needs replacement, typically $120 to $200 installed at an auto parts store or shop. Batteries in cold climates commonly last three to five years before cold weather exposes their weakness. If your battery is over four years old, replace it. Do not wait for a second failure.

## Is It Safe to Wait Outside for Help in the Cold?

Stay in the car if you are not on an active roadway. Your vehicle is a windbreak and retains heat far better than standing outside. If you have blankets, a coat, or an emergency kit in the trunk, get them. Run the engine in short intervals to stay warm if it starts but will not stay running, and crack a window slightly to prevent carbon monoxide buildup. Never run the engine in a closed garage.

If you are broken down on a highway shoulder in freezing conditions, get out of the car on the passenger side away from traffic and move behind a guardrail if one is present. A stationary vehicle on a highway shoulder is a serious hazard in reduced visibility conditions, which winter weather makes worse. ## Common Questions

**Q: Will warming up my car help it start in cold weather?**
A: Not if the battery is dead. Warming up helps with oil viscosity once the engine is running, but it does nothing for a battery that lacks the cranking amps to turn the engine over. Fix the battery problem first.

**Q: Can cold weather damage my battery permanently?**
A: Yes. If a battery discharges completely in freezing temperatures, the electrolyte inside can freeze and crack the plates. A battery that froze and thawed may show a full charge on a voltmeter but fail under load. Have it load-tested, not just voltage-tested, before trusting it.

**Q: My car started fine yesterday. Why won't it start today after one cold night?**
A: Temperature dropped below the battery's threshold. Batteries that are 70 to 80 percent healthy handle mild cold but fail suddenly when temperatures hit the teens or single digits. One cold night is often the event that exposes a battery that was already on its way out.

**Q: How long does a jump start take to charge a dead battery enough to start?**
A: Let the running vehicle idle for at least two minutes, then attempt the start. If it does not start in two tries, the battery is likely too far gone for a jump start to help, or the problem is not the battery at all.

**Q: Should I call a tow truck or a mobile battery service?**
A: Call a mobile battery service first if you are in a safe location like a parking lot or driveway. They can test and replace the battery on the spot, often for less than the cost of a tow plus shop labor. If you are on a highway or in a genuinely unsafe spot, call for a tow.
