> **Quick Answer:** If your automatic won't go into gear, press the brake firmly, make sure the engine is running, and try shifting again. If the lever is stuck in Park, use the shift lock release slot near the shifter. If it moves but the car doesn't, or the lever is loose with no resistance, stop trying. Don't force it. Call a flatbed tow.

## Why Won't My Automatic Car Go Into Gear?

Most of the time it's one of four things: low fluid, a failed shift interlock, a broken shifter cable, or a transmission that has lost hydraulic pressure.

Start by sorting out which symptom you have, because it decides your next move:

- **Lever won't leave Park:** Usually the brake switch or the shift interlock solenoid. Often a cheap fix.
- **Lever moves freely but the car doesn't move:** The shifter cable may have snapped or popped off the transmission. The lever is no longer connected to anything.
- **Engages with a clunk, then loses power:** Low fluid or a failing pump or clutch pack.
- **Dash shows D but nothing happens:** Possible fluid loss, internal failure, or a stripped torque converter.

## What Do I Do First When the Shifter Won't Move Out of Park?

Press the brake pedal hard with your foot, keep the engine running, and try again. The shifter is locked until the brake is pressed, and a bad brake light switch will keep it locked even when you do.

Quick test: have someone check whether your brake lights come on when you press the pedal. No brake lights usually means a failed brake light switch, which is the most common cause of a stuck shifter.

If that's the problem, use the shift lock override:

1. Set the parking brake and turn the engine off.
2. Look for a small slot or cap next to the shifter. Pry the cap off with a key or flathead.
3. Push a key or screwdriver into the slot and hold it down.
4. Press the brake, start the engine, and move the lever to Neutral or Drive.

Many Toyotas, Hondas, Fords, and GMs have this slot. Check your owner's manual for the exact spot. This gets you moving, but the brake switch still needs replacing, and a dead switch also means no brake lights on the car.

## Is It Safe to Keep Trying to Force It Into Gear?

No. Forcing a shifter that has no resistance, or revving the engine while it refuses to engage, can turn a $50 cable repair into a transmission rebuild.

Stop and tow if you notice any of these:

- The lever feels loose, floppy, or has no detent clicks
- A burning smell or smoke from under the car
- Red or brown fluid pooled under the transmission
- Engine revs but the car doesn't move, or it moves and then drops out
- Grinding or whining when you shift

Check the fluid only if your car has a dipstick, with the engine warm, running, and parked level. Fluid should be bright red or pink. Dark brown, black, or a burnt smell means the transmission has been cooking. Many newer cars have sealed transmissions with no dipstick, so don't hunt for one.

## Do I Need a Flatbed Tow for a Transmission That Won't Shift?

Yes, ask for a flatbed. A car stuck in Park or with a damaged transmission should not be dragged on its drive wheels, and a car that won't shift into Neutral can't be wheel-lifted safely.

Dragging a front-wheel-drive car on its front wheels with a bad transmission can finish off whatever's left inside. Tell the dispatcher up front: "automatic transmission, won't go into gear, needs a flatbed." That avoids a truck showing up that can't do the job.

## What Will the Repair Cost?

A shift cable, brake switch, or shift interlock solenoid is typically an inexpensive repair, usually a couple hundred dollars at most with labor. A low fluid fix is cheaper still if there's no leak.

Internal failures run much higher. A rebuild or replacement can cost several thousand dollars depending on the car. Get a written diagnosis before approving any transmission work, and ask the shop to show you the failed part or confirm whether a cable, switch, or fluid issue was ruled out first.

## Common Questions

**Q: Why won't my car shift out of Park even though I'm pressing the brake?**
A: The most common cause is a failed brake light switch or shift interlock solenoid. Check whether your brake lights work, then use the shift lock release slot beside the shifter to get the lever moving.

**Q: Can I drive with the shifter stuck in Park?**
A: No. If the shifter is stuck in Park and you override it, you still have a failing component. Use the override only to move the car to a safe spot or onto a flatbed, then fix the cause.

**Q: What does it mean if the shifter moves but the car doesn't go anywhere?**
A: The shift cable has likely snapped or disconnected from the transmission, or the transmission has lost fluid pressure. Either way, stop trying to drive and tow it.

**Q: Will low transmission fluid stop a car from going into gear?**
A: Yes. Low fluid reduces the hydraulic pressure needed to engage gears, causing delayed engagement, slipping, or no movement at all. Topping off may help temporarily, but find the leak.

**Q: Should I put the car in Neutral before towing?**
A: If it will go into Neutral, yes. If it won't, tell the tow operator so they use a flatbed and a proper winch procedure instead of dragging it.
