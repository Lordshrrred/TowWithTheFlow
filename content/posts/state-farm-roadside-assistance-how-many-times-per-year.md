> **Quick Answer:** State Farm does not publish a fixed annual limit on roadside assistance calls. Most policyholders can use it multiple times per year without a problem. However, State Farm reserves the right to non-renew your policy if they consider your usage excessive, and the threshold is not spelled out in the policy documents. Three to four calls in a year is generally fine. A pattern of monthly calls is not.

## Does State Farm Roadside Assistance Have an Annual Limit?

There is no printed number in your State Farm policy that says "you get X calls per year." The Emergency Road Service add-on is pay-per-use in one sense: each call triggers a claim, and that claim goes on your record. State Farm uses internal claim frequency guidelines to flag accounts, not a public cap. Think of it less like a punch card and more like a credit score. A few uses are fine. Too many in a short window and you risk a non-renewal notice at the end of your policy term.

This is different from AAA, which gives Classic members a specific number of free service calls per year. [AAA's structure is more transparent about limits](/aaa-towing-coverage-miles-per-year-limit/) if you want a direct comparison.

## What Does Each Roadside Assistance Claim Actually Cover?

State Farm's Emergency Road Service add-on covers towing to the nearest qualified repair facility, battery jump-starts, flat tire changes using your spare, lockout service, fuel delivery (up to a few gallons), and winching if you're stuck within 100 feet of a road. The coverage pays per incident up to the limit listed in your policy, which is typically $80 to $100 per call in standard policies, though your specific limit depends on your state and plan. Anything over that limit comes out of your pocket.

One thing drivers miss: if your spare tire is also flat, the coverage still only applies to one service call for that breakdown. You'd need a tow, not a tire change. [If you're in that situation, here's what to expect cost-wise](/flat-tire-spare-is-also-flat-tow-cost/).

## Will Using It Multiple Times Raise My Premium or Cancel My Policy?

Using roadside assistance does not directly raise your premium the way an at-fault accident does. It is not a collision or liability claim. But each call is logged, and State Farm's underwriting team reviews claim frequency. If you call four or five times in one policy year, you're unlikely to have any problem. If you're calling every few weeks, that pattern can lead to a non-renewal letter when your policy comes up.

A non-renewal is not the same as a cancellation mid-term. State Farm gives you notice before the policy ends, which gives you time to find another insurer. Still, it's worth knowing this is a real outcome for heavy users.

## Common Questions

**Q: How many times can I call State Farm roadside assistance before they drop me?**
A: State Farm doesn't publish a specific number. A few calls a year is generally not a problem. Frequent use, say, six or more calls in a single policy year, puts you at risk of non-renewal. The decision is made by underwriting, not by a fixed rule.

**Q: Does calling State Farm roadside assistance count as a claim on my insurance record?**
A: Yes, it is logged as a claim even though it typically does not affect your premium the way an accident claim does. The frequency of these claims is what underwriters look at, not the dollar amount.

**Q: Does State Farm roadside assistance cover towing costs if my car breaks down on the highway?**
A: Yes, it covers a tow to the nearest qualified repair shop up to your per-incident dollar limit, usually $80 to $100. If the tow costs more than that limit, you pay the difference. Long-distance towing is not covered.

**Q: Can I use State Farm roadside assistance for someone else's car?**
A: No. The coverage is tied to the vehicle listed on your policy, not to you as a person. If you're driving someone else's car and it breaks down, their insurance or a service like AAA would need to cover it.

**Q: Is State Farm roadside assistance worth adding to my policy?**
A: It depends on what you're comparing it to. At a few dollars per month, it's cheap. But the per-incident dollar cap is low, and heavy use risks your policy. If you drive a lot or own an older car with reliability issues, a standalone plan or [a dealership-based roadside plan](/roadside-assistance-through-car-dealership-worth-it/) might give you better coverage without the claim-frequency risk.
