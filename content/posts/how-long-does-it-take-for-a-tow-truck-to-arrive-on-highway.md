> **Quick Answer:** Most highway tow trucks arrive in 30 to 60 minutes. Urban interstates during off-peak hours can be faster. Rural stretches, rush hour, storms, and nights or holidays can stretch the wait to two hours or more. Ask the dispatcher for a specific ETA, give your exact mile marker, and stay out of the car on the guardrail side while you wait.

## How Long Does a Tow Truck Take to Arrive on the Highway?

Plan on 30 to 60 minutes in most cases, with 20 minutes possible in a dense metro and two hours or more in remote areas. There is no single number because wait time depends on how many trucks are near you and how fast they can reach you.

Here's what moves the clock:

- **Location:** A truck already patrolling a busy interstate corridor reaches you faster than one dispatched from 40 miles away on a rural stretch of I-80 in Wyoming.
- **Time of day:** Rush hour traffic slows the truck down. Overnight, fewer drivers are on call.
- **Weather:** Snow, ice, and heavy rain create more breakdowns and slow every truck on the road. Everyone is calling at once.
- **Who dispatches:** Insurance and club-assigned trucks go through a middleman, which can add time. Calling a local company directly is sometimes faster.
- **Truck type:** A flatbed for an all-wheel-drive car or a heavy-duty rig for an SUV or van may not be the closest truck available.

## What Should I Tell the Dispatcher to Get the Fastest Response?

Give your exact location first, because dispatchers cannot send a truck to "somewhere on the interstate." Every delay in the first two minutes of the call is a delay in your rescue.

Have these ready:

1. **Highway and direction:** "I-25 northbound," not "the highway."
2. **Mile marker or nearest exit.** The small green markers on the right shoulder work. If you can't see one, open your phone's maps app, drop a pin, and read the coordinates or cross street.
3. **Which side of the road you're on:** Right shoulder, left shoulder, or median.
4. **Vehicle details:** Make, model, drive type (AWD and 4WD often need a flatbed), and whether it rolls and steers.
5. **The problem:** Dead battery, flat tire, overheating, no start. This tells them what truck to send.
6. **Hazards:** Say so if you're on a blind curve, a narrow shoulder, or in active traffic.

Then ask: "What's the ETA, and will I get a call when the driver is close?" A vague answer like "soon" is not an ETA.

## Is It Safe to Wait on the Highway Shoulder?

Waiting on the shoulder is one of the most dangerous parts of a breakdown, so how you wait matters more than how long. Cars drifting onto the shoulder at highway speed are a real threat.

If you can still move, get as far right as possible, with your wheels turned away from the road. Hazards on. If you're near an exit, ramp, or wide pullout, creep there on a flat tire or low speed instead of stopping in a narrow spot. A dead car on a blind curve is worse than a rim ground down on a safe pullout.

Once stopped:

- Get everyone out on the passenger side, away from traffic, and stand well behind the guardrail if there is one. Do not stand between your car and traffic.

## What Can I Do If the Tow Truck Is Taking Too Long?

If your ETA has passed by 20 to 30 minutes with no update, call the dispatcher back for a status check, and start a second option at the same time. Don't wait in silence.

Call the company back and ask where the driver is. Trucks do get reassigned or delayed by earlier calls. If you used insurance or an auto club, ask whether you can cancel and have a local company dispatched directly, and whether they'll reimburse you. Check your policy first.

Open a second line to a local tow company in parallel. Cancel whichever truck arrives second, and be polite about it, since drivers lose time too. If you feel unsafe, say so: on a highway, a dispatcher may escalate or contact the state highway patrol. Call 911 if you're in immediate danger, such as stopped in a live lane or being approached by someone threatening.

## Common Questions

**Q: How long does AAA take to arrive on the highway?**
A: Expect roughly 30 to 60 minutes in most areas, longer in rural stretches, storms, or holiday weekends. The dispatcher quotes an ETA at the time of the call, and you can check status through the AAA app.

**Q: Will highway patrol send a tow truck faster?**
A: Sometimes. If you call 911 for a stalled vehicle in a travel lane, police may secure the scene and call a rotation tow, which can be quick but isn't always cheaper. On many interstates, state DOT patrols or highway assistance programs also help.

**Q: Can I call a tow truck on a highway myself, or does it have to go through police?**
A: You can call a tow company directly. Police involvement is only required in accidents, blocked lanes, or abandoned vehicles. Give the dispatcher your mile marker and direction.

**Q: Is a tow truck faster than waiting for roadside help from my insurance?**
A: It can be. A direct call to a local company skips a middleman, but you'll usually pay up front and seek reimbursement later. Insurance dispatch is slower in busy periods but costs you less out of pocket.

**Q: Should I stay in my car or get out while waiting on the highway?**
A: Get out and stand well away from traffic, behind a guardrail if possible, if your car is on a narrow shoulder or in a vulnerable spot. At night or with no safe place to stand, staying belted in with hazards on can be safer. Use whichever keeps you farthest from moving traffic.
