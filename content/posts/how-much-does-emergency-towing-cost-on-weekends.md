> **Quick Answer:** Emergency towing on weekends typically costs $100 to $250 for a local tow, which is often $25 to $100 more than the same tow on a weekday. The extra comes from after-hours or weekend surcharges, higher demand, and fewer drivers on call. Ask for a total price before dispatch, and check whether your insurance or roadside plan already covers the tow.

## How Much Does Emergency Towing Cost on Weekends?

Expect roughly $100 to $250 for a local weekend tow, depending on your city, distance, and time of day. A weekday daytime tow in the same area might run $75 to $150.

Most companies price in three parts:

- **Hookup fee:** a flat charge, often $50 to $125, for getting your car onto the truck.
- **Per-mile fee:** typically a few dollars per mile after the first few miles, which are sometimes included.
- **After-hours or weekend surcharge:** a flat add-on or a percentage on top of the base rate.

Saturday night and Sunday morning tend to be the most expensive windows. Holiday weekends can push rates higher still. Rates vary a lot by metro, so check a local breakdown like [towing cost in Atlanta per mile](/towing-cost-in-atlanta-georgia-per-mile/) or [how much a tow truck costs in Houston](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-in-houston-texas/) to see what your area looks like on a normal day.

## Why Is Weekend Towing More Expensive Than Weekday Towing?

Weekend towing costs more because fewer drivers are on shift and more people are calling at once. Tow operators pay overtime or call-out premiums to staff nights and weekends, and they pass that on.

Demand also spikes. Weekends bring more road trips, more drinking-related breakdowns, and more drivers who skip maintenance until something fails. When dispatch is backed up, the closest truck may be 40 minutes away, and some companies charge extra for the longer run to reach you.

Vehicle type matters too. A flatbed costs more than a wheel-lift, and all-wheel-drive cars, low-clearance cars, and anything with a failed transmission generally need a flatbed. If you're not sure which you need, read [how to know if your car needs a flatbed tow](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/) before you call so you don't pay for the wrong truck.

## What Should I Ask Before I Agree to a Weekend Tow?

Get a total price in writing or by text before the truck leaves the shop. Say these exact things on the phone:

1. "What's the total, all-in, including the weekend surcharge?"
2. "Is the hookup fee included in the first miles, or separate?"
3. "Do you charge for wait time or storage if the shop is closed?"
4. "Is this a flatbed or a wheel-lift?"

The storage question is the one that burns people. If you tow to a repair shop on Saturday night and it's closed until Monday, some lots charge a daily fee, and some shops won't unlock a drop-off area. Confirm the destination accepts weekend drop-offs, or ask about a key lockbox.

A company that won't quote a number, or says "we'll figure it out when we get there," is a red flag. Call the next one.

Often, yes. Check coverage before you pay out of pocket, because many drivers already have it and forget.

- **Auto insurance:** many policies include roadside add-ons with towing allowances. See [how many times per year GEICO roadside assistance can be used](/geico-roadside-assistance-how-many-times-per-year/) for an example of how limits work.
- **AAA or similar clubs:** basic tiers cover a short tow, and higher tiers cover longer distances. If the car isn't in your name, read [whether AAA covers towing in that case](/does-aaa-cover-towing-if-car-is-not-registered-in-my-name/).
- **Credit card or manufacturer roadside:** some cards and new-car warranties include towing.

If you have none of these, you can sometimes cut cost by towing only to the nearest safe, open location rather than your preferred shop across town. Per-mile charges add up fast on a long weekend haul.

If you're stuck on a highway, stay out of the car and away from traffic while you sort out pricing. Safety comes before the bill. Pull as far onto the shoulder as you can, hazards on, and call 911 if you're in a live lane or feel unsafe.

## Is It Worth Waiting Until Monday to Tow?

Only if the car is safe where it sits and not blocking anything. Waiting can save $25 to $100 in surcharges, but it can cost you more in other ways.

Leaving a car on a highway shoulder, in a tow-away zone, or in a private lot can get it ticketed or towed by someone else at a worse price. Parked on a quiet residential street or in your own driveway, waiting until Monday is a legitimate way to save money. Check any posted parking limits first.

Never wait on a fluid leak that could spread, or any problem that makes the car a hazard. If it's already late, [how long it takes a tow truck to arrive](/how-long-does-it-take-for-a-tow-truck-to-arrive-on-highway/) can help you decide whether to call now or tomorrow.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is there a weekend surcharge for towing?**
A: Many companies add one, either a flat fee or a percentage, for nights, weekends, and holidays. Not all do, so ask for the all-in price up front.

**Q: What is the cheapest day to get towed?**
A: Weekday daytime hours, usually Monday through Friday, are cheapest. Saturday night, Sunday, and holidays typically cost the most.

**Q: Do tow trucks charge more at night?**
A: Often yes. After-hours rates usually apply late evening through early morning, and they stack with weekend pricing.

**Q: Can I negotiate a weekend towing price?**
A: Sometimes. If you have a competing quote, mention it politely. Flat-rate local quotes have more room than per-mile pricing, and calling two or three companies takes only a few minutes.

**Q: Does roadside assistance cover weekend towing?**
A: Usually yes, and weekends don't change your covered tow allowance. You may still owe the difference if you exceed the mileage limit, so check your plan's cap.
