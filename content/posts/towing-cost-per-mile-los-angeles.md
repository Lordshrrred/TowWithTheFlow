## What Towing Costs Per Mile in Los Angeles

LA towing is priced in two parts: the hookup fee and the mileage rate. The hookup fee covers the truck showing up and getting your car loaded. The per-mile rate kicks in once the wheels are turning.

**Typical Los Angeles towing rates in 2026:**

- Hookup or base fee: $75 to $150
- Per mile: $3 to $7
- After-hours (nights, weekends, holidays): add $25 to $75 flat
- Flatbed upgrade: add $25 to $50
- Freeway service patrol (CHP-dispatched): sometimes free for the first 65 feet, then billed

A 5-mile tow from a surface street costs roughly $90 to $185. A 20-mile tow from the 405 to a shop in the Valley runs $135 to $290, sometimes more if the driver is billing hazard or freeway fees.

If you broke down during rush hour on the 10 or 101, the driver may quote a higher base rate due to traffic delay charges. That is not always legitimate. Ask for the rate card before you agree.

For a full breakdown of how peak traffic affects your bill, see [Towing Cost Los Angeles Rush Hour Traffic](/towing-cost-los-angeles-rush-hour-traffic/).

## What Drives the Price Up in LA

Los Angeles has specific conditions that add to a standard tow bill.

**Freeway breakdowns.** CHP sometimes dispatches a Freeway Service Patrol truck at no charge for a short move to the nearest exit. If you call a private company directly or the FSP cannot handle your vehicle type, you pay full rate. Read more about what a [freeway breakdown in LA actually costs](/towing-cost-in-los-angeles-california-freeway/).

**Distance to a shop.** Most breakdowns in LA are not close to a mechanic. If you are stuck in Culver City and your trusted shop is in Burbank, you are paying for 20-plus miles. Know your destination before you call.

**Vehicle type.** Standard sedans tow at base rates. AWD vehicles, lifted trucks, and low-clearance sports cars require a flatbed, which adds cost and sometimes availability time.

**Time of day.** After 10 PM and before 6 AM, expect a surcharge. Same for Sundays and holidays.

## How to Avoid Getting Overcharged

1. **Ask for a written or verbal quote before they hook up.** In California, tow companies are required to provide a price estimate on request. Get the hookup fee, per-mile rate, and any applicable surcharges confirmed before the truck moves your car.
2. **Look up the destination address ahead of time.** Know the mileage so you can verify the final bill.
3. **Use your insurance roadside assistance if you have it.** It is almost always cheaper than paying out of pocket, even after factoring in the claims process.
4. **Do not let a police-dispatched driver take your car to a storage lot unless required.** If you can arrange your own tow fast enough, you keep control of where it goes.
5. **Take a photo of the odometer or note mileage at pickup and drop-off.** Mileage padding is a documented problem in high-volume towing markets like LA.

## Stay Safe While You Wait

- Pull as far off the road as possible. On freeways, get past the white line and onto the shoulder completely.
- Turn on hazard lights immediately.
- Exit the vehicle and stand behind a guardrail if one is available. Do not stand between your car and traffic.
- If you are on a freeway and cannot safely exit the vehicle, stay inside with your seatbelt on and call for help.
- At night, stay visible. A flashlight or reflective triangle placed 50 to 100 feet behind the car helps.
