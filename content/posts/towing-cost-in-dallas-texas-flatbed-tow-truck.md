> **Quick Answer:** A flatbed tow truck in Dallas typically runs $95 to $175 for a local tow under 10 miles. Per-mile rates beyond the base distance land between $3.50 and $6.00. Where you're stranded matters: a pickup on I-35E near the downtown mixmaster costs more than one in a quiet Oak Cliff parking lot. Expect to pay more after hours, on weekends, and if your car requires wheel lifts or extra rigging.

## How Much Does a Flatbed Tow Truck Cost in Dallas Right Now?

A flatbed tow in Dallas starts with a hook-up fee of $75 to $125, then adds a per-mile rate on top. For a standard 5-mile haul across a single neighborhood, you're looking at $110 to $165 total. Push that to 15 miles, say from Garland into Uptown, and the bill climbs to $150 to $230. These are private-market rates. If the city or a PD-contracted company hooks your car on I-635 (LBJ Freeway), you may face a different rate structure set under contract with the city of Dallas.

Flatbeds cost more than wheel-lift trucks because the equipment is heavier, the setup takes longer, and they burn more fuel. If your car is all-wheel drive, has a damaged drivetrain, or sits very low, you need a flatbed. There is no cheaper workaround.

## Why Is the Price Higher When I Break Down on I-35E or the Mixmaster?

Towing on a major Dallas freeway costs more for two reasons: positioning and time. Getting a flatbed safely into a live lane or the narrow shoulders around the I-35E/I-30 Mixmaster interchange takes longer and puts the driver at real risk. Many operators charge a highway premium of $25 to $50 on top of standard rates for exactly that reason.

Response time also stretches on the busiest corridors. During rush hour on I-635 or US-75 (Central Expressway), a truck that is 8 miles away can take 40 minutes to reach you. Longer wait, same base fee. ## Does It Cost More for a Flatbed vs. a Regular Tow Truck in Dallas?

Yes, flatbeds run $20 to $50 more per job than a standard hook-and-chain or wheel-lift truck. The hook-up fee is higher, and the per-mile rate can be a dollar or more above a basic wheel-lift haul. A wheel-lift truck in Dallas may start at $55 to $75 and charge $3.00 to $4.50 per mile. A flatbed starts at $75 to $125 and charges $3.50 to $6.00 per mile.

The question of which type you actually need is worth checking before you call. Lowered cars, all-wheel-drive vehicles, and anything with front-end damage all require a flatbed. For a full side-by-side on the cost difference, see [towing cost in Dallas Texas hook and chain vs flatbed](/towing-cost-in-dallas-texas-hook-and-chain-vs-flatbed/). Do not let a company put a wheel-lift under a car that needs a flatbed. That will cost you far more at the shop than the $30 you saved on the tow.

## How Do I Know If I'm Being Charged a Fair Price?

Call two companies and compare. Prices in Dallas are not regulated by a city cap for private-party tows, so the spread between operators can be $60 or more for the same job. ## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average flatbed tow truck cost in Dallas for a 10-mile tow?**
A: For a 10-mile flatbed tow during daytime hours in Dallas, expect to pay $130 to $185. That includes the hook-up fee plus per-mile charges, assuming no extra rigging or highway premiums.

**Q: Does Dallas have any price regulation on towing fees?**
A: The city of Dallas regulates rates for police-dispatched tows through contracted companies, but private tows you call yourself are not subject to a city price cap. Always ask for an itemized estimate before the truck hooks up.

**Q: Can I request a flatbed if a regular tow truck shows up?**
A: Yes. If the truck that arrives is a wheel-lift and your car requires a flatbed (AWD, low suspension, front-end damage), you can and should insist on the right equipment. It may mean a longer wait, but it prevents drivetrain damage that costs thousands.

**Q: Will my insurance cover the flatbed tow cost in Dallas?**
A: It depends on your policy. Comprehensive or collision coverage often includes towing reimbursement, and some policies cover flatbed specifically. Check your declarations page or call your agent before paying out of pocket.

**Q: How long does it take for a flatbed tow truck to arrive in Dallas?**
A: In most Dallas neighborhoods, 30 to 45 minutes is typical during business hours. On congested highways like I-635 or US-75 during rush hour, plan for 45 to 60 minutes. Early morning and late night calls usually come in faster.
