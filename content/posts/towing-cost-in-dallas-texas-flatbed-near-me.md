## How Much Does a Flatbed Tow Cost in Dallas?

A local flatbed tow in Dallas typically costs $100 to $250. Prices vary by company, so treat this as a realistic range, not a guarantee.

Most Dallas operators charge a base hookup fee, then add a per-mile rate once you pass the included distance. A tow from a shoulder on I-35E near downtown to a shop in Oak Cliff is a short job. A tow from Plano to a dealer in Irving is not, and the mileage adds up fast.

Flatbeds cost more than hook-and-chain or wheel-lift trucks. The premium is worth it in specific cases (see below). For a side-by-side look, read [towing cost in Dallas hook and chain vs flatbed](/towing-cost-in-dallas-texas-hook-and-chain-vs-flatbed/).

## When Do I Actually Need a Flatbed Instead of a Regular Tow Truck?

You need a flatbed if your car is all-wheel drive, four-wheel drive, low to the ground, or has transmission or drivetrain damage. A flatbed puts all four wheels off the pavement, so nothing spins or drags.

Specific cases where I'd insist on one:

- **AWD vehicles** (Subaru, Audi Quattro, most newer SUVs). Towing with two wheels down can damage the center differential.
- **Lowered or sports cars.** A wheel-lift can scrape the front bumper and splitter.
- **Won't shift into park or neutral.** If the transmission is locked, see [car won't go into gear automatic transmission emergency](/car-wont-go-into-gear-automatic-transmission-emergency/).
- **Flat tires, broken axles, or seized engines.** Rolling wheels is a bad idea. See [car engine seized up: can it be towed safely](/car-engine-seized-up-can-it-be-towed-safely/).

If you drive a basic front-wheel-drive sedan with a dead battery, a wheel-lift is often fine and cheaper. Say so on the phone and ask for both prices.

## What Makes a Dallas Tow Cost More Than the Quote Range?

Time of day, location, and distance push the bill up. The three biggest add-ons in Dallas are after-hours surcharges, highway or high-traffic pickup, and long mileage.

- **After hours and weekends:** Many companies add a surcharge. See [tow truck cost in Dallas Texas after business hours](/tow-truck-cost-dallas-texas-after-business-hours/).
- **Highway pickup:** Getting a truck onto the shoulder of I-635 (LBJ Freeway) at rush hour or the High Five interchange at I-635 and US-75 is harder and slower. Some operators charge for it.
- **Distance:** Crossing the metroplex, say from Mesquite to Frisco via the Dallas North Tollway, can double or triple a local tow price.
- **Winching or recovery:** If your car is in a ditch, a parking structure, or off the road, expect extra labor charges.

## How Do I Find a Flatbed Near Me Without Getting Overcharged?

Call two or three companies and ask for a total price before you agree to anything. This takes five minutes and can save you $50 or more.

Use these questions:

1. "Is this a flatbed, and what's the total to tow from [exact location] to [exact destination]?"
2. "Does that include hookup, mileage, and any fees?"
3. "What's your ETA?"
4. "Do you take cards, and will I get an itemized receipt?"

Tip: Have your destination ready before you call. If you don't have a shop picked, tow to a shop within a few miles, not your home across town, and arrange repairs from there.

Be cautious of unmarked trucks that show up without being called, especially near accidents. Only accept service from a company you contacted or your insurer or roadside plan dispatched. ## What Should I Do While Waiting for the Flatbed?

Get to a safe spot, turn on your hazards, and stay out of the travel lane. On a Dallas freeway, safety comes before cost.

- Pull as far right as possible, ideally past an exit ramp or onto a wide shoulder.
- Stay in the car with your seatbelt on if you're on a busy highway shoulder. If traffic is close, exit on the passenger side and wait behind the guardrail.
- Photograph your car's condition and the odometer before loading. This protects you if there's a damage dispute.
- Keep your keys, registration, and proof of insurance accessible.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive than a regular tow in Dallas?**
A: Usually yes, by roughly $25 to $75 on a local job, depending on the company. It's worth paying for AWD, low-clearance, or damaged vehicles.

**Q: Can I get a flatbed tow in Dallas without insurance?**
A: Yes. You pay out of pocket, usually by card at drop-off. Pricing is the same, but you won't get reimbursement. See [towing cost in Dallas Texas flatbed no insurance](/towing-cost-in-dallas-texas-flatbed-no-insurance/).

**Q: How long does a flatbed take to arrive in Dallas?**
A: Often 30 to 60 minutes, longer during rush hour, storms, or late nights. Ask for an ETA when you call and a text with the driver's name.

**Q: Do Dallas tow companies charge by the mile?**
A: Most charge a hookup fee plus a per-mile rate after an included distance. Always confirm the total for your specific route before you agree.

**Q: Should I pay the tow driver in cash?**
A: Ask first, since some drivers prefer card or cash. Request an itemized receipt either way, and don't pay for anything not in your original quote.
