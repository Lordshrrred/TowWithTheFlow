> **Quick Answer:** State Farm's roadside assistance typically covers towing up to a set dollar amount per disablement, commonly $75 to $100 depending on your policy and state. That limit often does not cover a long tow, an after-hours call, or a flatbed. Anything above the cap comes out of your pocket. Call your agent or check your declarations page to confirm your exact limit before you need it.

## What Is the Dollar Limit on State Farm Towing Coverage?

State Farm's roadside assistance benefit is structured as a dollar cap per tow, not a mileage allowance. The standard limit on most personal auto policies sits between $75 and $100 per disablement event, though some states and policy tiers carry different amounts. That number covers the tow truck dispatch fee and the first several miles to the nearest qualified repair facility. If the bill runs higher, you pay the difference directly to the tow company.

This is different from standalone roadside programs like AAA, which typically measure coverage in miles. With State Farm, once the invoice exceeds your cap, the overage is due on the spot.

## Does $75 or $100 Actually Get Your Car Towed Anywhere Useful?

In most metro areas, no. A local tow within 5 miles commonly runs $85 to $125 depending on time of day, truck type, and market. A flatbed, which AWD vehicles, all-wheel-drive crossovers, and most luxury cars require, adds another $20 to $50 on top of base rate. After-hours calls carry a premium on top of that.

So if your limit is $75 and the flatbed bill comes to $145, you owe $70 before the driver will drop your car. Have a card ready. ## How Do I Find My Exact Dollar Limit Before I'm Stranded?

Pull up your declarations page, either on the State Farm mobile app or by logging into your account at statefarm.com. Look for the roadside assistance section, sometimes listed as "Emergency Road Service" on older policy documents. The per-disablement dollar amount is listed there.

If you cannot access your policy right now, call the State Farm roadside dispatch line listed on your insurance card. The dispatcher can confirm your coverage limit before the truck rolls, so you know exactly what you will owe. Do not assume the limit is higher than it is.

## What Happens If the Tow Bill Exceeds My State Farm Dollar Limit?

You pay the difference directly to the tow operator at time of service. The tow company does not bill State Farm and wait. The process typically works like this: State Farm pays its covered portion to the tow company, and you cover the balance before or at drop-off.

Keep the itemized receipt. Some policies allow you to submit the overage for reimbursement consideration, though roadside assistance dollar limits are generally hard caps, not subject to reimbursement above the stated amount. Confirm this with your agent after the fact.

## Should I Use State Farm Roadside or Call a Private Tow Company?

Use State Farm if your covered amount is close to the expected bill, because even partial coverage beats paying the full amount. Skip it if your vehicle needs a long-distance tow, because State Farm's standard benefit does not cover multi-hour hauls to a distant dealer or specialty shop. In that case, calling a private tow company and negotiating a flat rate may be cheaper than burning your State Farm benefit on a partial credit.

One real scenario where the limit matters: if you break down at night on an interstate and the nearest in-network shop is 40 miles away, a wheel-lift tow at $3 to $5 per mile adds up to $120 to $200 before hookup fees. Your $75 cap barely touches that. Know your number before you call. ## Common Questions

**Q: Does State Farm roadside assistance cover multiple tows per year?**
A: Yes. The coverage applies per disablement event, not per year. Each separate breakdown qualifies for its own dollar limit. However, frequent claims can affect your policy at renewal, so use it for real breakdowns, not routine tows.

**Q: Is the State Farm roadside dollar limit the same in every state?**
A: No. Dollar limits vary by state regulation and by the specific policy endorsement you purchased. Some states require higher minimums. The only way to confirm your exact limit is to read your declarations page or call your agent.

**Q: Can I upgrade my State Farm towing coverage to get a higher dollar limit?**
A: In some states, State Farm offers enhanced roadside or Premier Service endorsements with higher per-event limits. Ask your agent specifically whether an upgrade is available on your policy. Not all states or policy types offer this option.

**Q: Does State Farm roadside assistance cover a flatbed tow, or only standard wheel-lift?**
A: State Farm does not specify truck type. The coverage is dollar-based, so it applies toward whatever tow is dispatched. If a flatbed is required and costs more, that higher bill still hits your same dollar cap, leaving a larger overage for you to cover.

**Q: Will using State Farm roadside assistance raise my insurance premium?**
A: Roadside assistance claims are generally not counted as at-fault accidents and do not directly trigger a rate increase in most states. However, multiple claims in a short window can be a factor at renewal. Check your policy language or ask your agent to be sure.
