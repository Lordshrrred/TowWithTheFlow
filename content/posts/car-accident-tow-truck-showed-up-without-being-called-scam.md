> **Quick Answer:** Yes, this is almost always a scam called "chaser" or "bandit" towing. A tow truck that shows up at your accident without being dispatched by police, your insurance, or you is there to hook your car and charge whatever they want. You are not obligated to let them touch your vehicle. Stay calm, say no, and call your own tow truck or your insurer.

## Why Did a Tow Truck Show Up at My Accident Without Me Calling One?

That driver heard about your accident on a police scanner, and they drove to the scene to get your business before you could arrange your own tow. This practice is called "chasing," and the people running it are called bandit towers or chasers. Some pay spotters near busy intersections or hospital dispatch points to tip them off. It is legal in some states and illegal in others, but the business model almost always involves inflated rates and pressure tactics designed to take advantage of someone who is shaken up and not thinking about fine print.

## How Do I Know If the Tow Truck Driver Is Legitimate or Running a Scam?

A legitimate tow truck dispatched by police or a transportation authority will arrive after the officer on scene has already told you a tow is coming. A scammer shows up before any official has mentioned it. Other red flags: the driver refuses to show a company name and license number, quotes a price verbally with no written estimate, pressures you to sign anything immediately, or says your car "has to go right now" before police have cleared the scene. Legitimate operators will also tell you exactly which impound yard the car is going to. If they dodge that question, refuse and walk away.

## What Do I Say to Make Them Leave Me Alone?

Tell them directly: "I did not call you and I am not authorizing a tow." That is all you need to say. Do not sign anything they hand you. If they have already hooked your car without your permission, that is unauthorized towing, which is illegal in every state. If the driver becomes aggressive, call 911 immediately. The responding officer can intervene. Some states, including California and Texas, require tow operators at accident scenes to be dispatched by law enforcement only, not self-dispatched. Knowing that gives you standing to be firm.

## What Happens If I Already Signed Something or Let Them Tow My Car?

Act fast. Get the full company name, truck number, and the name of the yard where your car went. Contact your insurance company immediately and tell them what happened. If the final bill is wildly inflated, that is called predatory or price-gouging towing, and most states have a consumer protection office or a public utilities commission that handles complaints. Your insurance adjuster has seen this before and can often negotiate the bill down or dispute it directly. Do not pay in cash on the spot without a fully itemized written invoice, because cash payments are nearly impossible to dispute later. ## Common Questions

**Q: Is it illegal for a tow truck to show up at an accident without being called?**
A: It depends on the state. Texas, California, Florida, and several others ban self-dispatched towing at accident scenes. In states without that law, it is technically legal but still often subject to price-gouging statutes. Regardless of legality, you are never required to use them.

**Q: Can they tow my car if I say no?**
A: Not without your signature or a police order. If a driver hooks your vehicle after you have verbally refused, that is unauthorized towing. Call 911 on the spot and document everything with your phone camera before they can move the truck.

**Q: What if the tow truck driver says the police called them?**
A: Ask the officer on scene directly. Do not take the driver's word for it. If police did dispatch them, the officer will confirm it immediately. If the officer has no idea who called that truck, you have your answer.

**Q: How much can a scam tow truck actually charge?**
A: Predatory towers have charged thousands of dollars for a single local tow, then added daily storage fees that stack up fast. Legitimate post-accident tows in most cities run $100 to $250 for a local drop. Anything far above that without a written, itemized reason is a red flag.

**Q: Should I move my car before dealing with an uninvited tow truck?**
A: Only if the car is driveable and it is safe to do so. If the car can be driven off the road or into a parking lot, that removes the urgency the scammer is counting on. Never move a car that may have frame, wheel, or brake damage from the accident.
