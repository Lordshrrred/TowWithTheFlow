> **Quick Answer:** Get off the travel lanes first. If you can limp to a shoulder or exit, do it. Then call your roadside assistance provider (AAA, your insurance's roadside line, or a local tow company directly). If you have no spare or the tire shredded, you need a tow truck, not just a service call. If you're in a dangerous spot, call 911 first.

## Who Do I Actually Call When I Have a Flat Tire on the Freeway?

Call your roadside assistance provider first. If you have AAA, your insurance includes roadside (State Farm, GEICO, USAA, Liberty Mutual all offer it), or your car came with a manufacturer's plan like Ford Roadside Assistance, that's your first call. No coverage? Call a local tow company directly by searching "tow truck near me" on your phone. If you're in a truly dangerous position on the freeway, such as blocking a lane or stuck on a blind curve, call 911 before anyone else.

Most states also run Highway Patrol or DOT roving patrol programs that cruise major interstates and can help you out for free, especially during peak hours. In California it's the Freeway Service Patrol. In Texas, TxDOT runs a similar program. Knowing 511 (the national traffic info line) can help you reach state DOT resources if you're unsure who covers your stretch of road.

## Do I Need a Tow Truck or Just a Tire Change?

You need a tow truck if the tire is completely shredded, you have no spare, or your spare is flat or a donut that won't safely get you to a shop. If you have a good full-size spare and the tools to change it, a roadside assistance tech can handle the swap on the shoulder, and you won't need a tow at all.

If the rim is bent or the wheel well is damaged from a blowout at highway speed, a spare isn't the answer. Get a flatbed tow. Running on a damaged rim destroys the bead on any tire you put on it. See [Tire Blowout on Highway: What to Do Right Now](/tire-blowout-on-highway-what-to-do/) for more on assessing rim and suspension damage after a high-speed blowout.

If you genuinely have no spare and no roadside coverage, check out [Flat Tire on Freeway No Spare Tire Options](/flat-tire-on-freeway-no-spare-tire-options/) before you start calling random shops.

## How Much Will a Tow Truck Cost Me Here?

A local tow from a freeway shoulder to the nearest tire shop typically runs $75 to $125 for the first 5 miles, with a per-mile rate of $3 to $7 after that. Flatbed service costs more than a wheel-lift, and some operators charge a hookup fee on top of the mileage rate. If it's after midnight or on a holiday, expect a 20-30% premium.

If your insurance includes roadside assistance, the tow may be fully covered or cost you nothing out of pocket, though some policies cap reimbursement at a set dollar amount rather than covering unlimited distance. ## What Do I Do Right Now to Stay Safe Until Help Arrives?

Get as far off the travel lanes as possible. If you can reach a full exit, take it. A flat tire ruins the rim but that's a few hundred dollars. Getting rear-ended on a freeway shoulder is far worse. Once stopped, turn on your hazard lights immediately.

Put at least 50 feet of distance between yourself and the vehicle if you're on a narrow shoulder. Step over the guardrail if there's one and it's safe to do so. Do not stand behind the car or between the car and traffic. If you have road flares or reflective triangles, set them 200 feet back from the vehicle in the lane closest to your car.

Stay on the passenger side of the car away from traffic while you make your calls. Don't attempt to change the tire yourself on a live freeway shoulder unless you're in a wide breakdown lane with excellent sightlines. The risk is not worth it. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive slowly on a flat tire to the next exit?**
A: You can drive very slowly on a flat for a short distance if it's safer than stopping in the travel lane. Keep it under 20 mph, put on your hazards, and accept that you will likely damage the rim. Getting to a safer location is worth the rim cost.

**Q: Will 911 send a tow truck for a flat tire on the freeway?**
A: 911 will send highway patrol to check on you and can contact DOT road crews, but they don't dispatch tow trucks directly. The officer can help you call a tow or contact a contracted wrecker, but you'll still pay for that service unless your state's free patrol program covers it.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to reach me on a freeway?**
A: Response time is usually 30 to 60 minutes during normal conditions. Rush hour, bad weather, or remote highway stretches can push that to 90 minutes or more. Give the dispatcher your exact mile marker or nearest exit number so they can route accurately.

**Q: What if my spare tire is also flat?**
A: You need a tow, full stop. Don't try to air it up roadside unless you have a compressor and the spare holds pressure reliably. [Flat Tire Spare Is Also Flat: What a Tow Will Cost You](/flat-tire-spare-is-also-flat-tow-cost/) covers your options and what to expect on cost.

**Q: Does AAA cover a flat tire change on the freeway or just towing?**
A: AAA covers both. A flat tire change is a standard service call, so if you have a usable spare, they'll change it on the spot at no extra cost per incident. Towing is also covered, with the distance limits depending on your membership tier (Classic covers 5 miles, Plus covers 100 miles).
