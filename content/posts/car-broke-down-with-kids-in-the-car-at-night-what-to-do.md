> **Quick Answer:** Get as far off the road as possible, turn on your hazard lights, and keep the kids inside the car with seatbelts on. Do not let children out near traffic, even for a moment. Call 911 if you feel unsafe or cannot reach a tow service. Stay in the car with doors locked until help arrives. Your car is more visible to other drivers than you are standing outside at night.

## What Do I Do First When My Car Breaks Down at Night With Kids?

Get off the road completely. If the car is still moving when it starts failing, steer toward the right shoulder, an exit ramp, a parking lot, or a lit gas station. The farther you get from active lanes, the better. Once stopped, hazards on immediately, then put the car in park and set the parking brake.

Keep the kids buckled. That is not optional. A child wandering near a highway shoulder at night is a serious hazard, even if traffic feels light. Reassure them, keep your voice calm, and tell them exactly what is happening in simple terms. Kids read your stress level faster than you think.

If you can safely move the car but cannot get fully off the road, place road flares or reflective triangles behind the vehicle. The standard guidance is one at 10 feet, one at 100 feet, and one at 300 feet behind the car. If you do not have those, your hazard lights are the minimum. Do not crack the door to wave down help unless you are completely off the road.

## Is It Safer to Stay in the Car or Get Out?

Stay in the car, with doors locked. At night, especially on a highway, the inside of your car is safer than the shoulder. Other drivers may not see you standing outside until it is too late, and a car is a physical barrier if someone drifts off the road.

The exception is smoke or fire from the engine. If you see smoke coming from under the hood, get everyone out immediately and move well away from the vehicle, at least 100 feet, staying behind a barrier like a guardrail if one is available. For more on that specific situation, see [what to do if your car is smoking from the hood](/smoking-from-hood-stopped-on-side-of-road-what-to-do/).

If you are in a lane or partially blocking traffic and cannot move the car, get everyone out on the passenger side away from traffic, climb the embankment or move behind the guardrail, and call 911. That is the one scenario where being outside is clearly safer than staying in.

## How Do I Call for Help and What Do I Say?

Call 911 first if you feel threatened, if anyone is injured, or if the car is blocking a lane. Dispatchers can contact highway patrol and get a safety unit to your location fast.

For a standard breakdown where everyone is safe, call your roadside assistance provider next. AAA, your insurance company's roadside line, or your automaker's roadside program (Ford Roadside, GM Roadside, etc.) are your first options. Give them your exact location, including the highway number and the nearest milepost marker or exit sign. Milepost markers are the small green signs along the highway edge, and they are the fastest way to pinpoint you in the dark.

## How Do I Keep the Kids Calm and Safe While Waiting?

Tell them the truth in simple terms: the car stopped working, you called for help, and someone is coming. Kids get more anxious from sensing that you are hiding something than from knowing there is a problem.

Keep interior lights low or off so the battery does not drain further and so you can watch approaching traffic more easily. If it is cold, run the heater in short intervals rather than continuously, and crack a window slightly if you run the engine to avoid carbon monoxide buildup inside a sealed car. If it is hot, same principle: run the AC in bursts. Do not let the tank hit empty while idling.

Snacks, a phone game, or a playlist can keep a young child occupied for 30 to 45 minutes without much effort. Most tow trucks arrive within that window in a metro area, longer in rural stretches.

## Common Questions

**Q: Should I turn off my hazard lights to save the battery?**
A: No. Hazard lights are your primary safety device at night and they draw very little power. Leave them on until help arrives, even if the car will not start.

**Q: What if my kids need to use the bathroom while we wait?**
A: If you are completely off the road on a wide shoulder, you can briefly exit on the passenger side away from traffic and stay right next to the car. Never let a child walk toward the road. A parking lot, rest area, or gas station is obviously better if you can get there.

**Q: Can I let the car run while we wait for the tow truck?**
A: Yes, in short intervals, as long as the exhaust is not blocked by snow or debris and there is gas in the tank. Keep a window cracked slightly. Do not idle continuously for more than 10 to 15 minutes at a stretch.

**Q: What if another driver stops and offers help?**
A: Keep the doors locked and crack the window slightly. Ask them to call 911 or a tow service on your behalf if you cannot make calls. You do not need to exit the vehicle or accept a ride from a stranger, even a well-meaning one.

**Q: How do I find my exact location to give the tow company?**
A: Look for the nearest green milepost marker on the highway edge or the nearest exit sign. If you have GPS on your phone, open Maps and read the coordinates or address from the screen. On your phone's Maps app, a long press on your location will show GPS coordinates you can read to a dispatcher.
