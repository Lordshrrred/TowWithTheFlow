## What Towing Actually Costs in Manhattan

Manhattan is one of the most expensive places in the country to need a tow. The reasons are straightforward: congestion, limited truck access, city permits, and high operator overhead. You will pay for all of it.

Here is what to expect from a private tow company:

- **Hook-up fee:** $75 to $125
- **Per-mile rate:** $5 to $15 per mile
- **Short local tow (under 5 miles):** $150 to $250
- **Midtown or Times Square area:** add $50 to $100 for difficulty and traffic
- **Night or weekend:** expect a 20 to 30 percent surcharge
- **Bridge or tunnel crossing (to Brooklyn, Queens, NJ):** add the toll plus a crossing fee, often $25 to $50 extra

If your car broke down on the FDR Drive, the West Side Highway, or a tunnel approach, the tow company may need police authorization before they can even get to you. That adds time and sometimes cost.

**City impound is a different situation entirely.** If the NYPD or a city contractor tows your car off the street, you are dealing with the NYC Marshal or a city-contracted lot. Current fees through NYC's system: $185 base tow charge, plus $20 per day storage starting on day one. You will need your registration, ID, and payment in cash or card at the pound. Find which lot has your car at 311 or the NYC towed car lookup at nyc.gov.

## What To Do Right Now

1. **Get safe first.** If you are on a highway or bridge, turn on hazards and stay in the car with your seatbelt on unless you can safely reach a barrier. On city streets, move the car to the curb if it will roll.
2. **Check your coverage before you call a private company.** Pull up your insurance app or your roadside assistance card. Many policies cover towing and calling through them is cheaper than paying out of pocket. [GEICO roadside assistance towing](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-how-much-pays/), [Progressive](/progressive-roadside-assistance-covers-how-much-towing/), and others each have specific payout caps, so know your limit before you authorize a long tow.
3. **Call 311 or check if the city already has your car.** If you came back to a missing car, search the NYC towed vehicle lookup before calling anyone.
4. **Get a price confirmed before authorizing the tow.** Ask the dispatcher: what is the hook-up fee, what is the per-mile rate, are there any extra charges for your location. Get it in writing via text if possible.
5. **Know where your car is going.** Give the driver a specific address. Vague instructions end up costing more miles. If you are heading to a shop, call the shop first and confirm they can accept the car.
6. **Take photos of your car before it is loaded.** This protects you if there is any dispute about pre-existing damage.

## Stay Safe

- Do not stand behind or in front of your vehicle on any Manhattan bridge, highway on-ramp, or tunnel approach.
- On surface streets, use your hazards and get to the sidewalk side of the car when interacting with the driver.
- If it is dark, stay in your locked car until the tow truck arrives if you cannot reach a safe sidewalk.
- Do not leave valuables in the car if it is going to a pound. Access is not guaranteed the same day.
- Confirm the lot address before the tow truck leaves your sight. Getting that info after the fact is a headache you do not need.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I negotiate the towing price with a private company in Manhattan?**
A: You can ask, but most Manhattan tow operators use fixed rate sheets and rarely budge on the hook-up fee. Your best leverage is getting quotes from two or three companies before authorizing anyone to load your car.

**Q: How do I find out which lot has my impounded car in NYC?**
A: Use the towed vehicle search tool at nyc.gov or call 311 with your license plate number. You will need your registration and a valid ID to retrieve the car once you locate it.

**Q: Does AAA cover towing in Manhattan?**
A: Yes, AAA memberships include towing, but coverage limits vary by membership tier. Basic members typically get up to 5 miles of free towing, while higher tiers extend that to 100 miles or more per incident.
