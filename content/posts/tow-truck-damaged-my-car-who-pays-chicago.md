> **Quick Answer:** The towing company is liable for damage they cause to your vehicle. In Chicago, tow truck operators must carry insurance and are responsible for damages during transport. Document everything immediately and file claims with both the towing company and your insurance.

## What To Do

1. **Stop the tow immediately** if you see damage happening. Don't let them continue. If the driver already hooked up your car and is pulling away, flag them down before they reach the street. Damage caught mid-tow is far easier to prove than damage discovered at the impound lot.

2. **Take photos** of all damage from multiple angles. Include the tow truck's license plate, company name, and driver information. Photograph the straps or chains still attached if possible. If there are fresh scrapes, gouges, or bent metal, get close-up and wide-angle shots before anything moves.

3. **Get the driver's information**: name, license number, insurance company, and policy number. Chicago requires tow operators to carry minimum $100,000 liability coverage. Ask for the company's USDOT number as well, which appears on the truck's door panel and ties directly to their licensing records.

4. **Document everything in writing**. Note the time, location, weather conditions, and exactly what happened. Write it down on your phone while you're standing there, not an hour later. Details like "rear bumper was intact when hooked, cracked when released" are exactly what an adjuster needs.

5. **Call Chicago Police** if the damage is significant or the tow company refuses to cooperate. File a report. A police report creates an independent, timestamped record that insurance companies take seriously. It also puts the driver on notice that you are not dropping it.

6. **Contact the towing company's insurance** within 24 hours. Most Chicago tow companies use commercial carriers like Progressive or State Farm. Ask for a claim number in writing and the adjuster's direct contact. Do not communicate verbally only.

7. **Notify your insurance company** even if you plan to file with the towing company first. They can provide backup coverage and sometimes act as a negotiator on your behalf. Some policies cover towing-related damage under comprehensive.

8. **Get repair estimates** from at least two licensed mechanics. Use shops familiar with insurance claims. A shop that deals with adjusters daily knows how to write an estimate that accounts for parts, labor, and alignment checks that a quick eyeball quote will miss.

9. **Keep all receipts** for rental cars, additional towing, or storage fees caused by the damage. If the original tow company left your car undriveable, every cost from that point forward is potentially recoverable.

## What It Might Cost

Chicago towing companies typically pay for:
- All repair costs for damage they caused
- Rental car while your vehicle is being repaired
- Diminished value if your car's worth dropped due to damage
- Additional towing to a repair shop

Diminished value claims are worth pursuing on vehicles less than 7 years old or with low mileage. A car that has a repaired collision on its Carfax loses resale value even after a perfect repair, and Illinois allows you to claim that difference. Get a written appraisal from a certified appraiser, not just the body shop's opinion.

Your deductible may apply if you file through your own insurance first. Filing directly against the tow company's liability policy avoids your deductible entirely, which is the better move if the liability is clear.

## Common Questions

**Q: What if the tow company says the damage was already there before they touched my car?**
A: This is the most common defense tow companies use. Your timestamped photos taken before and during the tow are your best counter. If you have recent repair records or a pre-tow inspection showing the vehicle was undamaged, bring those to the adjuster. Witness statements from anyone who saw the condition of the car before the tow also carry real weight.

**Q: Can I refuse to pay the towing bill if they damaged my car?**
A: In Illinois, tow companies can hold your vehicle until fees are paid, even if there is a damage dispute. Pay under protest, put that in writing on any receipt you sign, and then pursue the damage claim separately. Withholding payment typically costs you access to your car and does not speed up your claim.

**Q: How long do I have to file a claim against a Chicago tow company?**
A: Illinois has a 2-year statute of limitations for property damage claims. That said, file as fast as possible. Evidence disappears, drivers move on, and insurance companies are harder to deal with on cold claims. Start the process within 48 hours if you can.

## Stay Safe

- Never sign anything admitting fault or limiting the towing company's liability
- Don't accept cash settlements on the spot without getting estimates first
- Keep damaged parts if the tow company requests them for inspection
- File complaints with the Illinois Commerce Commission if the company won't cooperate
- Consider hiring an attorney for damages over $5,000 or if the company denies obvious liability
