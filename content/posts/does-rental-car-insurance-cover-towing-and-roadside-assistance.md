> **Quick Answer:** Usually, no. The insurance you buy at the counter (a collision damage waiver, or CDW/LDW) covers damage to the rental, not a breakdown tow. Towing and roadside help come from the rental company's own assistance program, which is often included or sold as an add-on. Call the number on your rental agreement or key tag before you call any tow truck.

## Does Rental Car Insurance Cover Towing and Roadside Assistance?

Rental car insurance generally does not pay for towing from a mechanical breakdown. A CDW or LDW waives what you owe if the car is damaged or stolen. A dead alternator, a flat, or a stalled engine is a maintenance problem, and that falls on the rental company.

Where towing does get covered depends on why the car is stuck:

- **Mechanical failure or flat tire:** Handled by the rental company's roadside program, usually at no cost to you.
- **Accident:** The tow can fall under the CDW, your personal auto policy, or the credit card benefit, depending on what you bought and what you carry.
- **Your mistake (empty tank, lost keys, wrong fuel):** Often billed to you, even with a roadside add-on.

## What Do I Do First If My Rental Car Breaks Down?

Call the roadside number on your rental agreement or the key fob tag before you do anything else. Calling your own tow company first can leave you paying out of pocket for something the rental company would have covered.

1. Get to a safe spot. Pull onto the shoulder, turn on your hazards, and if you're on a highway, get everyone out and away from traffic.
2. Call the rental company's roadside line. Ask for a case or reference number and write it down.
3. Ask whether they'll send a tow or a replacement vehicle, and how long the wait will be.
4. Photograph the dashboard, the mileage, and any warning lights.

One tip worth knowing: Hertz, Enterprise, Avis, and most other major renters run their own dispatch. If you hire a tow yourself without authorization, expect a fight getting reimbursed. Get approval first, in writing if possible.

## Does My Credit Card or Personal Auto Insurance Cover a Rental Car Tow?

Sometimes, but not the way most people assume. Many credit cards include rental car collision coverage, and a few offer separate roadside dispatch services. These are usually secondary, meaning they apply after other coverage, and the details vary by card. Read your card's benefits guide or call the number on the back.

Your personal auto policy may also extend to a rental. If you added roadside coverage to it, that can apply, but check whether it covers rentals specifically. ## What Does a Rental Tow Cost If Nobody Covers It?

If roadside isn't covered, expect to pay market rates, which typically start around $75 to $150 for a short local tow and climb with distance, after-hours service, and flatbed requirements. Rental cars, especially newer ones, are often all-wheel drive or have electronic parking brakes, which usually means a flatbed.

Rates vary a lot by city and time of day. Our guide on [how much emergency roadside assistance costs without insurance](/how-much-does-emergency-roadside-assistance-cost-without-insurance/) lays out what to expect, and the existing post on [does rental car insurance cover towing cost](/does-rental-car-insurance-cover-towing-cost/) goes deeper on the billing side.

If you pay out of pocket, keep the itemized receipt, the tow company's name, and your rental case number. Submit all of it to the rental company for reimbursement.

## What If I Have AAA or Roadside Coverage Through My Own Insurer?

AAA membership follows you, not the car, so it generally works in a rental. The tow still counts against your plan's mileage limits, so check those before dispatching. See [how far AAA will tow your car for free](/how-far-will-aaa-tow-my-car-for-free/) for plan-by-plan distances.

Call the rental company first anyway. If their program covers it free, you save your AAA service call for something else. If they take too long or can't help, AAA is your backup.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does the CDW or LDW I bought at the rental counter cover towing?**
A: No. A collision or loss damage waiver covers damage to or theft of the vehicle. A breakdown tow is a separate service the rental company provides.

**Q: Who pays if the rental car breaks down on its own?**
A: The rental company should, since mechanical failure isn't your fault. Call their roadside line, get a case number, and don't pay a tow yourself without approval.

**Q: Will the rental company send a replacement car?**
A: Often yes, if the breakdown is mechanical, though it depends on location and fleet availability. Ask when you call and get the timing confirmed.

**Q: Am I charged for towing if I ran out of gas or lost the keys?**
A: Usually yes. Roadside programs often cover mechanical failures but bill customer-caused problems like an empty tank, wrong fuel, or lost keys.

**Q: Can I use my own tow company instead of the rental company's?**
A: You can, but you risk paying for it yourself. Get approval from the rental company first so you can request reimbursement with a receipt and case number.
