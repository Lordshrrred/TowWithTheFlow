> **Quick Answer:** Expect to pay roughly $100 to $200 for a short Seattle tow, usually a hookup fee plus a per-mile charge. Nights, weekends, flatbeds, and freeway pickups on I-5 or SR 520 push the price higher. Get the total quoted before the driver hooks up, ask what is included, and check whether your insurance or credit card covers roadside help first.

## How Much Does a Tow Cost in Seattle After a Breakdown?

A typical Seattle tow runs $100 to $200 for the first few miles, and longer hauls can reach $250 or more. Prices vary by company and are not regulated to one number, so treat these as ballpark figures, not guarantees.

Most Seattle companies charge two parts: a hookup fee (the base charge for loading your car) and a per-mile rate after an included distance. Ask for the all-in number, not just the hookup fee. A $65 hookup with a high mileage rate can cost more than a $95 flat quote.

## What Makes a Seattle Tow More Expensive?

The biggest price drivers are timing, truck type, and location. Here is what moves the bill:

- **After-hours service.** Nights, weekends, and holidays often add a surcharge.
- **Flatbed vs. wheel-lift.** All-wheel-drive Subarus, EVs, and low-clearance cars should go on a flatbed, which costs more. See [flatbed vs wheel lift towing cost in Seattle](/towing-cost-seattle-washington-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/) for the difference.
- **Freeway pickup.** A breakdown on the I-5 express lanes or the SR 520 bridge requires a more careful recovery, and some companies charge extra.
- **Traffic.** Stop-and-go on I-5 through downtown or I-90 toward Bellevue means the driver bills for slow miles and a late arrival.
- **Winching.** If your car is stuck off the shoulder or in a ditch, expect an added recovery fee.

Mileage matters too. A tow from Capitol Hill to a shop in Ballard is far cheaper than from Sea-Tac to a shop in Everett. If you can choose the shop, pick one close to where you broke down.

## What Should I Do Right After My Car Breaks Down in Seattle?

Get to a safe spot first, then call for a tow before you start comparing prices. Seattle freeways have tight shoulders, and rain cuts visibility.

1. Turn on your hazards and coast to the right shoulder or the nearest exit.
2. If you are on I-5, SR 520, or I-90, stay in the car with your seatbelt on if traffic is close. Exit on the passenger side only if it is safe.
3. Call 911 if you are in a live lane or feel unsafe. Otherwise call your roadside provider or a local tow company.
4. Ask for the total price, the truck type, and the arrival time before you agree.
5. Photograph your car and the odometer before hookup.

Washington State's 511 service and the WSDOT traffic app show closures and incidents. Check them if you are stuck near the Alaskan Way Viaduct replacement area or the SR 99 tunnel approaches. # How Can I Avoid Overpaying for a Tow in Seattle?

Call before you accept anyone, and never take a tow from an unsolicited driver who shows up at your car. Predatory tow operators do circle breakdowns on busy freeways.

Do these things:

- Ask for a written or texted quote with the hookup fee, per-mile rate, and any surcharges.
- Check coverage first. Many auto insurance policies, AAA memberships, and some credit cards include towing. If you have a roadside plan, use it before paying out of pocket. GEICO members can read about [how many times per year roadside assistance can be used](/geico-roadside-assistance-how-many-times-per-year/).
- Ask where the car is going. Confirm the shop's address and hours so you are not billed for storage.
- Pay with a card so you can dispute a charge that does not match the quote.

Without coverage, the numbers are still manageable if you ask the right questions. See [towing cost in Seattle with no insurance](/towing-cost-in-seattle-washington-no-insurance/) for what to expect.

## Does Tow Cost Change on Bridges and Mountain Passes?

Yes. A breakdown on a floating bridge or on a pass can add cost and wait time. If you are heading east on I-90 toward Snoqualmie Pass, a breakdown there is a long-distance tow with winter traction rules in play, and the bill reflects the mileage. ## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a basic tow in Seattle?**
A: A short local tow usually lands between $100 and $200. Hookup fees alone often start around $65 to $100, with per-mile charges added after the included distance.

**Q: Is a flatbed required for my car?**
A: Often yes. All-wheel-drive cars, EVs, low-clearance sports cars, and anything with transmission damage should go on a flatbed to avoid further damage. Tell the dispatcher your make and model when you call.

**Q: Will a tow company charge more at night in Seattle?**
A: Many do. After-hours and weekend surcharges are common, so ask whether the quote you got includes them.

**Q: Can I pay a tow driver with a card?**
A: Most accept cards, but confirm when you call. Paying by card gives you a dispute option if the final charge does not match the quote.

**Q: Does my insurance cover towing after a breakdown?**
A: Only if you added roadside or towing coverage to your policy. Check your app or declarations page, and look at AAA or credit card benefits too, since you may already have a free tow.
