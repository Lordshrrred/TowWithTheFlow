> **Quick Answer:** After an accident in Chicago, expect to pay $125 to $250 for a local tow of 5 to 10 miles. If your car is on the Dan Ryan (I-90/94) or the Eisenhower (I-290) and IDOT dispatches their own contracted tow, the rate is set by the city's towing schedule. If you call privately, prices vary and can spike. Your insurance's roadside coverage or AAA can cap your out-of-pocket cost significantly.

## What Does a Tow Cost After an Accident in Chicago?

A private tow after an accident in Chicago typically runs $125 to $250 for the first 5 to 10 miles, plus a hookup fee of $75 to $100. After that, you're looking at $4 to $7 per additional mile. Flatbed service, which most damaged vehicles require post-crash, adds to the base rate. A totaled car being moved from the I-90/94 interchange at the Circle (where the Dan Ryan meets the Eisenhower near I-290) to a body shop in Bridgeport or Pilsen will often land between $175 and $225 all in.

If the Chicago Police Department calls in the tow, it goes through the city's rotation of licensed towers. Those operators are bound to the Chicago Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection (BACP) rate schedule, which caps the base hookup and per-mile charges. That protection disappears if you call a truck yourself before police arrive, so let dispatch make the call when there are injuries or major damage.

## Does Insurance Pay for the Tow After a Chicago Accident?

Yes, if you carry roadside assistance or comprehensive/collision coverage with a towing endorsement. Most policies cover $50 to $150 of the tow, and some reimburse the full amount if you use an approved provider. Call your insurer from the scene before you authorize any tow. If you're using a policy-dispatched tow, make sure you get the driver's name, company name, and a written estimate before the car goes on the hook.

If the other driver caused the crash, their liability insurance should cover towing as part of your property damage claim, but that reimbursement comes later, not at the scene. You may need to pay out of pocket and submit receipts. Keep every document the tow company gives you.

For a direct comparison of what AAA covers versus paying out of pocket in Chicago, see [AAA vs Non-AAA Tow Truck Costs in Chicago](/tow-truck-cost-aaa-vs-non-aaa-chicago/).

## What Are the Biggest Cost Traps After a Chicago Highway Accident?

The worst one is predatory towing near high-accident corridors. The I-94 stretch through the South Side, the I-290 ramp from the Tri-State, and the I-55 near Damen Avenue are all spots where independent tow trucks sometimes arrive before police and push drivers to sign authorizations fast. Once you sign, you've agreed to their rate, not the city's schedule.

Second trap: storage fees. If your car gets towed to a lot and you don't retrieve it within 24 to 48 hours, Chicago storage fees run $35 to $75 per day. That accumulates fast if the car is totaled and you're waiting on an insurance adjuster.

Third trap: distance padding. Some operators route your car to a facility farther away than necessary to inflate the mileage charge. Ask explicitly where the car is going before it leaves.

If a tow truck damages your vehicle during the move, [the rules on who pays in Chicago are specific and worth knowing](/tow-truck-damaged-my-car-who-pays-chicago/) before you sign anything.

## How Do I Keep the Total Bill Under Control?

Get a written itemized quote before the truck moves your car. The quote must list the hookup fee, mileage rate, destination, and any after-hours surcharges. Chicago tow operators must provide this under BACP rules.

Use your insurer's dispatch line or AAA rather than accepting a street-level solicitation. If you're paying out of pocket, ask whether the operator is on the city's rotation for that zone. Rotation towers have published maximum rates. Off-rotation operators do not.

## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average tow truck cost after a car accident in Chicago?**
A: For a 5 to 10 mile tow on a flatbed, budget $150 to $250. City-rate operators on the rotation schedule are cheaper and more predictable than private trucks flagged down at the scene.

**Q: Can I refuse a tow truck that shows up uninvited at my accident scene?**
A: Yes. You are not obligated to use any tow operator who arrives unsolicited. Wait for the police-dispatched rotation truck or call your own insurance-approved provider. Do not let anyone hook your car before you have a written rate.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover the full tow cost after an accident in Illinois?**
A: It depends on your policy. Roadside assistance riders typically cover $50 to $150. Collision coverage handles vehicle damage but towing reimbursement varies. Call your insurer from the scene and confirm coverage before authorizing the tow.

**Q: What happens if I can't afford to pay the tow company on the spot?**
A: Most tow operators accept credit cards. If you genuinely cannot pay, the car may be held at their lot with daily storage fees accruing. Contact your insurer immediately. If fault belongs to the other driver, their liability coverage should reimburse towing as part of your property damage claim.

**Q: Does AAA cover accident towing in Chicago or only mechanical breakdowns?**
A: AAA covers towing after accidents in most cases, not just mechanical failures. Coverage limits depend on your membership tier. Basic members get up to 5 miles free, Premier members up to 200 miles. Confirm your tier before the tow rolls so you know your exact benefit.
