> **Quick Answer:** Maybe. Many Visa Signature and Mastercard World cards include roadside dispatch, but it's often pay-per-use (you pay the tow company, not the card). Some premium cards include reimbursement. Others cover nothing. Call the number on the back of your card, ask for "roadside assistance benefit," and confirm the dollar cap, the mileage limit, and whether you pay first.

## Does My Credit Card Actually Cover Towing?

Some do, most don't include free towing, and you won't know which kind you have without checking. Coverage falls into three tiers:

- **Dispatch only:** The card connects you to a tow company, but you pay the bill. Common on basic Visa Signature and Mastercard World cards. Useful because it gets a truck moving, not because it saves money.
- **Reimbursement:** You pay the tow company, then file a claim for a capped amount, often $50 to $100 per event.
- **Included service:** A handful of premium travel and auto-focused cards cover a tow of a set distance at no charge, usually with a limit on events per year.

A card with no auto benefit at all is common too, especially store cards and no-frills cash back cards.

## How Do I Check What My Card Covers Right Now?

Call the number on the back of the card and ask the representative for the roadside assistance benefit. Do it before you call a tow truck, since coverage often requires dispatch through the card's own network.

Ask these exact questions:

1. Is this dispatch only, reimbursement, or included service?
2. What is the maximum dollar amount and mileage per tow?
3. How many events per year?
4. Do I have to call your number first, or can I use my own tow company and submit a receipt?
5. Does it cover flatbed tows? (All-wheel-drive cars, low-clearance cars, and most EVs need flatbeds.)

If you can't reach anyone, the benefits guide is usually in the card issuer's app or website under "Guide to Benefits." Search the PDF for "roadside" or "emergency dispatch."

**One tip that saves money:** if the benefit is reimbursement-based, get a itemized receipt showing the tow company name, mileage, and total. Claims get denied for missing paperwork more than for any other reason.

## What Does Credit Card Roadside Coverage Usually Not Include?

Credit card coverage almost never matches a real roadside plan. The common gaps:

- **Short mileage caps.** Many benefits cover only five to ten miles. A highway tow to a shop across town can blow past that, and you pay the difference.
- **Dollar caps.** A $50 to $100 reimbursement against a $150 tow leaves you covering the rest.
- **Vehicle limits.** Some exclude commercial vehicles, rentals, or cars over a certain age.
- **Service limits.** Jump starts, fuel delivery, and lockouts may be listed, but sometimes at "cost of service only," meaning they send help and you pay.
- **Winching and recovery.** If you're stuck in a ditch or snow, many cards exclude it.

## Is My Card Better Than Insurance or AAA for Towing?

Usually not. Insurance add-ons and auto clubs tend to cover more miles and don't require you to pay first. Card benefits work best as a backup when you have no other coverage.

Check your auto policy before the card. Many insurers cover towing as a low-cost add-on. ## What Do I Do If My Card Doesn't Cover Towing and I'm Stranded?

Call your insurer first, then an auto club if you have one, then a local tow company directly. Ask any tow company for the total price before they hook up: hookup fee, per-mile rate, and any after-hours or flatbed surcharge.

If money is the problem, [what to do when you have no money for a tow truck](/car-broke-down-no-money-for-tow-truck-what-are-my-options/) lays out the realistic options. Never drive a car that's overheating, losing brake pressure, or grinding to avoid a tow bill. A repair after a failure costs more than the tow.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does Visa or Mastercard roadside assistance cover towing for free?**
A: Usually not. Most Visa and Mastercard roadside benefits are dispatch only, meaning they send a tow truck but you pay the bill. A few premium cards reimburse part of the cost or include a limited free tow.

**Q: Do I have to call the credit card company before calling a tow truck?**
A: Often yes. Many benefits only apply if the card's dispatch service arranges the tow. Calling your own tow company first may void reimbursement, so check the terms before you book.

**Q: How much will a credit card reimburse for towing?**
A: It varies by card, but caps are commonly in the range of $50 to $100 per event. Premium cards may offer more. Your card's Guide to Benefits lists the exact limit.

**Q: Does credit card roadside assistance cover a rental car or someone else's car?**
A: Often not. Many benefits apply only to vehicles you own or are listed on, and some exclude rentals. Confirm with the issuer, because coverage on borrowed or rented vehicles is frequently excluded.

**Q: Can I use my credit card benefit for a flat tire or dead battery?**
A: Sometimes. Many cards list jump starts, tire changes, and lockouts, but typically at cost of service only. The card sends a technician and you pay for the visit.
