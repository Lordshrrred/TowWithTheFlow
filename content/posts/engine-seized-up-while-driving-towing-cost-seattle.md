> **Quick Answer:** Engine seizure while driving requires immediate towing. In Seattle, expect $100-200 for local tows, $3-5 per mile for longer distances. Pull over safely, turn off engine immediately, and call for tow service. Do not attempt to restart the engine.

## What To Do

1. **Pull over immediately** when you hear grinding, knocking, or the engine suddenly stops
2. **Turn off the engine** and do not attempt to restart it
3. **Turn on hazard lights** and move to the shoulder if possible
4. **Call a tow truck** - seized engines cannot be driven
5. **Contact your insurance** to check if towing is covered
6. **Have the car towed to a mechanic** for diagnosis, not a quick-lube place

A seized engine means the internal components have stopped moving, usually because of complete oil starvation, catastrophic overheating, or a spun bearing that locked the crankshaft. When that happens, the drivetrain freezes. If your car was moving and the engine locked, you may have also lost power steering and power brakes in the same instant. Steer firmly and pump the brakes with steady, hard pressure to slow down. Do not coast in neutral hoping the engine will free up. It won't.

Watch for these warning signs before a full seizure: a sudden loud knock that gets worse with RPMs, the oil pressure warning light flickering or dropping to zero, a burning smell from the hood, or the temperature gauge pegging into the red. If you catch even one of those, pull over immediately. Every second you keep driving grinds metal against metal.

On Seattle's interstates like I-5 or I-90, aim for an offramp rather than stopping on the shoulder if you have any momentum left. The shoulder on those corridors is narrow and fast-moving traffic is close. If you have no choice but to stop on the highway, get as far right as possible, get everyone out of the vehicle through the passenger side, and move behind the guardrail.

## What It Might Cost

**Seattle Towing Rates:**
- Local tow (under 10 miles): $100-200
- Per mile rate: $3-5 for longer distances
- After-hours surcharge: $25-50 additional
- Flatbed service: $20-40 extra (recommended for seized engines)

Always request a flatbed for a seized engine. A wheel-lift tow truck drags the drive wheels along the pavement, which can put rotational force back into a locked drivetrain and cause additional damage to the transmission or axles. A flatbed lifts all four wheels clear. That extra $20-40 is cheap insurance compared to a $1,500 transmission repair on top of the engine problem.

If you are towing from a remote area outside Seattle, say Snoqualmie Pass or the North Cascades foothills, the per-mile rate adds up fast. A 60-mile tow at $4 per mile is $240 before the base hookup fee. Get the total price in writing before you agree.

**Engine Repair Estimates:**
- Diagnosis: $150-300
- Engine rebuild: $3,000-6,000
- Engine replacement: $4,000-8,000
- Often totals the vehicle if it's older

A mechanic needs to pull the oil pan and inspect the bearings before giving you a firm number. A hydraulic lock from a coolant leak into a cylinder is a different problem than a fully spun bearing, and the repair costs are not the same. Get a teardown estimate first, usually $300-500, then decide whether repair or replacement makes sense based on the vehicle's actual market value.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can a seized engine be fixed, or do I need a whole new one?**
A: It depends on how far the damage went. Caught early, a seized engine from oil starvation sometimes only needs new bearings and a crank grind, which is cheaper than a full replacement. A fully locked engine with scored cylinder walls and a bent rod usually means replacement is the smarter financial call.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover the tow if my engine seizes?**
A: Standard liability insurance does not. If you have roadside assistance added to your policy, or a separate plan through AAA or your vehicle manufacturer, that typically covers the tow but not the engine repair itself. Call your insurer before you call the tow truck to know what you are actually covered for.

**Q: How long does it take for a tow truck to reach me in the Seattle area?**
A: In the city and close suburbs, expect 30-60 minutes on average. During peak hours on I-5 or after a major weather event, wait times can stretch to 90 minutes or more. Give your exact milepost or cross street when you call, because a vague location adds time.

## Stay Safe

- Do not try to restart a seized engine - you'll cause more damage
- Use hazard lights and stay visible to traffic
- Exit on the side away from traffic if stopped on a highway
- Keep doors locked if in an unsafe area
- Have your registration and insurance ready for the tow driver
