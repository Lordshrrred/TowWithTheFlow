> **Quick Answer:** Without insurance, you pay per service or buy a membership. A jump start or lockout typically runs $50 to $100, a tire change $75 to $150, fuel delivery $75 to $125 including fuel, and a tow $75 to $200 for a short local haul. A membership like AAA runs roughly $60 to $200 a year depending on tier and region. Prices climb at night, on weekends, and on highways.

## How Much Will I Pay Right Now If I Have No Insurance and No Membership?

Expect $50 to $250 depending on what the car needs, and the tow is almost always the biggest charge. Prices vary by region, so treat these as ballparks and get a quote before anyone hooks up.

- **Jump start:** $50 to $100
- **Lockout:** $60 to $120
- **Flat tire change (using your spare):** $75 to $150
- **Fuel delivery:** $75 to $125, plus the cost of the fuel
- **Winching out of a ditch or mud:** $100 to $250 or more, billed by time and difficulty
- **Local tow:** $75 to $200 for the first 5 to 10 miles, then $3 to $7 per additional mile

Heavy-duty or flatbed work costs more. If your car is all-wheel drive or has a damaged drivetrain, you likely need a flatbed. Our [flatbed checklist](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/) covers how to tell before the driver arrives.

## Is a Roadside Membership Cheaper Than Paying Per Call?

If you break down even once a year, a membership usually wins. A basic AAA plan runs roughly $60 to $90 a year in many regions, with higher tiers at $100 to $200 that stretch towing to 100-plus miles. One $150 tow pays for a full year of basic coverage.

Memberships have waiting periods and limits, so read them. Most cover the member, not the car, so a friend driving your vehicle may not be covered. We break down that exact situation in [does AAA cover towing when someone else is driving your car](/does-aaa-cover-towing-for-someone-else-driving-my-car/).

You can also buy cheaper standalone plans or add roadside to an auto policy for a few dollars a month, as covered in [does car insurance cover roadside assistance if I add it](/does-car-insurance-cover-roadside-assistance-if-i-add-it/). That helps for next time, not for the tow truck you need today.

## What Makes the Bill Jump Higher?

Time, location, and distance drive the price more than the problem itself. Nights, weekends, and holidays add $25 to $100. A highway pickup costs more than a parking lot because the driver takes on more risk and often needs extra equipment. Long distance adds a per-mile fee that stacks fast.

Cities also swing the numbers. A tow in San Francisco or Boston usually costs more than the same job in Indianapolis or Memphis. If you want local figures, check the [weekend emergency towing guide](/how-much-does-emergency-towing-cost-on-weekends/) for how timing changes the rate.

Your bill also changes if you were cited or the car was impounded. Impound and storage fees are a separate, much larger problem.

## What Can I Do If I Can't Afford the Tow?

Call around for quotes, because rates differ by tens of dollars between companies, and tell dispatch your budget upfront. Ask if they offer a payment plan or accept a split between cards. If the car is unsafe to leave and you're short on cash, our guide for [a car that broke down with no money for a tow truck](/car-broke-down-no-money-for-tow-truck-what-are-my-options/) lists realistic options.

If you're on a highway, safety comes before price. Get everyone out of the car and away from traffic if it's not safe inside, and call 911 if you're in a live lane. A lower quote isn't worth standing next to fast traffic.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a tow cost without insurance for a short distance?**
A: Plan on $75 to $200 for a local tow of about 5 to 10 miles, including the hookup fee. Each extra mile typically adds $3 to $7, so a 30-mile tow can reach $200 to $350.

**Q: Is it cheaper to pay per call or buy a roadside membership?**
A: Per call is cheaper if you rarely break down. If you expect even one tow or two small calls a year, a $60 to $200 membership usually costs less than paying out of pocket.

**Q: Can I get roadside assistance without car insurance?**
A: Yes. Roadside services are sold separately from insurance. You can buy a membership like AAA, use a pay-per-use app, or call a local tow company directly.

**Q: Does a jump start cost extra if I have no membership?**
A: Usually $50 to $100 from a roadside service. A portable jump starter pack, often $60 to $120 one time, can save you from paying that fee again.

**Q: Do roadside prices go up at night or on weekends?**
A: Yes. After-hours, weekend, and holiday calls commonly add $25 to $100 on top of the normal rate, so ask for the full total before they dispatch.
