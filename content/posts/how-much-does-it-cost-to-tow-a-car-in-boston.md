> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in Boston usually costs $100 to $200 for the hook-up and the first few miles, then roughly $4 to $8 for each additional mile. Flatbeds, nights, weekends, snow, and tight city streets push the price higher. Ask for a total quote before the driver touches your car. AAA and many insurance policies cover the tow for free.

## How Much Does a Tow Cost in Boston Right Now?

Expect $100 to $200 for a standard local tow inside Boston, with a per-mile charge of about $4 to $8 after the included distance. These are typical ranges, not fixed rates. Private towers set their own prices, and the final bill depends on the details below.

What moves the number:

- **Hook-up fee:** The flat charge to load your car, often $75 to $125 on its own.
- **Mileage:** Counted from the truck's start point or from your car to the drop-off, depending on the company. Ask which.
- **Truck type:** All-wheel-drive cars, EVs, and low-clearance vehicles usually need a flatbed, which costs more.
- **Time of day:** Nights, weekends, and holidays often add a surcharge.
- **Conditions:** Snow, ice, or a car stuck in a garage or tight spot adds labor charges.

## What Should I Ask the Tow Company Before I Say Yes?

Ask for the total price, out the door, before the truck is dispatched. A reputable company will quote you over the phone. If the dispatcher dodges, call someone else.

Ask these five questions:

1. What is the total including hook-up, mileage, and any surcharges?
2. Is that a flatbed or a wheel-lift?
3. Where is the truck coming from, and what is the ETA?
4. Do you accept cards, or is it cash only?
5. Will I get an itemized receipt?

Write down the company name and the quote. If the final bill is higher than what you were told, you have grounds to dispute it. Our guide on [how to dispute a towing charge](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-if-overcharged/) walks through exactly how.

## Does Location Change the Price? What About I-93, the Pike, or Downtown?

Yes. Highway tows and downtown tows cost differently, and the stretch of road matters. A breakdown on I-93 near the Zakim Bridge or on the Mass Pike (I-90) near the Allston/Brighton tolls often means a faster response because state-contracted tow operators patrol those corridors. Under tunnels like the Ted Williams or Sumner, a dedicated recovery crew handles removal, and you generally have less choice about who tows you.

Side streets in the North End, Back Bay, or Beacon Hill are harder. Narrow one-way streets, resident parking, and double-parked cars slow a truck down, and some companies charge extra for tight-access pickups. A tow from Dorchester to a shop in Quincy is a short hop. A tow from Boston to Worcester is a long haul priced almost entirely by the mile.

## Will My Insurance or AAA Cover the Tow?

Often yes, and you should check before paying out of pocket. AAA membership typically covers a set tow distance at no charge, with limits that depend on your plan level. Many auto insurance policies include roadside assistance as a cheap add-on, and some credit cards carry it too.

Check in this order:

1. Your AAA card or app.
2. Your insurance app or policy declarations page.
3. Your credit card benefits.
4. Your car's manufacturer roadside program, which is free on many newer vehicles.

If you are unsure what your policy includes, read [whether roadside assistance covers a flat tire or just towing](/does-roadside-assistance-cover-flat-tire-or-just-towing/). Paying $150 out of pocket when a free tow was available is the most common and most avoidable mistake.

## Does Winter Make Towing in Boston More Expensive?

Yes. Snowstorms and cold snaps spike demand, and prices and wait times both climb. After a nor'easter, expect surcharges, longer waits, and some companies refusing calls until roads clear. Cars buried by plows or stuck in snowbanks can add winching or digging fees.

Dead batteries peak in January and February. A jump-start from a roadside service often costs far less than a tow, so try that first if the car simply will not crank. ## Common Questions

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow company directly or use an app?**
A: Calling a local company directly usually gets you a firm quote and often a lower price. Apps add convenience but may include service fees. Get the total in writing either way.

**Q: Do I have to pay cash for a tow in Boston?**
A: No, most companies take cards, but some small operators are cash only. Ask when you call so you are not stuck at the drop-off without a way to pay.

**Q: Can the police pick the tow company for me?**
A: Yes, after an accident or on certain highways, police or the state may call a rotation tower. You can often request a different company if you have your own, so say so before the truck loads your car.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive in Boston?**
A: Usually 30 to 60 minutes in normal conditions. Rush hour, storms, and major events near TD Garden or Fenway can stretch that well past an hour.

**Q: Should I tow my car to a mechanic or my home?**
A: Tow to the shop if you plan to repair it soon, since it saves a second tow. Home works if you need time to decide, but you will pay mileage twice if you move it again.
