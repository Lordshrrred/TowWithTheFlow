> **Quick Answer:** Whether your insurer covers towing after an accident depends on your coverage type. Comprehensive and collision policies often include towing, but your deductible usually applies to the overall claim, not just the tow. A separate roadside assistance add-on typically covers towing with no deductible at all. Check your policy type before you call a tow truck.

## What To Do

1. **Call 911 or police first if there are injuries or the car is blocking traffic.** Get a police report number. Some insurers require it before they'll pay towing from an accident scene. See what that process looks like at [towing cost accident scene police report required](/towing-cost-accident-scene-police-report-required/).

2. **Pull up your insurance app or card and find your coverage type.** Look for these specific terms: "roadside assistance," "towing and labor," "comprehensive," or "collision." These are the four places towing coverage hides.

3. **Understand how your deductible actually works here.** If you file a collision claim after an accident, your deductible applies to the entire claim. The tow truck bill gets rolled into that. If the total damage is less than your deductible, the insurance company pays nothing, including the tow.

4. **Check if you have a separate roadside assistance rider.** This is a low-cost add-on sold by most carriers. If you have it, the tow is usually covered with no deductible, regardless of whether it was an accident or a breakdown. This is the cleanest path.

5. **Call your insurer before authorizing the tow truck.** Tell the dispatcher you were in an accident and ask two questions: Is towing covered under my current claim? Will I need to pay out of pocket and get reimbursed? Getting a claim number before the truck hooks up protects you.

6. **Take photos of the scene and your car before it gets towed.** This supports your claim and prevents disputes if the car is damaged further during transport.

7. **Do not let the at-fault driver's insurance arrange your tow unless you confirm in writing what they'll cover.** Third-party claims take longer to settle and the towing company will still invoice you if the other insurer delays.

8. **If you're paying out of pocket right now, get the towing invoice.** Submit it to your insurer for reimbursement. Most carriers accept post-incident towing claims within 30 to 60 days. ## What It Might Cost

If insurance doesn't cover the tow, here's what you're looking at:

- **Standard accident tow (under 10 miles):** $75 to $150
- **Flatbed from highway or freeway:** $100 to $250
- **Long-distance tow to a specific repair shop:** $200 to $600+
- **After-hours or weekend premium:** Add $50 to $100 on top of the base rate

## Stay Safe

- If your car is in a travel lane after the accident, get yourself and passengers behind a guardrail or well off the shoulder. Wait there, not next to the car.
- Do not stand between your car and oncoming traffic while on the phone with your insurer.
- Turn on hazard lights immediately and leave them on until the tow truck arrives.
- If police clear the scene before the tow arrives, get the officer's name and badge number. Some towing disputes require proof the vehicle was released to you legally.
- If you are not sure whether to drive the damaged car away or wait for a tow, err on the side of the tow. Driving a car with hidden structural or brake damage after a crash is not worth the risk. ## Common Questions

**Q: If my deductible is $500 and the tow only costs $150, does insurance pay anything?**
A: No. If the tow is the only charge and it falls below your deductible, your insurer pays nothing toward it. You would owe the full towing bill out of pocket unless you have a separate roadside assistance add-on, which typically has no deductible.

**Q: Can I use roadside assistance after an accident, or is it only for breakdowns?**
A: Most roadside assistance add-ons cover towing regardless of the reason, including accidents. The key advantage is that using roadside assistance does not require you to file a collision or comprehensive claim, so your deductible does not come into play and your rates are less likely to be affected.

**Q: How long do I have to submit a towing receipt for reimbursement if I paid out of pocket?**
A: Most insurers allow you to submit towing invoices for reimbursement within 30 to 60 days of the incident. Contact your insurer as soon as possible after the accident, get a claim number, and keep your original receipt from the towing company to support the submission.
