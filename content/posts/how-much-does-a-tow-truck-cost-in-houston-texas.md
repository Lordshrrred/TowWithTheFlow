> **Quick Answer:** A standard tow in Houston typically runs $90 to $200 for the first several miles, which covers the hookup fee and a base mileage allowance. After that, expect roughly $4 to $8 per extra mile. Nights, weekends, flatbeds, and heavy vehicles push the total higher. Get the full price quoted before you agree, and ask what is included.

## How Much Does a Basic Tow Cost in Houston?

A basic Houston tow usually costs $90 to $200 for a short haul under 10 miles. That price is a hookup fee plus a few included miles, and it varies by company and time of day.

Most Houston operators charge in two parts: a flat hookup fee, then a per-mile rate once you pass the included distance. Houston is huge, so mileage adds up fast. A breakdown on the Katy Freeway (I-10) near the Beltway 8 interchange and a shop in Pasadena can be 25 miles apart, which is a very different bill than a tow across the same neighborhood.

For a detailed per-mile breakdown, see [towing cost in Houston Texas per mile](/towing-cost-in-houston-texas-per-mile/).

## What Makes a Houston Tow Cost More?

Distance, time of day, vehicle type, and how hard your car is to reach are what raise the price. Each one can add $25 to $100 or more.

- **Flatbed vs. hook:** A flatbed is the right choice for all-wheel-drive vehicles, low cars, and anything with transmission trouble. It usually costs more. Compare the two in [flatbed vs. hook and chain towing in Houston](/towing-cost-houston-texas-flatbed-vs-hook/).
- **Nights and weekends:** After-hours calls often carry a surcharge. See [what a tow costs at night in Houston](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-in-houston-texas-at-night/).
- **Vehicle size:** Trucks, dually pickups, and vans cost more than sedans.
- **Winching:** If your car is in a ditch, off a service road, or wedged in a tight lot, expect a recovery charge on top of the tow.
- **Wait time and storage:** Some yards charge daily storage the moment your car arrives.

## What Should I Ask the Tow Company Before I Say Yes?

Ask for the total price, not just the hookup fee, before the truck rolls. A legitimate dispatcher will give you a number or a clear formula.

Ask these four questions:

1. What is the total to tow from my exact location to my exact destination?
2. Is that flatbed or hook, and does the price change?
3. Are there fees for nights, fuel, winching, or wait time?

## How Do I Avoid Getting Ripped Off on a Houston Freeway?

Never accept a tow from an unsolicited truck that pulls up beside you. Stranded drivers on I-45, the Gulf Freeway, and the 610 Loop are common targets for roaming operators who quote low and bill high.

Call your own tow or your insurer's roadside line instead. If a driver you did not call shows up, you are not obligated to use them. If your car is blocking traffic and you are unsure who to call, the steps in [who to call when your car breaks down on a Houston freeway](/car-broke-down-on-houston-freeway-who-to-call/) walk through it, including when to involve law enforcement.

Check your auto policy or credit card before paying cash. Many include roadside coverage with a free tow of a set distance. If you have no coverage, [Houston flatbed towing costs without insurance](/towing-cost-houston-texas-flatbed-no-insurance/) covers what to expect.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow per mile in Houston?**
A: Most operators charge roughly $4 to $8 per mile after the included base miles. Long hauls across the metro, like Katy to Pasadena, can add up quickly.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive than a regular tow in Houston?**
A: Usually, yes, by a modest amount. It is worth it for AWD vehicles, low cars, and transmission problems, since towing the wrong way can damage the drivetrain.

**Q: Do Houston tow trucks charge more at night?**
A: Many do. After-hours and holiday calls often carry a surcharge, so ask whether the quote already includes it.

**Q: Can I get a free tow in Houston?**
A: Possibly. Many insurance policies, credit cards, and memberships like AAA include limited free towing. Check before you pay out of pocket.

**Q: What if I break down on a Houston freeway with no phone signal?**
A: Stay in the vehicle with your hazards on, and wait for help from a passing patrol or another driver with service. Read [what to do with no phone signal on a Houston highway](/car-broke-down-on-houston-highway-no-phone-signal/) before you leave the car.
