## What Just Happened to Your Engine

A seized engine means internal metal parts, usually pistons or bearings, have stopped moving. The most common causes are low oil pressure, catastrophic overheating, or a spun bearing that finally let go completely. You probably heard a loud knock or bang, then the engine went silent and the car died. Or it stalled without warning and refuses to crank.

Either way, the engine is done moving on its own. Trying to restart it can cause additional damage, including a cracked block or bent connecting rods, that turns a $3,000 repair into a boat anchor.

## What To Do Right Now

1. **Get off the road safely.** If the engine dies while moving, use your remaining momentum to coast to the shoulder or a parking lot. Put on your hazard lights immediately.

2. **Do not attempt to restart the car.** Turn the key to the off position and leave it there. If the engine is locked, forcing it to crank will only add damage.

3. **Check for smoke or fluid under the car.** A seized engine often comes with oil smoke or burning smells. If you see flames, get everyone out and move at least 100 feet away. See what to do if your [car caught fire on the highway](/car-caught-fire-on-highway-what-to-do/).

4. **Call for a flatbed tow truck.** A seized engine vehicle cannot be towed on a wheel-lift where the drive wheels stay on the ground. You need a flatbed. Tell the dispatcher the engine is seized so they send the right truck.

5. **Call your insurance or roadside assistance.** Most policies with roadside add-ons cover at least a base tow. Pull up your policy app or card before you call a private tow company so you know what reimbursement you are working with.

6. **Stay in your car with doors locked if you are on a busy highway.** If you are on an interstate shoulder, stay buckled in the passenger side until help arrives. For more detail on shoulder safety, read [car died on highway shoulder: is it safe to wait for a tow](/car-died-on-highway-shoulder-safe-to-wait-for-tow/).

7. **Tell the tow driver your destination before he hooks up.** Decide in advance: dealership, independent mechanic, or home. Changing the drop location mid-tow can add mileage fees.

## What It Might Cost

Towing a seized engine vehicle costs more than a standard breakdown tow for one reason: it requires a flatbed. Here is a realistic breakdown:

- **Hookup fee:** $50 to $100 flat, charged before the truck moves
- **Per-mile rate:** $3 to $7 per mile depending on region
- **Flatbed premium:** Some companies add $25 to $50 over a standard wheel-lift rate
- **After-hours or weekend surcharge:** $25 to $75 extra

A 10-mile tow on a weekday in most U.S. cities will run $100 to $175. The same tow on a Friday night in a dense metro can push $250 to $300. If you need to move the car from a highway to a dealership across town, budget $200 to $400 and verify your insurance covers part of it before you authorize the tow. Check [towing cost from interstate to dealership](/towing-cost-from-interstate-to-dealership/) for city-specific numbers.

If your roadside assistance caps at $50 or $100 in reimbursement, you will pay the difference out of pocket. Know that number now.

## Stay Safe

- Turn hazard lights on the second the engine dies, before you do anything else.
- If you stop on a highway shoulder at night, do not stand behind or beside the car. Stay inside or move completely off the road.
- If other warning lights were flashing before the seizure (oil pressure, temperature), tell the tow driver and the mechanic. That history matters for diagnosis.
- Do not let a tow company pressure you into a shop you did not choose. You have the right to name your destination.
- A seized engine is not always a total loss. A spun bearing on an otherwise solid engine can sometimes be rebuilt. Get a written diagnosis before anyone quotes you a replacement engine.
