## What Do I Do First If I'm Stuck in Snow in Denver Right Now?

Get off the gas and stop digging yourself in deeper. Spinning your tires on packed snow just burns a hole under your drive wheels and makes extraction harder and more expensive. Turn on your hazards, shift to park, and assess where you are.

If you're on a main road like Colorado Boulevard, Colfax, or Federal, other drivers will see you. If you slid off I-70 near the Mousetrap or into a median on I-25 near the University Boulevard interchange, you're in a higher-risk spot. Get out of the car only if it's safe to do so, otherwise stay inside with your seatbelt on.

Clear your exhaust pipe if snow is packed around it. A blocked tailpipe with the engine running can push carbon monoxide into the cabin fast. That's not a possibility, it's a real and documented risk.

## How Do I Get a Tow Truck in Denver Without AAA?

Search "snow recovery Denver" or "winch out Denver" and call directly. Do not just call a generic towing number and assume they handle snow extraction. Ask the dispatcher: "Do you do winch-outs and snow recoveries?" Some standard tow operators don't carry the right rigging for it.

Local independent operators are often faster than the big national dispatch chains during a Denver storm. During a major snowfall on I-70 or along the 6th Avenue corridor, some dispatch chains are routing calls through out-of-state call centers with no real grip on which trucks are actually available locally. A direct call to a Denver-based operator gets you on an actual queue.

If you're on a state highway or interstate, CDOT's Incident Management Team patrols I-25, I-70, and I-225 and can assist or call for help. You can also dial 511 for Colorado road conditions and assistance information.

## How Long Will I Wait for a Tow During a Denver Snowstorm?

During a heavy snow event, plan for one to three hours, sometimes longer. Denver tow trucks get stretched thin fast when the Front Range gets significant accumulation. A storm that drops eight inches in the foothills above Morrison and then pushes into the city will have I-70 westbound and C-470 backing up with stuck vehicles well before rush hour.

Prioritization goes to accidents and blocking vehicles first. If you're stuck in a parking lot in Stapleton or Capitol Hill but not blocking traffic, you're lower on the list than someone who went into the ditch on US-36 near Broomfield.

Use that wait time well. Run the engine in short intervals to stay warm rather than continuously, especially if you're not sure how long you'll be there or how much gas you have. Keep a window slightly cracked. If you have a blanket, use it. Sometimes, yes. If your tires have any traction at all, try these in order:

- **Rock the car.** Shift between drive and reverse in short bursts. Let the car's momentum build slightly in each direction before switching. Don't rev hard.
- **Clear around the tires.** A shovel,

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I call 911 if I'm stuck in snow in Denver?**
A: Call 911 only if you're in immediate danger, blocking a lane on a major highway, or injured. For a standard stuck-in-snow situation, 911 will typically redirect you to find a tow on your own. They won't dispatch a tow truck to you.

**Q: Will CDOT help me if I'm stuck on I-70 in Denver?**
A: CDOT's Incident Management Team patrols I-70 and I-25 actively during storms and can radio for help or provide safety equipment. They cannot tow you themselves, but they can stay with you until a tow arrives and make sure you're not a hazard to other traffic.

**Q: How do I find a legit tow company in Denver fast if I'm in a panic?**
A: Search "snow recovery Denver" or "winch out Denver" on Google Maps. Look for companies with reviews specifically mentioning snow or stuck vehicles. Call directly, confirm they're actually in Denver and not routed through an out-of-state dispatcher, and ask for an ETA before you commit.

**Q: Is it safe to run my engine to stay warm while waiting for a tow?**
A: Yes, but check your exhaust pipe first. If snow is packed around it, clear it before starting the engine. Run the heat in intervals rather than continuously if you're low on fuel, and crack a window slightly to allow fresh air in.

**Q: Does my car insurance cover a tow if I'm stuck in snow and have no AAA?**
A: It depends on your policy. If you have comprehensive coverage with a roadside assistance add-on, yes, many policies will reimburse you for a tow or dispatch one directly. Call your insurer's roadside line from the scene or check your app before calling a tow truck so you know who's paying.
