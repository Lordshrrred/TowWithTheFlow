> **Quick Answer:** A tow truck in Dallas, Texas costs $75 to $150 for a local tow under 10 miles. Longer hauls run $3 to $7 per mile after a base hookup fee of $50 to $75. Flatbed trucks, after-hours calls, and highway pickups push that higher. If you're on I-635 or I-35E with a disabled vehicle, expect to pay toward the top of those ranges.

## What Does a Tow Truck Actually Cost in Dallas Right Now?

Most local tows in Dallas land between $75 and $150 for the first 5 to 10 miles. After that, add $3 to $7 per mile. A hookup or service fee of $50 to $75 is standard and gets charged before the wheels even roll. If you're being towed from Oak Cliff to a shop in Plano, you're covering 20-plus miles, which means a total bill of $150 to $250 is realistic.

Prices aren't regulated by the City of Dallas for private tows you arrange yourself, so there's real variance. Always ask for a total estimate before you authorize anything.

## Does It Cost More If I'm Broken Down on the Highway?

Yes, a highway breakdown almost always costs more. Drivers stranded on I-35E near the Mixmaster, on I-635 (the LBJ Freeway), or on US-75 through the Uptown corridor face higher rates because tow operators take on more risk pulling onto a busy freeway. Some companies charge a separate highway access fee of $25 to $50 on top of the base rate.

TXDOT also runs the Incident Management program on major Dallas freeways, where a free service patrol truck may push your car to the next exit. That only applies to certain corridors during daytime hours and does not include a tow to a shop. If you need to go further than the nearest exit, you'll still need to call a private tower. For more detail on what a highway tow specifically runs, see [Towing Cost in Dallas Texas After Highway Breakdown](/towing-cost-in-dallas-texas-after-highway-breakdown/).

## Flatbed vs. Wheel-Lift: Does the Truck Type Change the Price?

Flatbed tows cost more, typically $25 to $75 above a standard wheel-lift rate. A flatbed is the right call for all-wheel-drive vehicles, low-clearance cars, and anything with front-end or suspension damage. Trying to wheel-lift a Subaru Outback or an AWD Ford Explorer with all four wheels on the ground can cause serious drivetrain damage.

If your car had a collision, has a blown tire with a damaged rim, or won't shift to neutral, insist on a flatbed. You can compare exactly what you'll pay for each option in Dallas with this breakdown: [Towing Cost in Dallas Texas Hook and Chain vs Flatbed](/towing-cost-in-dallas-texas-hook-and-chain-vs-flatbed/).

## What Happens to the Price After Hours or on Weekends?

After-hours towing in Dallas costs meaningfully more. Most private tow companies add a $25 to $75 surcharge for calls placed between roughly 10 p.m. and 6 a.m., and again on holidays. Weekend rates vary by company but expect a 15 to 25 percent premium over standard daytime pricing.

## Does My Insurance or Roadside Plan Cover Any of This?

It depends on what you have. If your auto policy includes comprehensive or roadside assistance coverage, a tow is often covered up to a dollar cap, commonly $50 to $100, which may not cover a full Dallas tow depending on distance. GEICO, State Farm, and AAA all offer roadside towing benefits but the reimbursement limits vary by plan tier.

Call your insurer before you call a tow company if you have time to do so safely. Give the dispatcher your policy number and ask what towing benefit is included and whether you need to use a specific network provider. Using an out-of-network tower does not automatically void coverage, but it may require you to pay upfront and submit a reimbursement claim later.

If you have no insurance at all, you're paying out of pocket. That means $75 to $200 for a typical local tow in Dallas. See [Tow Truck Cost in Dallas Texas With No Insurance](/tow-truck-cost-dallas-texas-no-insurance/) for specifics on what to expect and how to negotiate.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does it cost to tow a car 5 miles in Dallas?**
A: A 5-mile tow in Dallas typically costs $75 to $120, including the hookup fee. Some companies charge a flat local rate in that range rather than billing per mile for short hauls.

**Q: Is there a free tow service on Dallas freeways?**
A: TXDOT's Incident Management trucks patrol major Dallas freeways like I-635 and I-35E during certain hours and can push your car to the next exit at no charge. They do not tow to a shop. Once you're off the freeway, you need a private tow company.

**Q: How do I avoid getting overcharged by a tow truck in Dallas?**
A: Get a verbal or written total estimate before authorizing the tow. Ask specifically about the hookup fee, per-mile rate, and any surcharges. If a company won't give you a number upfront, call a different one.

**Q: How long does it take for a tow truck to arrive in Dallas?**
A: Typical wait time in Dallas is 30 to 60 minutes for a private tow. On heavily congested days on I-30 or I-75, or during bad weather, that can stretch to 90 minutes or more. AAA members often see similar or longer waits depending on demand.

**Q: Do Dallas tow trucks charge more for heavy vehicles like trucks or SUVs?**
A: Yes. Full-size pickups, SUVs, and vans often carry a $25 to $50 surcharge because they require more equipment and take longer to secure. Always mention your vehicle type when you call so the dispatcher sends the right truck and quotes you accurately.
