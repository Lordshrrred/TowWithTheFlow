> **Quick Answer:** Call 911 if you or anyone is injured or if you're in a live lane. If you're safely on the shoulder, call a tow truck first, then your insurance or roadside plan. TxDOT's Highway Emergency Response Operators (HERO) patrol Houston's main freeways and will stop to help for free. Turn on your hazards, get out on the passenger side, and move behind the guardrail if there is one.

## Who Do I Actually Call First When My Car Breaks Down on a Houston Freeway?

Call 911 if there's an injury, you're blocking traffic, or the situation feels unsafe. Otherwise, your first call is a tow truck, not 911. Houston freeways see thousands of breakdowns weekly, and tying up 911 for a non-emergency slows response for people who need it more.

If you have AAA, call them. If you don't, call a private tow company directly. Know that dispatchers will ask for your exact location, so look for a milepost marker, a freeway sign, or the nearest cross street. On I-10 westbound near the Katy Managed Lanes, for example, green milepost markers are bolted to the right-side barrier every mile. Read one off and your ETA improves immediately.

TxDOT's HERO program runs patrol trucks on I-10, I-45, US-59/69 (Southwest Freeway), the Sam Houston Tollway (Beltway 8), and I-610. They push disabled vehicles to the next exit, help change tires, and can call a tow if needed, all at no charge. You don't call them; they find you. But you need to be visible with hazards on.

## What Do I Do in the Minutes Before Help Arrives on a Houston Freeway?

Get as far right as possible, ideally completely off the travel lanes onto the shoulder or the frontage road. The inner HOV lane on I-45 South near Almeda is one of the most dangerous places in Houston to be stuck because there's almost no shoulder clearance. If your car dies there, get everyone out and over the concrete barrier onto the walkway if possible.

Once stopped: hazards on, engine off if it's overheating or smoking. Get all passengers out through the passenger-side door and move to the far side of any guardrail. Stay there. Do not stand between your car and moving traffic.

If it's dark, use your phone flashlight or road flares pointed back toward oncoming traffic. Houston's freeways, especially I-10 near the Gessner interchange, carry heavy traffic around the clock.

## How Much Will a Tow Truck Cost on a Houston Freeway?

Most Houston tow companies charge a hookup fee of $75 to $100, then $3 to $5 per mile after that. A tow from a breakdown on I-45 near the South Loop to a shop in Pearland or the Medical Center will run $100 to $175 depending on the company and time of day. After midnight or on weekends, expect the higher end of those ranges.

## What If I Have No Cell Signal on the Houston Freeway?

Some stretches of Houston freeways, particularly elevated sections of I-69/US-59 through Midtown or the older elevated portion of I-45 near downtown, can have spotty signal inside a vehicle. Step outside the car (safely, away from traffic) and try again. Signal is usually better at shoulder level than inside a car surrounded by metal.

If you truly cannot get a call out, turn on your hazards and wait for a HERO truck or a Houston Police Department officer. HPD patrols major freeways and will call for a tow on your behalf. You can also try texting 911, Houston's Harris County 911 system accepts text messages in most areas.

## Does My Insurance Cover a Freeway Tow in Houston?

It depends on your policy. Standard liability-only coverage does not include roadside assistance. If you have comprehensive coverage and added roadside assistance as a rider, you're likely covered for one tow per incident, often with a per-incident cap around $100. Call the roadside number on the back of your insurance card to confirm before you hire a private company.

If your coverage won't pay, you pay the tow company directly and you can sometimes submit for partial reimbursement later. Keep the receipt either way.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does TxDOT's HERO program tow my car or just push it to the shoulder?**
A: HERO trucks can push your car to the nearest exit and help with minor issues like a tire change, but they don't tow to a repair shop. You'll still need a private tow company for that part.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive on a Houston freeway?**
A: Typical wait is 30 to 60 minutes during normal traffic. During peak hours on corridors like I-10 West or the Beltway 8 South, it can run longer. Give the dispatcher your exact milepost and which direction you're traveling so they can route the closest truck.

**Q: Can I leave my car on the Houston freeway shoulder overnight?**
A: No. TxDOT and HPD will have an abandoned vehicle on a freeway shoulder tagged and towed, often within a few hours. Storage fees at the impound lot add up fast. Get it moved the same day.

**Q: What if my car breaks down in the HOV lanes or managed lanes on I-10?**
A: Get to the far left wall if you can stop safely, turn on hazards, and call 911 immediately. There is almost no usable shoulder in the Katy Managed Lanes. This is a genuine safety emergency and warrants a 911 call even without an injury.

**Q: Is it safe to wait inside my car on a Houston freeway shoulder?**
A: Only if you cannot safely exit. The shoulder is one of the most dangerous places on any highway. If you stay in the car, keep your seatbelt on. The better move is to exit through the passenger door and get behind a barrier away from traffic.
