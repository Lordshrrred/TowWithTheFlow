> **Quick Answer:** Towing a car in Las Vegas runs $75 to $150 for a short local haul, roughly 5 to 10 miles. Longer tows, Strip-area calls, or late-night hookups push that to $200 or more. Expect a hookup fee of $50 to $100 just to get the truck rolling, plus $3 to $7 per mile after that. No insurance? Budget on the higher end.

## How Much Does a Tow Actually Cost in Las Vegas?

A local tow in Las Vegas costs $75 to $150 for the first 5 to 10 miles. The hookup fee, sometimes called a base rate or dispatch fee, typically runs $50 to $100, then mileage adds $3 to $7 per mile on top.

Here is how that shakes out in real numbers:

| Distance | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|
| Under 5 miles | $75 to $110 |
| 5 to 15 miles | $110 to $175 |
| 15 to 30 miles | $175 to $250 |
| 30+ miles (Henderson to Summerlin, for example) | $250 to $400+ |

If you break down on I-15 near the Tropicana interchange or on US-95 heading toward Summerlin, you are squarely in the local rate zone. If you are out on I-15 south toward Jean, or east on I-215 past the Henderson city limits, distance fees add up fast.

## Does It Cost More to Get Towed on the Las Vegas Strip?

Yes, Strip-area tows frequently carry a premium. Getting a truck to Las Vegas Boulevard between the Stratosphere and Mandalay Bay during heavy traffic means the driver sits longer and may charge a congestion or wait fee on top of the standard hookup rate. Premium Strip-area calls can run $150 to $225 even for a short move.

If your car quits in a casino parking structure on Flamingo Road or Harmon Avenue, the tow company may also charge an extra $25 to $50 for confined-space or underground parking retrieval. For more detail on exactly what Strip calls cost, see [Towing Cost Las Vegas Nevada Strip Area Rates](/towing-cost-las-vegas-nevada-strip-area-rates/).

## What Factors Push the Price Up the Most?

Time of day and truck type move the needle the most. After-hours calls, roughly 8 PM to 6 AM, regularly add $25 to $75 to any base rate. Weekend nights on or near the Strip push that higher.

The other big variable is truck type. A standard wheel-lift tow is the cheapest option. A flatbed costs more, usually $20 to $50 extra, but if you are driving an all-wheel-drive vehicle, a luxury car, or anything low to the ground, a flatbed is not optional. Las Vegas summer heat also plays a role: asphalt temperatures routinely exceed 150 degrees Fahrenheit, and if your car has been sitting disabled for a while in that heat, a responsible driver will take extra care loading it, which can add time to the call. For details on flatbed-specific pricing, [Towing Cost in Las Vegas Nevada Flatbed Rate](/towing-cost-in-las-vegas-nevada-flatbed-rate/) covers that in full.

## What If I Don't Have Insurance or Roadside Coverage?

Without insurance or a roadside assistance membership, you pay the full out-of-pocket rate, and Las Vegas tow companies know it. Expect no discounts and no negotiating room on emergency calls.

## Is There Anything I Can Do to Lower the Cost Right Now?

Call from a safe spot, have your exact location ready, and ask for the total cost upfront before you authorize anything. Dispatchers are required to give you an estimate. If the driver quotes you a higher number on arrival, you can refuse service before the truck hooks up.

Know your location precisely. "Near the Palms Casino" is not enough. Tell them: "Westbound I-15, just past the Charleston Boulevard exit, right shoulder." That saves the driver time and can shorten your wait, which also reduces any wait-time fees. If your situation is not an immediate safety hazard, parking in a nearby lot, not on the shoulder of US-95 near the Spaghetti Bowl interchange, can sometimes allow you to shop rates more calmly. ## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow truck in Las Vegas at night?**
A: After-hours calls in Las Vegas typically add $25 to $75 to the base rate, putting most nighttime tows in the $125 to $250 range depending on distance. Weekend nights near the Strip run on the higher end.

**Q: Does AAA cover towing in Las Vegas?**
A: Yes. AAA Classic covers the first 5 miles free, with per-mile charges after that. AAA Plus extends free towing to 100 miles. You still pay if the tow exceeds your plan's mileage limit.

**Q: How far will a Las Vegas tow truck go?**
A: Most local companies will tow anywhere in the Las Vegas Valley, including Henderson and North Las Vegas, without special arrangements. Tows to Laughlin, Pahrump, or outside the metro require a long-distance rate, usually $4 to $7 per mile for the whole trip.

**Q: Can I negotiate the tow price after my car is already hooked up?**
A: Once the vehicle is on the truck, you have very little leverage. Get the price confirmed before hookup. If the final number on arrival is higher than what dispatch quoted, refuse service while the car is still on the ground.

**Q: How long does it take to get a tow truck in Las Vegas?**
A: Most calls see a truck within 30 to 60 minutes. During peak hours on I-15 or around the Strip, or during extreme heat events when breakdowns spike, waits can stretch to 90 minutes or more.
