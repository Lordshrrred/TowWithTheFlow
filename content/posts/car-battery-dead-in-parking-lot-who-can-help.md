## Who Can Help Me With a Dead Battery in a Parking Lot?

Your best first call is whoever already covers you: AAA, your auto insurer, your car maker's roadside program, or a credit card with roadside benefits. Check the back of your insurance card and your glovebox paperwork. Many people have coverage they forgot about.

If you have none of those, work down this list:

- **Mobile jump-start or battery service.** Search "mobile battery service near me." Many will test and replace the battery on the spot.
- **A local tow company.** Most will do a jump-start call, not just a tow.
- **Another driver.** If you have jumper cables, ask someone nearby. If you don't, skip the stranger hunt and call a pro.
- **Store staff.** Auto parts stores like AutoZone and O'Reilly will often test a battery for free and sell you a new one. If you're parked at or near one, walk in.

Not sure what your policy includes? Read [does car insurance cover roadside assistance if I add it](/does-car-insurance-cover-roadside-assistance-if-i-add-it/) before you assume you're covered or not.

## How Do I Know It's the Battery and Not Something Else?

A dead battery usually means you turn the key and get clicking, a weak crank, or nothing at all, with dim or dead dash lights and interior lights. A bad alternator or starter looks different.

Quick tells:

- **Rapid clicking, dim lights:** Battery is too weak to turn the starter. Classic dead battery.
- **One loud click, then nothing, lights bright:** Likely the starter or its solenoid, not the battery.
- **Car ran fine, then died while driving and now won't restart:** Suspect the alternator. The battery drained because nothing was recharging it.
- **Engine cranks normally but won't fire:** Not a battery problem. Look at fuel or spark.
- **Battery warning light was on recently:** Points to charging system trouble.

If a jump works but the car dies again within a day, don't just buy another jump. Get the battery and alternator tested. A jump only fixes the symptom.

## What Does a Jump Start Cost If I'm Not Covered?

Expect about $40 to $100 for a mobile jump-start, and it varies by city, time of day, and company. Nights, weekends, and holidays cost more. Ask for the total price on the phone before anyone is dispatched, including any service fee.

If the battery is simply dead and old, a mobile service that replaces it on-site typically costs more than a jump but saves you a second breakdown. Batteries commonly last three to five years, so if yours is in that range, replacement is usually the smarter spend.

Park the donor car nose to nose with yours, close enough for the cables to reach but not touching. Turn both cars off, then connect in this exact order:

1. Red clamp to the **positive (+)** terminal on the dead battery.
2. Other red clamp to the **positive (+)** terminal on the good battery.
3. Black clamp to the **negative (-)** terminal on the good battery.
4. Last black clamp to **bare, unpainted metal** on your engine block or a thick bolt, away from the battery. Not the negative terminal.

Start the donor car and let it run a couple of minutes. Then try yours. Once it starts, remove the clamps in reverse order.

Then drive it at least 20 to 30 minutes so the alternator can recharge the battery. Don't shut it off at the first stop.

Skip the jump if the battery case is cracked, bulging, leaking, or frozen. Call a pro instead.

## What Should I Do While I Wait for Help?

Get yourself and your car in a safe spot, then wait somewhere visible. Parking lots have moving cars and distracted drivers.

- If the car can be pushed safely out of a driving lane by hand, do it, but only with help and only if it's on level ground and is a manual or in neutral.
- Turn on your hazard lights if the battery still has enough charge.
- Stay near the car in a lit, visible area, or wait inside a nearby store.
- At night, keep your phone charged and tell the dispatcher your exact lot and any landmark, like the store entrance or a light pole number.

If the car is blocking a lane or you feel unsafe, say so when you call. Dispatchers prioritize that.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I get a jump start without cables or roadside assistance?**
A: Yes. Call a mobile jump-start service or a local tow company, which carry their own equipment. Many auto parts stores also loan out jumper cables or sell a portable jump pack.

**Q: Will AAA jump my car for free?**
A: If you're a current member, a jump start is typically included in your plan, though the number of free calls per year depends on your membership level. Have your membership number and exact location ready when you call.

**Q: How long should I drive after a jump start?**
A: At least 20 to 30 minutes of steady driving. Short trips won't recharge a drained battery, and you may be stranded again the next time you shut it off.

**Q: Can a dead battery be caused by leaving the lights on?**
A: Yes. Headlights, dome lights, or a phone charger left plugged in can drain a battery overnight, especially an older one. A single drain like that often recovers after a jump and a long drive, but repeated drains shorten the battery's life.

**Q: When should I tow instead of jump?**
A: Tow if the jump doesn't help, you smell burning or see smoke, the battery looks swollen or is leaking, or the car dies again right after starting. Those point to a charging or electrical fault.
