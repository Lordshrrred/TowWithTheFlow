> **Quick Answer:** A tow to a mechanic shop in Denver typically runs $75 to $125 for the first 5 miles, then $3 to $5 per mile after that. A short cross-town haul, say from Capitol Hill to a shop in Lakewood, usually lands between $100 and $175. Longer runs, like from I-70 near Stapleton to a shop in Englewood, can hit $200 or more. Distance is the biggest cost driver, but time of day matters too.

## What Does It Actually Cost to Tow My Car to a Mechanic Shop in Denver?

A local tow in Denver runs $75 to $125 for the hookup and first few miles, plus $3 to $5 per additional mile. Most in-city tows, think University Hills to a shop on South Broadway or from Five Points to a garage in Wheat Ridge, fall in the $100 to $175 range depending on traffic routing and whether you need a flatbed.

If you need a flatbed (required for AWD vehicles, low-clearance cars, or anything with a seized drivetrain), add $25 to $50 to those numbers. Wheel-lift trucks are cheaper but not appropriate for every car. If you're not sure which your car needs, ask the dispatcher before they roll a truck.

## How Much Does Distance Add to the Tow Bill in Denver?

Every mile past the base distance adds $3 to $5, billed per mile the truck actually drives with your car loaded. Denver's geography means routes can add real mileage fast.

A tow from a breakdown on I-25 near the Colorado Boulevard exit to a shop in Highlands Ranch is roughly 18 to 22 miles by road. At $4 per mile on top of a $90 hookup fee, that's $162 to $178 before any after-hours or fuel surcharges. A shorter run, like from the I-70 and I-25 stack interchange to a shop in the Baker neighborhood, might be only 6 miles and cost $110 to $125 total.

If your mechanic is on the other side of Denver from where you broke down, call ahead and confirm the shop will actually accept the car that day. A tow to a closed or full shop wastes money and time. For a full breakdown of distance-based fees, see [Towing Cost in Denver to Repair Shop Distance Fees](/towing-cost-in-denver-to-repair-shop-distance-fees/).

## Does It Cost More to Tow at Night or on the Weekend?

Yes. Most Denver towing companies add a 15% to 25% after-hours surcharge for calls placed between roughly 9 PM and 6 AM, and many charge the same premium on weekends. That $130 daytime tow can hit $160 after 10 PM on a Saturday.

If your car breaks down during evening rush on I-25 or on a Friday night in RiNo, expect both the surcharge and a longer wait. Plan for it financially. For specifics on what nighttime towing adds to the bill, [Towing Cost in Denver Colorado at Night](/towing-cost-in-denver-colorado-at-night/) has the full breakdown.

## Will My Insurance or Roadside Assistance Cover the Tow to a Shop?

It depends on your policy. Most roadside assistance programs, including AAA and the add-ons offered through State Farm, GEICO, and Allstate, cover towing to the nearest qualified repair facility. That phrase matters: "nearest qualified facility." If your preferred mechanic is across town and there's a closer shop, some programs will only pay for the shorter distance and bill you the difference.

## What Should I Do Right Now While I Wait for the Tow Truck?

Get your car as far off the road as possible, turn on your hazard lights, and stay out of traffic lanes. If you broke down on I-70 west of the mousetrap or on I-25 in the construction zone near 58th Avenue, do not stand between your car and moving traffic under any circumstances. Get behind the guardrail if there is one.

Call your mechanic before you call the tow truck. Confirm the shop is open and can take your car that day. If they can't receive it, you're paying for a tow to a parking spot, not a repair. Give the tow dispatcher the shop's exact address so they can quote you an accurate price up front. Most Denver operators will give you a firm quote over the phone if you provide pickup and drop-off addresses.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a 10-mile tow to a mechanic shop cost in Denver?**
A: Expect $120 to $150 for a standard wheel-lift tow covering 10 miles during daytime hours. A flatbed for the same distance runs $145 to $175.

**Q: Can I tell the tow truck driver which mechanic shop to take my car to?**
A: Yes, unless your insurer's roadside program specifies the nearest facility. If you're paying out of pocket, you direct the drop-off. Give the shop address when you call for the tow, not after the truck arrives.

**Q: What if my mechanic shop is closed when my car breaks down?**
A: Most shops will accept a towed vehicle and leave it in their lot if you call ahead and arrange it. Ask if they have a key drop. If not, you may need to store the car at a tow yard overnight, which adds $35 to $75 per day in storage fees.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow company directly instead of going through my insurance?**
A: Sometimes. Insurance roadside programs have negotiated rates, but their coverage caps may not cover the full distance to your shop. Calling a tow company directly lets you shop rates, but prices vary. Get a firm quote before agreeing.

**Q: Do Denver tow companies charge extra if my car is in a tight spot, like a parking garage or a steep driveway?**
A: Many do. Difficult access situations, including multi-level garages, steep grades common in Washington Park or the Highlands neighborhood, or cars with no neutral gear, often trigger a $25 to $75 additional fee. Ask about this when you call.
