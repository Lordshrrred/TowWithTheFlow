> **Quick Answer:** Yes, tow it. A snapped serpentine belt stops the water pump, alternator, and usually power steering at the same time. Driving even a few miles risks overheating the engine and a dead battery. Get off the road, shut the engine off, and call for a tow. The belt itself is cheap to replace, but only if the engine hasn't cooked first.

## Is a Snapped Serpentine Belt a Tow Situation?

Yes. On most cars, one belt runs the water pump, alternator, and power steering pump, and sometimes the A/C compressor. When it snaps, all of them quit together.

The water pump is the problem. With no coolant circulating, the engine can overheat within a few minutes of driving, and an overheated engine means warped heads or a blown head gasket. That turns a roughly $100 to $250 repair into a multi-thousand-dollar one. The alternator stops charging too, so you're running on battery alone with maybe 15 to 30 minutes of power left, less at night with lights on.

Some older cars run separate belts for each accessory. If yours does, losing one may not kill the water pump, but you can't tell by looking, so don't gamble.

## What Do I Do in the First 60 Seconds After the Belt Snaps?

Steer to the shoulder and shut the engine off. You'll feel the steering get heavy and hear a loud slap or squeal, and the battery and temperature lights will likely come on.

1. **Hold the wheel firmly.** Without power steering, the car still steers, it just takes real effort. Don't panic and don't brake hard.
2. **Signal and coast to the right shoulder.** Pick a spot well clear of traffic. Brakes still work, though the pedal may feel stiffer on some vehicles.
3. **Shift to park, kill the engine, hazards on.**
4. **Don't restart it** to "see if it's fine." A restart gives you nothing and costs you battery.

If you're stopped in a lane or on a narrow shoulder, get everyone out the passenger side and behind a guardrail. See [what to do when your car dies on the highway shoulder](/car-died-on-highway-shoulder-safe-to-wait-for-tow-truck/) for how long you can safely sit there.

## Can I Drive a Short Distance on a Snapped Belt?

Don't, unless you are literally pulling out of a live lane into a nearby parking lot. A snapped serpentine belt is not a "limp home" failure like a flat spare or a loose gas cap.

Here's why people get burned: the temperature gauge often lags. You can drive half a mile, see the needle barely move, and assume you're fine. By the time it spikes, the damage is already happening. Compare that to a [burst radiator hose](/radiator-hose-burst-while-driving-can-i-drive-to-shop/) or [fast coolant loss](/coolant-leaking-fast-can-i-drive-to-nearest-mechanic/): same overheating risk, same answer.

One exception worth knowing: if the car is a diesel or has an electric water pump and the belt only drove the alternator, you might get a short distance. Your owner's manual or a quick look under the hood tells you, but "might" is not worth an engine.

## How Much Does a Tow and Belt Replacement Cost?

Expect about $100 to $250 for the tow depending on distance and your area, plus roughly $100 to $250 for the belt itself at a shop. Costs run higher in big metros, at night, and on highways. For local ballparks, check our guides for [Houston](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-in-houston-texas/) or [Los Angeles](/towing-cost-in-los-angeles-california-after-breakdown/).

The belt is usually a short job. A new belt is cheap. A tensioner or idler pulley that failed and caused the snap adds cost, so ask the shop to inspect both, because a bad tensioner will eat the new belt.

Before you pay out of pocket, check whether your insurer or a membership covers it. Many policies include towing, and [GEICO's roadside coverage limits](/geico-roadside-assistance-how-many-times-per-year/) are worth reading if that's your carrier.

## Common Questions

**Q: How far can you drive with a snapped serpentine belt?**
A: Not far enough to count on. Without the water pump, engines can overheat in a few minutes, and the battery gives you maybe 15 to 30 minutes of power. Treat it as zero miles unless you're clearing a live traffic lane.

**Q: Will a snapped serpentine belt ruin my engine?**
A: Only if you keep driving. If you shut it off quickly, the engine is almost always fine and the fix is a new belt. Driving while overheating is what warps heads and blows head gaskets.

**Q: Can I replace a serpentine belt on the roadside myself?**
A: Possible on some cars, but you need the right-sized belt, a tool to release the tensioner, and the routing diagram, usually on a sticker under the hood. If any of that is missing, call a tow instead of improvising on the shoulder.

**Q: How do I know the serpentine belt is about to fail?**
A: Look for cracking, fraying, glazing, or missing ribs on the belt, plus squealing at startup or in wet weather. Many mechanics recommend replacing it somewhere around the 60,000 to 100,000 mile range, but check your manual for your specific engine.

**Q: Does a snapped belt always mean the water pump is dead?**
A: No. The belt snapping doesn't damage the pump, it just stops it from turning. If the engine didn't overheat, the pump is usually fine, though a seized pump or pulley can be what shredded the belt in the first place.
