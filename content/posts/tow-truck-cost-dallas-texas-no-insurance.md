## What a Dallas Tow Actually Costs Out of Pocket

Dallas tow companies are not regulated by a city rate card the way some Texas cities handle police-dispatched tows. That means prices vary, and without insurance covering any of it, the full bill is yours.

Here is what you can expect to pay in 2026 calling a private tow company in the DFW metro:

- **Hook-up fee:** $75 to $125
- **Per-mile rate:** $3 to $5
- **After-hours surcharge:** $20 to $50 on top
- **Flatbed upgrade:** Add $25 to $50 if your car cannot be wheel-lifted (AWD, low clearance, front-wheel-drive with no neutral)

A typical 10-mile haul from, say, I-35E to a shop in Oak Cliff runs $115 to $175 before any surcharges. If you broke down on I-30 near Mesquite at 11 p.m. on a Saturday, budget $180 to $250.

For context, Dallas tow prices are comparable to what drivers pay in other large Texas metros. See [tow truck cost Houston highway breakdown](/tow-truck-cost-houston-highway-breakdown/) for a side-by-side reference.

## What To Do Right Now

1. **Get off the road.** Move your car to the shoulder or a nearby parking lot. Turn on your hazards. If you're on a busy highway like I-635 or I-20, stay behind the guardrail and away from traffic while you make calls.

2. **Call at least two companies before you commit.** In Dallas, independent operators like DFW Towing or local shops near your breakdown point often beat the rates of national dispatch apps. Search "tow truck near me" and call directly. Do not use an app that just routes you to whoever accepts.

3. **Confirm the total price before they hook up.** Say: "What is your hookup fee and your per-mile rate? My destination is [address], how many miles is that?" Get the number. Any reputable company gives you that without hesitation.

4. **Ask about cash discounts.** Some Dallas independents knock $10 to $20 off if you pay cash on the spot.

5. **Know where your car is going.** If you do not have a mechanic in mind, ask the tow driver for a recommendation. Do not let them default to a storage yard. Storage fees in Dallas run $40 to $75 per day and add up fast.

6. **Consider a short-term roadside membership.** If you do not have coverage and this is not the last breakdown you'll ever have, look at [roadside assistance without insurance membership cost](/roadside-assistance-without-insurance-membership-cost/) to understand what an annual plan actually runs you versus paying per call.

## Stay Safe

- Do not stand behind your vehicle on a Dallas highway. DFW traffic is aggressive and drivers do not always yield to hazard lights.
- If you are on a bridge or elevated section of I-30 or US-75, call 911 first. TxDOT has roving service patrols on major Dallas interstates that may assist for free.
- Do not let a tow truck driver pressure you into signing anything before you know the final price.
- If your car is being stored after the tow, photograph it and its condition before the driver leaves.
- Night breakdowns in isolated areas: stay in your locked car with hazards on. Call for the tow, then wait inside.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does AAA or my car insurance cover towing in Dallas if I forgot to add roadside assistance?**
A: Standard auto liability and collision policies do not include towing. You need a roadside assistance rider added to your policy, or a separate membership like AAA, for any towing costs to be covered. If you are unsure, call your insurer directly before the tow truck arrives so you know exactly what you owe.

**Q: Can a Dallas tow company charge me more than the price they quoted over the phone?**
A: A company can only change the price if the actual mileage or job conditions differ from what you described. If you confirmed a specific total before hookup and the driver demands more at delivery, you have grounds to dispute it. Always get the quote confirmed verbally with the destination address before they touch your vehicle.

**Q: Are there free towing options on Dallas highways I should try before paying out of pocket?**
A: TxDOT runs a free Incident Management program on major DFW interstates, where roving patrol trucks help disabled vehicles during peak hours. Call 511 or 911 to request assistance if you are stuck on a highway like I-35, I-30, or I-635. This service is limited to moving you to a nearby safe exit, not delivering your car to a shop.
