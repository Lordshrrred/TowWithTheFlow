## What Do I Do Right Now If My Car Slid Into a Ditch in Denver?

Turn on your hazard lights immediately and stay in your car. If you slid off on a busy corridor like I-70 near the Mousetrap interchange or on C-470 in Jefferson County, getting out puts you in the path of other vehicles that may also be sliding.

Once you're safe inside:

1. Call 911 if you're in immediate danger or blocking active traffic lanes.
2. Call your roadside assistance provider (AAA, State Farm, GEICO, Progressive) or a local tow company directly.
3. Dial 511 to report your location on a state highway. This can also give you a sense of current road conditions and whether emergency crews are already in your area.
4. Note your exact location. Cross streets, mile markers, or the nearest exit ramp. On I-25 south of downtown, for example, telling a dispatcher "southbound I-25 near the Santa Fe Drive exit" gets a truck to you faster than just saying "on the highway."
5. Keep your exhaust pipe clear of snow if you plan to run the engine for heat. A blocked tailpipe causes carbon monoxide to back up into the cabin.

For more on who to contact depending on your situation, see [Car in Ditch After Storm in Denver: Who to Call Right Now](/car-in-ditch-after-storm-in-denver-who-to-call/).

## How Much Does It Cost to Tow a Car Out of a Snow Ditch in Denver?

A snow ditch recovery in Denver usually costs between $150 and $300. A standard local tow would run $75 to $150, but ditch recovery is not a standard tow. The truck needs to use a winch to pull your car out, and that work is billed separately from the hookup and mileage fees.

What drives the cost up:

- **Depth of the ditch.** A shallow slide off a surface street in Cherry Creek is a quick winch job. A vehicle that's fully buried off a mountain highway like US-285 near Conifer is a multi-hour operation.
- **Terrain.** Mud under the snow, steep embankments, or a guardrail blocking access all add time and labor.
- **Time of day.** After-hours and weekend calls carry a premium. Check [Towing Cost in Denver Colorado at Night](/towing-cost-in-denver-colorado-at-night/) for a breakdown of how much extra you can expect after hours.
- **Insurance.** If you have roadside assistance through your policy or a membership like AAA, a winch-out is often covered, at least partially. Call them first before paying out of pocket.

## Will My Insurance or Roadside Assistance Cover a Ditch Tow in Denver?

Most roadside assistance plans cover winch-out service, but the coverage limit matters. AAA Classic members get up to 5 miles of towing free per call. A ditch recovery in Denver that requires a heavy-duty winch setup may hit or exceed that distance allowance quickly if you're out on the eastern plains or in the foothills.

State Farm's roadside assistance adds winching as a covered service under their Emergency Road Service add-on. GEICO's Emergency Roadside Service covers it similarly. The key question to ask your provider: "Does this call cover a winch-out or just a standard tow?" Get that answer before the truck hooks up, because some companies treat them as separate billable events.

If you're paying out of pocket, get a price confirmed before any work starts. See [Tow Truck Cost Denver Snow Emergency](/tow-truck-cost-denver-snow-emergency/) for what to expect during heavy storm periods when demand is high and prices can spike.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I try to drive out of the ditch myself instead of calling a tow?**
A: Sometimes, but try only if the ditch is shallow, you have traction, and you can do it without spinning deeper into soft ground or mud. Rocking between drive and reverse can work on a light slide. If the tires are spinning free or the frame is resting on the ground, stop. You will dig in deeper and make the tow harder.

**Q: How long will I wait for a tow truck in a Denver snowstorm?**
A: During active snowstorms, expect 60 to 120 minutes or longer. Tow companies in Denver get flooded with calls when a storm hits, especially along I-70 and the I-25 corridor. Calling multiple providers at once is reasonable; just cancel the ones you don't need when one confirms.

**Q: Do Denver police respond to cars stuck in ditches?**
A: Denver Police and Colorado State Patrol respond if you're blocking traffic or in a dangerous location. They won't pull your car out, but they will set up a safety perimeter and help coordinate a tow if needed. On state highways, CDOT also runs courtesy patrol trucks that can sometimes assist.

**Q: Does a winch-out count as a tow on my insurance claim?**
A: It depends on your policy language. Many roadside assistance add-ons count a winch-out as a separate service from a tow. Read your declarations page or call your insurer before the truck arrives so you know exactly what's covered and what you'll owe.

**Q: What if I slid off the road in the mountains west of Denver and I'm in a remote area?**
A: Call 911 first if you're injured or in danger. If you're safe but stuck, call Colorado State Patrol and give your mile marker on the specific highway (US-6 through Clear Creek Canyon, US-285, or wherever you are). Mountain tow recovery takes longer, costs more, and requires specialized equipment. Do not attempt to hike out in winter conditions unless you're in immediate danger where you are.
