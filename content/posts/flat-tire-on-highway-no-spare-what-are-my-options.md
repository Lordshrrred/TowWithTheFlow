> **Quick Answer:** With no spare, your real options are a tow to a tire shop, a mobile tire service that brings a tire to you, or a roadside assistance call through your insurer, AAA, or a credit card. A tire plug or sealant can work only for small tread punctures. Get fully off the road first, turn on your hazards, and call from outside the car if you can safely do so.

## What Should I Do First When I Have a Flat on the Highway and No Spare?

Get your car as far from traffic as possible, turn on your hazards, and stop trying to fix it yourself. The first job is surviving the next ten minutes, not solving the tire.

Steer to the right shoulder and keep the wheel straight. Don't brake hard. Let the car slow down on its own. If the tire is shredded, you may have to roll on the rim for a short distance, which is fine for a few hundred feet and far better than stopping in a travel lane.

Once stopped:

- Turn the wheels away from traffic and set the parking brake.
- If the shoulder is narrow or traffic is fast, stay buckled in the car and call from inside. Exiting onto a narrow shoulder is how people get hit.
- If you can safely get out, stand well away from the car, behind the guardrail if there is one.
- Call 911 if you can't get off the travel lanes or you feel unsafe. For a flat that's safely on the shoulder, call roadside help instead.

If the shoulder is too dangerous to work on, read [what to do when a flat is too dangerous to change](/flat-tire-on-highway-too-dangerous-to-change/).

## Who Can I Call for Help If I Don't Have a Spare Tire?

Call your roadside assistance provider first, then a mobile tire service if you want a new tire installed on the spot. Most people already have coverage and don't know it.

Check these in order:

1. **Your auto insurance app or card.** Many policies include roadside add-ons. See [whether car insurance covers roadside assistance if you add it](/does-car-insurance-cover-roadside-assistance-if-i-add-it/).
2. **AAA or a similar club.** They'll tow you to a shop, though coverage limits vary. See [how far AAA will tow your car for free](/how-far-will-aaa-tow-my-car-for-free/).
3. **Your credit card.** Some cards include roadside dispatch. See [whether your credit card covers roadside assistance towing](/does-my-credit-card-cover-roadside-assistance-towing/).
4. **Your car's manufacturer roadside program,** often free for the first few years on a new vehicle.
5. **A mobile tire service.** Search "mobile tire repair near me." They carry tires and mount them on the shoulder or in a parking lot.

Key point: a tire service truck often can't help if the damage is a sidewall tear or the tire needs a size they don't carry. A flatbed tow is the fallback.

## Can I Fix a Flat Tire Without a Spare?

Sometimes, but only if the puncture is in the tread, not the sidewall. A nail or screw in the tread can often be sealed with a plug kit or sealant, then driven on to a shop.

What works:

- **Tire plug kit.** A reamer and sticky rubber plug can seal a tread puncture under about a quarter inch. You'll need a portable air compressor to reinflate. This is a temporary fix, and a shop should patch it from the inside.
- **Tire sealant and inflator canister.** Fine for small tread holes. Know that sealant can foul the tire pressure sensor (TPMS) and make a later repair messier.

## How Much Will a Tow or Mobile Tire Service Cost?

Expect roughly $75 to $200 for a local highway tow, with mobile tire service call-out fees often adding to the cost of the tire itself. Prices run higher at night, on weekends, and on interstates where fewer operators are licensed to work.

The numbers move with your situation. A short tow to the nearest tire shop is the cheapest path if you're close to one. A mobile tire truck charges a service fee on top of the tire price but saves you a tow and a shop wait. Ask the dispatcher for the total, including hookup and per-mile fees, before the truck rolls.

Highway tow trucks are often dispatched through rotation, so you may not get to choose the company. You can still ask for the price up front. If something feels off afterward, [here's how to dispute a towing charge if you were overcharged](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-if-overcharged/).

## Is It Safe to Drive on a Flat Tire to the Next Exit?

No. Driving on a flat destroys the tire and can bend or crack the wheel, turning a $150 tire problem into a $500-plus wheel problem. It also hurts steering control, especially at highway speed.

The one exception: if you're stopped in a spot where waiting is more dangerous than creeping forward, such as a narrow lane with no shoulder or a blind curve, move slowly with hazards on to the nearest safe spot. A few hundred feet at walking speed is acceptable. A mile at 40 mph is not.

Do not drive a donut into this situation if you don't own one, and if you do, note that [donut spares have strict mileage and speed limits](/how-long-can-you-drive-on-a-spare-donut-tire-before-it-fails/).

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I call a tow truck for just a flat tire?**
A: Yes. A flat you can't change is a legitimate reason for a tow, and many roadside plans cover it. A flatbed can take you to a tire shop.

**Q: Will my insurance pay for a tow if I have no spare?**
A: Only if your policy includes roadside or towing coverage. Basic liability usually doesn't. Check your app or call the number on your insurance card. See [whether liability only insurance covers towing](/does-liability-only-insurance-cover-towing/).

**Q: Can a mobile tire shop come to me on the highway shoulder?**
A: Often yes, but some won't work on busy interstate shoulders for safety reasons. They may ask you to be towed or moved to a safer lot.

**Q: Should I use tire sealant from a can?**
A: Only for a small tread puncture, as a way to reach a shop. It won't fix sidewall damage, and it can complicate a proper repair later.

**Q: How long will I wait for help?**
A: It varies widely with time of day, weather, and location. Highway calls can take under an hour or much longer during peak periods. Stay in the car with hazards on while you wait, and see [real roadside assistance wait times](/roadside-assistance-how-long-do-you-have-to-wait/).
