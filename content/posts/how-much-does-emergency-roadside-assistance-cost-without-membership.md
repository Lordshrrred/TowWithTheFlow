> **Quick Answer:** Without a membership, emergency roadside assistance runs $50 to $150 for a jump start or lockout, $75 to $300 for a tow, and $50 to $100 for fuel delivery or a flat tire change. Prices vary by location, time of day, and service type. You pay the full cost out of pocket, with no discounts or reimbursement unless your auto insurance includes roadside coverage.

## What Does Pay-Per-Use Roadside Assistance Actually Cost?

Every service has its own price range, and there is no flat "roadside call" fee. Here is what you will typically pay in 2026 when you call a provider without any membership:

| Service | Typical Cost (No Membership) |
|---|---|
| Jump start | $50 to $100 |
| Flat tire change (spare to rim) | $50 to $100 |
| Fuel delivery (service only, not gas) | $50 to $100 |
| Lockout / keys locked in car | $50 to $150 |
| Winching / extraction from ditch | $100 to $300+ |
| Local tow (first 5-10 miles) | $75 to $150 |
| Long-distance tow (per mile beyond base) | $2 to $7 per mile |

After-hours calls, typically after 6pm or on weekends, often add $25 to $75 to whatever base rate you are quoted. If you are stuck somewhere rural, expect the higher end of every range above.

For a deeper look at what a tow alone costs when you have no coverage, see [how much a tow truck costs without insurance](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-without-insurance/).

## Can I Get Roadside Assistance Without AAA or a Membership Right Now?

Yes, several options will dispatch someone to you without any prior membership or signup. Here are your real choices:

**Call a local tow company directly.** Search "roadside assistance near me" and call whoever is closest. You pay at the time of service, usually cash or card.

**Use an app-based on-demand service.** Urgently and HONK are two platforms that connect you with local providers on demand. You get an upfront price before confirming. Urgently, for example, shows you the dispatcher's arrival time and final cost before you approve anything, which removes the surprise invoice problem.

**Check your auto insurance policy first.** Many policies from State Farm, Progressive, and GEICO include roadside assistance as an add-on for $5 to $15 per month. If you added it at any point, you likely have coverage right now. Call the number on the back of your insurance card and ask specifically about roadside before paying out of pocket. Progressive's roadside add-on, for instance, reimburses up to a set dollar limit per disablement, not per year.

**Manufacturer programs.** If your car is under the original factory warranty, brands like Hyundai, Kia, Toyota, and BMW include free 24-hour roadside assistance for the duration of that coverage. Check your glove box for the manufacturer's card.

If your breakdown involves something mechanical, like a [serpentine belt that snapped while driving](/serpentine-belt-snapped-while-driving-can-i-tow-or-drive/), knowing whether you actually need a tow or just a quick fix affects which service type you call and what you pay.

## Why Is Pay-Per-Use So Much More Expensive Than a Membership?

You are paying a premium for instant access with no prior commitment. A AAA Classic membership costs roughly $60 to $80 per year and covers multiple service calls. One pay-per-use tow can cost as much as the entire annual membership fee. That math only works in your favor if you break down rarely.

## What Should I Watch Out for When Calling Without a Membership?

Get a price in writing before anyone hooks a chain to your car. That is the single most important thing you can do. Some operators quote a base fee on the phone and then add mileage charges, fuel surcharges, and "after-hours" fees that double the final bill. You have the right to ask for a full, itemized quote before authorizing service.

A few specific things to confirm before you agree:

- The per-mile rate if a tow is involved, not just the hookup fee
- Whether the quoted price covers drop-off at a specific address or just the nearest open shop
- What payment methods they accept, because some smaller operators are cash only

If a tow truck shows up at your breakdown without you calling anyone, be cautious. That situation has its own set of problems worth understanding before you sign anything.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is there a way to get roadside assistance immediately with no membership and no app?**
A: Yes. Call a local tow company directly. Search "roadside assistance" or "tow truck" plus your city and call the first result. Ask for a full price quote before they dispatch. Payment is due when the job is done.

**Q: Does my credit card cover emergency roadside assistance?**
A: Some do. Visa Signature, certain Mastercard World cards, and American Express cards include roadside dispatch as a cardholder benefit. Call the number on the back of your card and ask. Coverage limits vary, typically capping reimbursement at $50 to $100 per service call.

**Q: How much does a jump start cost without AAA?**
A: Expect to pay $50 to $100 for a pay-per-use jump start. After-hours calls can push that to $125. If the battery is genuinely dead in cold weather rather than just weak, a jump may not hold and you could end up paying for a tow on top of it.

**Q: Will my car insurance pay for roadside assistance if I don't have a membership?**
A: Only if you added the roadside assistance rider to your policy. Standard liability or even full coverage does not automatically include it. Call your insurer before calling a tow company so you know whether you have coverage before agreeing to a price.

**Q: Is it worth buying a AAA membership after I'm already broken down?**
A: Not for your current breakdown. Most membership plans have a waiting period of 24 to 72 hours before your first call is covered. Pay out of pocket now, then decide if the annual fee makes sense going forward based on [how a single tow compares to the membership cost](/aaa-membership-worth-it-single-tow-cost-comparison/).
