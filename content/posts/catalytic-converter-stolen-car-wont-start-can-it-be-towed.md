> **Quick Answer:** Yes, your car can and should be towed after a catalytic converter theft. Most cars won't start or run properly without one because the exhaust system is now open and the engine management system may throw fault codes that prevent normal operation. Do not drive it. Call a tow truck and get it to a shop or your insurance company's preferred repair facility.

## Why Won't My Car Start If the Catalytic Converter Was Stolen?

Some cars will crank and run after cat theft, but many won't, and here's the short version of why. The catalytic converter is part of a sealed exhaust system. When it's cut out, the oxygen sensors upstream and downstream of where it sat start reading garbage data. On many modern vehicles, especially those with drive-by-wire throttle systems, the ECU interprets those sensor signals as a critical fault and either limits power severely or prevents the engine from running at all. On top of that, if the thieves nicked a wiring harness or oxygen sensor connector during the cut, that's an additional open circuit the ECU will flag immediately.

## Can I Drive the Car to the Shop Myself or Do I Need a Tow?

Do not drive it, even if it does start. An open exhaust pipe under the hood or mid-chassis dumps raw exhaust fumes, including carbon monoxide, directly into the engine bay and potentially into the cabin through the firewall. Carbon monoxide is colorless and odorless. You won't notice until you're impaired. Beyond the safety issue, running an engine without a catalytic converter can damage the remaining oxygen sensors and the engine itself from abnormal backpressure conditions. You need a tow. If the car won't start at all, you have no choice anyway.

## What Kind of Tow Truck Do I Need for a Stolen Catalytic Converter?

A standard wheel-lift tow is usually fine unless your vehicle has all-wheel drive or the theft caused additional undercarriage damage. If the thieves left cut metal protruding or damaged brake lines or fuel lines in the process, a flatbed is the safer call because nothing on the undercarriage will drag or snag during loading. When you call the tow company, tell them the catalytic converter was cut out and describe what you can see underneath. Let them decide on the equipment. If you're not sure whether you need a flatbed, [this breakdown of when your car needs flatbed towing](/how-do-i-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/) covers it in detail.

## What Do I Do Right Now at the Scene?

First, call the police and file a theft report. You need that report number for your insurance claim. Without it, most comprehensive insurance policies will not cover the repair, which typically runs $1,000 to $3,000 depending on the vehicle. ## How Long Will I Be Without My Car?

Realistically, one to three weeks, sometimes longer. Replacement catalytic converters for common targets like the Prius or Honda CRV are often backordered because demand from theft replacement surges with theft rates. An aftermarket cat can be installed faster than an OEM unit. Ask your shop about both options and whether your insurance policy requires OEM parts. Labor time for the replacement itself is usually two to four hours. The delay is almost always parts availability, not labor.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will my car start at all without a catalytic converter?**
A: Some vehicles will start and run rough, others will not start at all due to ECU fault codes triggered by missing or damaged oxygen sensors. Either way, do not drive it.

**Q: Does insurance cover a stolen catalytic converter?**
A: Yes, if you have comprehensive coverage. File a police report first because insurers require the report number to process the claim. Your deductible will apply.

**Q: How much does it cost to replace a stolen catalytic converter?**
A: Parts and labor combined typically run $1,000 to $3,000. Vehicles with high-value converters like the Toyota Prius or Ford F-250 can run higher. Get at least two shop quotes.

**Q: Can I get a tow truck even if my car is in a parking lot or garage?**
A: Yes. Tow companies handle parking lots and garages routinely. If the car won't roll at all because the theft damaged a brake line or the wheels are locked, let the dispatcher know so they bring the right equipment.

**Q: What can I do to prevent this from happening again?**
A: A catalytic converter shield (a steel plate bolted over the converter) is the most effective deterrent. Brands like Cat Security and MillerCAT make model-specific shields. Installation typically costs $200 to $400 at a shop. It won't make theft impossible, but most thieves move on to easier targets.
