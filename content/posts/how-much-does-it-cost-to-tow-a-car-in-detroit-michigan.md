## How Much Does a Tow Cost in Detroit?

A standard local tow in Detroit runs $85 to $175 for the hook-up fee and the first 5 to 10 miles. Beyond that, expect $4 to $7 per additional mile. These are typical ranges, not guarantees, and prices vary by company and conditions.

Here is how it usually breaks down:

| Service | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Hook-up and short local tow | $85 to $175 |
| Extra mileage | $4 to $7 per mile |
| Flatbed surcharge | $25 to $75 extra |
| After-hours or holiday | $30 to $100 extra |
| Winch-out from ditch or snowbank | $75 to $200 |

## What Makes a Detroit Tow More Expensive?

Four things move the price most: distance, truck type, timing, and recovery difficulty. Detroit adds a few local twists.

**Distance.** A tow from I-94 near the Southfield Freeway to a shop in Dearborn is a short haul. A tow from the I-75 and I-696 interchange in Hazel Park out to a dealer in Troy adds real mileage.

**Flatbed vs. wheel-lift.** All-wheel-drive vehicles, low sports cars, and anything with transmission or drivetrain trouble need a flatbed. Dragging an AWD car on two wheels can wreck the center differential. Our guide on [how to know if your car needs a flatbed](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/) covers the quick checks.

**Timing.** Friday rush on the Lodge Freeway (M-10) or a 2 a.m. call costs more than a Tuesday morning call. Winter is the big one. After a lake-effect snow or a freeze-thaw cycle that opens potholes on the Chrysler Freeway (I-75), every truck in the metro is busy and dispatchers charge accordingly.

**Recovery.** If your car slid off the shoulder on I-94 and needs a winch, that is a separate line item from the tow itself.

## What Do I Do Right Now If My Car Breaks Down in Detroit?

Get the car as far right as you safely can, turn on your hazards, and call for help before you start comparing prices. On a freeway like I-94, I-75, or the Lodge, stay in the car with your seatbelt on if you cannot reach a safe shoulder spot. Standing outside next to 70 mph traffic is the bigger danger.

1. Call 911 if you are in a live lane, hurt, or smell fuel or smoke.
2. Check whether you have free towing: your insurance app, a credit card benefit, your car's manufacturer roadside plan, or AAA.
3. If you are paying out of pocket, call two companies if you can and ask for a total price, not just the hook-up fee.
4. Confirm the truck type (flatbed or wheel-lift), the destination, and the arrival time.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged on a Detroit Tow?

Ask for the all-in price on the phone and get it repeated before the driver 
![tow truck loading car](/images/how-much-does-it-cost-to-tow-a-car-in-detroit-michigan/mid.jpg)
*Photo: Pexels*
hooks up. The classic problem is a low quoted hook-up fee that balloons with mileage, "after-hours" fees, and storage charges.

Ask these exact questions:
- What is the total price to [your shop or address]?
- Is that a flatbed, and is the flatbed fee included?
- Are there any fees on top of that quote?
- What is the payment method? (Many drivers want card or cash, so know before they arrive.)

Be wary of any truck that shows up unrequested at a freeway breakdown. Unsolicited "courtesy" tows can come with inflated rates and a pull toward a specific storage lot. If you do get hit with a bill that does not match the quote, here is [how to dispute a towing charge](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-if-overcharged/).

Take photos of your car before it is hooked up. If something gets scratched, you will want the before-and-after proof.

## What If I Don't Have Insurance or Roadside Coverage?

Without coverage, you pay the full cash price, so the quote-first approach matters even more. Expect the higher end of the range if it is night or winter. Some Detroit-area tow operators accept card payment at the scene, others want cash, so ask when you call.

Practical ways to cut cost:
- Tow to a shop within a few miles instead of your preferred mechanic across town. You can arrange a second move later or have the shop quote the repair and move the car.
- Skip the flatbed if the car is front-wheel drive and has no drivetrain damage.
- Avoid peak snow-event hours if the car is safe where it is and you are not in traffic.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a flatbed tow in Detroit?**
A: Most flatbed tows in Detroit cost $25 to $75 more than a standard wheel-lift tow, so figure $110 to $250 for a short local haul. AWD and low-clearance cars should always go on a flatbed.

**Q: How much does it cost to tow a car from Detroit to Ann Arbor?**
A: Ann Arbor is roughly 40 miles from Detroit, so a straight tow can run $200 to $400 depending on the company, truck type, and time of day. Always get a flat, all-in quote for long hauls.

**Q: Does my insurance pay for towing in Michigan?**
A: Only if you added roadside assistance or towing coverage to your policy. Many policies include a set mileage limit or dollar cap, so check your app or call your agent. Our article on [how far insurance will tow your car](/how-far-will-insurance-tow-my-car-after-a-breakdown/) explains typical limits.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow truck directly or go through insurance?**
A: If your plan includes towing, it is almost always cheaper to go through insurance or AAA since the cost is covered up to your limit. If you do not have coverage, calling a local operator directly and getting a quote works.

**Q: How long does it take a tow truck to arrive in Detroit?**
A: Expect 30 to 60 minutes in normal conditions and 1 to 3 hours during a snowstorm or heavy rush hour. Ask the dispatcher for a realistic arrival time before you confirm.
