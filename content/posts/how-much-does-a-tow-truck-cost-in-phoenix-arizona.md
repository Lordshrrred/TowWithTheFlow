> **Quick Answer:** A standard tow in Phoenix typically costs $75 to $150 for the hookup, plus roughly $4 to $7 per mile after the first few miles. Most local tows land between $100 and $250 total. Flatbeds, nights, weekends, and freeway pickups on the I-10 or Loop 101 cost more. Get the full price quoted before the driver hooks up.

## How Much Does a Tow Truck Cost in Phoenix Right Now?

Expect $100 to $250 for a typical in-town tow in Phoenix, built from a hookup fee plus mileage. Prices vary by company, so treat these as ballpark ranges, not guarantees.

- **Hookup fee:** $75 to $150, often covering the first 5 to 10 miles
- **Per-mile rate after that:** about $4 to $7
- **Flatbed surcharge:** often $25 to $75 extra
- **After-hours or weekend surcharge:** commonly $30 to $100 extra

A 15-mile tow from a Tempe parking lot to a shop in Glendale could land around $150 to $225 on a weekday afternoon. The same tow at 2 a.m. can run noticeably higher.

For a deeper breakdown, see [towing cost in Phoenix Arizona](/towing-cost-phoenix-arizona/) and the [per-mile rate guide](/towing-cost-in-phoenix-arizona-per-mile/).

## What Makes a Phoenix Tow Cost More?

Distance, truck type, time of day, and how hard your car is to reach drive the final price. Phoenix is spread out, so mileage adds up fast.

**Distance.** Phoenix sprawls. A breakdown in Ahwatukee headed to a shop in north Scottsdale can easily be 25-plus miles.

**Truck type.** All-wheel-drive vehicles, lowered cars, and many newer automatics should go on a flatbed to avoid drivetrain damage. Flatbeds cost more. If you're unsure, check your owner's manual or tell the dispatcher your drivetrain. See [flatbed towing cost in Phoenix](/towing-cost-in-phoenix-arizona-flatbed/).

**Timing.** Nights, weekends, and holidays carry surcharges. Details are in the [after-hours towing guide](/towing-cost-phoenix-arizona-after-hours/).

**Location.** A car stuck on the shoulder of the I-17 near the Black Canyon Freeway interchange is harder and riskier to hook than one in an easy parking lot. Some companies add a freeway or recovery fee.

**Recovery work.** If you're in a ditch, sand, or off-road, you're paying for winching, not just towing.

## What Should I Ask Before the Driver Hooks Up My Car?

Ask for the total price, in writing if possible, before the truck touches your car. That one habit prevents most overcharging.

Ask these exact questions:

1. What's the total, including hookup, mileage, and any surcharges?
2. Is it a flatbed or a hook-and-chain wheel-lift?
3. Do you accept card, or is it cash only?
4. How long until you arrive?

Be cautious of unsolicited tow trucks that show up at a breakdown or accident scene without being called. Cash-only demands and refusal to quote a number are red flags. Call the company yourself, or use your insurer or AAA, so you know who is coming.

## Can I Get a Cheaper Tow in Phoenix?

Yes. Roadside coverage you already pay for is the biggest saver, and shorter distances cost less. Check these before you pay out of pocket:

- **Your auto insurance:** many policies include roadside add-ons covering a tow for a few dollars a month
- **AAA:** standard membership typically covers a set number of miles
- **Credit card or new-car warranty:** some include towing benefits
- **Manufacturer roadside:** often free for newer vehicles

If you have none of these, tow to the nearest competent shop, not your favorite one across town. You can move the car again later, or have the shop handle it. Breakdowns without coverage are covered in [towing cost in Phoenix with no insurance](/towing-cost-in-phoenix-arizona-no-insurance/).

## What Should I Do While Waiting for the Tow?

Get safe first, then call. On a Phoenix freeway, pull as far onto the right shoulder as possible, turn on your hazards, and keep your seatbelt on if traffic is close.

In summer, Phoenix heat is a real danger. Cabin temperatures can climb to dangerous levels within minutes. Stay out of the car only if you are well clear of traffic, and use shade and water. If you're stuck near an overpass or sound wall, move to the shaded side away from the roadway. Call 911 if anyone is overheating or you feel unsafe.

If heat caused the breakdown, read [what to do when your car overheats on a Phoenix highway](/car-overheating-on-highway-in-phoenix-arizona-tow-cost/). Use ADOT's AZ511 service by name for road conditions and closures.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow per mile in Phoenix?**
A: Most companies charge about $4 to $7 per mile after the included hookup distance. Some include the first 5 to 10 miles in the base fee, so ask how it's structured.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow truck myself or use insurance?**
A: Using roadside coverage is almost always cheaper, since many policies cover the tow outright or reimburse it. Check your policy or app first, then call a private company only if you have no coverage.

**Q: How much does a tow cost at night in Phoenix?**
A: Expect an after-hours surcharge, often $30 to $100 on top of the normal rate. Confirm the total with the dispatcher before the truck is sent.

**Q: Do I need a flatbed tow?**
A: Yes for all-wheel-drive, four-wheel-drive, lowered, or severely damaged vehicles, and often for newer automatics. Wheel-lift tows can damage drivetrains in those cases.

**Q: Will a Phoenix tow company accept a card?**
A: Many do, but some are cash-only. Ask when you call so you aren't stuck at the shop with a bill you can't pay.
