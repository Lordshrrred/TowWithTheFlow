> **Quick Answer:** A standard local tow in Indianapolis runs $75 to $125 for the hookup plus $3 to $5 per mile. After hours, nights, weekends, and holidays typically add a $25 to $75 surcharge on top of that base rate. A 10-mile tow at 2 a.m. will likely cost you $130 to $225 out of pocket depending on the company. Call at least two operators before agreeing to anything.

## What To Do

1. **Get off the road safely first.** If you're on I-65, I-70, or the I-465 loop, move as far right as possible and turn on your hazards. Don't stay in a travel lane waiting for a callback from a tow company.

2. **Call two or three towing companies and get a quote upfront.** Indianapolis has a competitive towing market. Companies like All American Towing, Bob's Towing, and several independent operators work around the clock. Ask for the hookup fee, the per-mile rate, and whether there is an after-hours or night surcharge. Get a number before they dispatch.

3. **Know the after-hours window.** Most Indianapolis tow companies define "after hours" as anything between 8 p.m. and 7 a.m., plus all day Sunday and federal holidays. Some start the surcharge at 6 p.m. on Fridays. Always ask.

4. **Check your insurance or roadside plan before you pay cash.** GEICO, State Farm, Progressive, and AAA all cover towing in Indiana. If you have coverage, the company may dispatch directly or reimburse you later. [GEICO roadside assistance towing limits](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-limits-how-many-miles/) cap at a set mileage, so confirm your distance is covered before they roll.

5. **Tell the dispatcher exactly where you are.** Use a cross street or a mile marker. Vague location descriptions slow response and can add mileage charges if the driver has to search for you.

6. **Confirm the destination before the truck moves.** Changing drop locations mid-tow can double your bill. Know which shop you're going to before you authorize the tow. If your regular shop is closed, most repair shops in Indy will let you drop in their lot overnight.

7. **Ask for a receipt that itemizes hookup fee, mileage, and any surcharges.** You need this for insurance reimbursement and in case you want to dispute the charge.

These are private pay estimates. If you're being towed from a police-dispatched call or an accident scene, [towing costs at accident scenes](/towing-cost-accident-scene-police-report-required/) operate under different rules and can be significantly higher.

If you're on a tight budget and can wait until morning, a daytime tow to the same destination will almost always be cheaper. That said, leaving your car overnight on a highway shoulder is not a safe option. A parking lot or a side street is a better holding spot than I-70 at 2 a.m.

## Stay Safe

- Stay behind the guardrail if you're on a highway. Do not stand between your car and moving traffic.
- Keep your hazard lights on the entire time, even if it drains the battery.
- If it's late and you're in an unfamiliar area, stay inside the locked car until the tow truck arrives.
- Do not accept a ride from a stranger who stops. Wait for the dispatched driver who can confirm the company name.
- If you're on I-465 or a INDOT-managed road, INDOT's Highway Helpers patrol during high-traffic hours and can sometimes assist for free or call a tow for you.

## Common Questions

**Q: Do Indianapolis tow companies charge more on Sundays even during the day?**
A: Yes, most Indianapolis tow operators treat all day Sunday as an after-hours period, so you will typically pay the same surcharge on a Sunday afternoon as you would at midnight on a weekday. Always confirm this when you call for a quote.

**Q: Can I refuse to pay the after-hours surcharge if no one told me about it before the truck arrived?**
A: You are within your rights to dispute any charge that was not disclosed before dispatch. This is why you should ask for the full quote including surcharges before authorizing the tow, and request an itemized receipt when it is complete. If a company refuses to itemize, you can file a complaint with the Indiana Attorney General's consumer protection office.

**Q: How long does a tow truck usually take to arrive in Indianapolis at night?**
A: Wait times in Indianapolis after hours typically range from 30 to 60 minutes, though high-demand periods like bad weather or Friday nights can push that to 90 minutes or more. Calling multiple companies at once and taking the fastest confirmed arrival is the best way to reduce your wait.
