## Who Do I Actually Call When My Tire Blows Out and the Car Won't Move?

Call roadside assistance first if you have it: AAA, State Farm, GEICO, Progressive, or whatever is on your insurance card. If you have no coverage, call a local tow truck directly by searching your location plus "tow truck" right now. 911 is the right call if you are in a dangerous spot, such as a live lane, a blind curve, or a tunnel, and cannot safely move to the shoulder.

Here is the priority order:

1. **911** if you or anyone is injured, or if the car is blocking traffic with no way to move it.
2. **Roadside assistance** (AAA, insurance, credit card benefit) if you have it.
3. **A local tow company** if you have no membership or policy coverage.
4. **511** on most state highway systems will connect you to a traffic management center that can send a highway patrol unit or DOT road-assist truck.

A destroyed tire, especially one that shredded the sidewall or took rim damage with it, will not roll safely to an exit. You need a flatbed or at minimum a wheel-lift tow. Do not let anyone talk you into driving on a blown rim.

## Is It Safe to Stay in the Car While I Wait for the Tow?

No. Get out of the car and move behind the nearest guardrail or as far from traffic as possible. Freeway shoulder accidents are one of the most dangerous situations a driver faces: a distracted driver drifting right at 65 mph will hit a stopped car before they react. Turn on your hazard lights the moment you stop, then get out on the passenger side if possible so you exit away from traffic. If you have emergency triangles or flares, place them at least 200 feet behind the vehicle.

Do not sit in the driver's seat waiting. Do not stand between the car and traffic. For more detail on keeping yourself protected while you wait, see [how to stay safe waiting for a tow truck on the highway](/how-to-stay-safe-waiting-for-tow-truck-on-highway/).

## Do I Need a Tow or Can Someone Just Bring Me a Spare?

You need a tow if any of these are true: the rim is bent or cracked, the tire shredded completely, the wheel well or suspension took damage from the blowout, or you have no spare. A blowout at freeway speed is not the same as a simple flat. The force of a blowout routinely destroys the rim and can bend suspension components, particularly on front tires. A new spare mounted on a bent rim is not safe to drive.

If the rim looks intact and the tire just deflated without major shredding, a roadside tire change is possible. But honest answer: if the car won't move at all, the rim is almost certainly compromised. ## What Should I Tell the Tow Dispatcher When I Call?

Tell them your exact location, the direction you were traveling, and what happened. Dispatchers need a mile marker, exit number, or cross street to find you fast. "I'm on I-80 westbound just past exit 47" is useful. "I'm on the highway somewhere near the city" is not.

Also tell them: the car won't move, you believe the rim may be damaged, and you want a flatbed if possible. This prevents a wheel-lift truck showing up that cannot safely handle a vehicle with a collapsed corner. Give them your phone number so the driver can call you when close.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I change a blown-out tire myself on the freeway shoulder?**
A: Only if the rim is fully intact, you have a full-size spare, and you are completely off the roadway on a stable surface. On a narrow freeway shoulder with traffic passing, the risk of being hit is real. If there is any doubt about the rim or the shoulder is tight, wait for a tow.

**Q: Will my car insurance pay for this tow?**
A: It depends on your policy. If you added roadside assistance or towing coverage, yes, usually up to a set dollar limit per incident. Liability-only policies do not include towing. Check your declarations page or call your insurer's claims line right now while you wait.

**Q: What if I have no phone signal to call anyone?**
A: Turn on your hazards, raise your hood, and tie something bright to your antenna or door handle. Stay visible and away from traffic. Highway patrol units actively look for stopped vehicles, and many states have DOT road-assist trucks that patrol major freeways during peak hours. For more options, see [how to signal for help if your car breaks down and you have no phone](/how-to-signal-for-help-car-breaks-down-no-phone/).

**Q: How long will I wait for a tow truck on the freeway?**
A: Average wait times run 30 to 60 minutes for a local tow in most metro areas. Rural stretches and peak traffic hours can push that to 90 minutes or more. Get your call in immediately and ask for an estimated arrival time so you know what you are dealing with.

**Q: The blowout also damaged my fender and wheel well. Does insurance cover that?**
A: Collision coverage will cover vehicle damage caused by a tire blowout if you hit something, like a guardrail. Comprehensive may cover it if road debris caused the blowout. A straight blowout with no collision is trickier and often falls on you unless you have specific tire and wheel protection added to your policy. Document all damage with photos before the tow.
