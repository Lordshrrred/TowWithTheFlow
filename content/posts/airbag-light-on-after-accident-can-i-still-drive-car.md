> **Quick Answer:** Don't treat it as a normal warning light. After a crash, a lit airbag light usually means the system is disabled, so your airbags may not deploy in a second collision. If the car also has frame damage, a deployed airbag, a seatbelt that won't lock, or any steering or brake problem, get it towed. If the car drives straight and normally, drive it directly to a shop, not around town.

## Is It Safe to Drive With the Airbag Light On After an Accident?

Not safe in the way you'd want it to be. The light means the SRS (Supplemental Restraint System) found a fault and has likely shut the airbags off. You can physically drive the car, but you've lost your main crash protection.

After an accident, the light often comes on for real reasons, not a glitch. Common causes:

- A crash sensor, often in the front bumper area or door, was damaged or knocked loose.
- The seatbelt pretensioner fired. These tighten the belt in a hit and are single-use.
- Wiring under the seat was pinched or pulled. Many cars run SRS connectors under the front seats.
- The airbag control module logged a crash event and is holding a fault code.

If you're only driving short and cautiously to get off the road or reach a repair shop, the risk is limited. As a daily driver, it isn't.

## When Do I Need a Tow Instead of Driving It?

Tow the car if any of these are true: an airbag already deployed, the hood or doors don't close properly, a tire is rubbing or leaning, fluid is dripping, or the steering pulls or feels loose.

A deployed airbag by itself is a tow situation. The car has taken a real hit, and what you can't see (bent suspension, cracked subframe, damaged brake lines) is what hurts you at 50 mph.

Check these before you decide:

1. Walk around the car and look for bent wheels, a leaning stance, or a fender touching a tire.
2. Look underneath for fresh fluid.
3. Test the brakes gently in a parking lot. A soft or sinking pedal means no driving. See [what to do if the brake pedal goes to the floor](/brake-pedal-went-to-floor-while-driving-what-to-do/).
4. Pull each seatbelt hard. If one won't lock or stays slack, the pretensioner or retractor is compromised.
5. Check for other lights, especially ABS, battery, or check engine. Multiple lights after a hit point to bigger electrical or sensor damage.

If you're on a busy road and can't tell, call for a tow. A flatbed costs far less than a second accident in a car with no airbags.

## Will My Insurance Cover the Tow and the Airbag Repair?

Often yes, but it depends on fault and your coverage. Collision coverage typically pays for repair of your own car regardless of fault, minus your deductible, and many policies include towing from the accident scene.

## How Much Does It Cost to Fix an Airbag Light After a Crash?

It ranges from under $200 to well over $1,500, depending on what failed. A loose connector or one bad sensor is on the cheap end. Replacing a deployed airbag, the control module, and pretensioners is the expensive end.

A scan tool reads the specific SRS fault code, which tells the shop what failed. Many general-purpose code readers can't read SRS codes, so ask whether the shop uses a scanner that covers airbag systems.

Be cautious about a quick "clear the code" fix. If the underlying sensor or module is damaged, the light returns and the system stays unreliable. Insist on a diagnosis, not a reset.

## Can I Drive to the Shop Myself, and How Should I Do It?

Yes, if the car passed the checks above, and you should go straight there in daylight at moderate speeds. Skip highways if you can, keep your following distance long, and make sure every passenger is belted. Don't carry kids in the front seat.

Tell the shop upfront that it was in an accident and that the SRS light is on, so they scan for crash data and inspect the pretensioners and sensors, not just the airbags. If something feels wrong on the way (pulling, vibration, new noises), pull over and call a tow. ## Common Questions

**Q: Will my airbags deploy if the airbag light is on?**
A: Probably not. The light usually means the system detected a fault and disabled the airbags, so assume they won't fire in another crash until a shop fixes it.

**Q: Why did the airbag light come on right after my accident?**
A: A crash sensor, seatbelt pretensioner, wiring connector, or the control module was likely damaged or triggered. The system logs a fault and lights up to warn you.

**Q: Can I just disconnect the battery to reset the airbag light?**
A: No. It may clear the light briefly, but if the damage is real the light comes back, and you've hidden a safety problem. Also, work near airbag connectors can be dangerous without proper procedure.

**Q: Is the airbag light the same as the "passenger airbag off" light?**
A: No. The passenger airbag off indicator reflects the occupant sensor and seat load, which is normal. A steady or flashing SRS or airbag warning light on the dash is the fault light you need to address. For a similar light with no crash involved, see [airbag light came on while driving](/airbag-light-came-on-while-driving-is-it-safe/).

**Q: Do I need to tell my insurance about the airbag light?**
A: Yes, if you're filing a claim. Document it with photos and tell the adjuster, since SRS repair can be a significant part of the repair estimate.
