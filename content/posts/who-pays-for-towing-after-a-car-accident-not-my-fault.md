> **Quick Answer:** The at-fault driver's liability insurance is ultimately responsible for your tow. But you will often pay the tow company or storage yard first, then get reimbursed. Your own collision or roadside coverage can pay upfront and recover the cost from the other insurer. Keep every receipt and the police report number.

## Who Actually Pays for the Tow When the Accident Wasn't My Fault?

The at-fault driver's liability insurance owes you the cost of towing and storage as part of your property damage claim. In practice, that money rarely arrives before the tow truck needs payment.

The tow driver wants payment or authorization at the scene or when the car is released. The other insurer may take days to accept responsibility, especially if fault is disputed. So the real question is who fronts the money. Your options:

- **The at-fault driver's insurer:** Open a claim with them and ask for the tow to be direct-billed. Some will, many won't until fault is confirmed.
- **Your own insurance:** If you have collision coverage or roadside assistance, use it. Your insurer pays, then pursues the other carrier through subrogation. Your deductible should be refunded if they recover.
- **You, then reimbursement:** Pay out of pocket, keep the itemized receipt, and submit it with your claim.

## What Should I Do at the Scene Before the Tow Truck Takes My Car?

Document everything before your car moves, because once it's on a hook or in a yard, you lose leverage and evidence.

1. Get the police report number. Without it, the other insurer has an easy reason to stall.
2. Photograph all vehicles, plates, damage, and the road position.
3. Exchange insurance information and take a photo of the other driver's insurance card.
4. Ask the officer whether the tow was police-ordered. A police-initiated tow is usually to a designated yard, and storage fees start accruing fast.
5. Write down the tow company's name and the yard location before you leave.

If you're on a freeway shoulder, safety comes first. Our guide on [how long you can wait for a tow truck on the highway](/how-long-can-you-wait-for-a-tow-truck-on-highway/) covers when to get out of the car and away from traffic.

## Will My Own Insurance or AAA Cover the Tow If the Other Driver Caused It?

Possibly, and it depends on the policy, not on who was at fault. Roadside assistance plans often treat an accident differently from a breakdown, and many cap the towing distance.

Collision coverage typically includes reasonable towing from the scene as part of the claim. Standalone roadside add-ons vary. Before you assume anything, read how [AAA handles towing after an accident versus a breakdown](/does-aaa-cover-towing-cost-after-accident-or-only-breakdowns/) and check your policy's mileage limit, since [how far insurance will tow your car](/how-far-will-insurance-tow-my-car-after-a-breakdown/) is where many drivers get surprised.

One practical tip: call your own insurer even though you weren't at fault. Their claims rep can authorize the tow and move the car

## How Much Will Towing and Storage Cost, and Who Covers the Daily Fees?

Expect a few hundred dollars for an accident tow, and storage fees that climb daily, so get the car out of the yard fast. Accident tows often cost more than a standard breakdown tow because of recovery work, such as winching a car out of a ditch or clearing debris. Flatbed requirements push the price up too.

Storage is where the bill balloons. Yards charge per day, and the clock starts the moment your car arrives, including weekends. The at-fault insurer generally owes reasonable storage, but "reasonable" ends when you stall. If your car sits for a week because you didn't move it, the other carrier can argue the extra days were your delay.

## What If the Other Driver Is Uninsured or Won't Admit Fault?

Your own policy becomes the safety net. Uninsured motorist property damage coverage, where your state offers it, or your collision coverage can pay for the tow and repair. You then pursue the other driver, or let your insurer do it.

If fault is disputed, don't wait for a ruling before moving your car. Authorize the tow through your own insurer or pay it yourself, keep the receipt, and add it to the claim. Fighting over fault is slow. Storage fees are not.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can the tow company hold my car until I pay?**
A: Yes. Tow yards can generally keep a vehicle until fees are paid, which is why storage grows quickly. Ask your insurer to send a letter of guarantee, or pay and get reimbursed.

**Q: Do I have to use the tow company the police call?**
A: If police ordered the tow for safety or because the car is blocking traffic, usually yes. If you're able to choose, call your insurer first and use their approved provider.

**Q: Will the other driver's insurance pay me back for towing?**
A: Yes, if their driver is found at fault. Submit the itemized tow and storage receipts with your claim and reference the police report number.

**Q: Does my rate go up if I use my insurance for a not-at-fault accident?**
A: Rate increases for not-at-fault claims are less common and vary by state and insurer. Ask your agent before filing if it's a concern.

**Q: What if my airbag light is on and the car looks drivable?**
A: Don't drive it. Airbag and sensor faults mean the safety system may not work as designed. See [airbag light on after an accident](/airbag-light-on-after-accident-can-i-still-drive-car/) and tow it instead.
