> **Quick Answer:** A standard tow in Columbus usually costs $75 to $150 for the hook-up and first few miles, then roughly $3 to $7 for each additional mile. Nights, weekends, snow, and flatbeds push the total higher. Always get the full price quoted before the driver hooks up, and ask if it is an all-in number.

## How Much Does a Tow Cost in Columbus Right Now?

Expect to pay $75 to $150 for a short local tow in Columbus, plus per-mile charges once you pass the included distance. A five-mile tow from a breakdown near the Short North to a shop in Clintonville often lands in the low-to-mid $100s. A 20-mile tow from the far east side out past Reynoldsburg can reach $200 or more.

Here's how the pieces stack up:

- **Hook-up fee:** $75 to $150, often with a few miles included
- **Per-mile rate:** roughly $3 to $7 after the included miles
- **Flatbed surcharge:** often $25 to $75 extra
- **After-hours or holiday fee:** commonly $25 to $100
- **Winch-out or recovery:** $75 to $250 depending on how stuck you are

## What Makes a Columbus Tow Cost More Than Quoted?

Most surprises come from distance, vehicle type, and timing. Tell the dispatcher your exact location and destination up front so the quote matches the real job.

Common price jumps in Columbus:

- **Highway pickups.** A breakdown on I-70 near the I-71 split downtown or on the I-270 outerbelt can cost more than a side-street pickup because of the safety setup and traffic.
- **AWD and EVs.** All-wheel-drive vehicles, many electric cars, and low-clearance cars need a flatbed. Dragging them on a wheel-lift can damage drivetrains.
- **Winter weather.** Lake-effect snow and ice storms spike demand. Wait times and prices both climb.
- **Nights and weekends.** After-hours rates are real. Ask for them by name.

One tip that saves money: ask, "Is that the total including mileage and fees?" Then ask them to repeat the number. A vague "it depends" at the start usually means a bigger bill at the end.

## Can I Pick the Tow Company, or Will Someone Pick It for Me?

You can pick your own tow company unless police order a specific one after an accident. If you break down on a freeway like I-71 or SR-315 and a trooper or officer arrives, the tow may be a rotation call. Ask whether you have the right to choose, and where the car is being taken.

## What Should I Check Before Calling a Tow Truck?

Check whether you already have free towing through your insurance, your car's warranty, or a roadside plan. Many policies include a tow of a few miles or up to a set dollar amount. Credit cards and new-car warranties sometimes cover it too.

Look for these in order:

- Your auto insurance app or card (roadside add-on)
- Manufacturer roadside assistance, usually in the owner's manual
- AAA or another membership
- Credit card benefits

## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow truck in Columbus per mile?**
A: Most Columbus companies charge roughly $3 to $7 per mile after the included distance. Some bundle the first few miles into the hook-up fee, so ask what is included before you agree.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive in Columbus?**
A: Usually yes, by about $25 to $75 over a standard wheel-lift tow. It's worth it for AWD cars, EVs, lowered vehicles, and anything with transmission trouble.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive in Columbus?**
A: Typically 20 to 45 minutes in normal conditions. Rush hour on I-270 or a winter storm can stretch that to an hour or more.

**Q: Do I have to tow my car to a repair shop?**
A: No. You can have it towed to your home, a trusted mechanic, or a storage lot. Towing to your driveway often saves money if the shop is far away.

**Q: Should I pay the tow driver in cash?**
A: Ask for a receipt either way. Cash is fine, but a card payment gives you a record if there's a billing dispute later.
