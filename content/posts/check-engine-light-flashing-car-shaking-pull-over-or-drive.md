> **Quick Answer:** Pull over and shut the engine off. A flashing check engine light with shaking means an active engine misfire, and unburned fuel is flowing into your catalytic converter, which can overheat and cause damage within minutes. Do not drive it home. Only move the car if you need to get to a safe spot, and call a tow.

## Should I Pull Over or Keep Driving With a Flashing Check Engine Light?

Pull over. A steady check engine light means "get this checked soon." A flashing one means "stop now." The flash is your car's computer telling you the misfire is severe enough to damage hardware.

When a cylinder misfires, raw fuel passes through the exhaust and burns inside the catalytic converter. Converters normally run hot, and that extra burning can push them past what the internal ceramic core can handle. A melted core can cost $1,000 to $2,500 or more to replace on many cars, and it can clog the exhaust badly enough to stall the engine.

If you're in traffic, signal, ease off the gas gently, and get to the shoulder or a parking lot. Don't brake hard or swerve. Shut the engine off once you're stopped.

## What Do I Do Right After I Pull Over?

Turn off the engine, turn on your hazards, and get everyone out of the car if you're on a highway shoulder. Stand well away from traffic, behind the guardrail if there is one.

Then work through this:

1. **Check for smoke or a burning smell.** If you see smoke from under the hood or smell raw fuel, stay clear of the car and call 911.
2. **Look under the car.** A puddle of fuel or a glowing-hot exhaust section means do not restart it.
3. **Wait a few minutes before touching anything.** The exhaust, especially around the catalytic converter under the middle of the car, can be hot enough to burn you or ignite dry grass.
4. **Call for a tow.** Don't gamble on one more restart.

If you're stuck on a freeway shoulder, read [how long you can wait on the side of the road before police tow your car](/how-long-can-you-wait-on-side-of-road-before-police-tow-your-car/), and don't sit inside the vehicle with traffic passing close.

## Why Is My Car Shaking With the Check Engine Light Flashing?

The shaking is the misfire itself. One or more cylinders aren't firing properly, so the engine runs unevenly and the whole car vibrates, usually worst at idle or under acceleration.

The common causes, roughly in order of likelihood:

- **Worn spark plugs or a failed ignition coil.** This is the most frequent culprit, especially on cars past 80,000 miles. A single bad coil-on-plug unit can trigger a flashing light.
- **A failed fuel injector** that isn't delivering fuel to one cylinder.
- **A vacuum leak** from a cracked intake hose.
- **Low compression** from a worn valve or, in worse cases, a [blown head gasket](/blown-head-gasket-symptoms-should-i-tow-or-drive/).

## Can I Drive a Short Distance to a Safe Spot or Shop?

Only to get out of danger, such as off a travel lane onto a shoulder. Driving a mile or two to a repair shop with an active misfire is a gamble, and the converter damage is what you're gambling with.

The exception is a light that flashes briefly and then goes solid, with the shaking gone. That can happen when you let off the gas, since misfires often occur under load. It's still a warning. Drive gently, avoid hard acceleration, and get to a shop soon, but if the flashing or shaking returns, pull over again.

If you're unsure whether the situation is safe to continue, the same logic applies to [an engine light that comes on while on the highway](/engine-light-came-on-highway-is-it-safe-to-keep-driving/). Steady light, drive carefully to a shop. Flashing light, stop.

A tow is cheap next to a ruined catalytic converter. Local tows typically run roughly $75 to $200 depending on the market, distance, and time of day. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive with a flashing check engine light if the car feels fine?**
A: No. The flash means a severe misfire is happening or has happened, even if you don't feel it at that moment. The converter can still be taking damage while the car seems smooth.

**Q: What's the difference between a flashing and a solid check engine light?**
A: Solid means a fault is stored and needs attention soon. Flashing means an active, damaging misfire right now. Treat solid as "schedule a visit" and flashing as "stop and tow."

**Q: Will the flashing light go away if I turn the car off and back on?**
A: Sometimes it clears for a few minutes, but the misfire is still there. A restart doesn't fix the cause, and the stored code stays in the computer for the shop to read.

**Q: Can bad gas cause a flashing check engine light and shaking?**
A: Yes. Water-contaminated or very low-quality fuel can cause misfires, especially right after filling up. Still, don't keep driving to find out. Have it towed and let a shop confirm.

**Q: How long can I drive with a flashing check engine light?**
A: There's no safe distance. Catalytic converter damage can begin within minutes of a heavy misfire. The only safe move is to stop, shut the engine off, and arrange a tow.
