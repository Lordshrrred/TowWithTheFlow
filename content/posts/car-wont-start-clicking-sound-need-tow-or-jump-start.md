> **Quick Answer:** A rapid clicking sound when you turn the key almost always means a dead or weak battery. A single loud click usually points to a bad starter solenoid. In most cases, try a jump start first. If the car starts and runs, you likely just need a new battery. If it won't jump or dies again immediately, call a tow.

## What Does the Clicking Sound Actually Mean?

Rapid clicking, sometimes described as a machine-gun sound, means the battery has enough charge to trigger the starter relay repeatedly but not enough to actually crank the engine. One single heavy click usually means the starter solenoid is engaging but the starter motor itself is seized or dead. These are two different problems with two different fixes, and knowing which one you have saves you time and money right now.

The rapid-click pattern is almost always a battery or connection issue. The single-click pattern is more likely a starter motor failure, and that one typically requires a tow.

## Can I Jump Start It or Do I Need a Tow?

Try the jump start first if you are getting rapid clicking. You need another vehicle, jumper cables, and about five minutes.

Connect red to the dead battery positive terminal, red to the good battery positive terminal, black to the good battery negative terminal, and black to an unpainted metal ground on the dead car's engine block, not the dead battery's negative post. Let the running car idle for two to three minutes before you attempt to start yours.

If the engine cranks and starts, drive directly to an auto parts store. Most AutoZone and O'Reilly locations will test your battery and alternator for free while you wait. If the battery tests bad, replace it on the spot. If the alternator is not charging, that is why the battery died and you need a shop.

If the jump start produces no change at all, or the car starts and immediately dies, call a tow. A battery that will not accept a charge is either fully dead (a shorted cell internally) or the alternator is not putting power back in. Either way, you are not driving out of there today. For more detail on what happens right after the car is loaded up, see [how long does it take for a tow truck to arrive](/how-long-does-it-take-for-a-tow-truck-to-arrive/) so you know what to expect while you wait.

## What If I Get One Click and Nothing Else?

One solid click with no follow-up cranking points directly to the starter solenoid or starter motor. The solenoid is the small cylinder bolted to the starter. It gets a signal from the ignition switch and is supposed to push a small gear into the flywheel ring gear, but when the starter motor itself is burned out or seized, you get that single thud and silence.

You can sometimes get a seized starter to fire one more time by tapping it firmly with a hammer or a wrench while someone else turns the key. This is not a repair, it is a temporary trick that works maybe half the time. The starter needs to be replaced. If tapping does not work, you need a tow. The article on [car won't start, clicks once and nothing else](/car-wont-start-clicks-once-and-nothing-else/) goes deeper into diagnosing that single-click failure.

## Is It Safe to Wait Where I Am, or Should I Move?

If you are in a parking lot or driveway, you are fine. Stay with the car, make your calls, and wait.

If you stalled in traffic or on a highway shoulder, get out of the car and away from the travel lanes. A broken-down car in an active lane is a serious hazard, and sitting inside it does not protect you. Move to the guardrail side, not the traffic side. Turn your hazards on before you exit. ## Common Questions

**Q: Why does my car click but won't start even with a new battery?**
A: A new battery rules out the most common cause, so look at the starter motor next. A bad starter solenoid or a seized starter motor produces the same clicking symptom. Also check that the battery cable clamps are tight and making solid contact with clean posts.

**Q: How many times should I try to jump start before giving up?**
A: Give it two solid attempts with the donor car running for at least three minutes each time. If it still will not crank after that, the battery may have a shorted cell and cannot accept a charge. Stop trying and call a tow. Continuing to crank a fully dead battery can overheat the starter.

**Q: Can a bad alternator cause clicking when I try to start?**
A: Yes. If the alternator stopped charging on your last drive, it ran the battery down. The battery then lacks the amperage to crank the engine, producing rapid clicking. Jump it, drive it, and get the charging system tested immediately. Driving with a dead alternator will drain the new or jumped battery within 20 to 40 miles.

**Q: Will roadside assistance from my insurance jump start my car?**
A: Most roadside assistance plans, including those from State Farm and GEICO, cover jump starts at no additional cost per incident. Check your policy before you call a private tow company. If you have the coverage, use it first.

**Q: How much does a tow cost if the jump start does not work?**
A: A local tow of 5 to 10 miles typically runs $75 to $150 depending on your city. Prices vary by region and time of day. Calling multiple companies and asking for a flat-rate quote upfront is the fastest way to avoid surprise charges.
