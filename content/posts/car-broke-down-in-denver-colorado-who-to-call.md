Your car just died in Denver and you need to know who to call and what happens next. Here is the short version, then the details.

> **Quick Answer:** If you broke down on a Denver highway, call 911 for a safety hazard or *CSP dispatch via 511* for a non-emergency on I-25 or I-70. For a side street breakdown, call a private tow company or your roadside assistance provider directly. A local Denver tow runs $75 to $150 for the hookup plus mileage. If you have AAA or insurance roadside coverage, use that first. Get off the road and stay behind the guardrail while you wait.

## Who Do I Actually Call First When My Car Breaks Down in Denver?

Call 911 if you or anyone is injured, if your car is blocking a lane of active traffic, or if you smell fuel or smoke. For everything else on a major highway, dial 511, Colorado's road conditions and traffic service, and follow the prompts for a non-emergency road assist on CDOT-managed roads like I-25 or I-70.

If you're on a side street in Capitol Hill, Five Points, or LoDo, 511 is not the right call. Go straight to a private tow company or your roadside plan.

Check in this order before you dial anything:

1. Do you have **AAA**? Call their dispatch line from the app or your card.
2. Does your **insurance include roadside assistance**? Check your insurer's app right now.
3. Do you have a **manufacturer roadside plan**? Many new vehicles include 24/7 coverage through the brand.
4. None of the above? Call a private tow company directly and pay out of pocket.

For a breakdown on I-70 near the Genesee exit heading into the foothills, response times can run longer than downtown. Factor that in before you decide to wait.

## What Do I Do to Stay Safe While I Wait for a Tow in Denver?

Get your car as far right as possible, turn on your hazard lights, and get out of the vehicle on the passenger side away from traffic. Do not stand behind or in front of the car.

On I-25 through downtown or I-70 near the Mousetrap interchange (where those two highways meet), traffic moves fast and lane changes are unpredictable. A breakdown there is genuinely dangerous. Get over the guardrail onto the shoulder embankment if there is one, and stay there until the tow truck arrives. Set road flares or reflective triangles at least 100 feet back if you have them. If it is dark, wear anything bright and keep your phone flashlight visible.

## What Will a Tow Cost Me in Denver Right Now?

Most Denver tow companies charge a hookup fee of $75 to $100, then a per-mile rate of $3 to $5. A 10-mile tow to a shop in Aurora or Lakewood will typically land between $110 and $150. Flatbed rates run $15 to $30 more than wheel-lift.

If this happened after an accident, the math changes. See [towing cost in Denver after an accident](/towing-cost-denver-colorado-after-accident/) for a full breakdown including what insurance typically covers.

Night rates and snow emergency rates are also higher. Get a price quoted verbally before the driver hooks up. A reputable company will give you a number up front.

## What if I Can't Get a Signal or I'm Stuck Somewhere Hard to Reach in Denver?

If you have no signal on surface streets or in a parking structure, find a business, wave someone down, or walk to higher ground. In Denver's grid, you are rarely more than a few blocks from a gas station or open business.

If you ran out of gas rather than broke down mechanically, that is a simpler fix. [Ran out of gas in Denver](/ran-out-of-gas-in-denver-who-to-call/) covers who delivers fuel and how fast they can reach you.

For a breakdown on I-70 in the mountains west of C-470 toward Evergreen or Genesee, cell coverage drops in spots. If you can reach 911, do it. Colorado State Patrol patrols that corridor regularly, and a trooper may reach you before a tow truck does. Stay with your vehicle.

## Common Questions

**Q: What number do I call when my car breaks down on I-25 in Denver?**
A: Dial 511 for a non-emergency on a CDOT highway like I-25. For any safety risk or if you're blocking traffic, call 911 directly. Have your milepost or nearest exit name ready.

**Q: How long will it take for a tow truck to reach me in Denver?**
A: Most Denver tow companies arrive in 30 to 60 minutes on a normal day. During a snowstorm or rush hour on I-25 or I-70, expect 60 to 90 minutes or more. See [tow truck response time in Denver](/tow-truck-response-time-denver-colorado/) for more detail.

**Q: Can I call a tow truck without insurance or AAA in Denver?**
A: Yes. Private tow companies in Denver will dispatch without any coverage. You pay out of pocket at the time of service, usually by card. Expect $100 to $150 for a local tow.

**Q: My car broke down in a Denver neighborhood, not on a highway. Who do I call?**
A: Call a private tow company or your roadside assistance provider. Denver Police do not tow vehicles for mechanical breakdowns on side streets unless the car is creating a hazard or blocking a lane.

**Q: Is it safe to wait in my car if I broke down on a Denver freeway?**
A: No, not on an active freeway shoulder. Exit the vehicle from the passenger side, move behind the guardrail, and wait there. Staying inside a stopped car on I-25 or I-70 puts you at serious risk from distracted or drifting drivers.
