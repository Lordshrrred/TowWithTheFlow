## How Much Does a Tow Actually Cost After a Crash in Miami?

A standard post-accident tow in Miami runs $125 to $250 for the first 5 to 10 miles, with per-mile rates of $4 to $7 beyond that. So if you're getting towed from a crash on I-95 near the Midtown interchange down to a body shop in Doral, you're looking at 15 to 18 miles and a bill in the $175 to $325 range before any extras.

Flatbed tows cost more than wheel-lift. After an accident, most shops and insurers prefer a flatbed because it keeps a damaged vehicle fully off the ground and reduces liability. Budget an extra $25 to $50 for that if your car has front-end or undercarriage damage.

## What Happens to My Car Right After the Accident: Does the City Tow It?

If your car is blocking traffic on a busy road, MDPD or FHP will call a rotation tow. Miami-Dade operates a wrecker rotation system, which means a pre-approved tow company gets dispatched, not necessarily the one you'd choose. That company will tow your car to their impound lot.

This matters because impound storage in Miami runs $40 to $75 per day. If you leave your car there for several days while dealing with insurance, the storage fees can easily exceed the tow itself. Call your insurance company the same day and tell them where the car was taken so they can authorize a release or a secondary tow to a preferred shop.

If you're on I-95, the 836 (Dolphin Expressway), or the 826 (Palmetto Expressway), Florida Highway Patrol handles the call. On surface streets in Miami Beach, Coral Gables, or Hialeah, the local police department controls it. Either way, you are not required to use the rotation tow for a secondary move.

## Will Insurance Pay for the Tow After an Accident?

Yes, if you have comprehensive or collision coverage, the tow is almost always covered, often with no separate deductible applied to the tow itself. Your collision deductible applies to the vehicle repair, not typically to towing. Check your declarations page to confirm.

If the other driver was at fault, their liability insurance should cover your tow under property damage liability. Get that claim number as early as possible because some carriers will only pay a tow that's authorized in advance.

If you pay out of pocket and want reimbursement, keep the itemized receipt from the tow company. You'll need it. For a detailed look at how insurance handles towing charges after a crash, [Does Insurance Cover Towing After an Accident Deductible](/insurance-covers-towing-after-accident-deductible/) walks through exactly what most policies will and won't pay.

## What Should I Do in the First 10 Minutes at the Crash Scene in Miami?

Move yourself and passengers out of traffic if the car is drivable. If it's not, stay inside with seatbelts on until help arrives, especially on I-95 or the 836 where traffic moves fast. Turn on your hazards immediately.

Call 911 if anyone is hurt or if the car is blocking a lane. Florida law requires you

## Are There Extra Fees I Should Expect After a Miami Accident Tow?

Yes, and they add up fast. Common add-ons after accident tows in Miami include:

- **Winching fee:** $50 to $150 if your car went off the road, into a ditch, or is in a position that makes a straight hook-up impossible.
- **After-hours fee:** $25 to $75 on top of the base rate for tows between roughly 10 PM and 6 AM or on weekends.
- **Dolly fee:** $40 to $75 if a four-wheel-drive or all-wheel-drive vehicle needs dollies to avoid drivetrain damage during transport.
- **Storage:** $40 to $75 per day at most Miami-area impound lots. After three days, this becomes the biggest cost on your bill.

Ask for an itemized estimate before the truck hooks up if at all possible. A legitimate company gives you one. If someone refuses to write anything down, that is a red flag.

## Common Questions

**Q: What's the average towing cost after an accident on I-95 in Miami?**
A: A tow from I-95 to a shop within Miami-Dade County typically runs $150 to $275, depending on the pickup location and drop-off distance. Flatbed service and after-hours fees can push that higher.

**Q: Can I choose my own tow company after a Miami accident, or am I stuck with whoever the police call?**
A: You can choose your own company for any secondary tow once the car is out of the road. If police dispatch a rotation tow to clear the scene, you are not required to use that same company for transport to a repair shop.

**Q: How long do I have to get my car out of an impound lot in Miami before storage fees get out of hand?**
A: Get it out within 24 to 48 hours if you can. Most Miami-Dade impound operators charge $40 to $75 per day, and some add a gate fee of $25 to $50 on top. A week of storage can cost more than the tow.

**Q: Does Florida require tow companies to give a written estimate before towing after an accident?**
A: Florida Statute 715.07 requires tow operators to post their rates, and many companies will provide a written estimate on request. You have the right to ask before authorizing the hook-up. Get it in writing.

**Q: If the other driver caused the accident, who pays for my tow in Miami?**
A: The at-fault driver's property damage liability insurance is responsible for your tow cost. Contact their insurer directly or go through your own carrier, which can subrogate the claim against the at-fault policy. Either way, save your receipt.
