> **Quick Answer:** GEICO's roadside assistance towing benefit does not specify a fixed mileage cap in most standard policies. Instead, it covers towing to the nearest qualified repair facility. If you want a tow to a shop farther away, you'll pay the difference out of pocket. The exact terms depend on whether your coverage comes through GEICO's own Emergency Road Service or a partner like Agero. Check your declarations page or call GEICO directly before assuming you're covered for a long haul.

## What Is GEICO's Actual Towing Distance Limit?

GEICO's Emergency Road Service (ERS) coverage is structured around towing you to the nearest qualified repair facility, not a specific mileage number. That phrase matters a lot: it means if there's a shop 2 miles away and you want to go to your trusted mechanic 25 miles away, GEICO may only cover the cost to the closer one, and bill you for the rest.

Some GEICO policies are administered through a third-party network, most commonly Agero. When Agero handles the dispatch, the coverage terms can vary slightly from what GEICO's own ERS description says. Your declarations page will tell you which program you're under.

The practical takeaway: there is no clean "50 miles free" or "100 miles included" rule with GEICO the way some standalone roadside programs advertise. Coverage is situational and tied to what's nearest and reasonable.

## What Happens If I Need a Tow Beyond the Nearest Shop?

You can still request a longer tow, but you'll likely pay the overage. GEICO's ERS will cover what it deems reasonable to reach the nearest facility. Any additional mileage beyond that gets billed to you at the tow company's standard per-mile rate, which typically runs $3 to $7 per mile depending on your location.

Say you break down on I-40 in a rural stretch. There's a small shop 8 miles away, but you want to get back to your dealership 60 miles out. GEICO covers the 8-mile equivalent. The remaining 52 miles, at $4 per mile, puts you at roughly $208 out of pocket. That's a number worth knowing before you insist on a specific destination.

One workaround: call GEICO first and explain there is no qualified facility closer to your breakdown location. Sometimes the claims rep will authorize a longer tow if they cannot locate an open shop nearby.

## How Do I Actually Use GEICO Roadside Assistance When I'm Broken Down?

Call the GEICO Emergency Road Service number on your insurance card or open the GEICO mobile app and tap Roadside Assistance. The app can share your GPS location automatically, which speeds up dispatch considerably. Have your policy number ready.

Once you report the breakdown, GEICO or their dispatch partner contacts a local tow company on your behalf. You do not choose the tow company. If you call your own tow truck independently and then try to get reimbursed, GEICO may cover it up to the policy's reimbursement cap, but this is slower and sometimes pays less than using their direct dispatch.

If you're on a highway at night or in a dangerous spot, move away from traffic immediately and turn on your hazard lights before you do anything else. Your phone call to GEICO can wait 60 seconds while you get safe. ## How Does GEICO's Coverage Compare to What I'd Pay Out of Pocket?

A local tow of 5 to 10 miles without insurance typically runs $75 to $125. A longer tow of 40 to 50 miles can easily hit $250 to $400 depending on your region and the type of truck required. GEICO's ERS add-on costs very little annually, usually under $20 per vehicle, so it pays for itself the first time you use it even for a short tow.

The risk is assuming the coverage stretches further than it does. If you need your all-wheel-drive vehicle flatbed-towed to a specialty shop 70 miles away, GEICO's ERS may cover only a fraction of that. Know what you're getting before you're standing on the shoulder making the call. ## Common Questions

**Q: Does GEICO roadside assistance have a mileage limit per tow?**
A: GEICO does not publish a specific mileage cap. Coverage applies to towing you to the nearest qualified repair facility. Miles beyond that are typically your cost at the tow company's per-mile rate.

**Q: Can I choose my own mechanic or dealership with GEICO roadside assistance?**
A: You can request a specific shop, but GEICO is only obligated to cover the cost equivalent to reaching the nearest facility. If your preferred shop is farther, you pay the difference.

**Q: How many times can I use GEICO Emergency Road Service in a year?**
A: GEICO does not publicly cap the number of uses per year for ERS, but excessive claims can affect your policy at renewal. Check your specific policy documents for any use limitations.

**Q: What if I already called my own tow truck before contacting GEICO?**
A: You can submit a reimbursement claim, but GEICO will only pay up to what their direct dispatch would have cost. You may not be reimbursed the full amount, especially if the tow was long or you used a premium operator.

**Q: Does GEICO roadside assistance cover towing after an accident or only breakdowns?**
A: GEICO ERS is primarily for mechanical breakdowns. If the vehicle is disabled after an accident, that tow is typically handled under your collision or comprehensive coverage, not ERS. The reimbursement process and limits are different.
