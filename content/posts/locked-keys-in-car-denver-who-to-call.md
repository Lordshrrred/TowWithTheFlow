## Who Should I Actually Call When I'm Locked Out in Denver?

Your best first call is a local locksmith, not a tow truck. A locksmith handles lockouts directly and charges less than most roadside services that outsource the job anyway. If you have AAA, your auto insurance roadside add-on, or a credit card with roadside benefits (many Visa and Mastercard products include this), call that number first. They dispatch a locksmith on your behalf and bill the provider, not you. If you have none of those, search "Denver locksmith" and call a shop with a local 303 or 720 area code number. Avoid third-party dispatch apps that pass your call through a call center and tack on fees.

One specific tip: if you're locked out in a parking garage downtown, in Cherry Creek, or near the 16th Street Mall, response times from local locksmiths tend to be faster than from tow operators because they're running lighter vehicles and don't need to navigate garage clearances. For a dead battery situation in a garage, the calculus is different. ## How Much Does a Car Lockout Cost in Denver?

Most locksmith lockout calls in the Denver metro run $65 to $120 for a standard door unlock on a basic lock. That covers the service call and the actual work. Complexity drives cost up fast. Newer vehicles, especially post-2018 models with reinforced door frames or proximity key systems (like a Toyota RAV4 with a smart key), can push the price to $150 or more because the locksmith may need to use a longer air wedge and reach tool rather than a simple slim-jim. After-hours calls add $20 to $40 on top.

If your roadside plan covers it, your out-of-pocket is zero or a small copay. If you're paying out of pocket, get a price confirmed before they start. Reputable Denver locksmiths will quote over the phone. If someone refuses to quote, hang up and call the next one. For a full look at what roadside help costs in the area, [How Much Does Roadside Assistance Cost in Denver Colorado](/how-much-does-roadside-assistance-cost-denver-colorado/) breaks it down.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can a locksmith unlock any car or are some too new to open without a key?**
A: A trained locksmith can open virtually any passenger vehicle, including newer models. The method changes. Older cars take a slim-jim, newer ones take an air wedge and long-reach tool to manually trigger the interior handle. Smart key or push-button start vehicles still have a physical door lock that can be manipulated from the outside.

**Q: What if I locked my keys in the car with the engine running in Denver?**
A: Same calls apply, but treat it as more urgent. A running engine means a warming interior in summer or a fuel drain you didn't plan on. A locksmith can still open the door the same way. For more detail on that specific situation, [Locked Keys in Car With Engine Running: Who to Call Right Now](/locked-keys-in-car-with-engine-running-who-to-call/) covers it fully.

**Q: Does my car insurance cover a lockout in Colorado?**
A: It depends on whether you added roadside assistance to your policy. Standard liability or collision coverage does not include lockouts. Check your declarations page or call your insurer's app. Many major carriers offer it as a $5 to $10 per month add-on, and it pays for itself the first time you use it.

**Q: Will a tow truck driver unlock my car if I can't reach a locksmith?**
A: Some will, some won't. Tow operators are equipped to move cars, not necessarily to unlock them. If a tow company offers lockout service, ask them to confirm the price before they arrive. It is rarely cheaper than a dedicated locksmith, and wait times may be longer because tow trucks are in higher demand.

**Q: Can a locksmith make me a replacement key on the spot if mine is lost?**
A: A locksmith can open your door on the spot. Cutting or programming a replacement key, especially a transponder or smart key, usually requires either a mobile key programmer or a trip to a dealer. Some mobile locksmiths carry programming equipment, but confirm that capability before they come if a new key is what you need.
