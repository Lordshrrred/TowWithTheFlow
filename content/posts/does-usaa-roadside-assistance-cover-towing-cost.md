> **Quick Answer:** Yes, USAA roadside assistance covers towing, but how much depends on your specific plan. The basic Emergency Road Service add-on covers towing to the nearest qualified repair facility. Some plans cap the tow distance or dollar amount. If your car needs to go farther than the plan allows, you pay the difference. Check your declarations page right now for the exact limit before you call.

## Does USAA Actually Cover Towing, or Just Roadside Services?

USAA covers towing as part of its Emergency Road Service (ERS) add-on, which you must have elected on your auto policy. It is not automatic with liability-only or basic coverage. The ERS add-on also covers battery jump-starts, flat tire changes, fuel delivery, and lockout service, but towing is the most expensive benefit and the one most members actually need. If you are not sure whether you added ERS, pull up the USAA app or your declarations page. The coverage will be listed as "Emergency Road Service" or "Roadside Assistance."

## What Is the Tow Limit and What Happens If My Car Needs to Go Farther?

USAA's ERS benefit typically covers towing to the nearest qualified repair facility, not necessarily your preferred shop across town. The covered distance is generally up to a certain mileage or dollar cap, and that limit varies by state and policy version. Many members discover their plan covers $100 to $150 toward a tow, which is enough for a short local haul but not a 40-mile trip. If the actual tow cost exceeds your covered amount, you pay the overage directly to the tow company. To avoid a surprise bill, ask the dispatcher to confirm the mileage to the destination before the truck rolls. For what towing costs without coverage, see [how much does a tow truck cost without insurance](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-without-insurance/).

## How Do I Actually File a USAA Roadside Assistance Claim on the Side of the Road?

Call USAA directly or use the USAA mobile app to request service. The app is faster because it pinpoints your GPS location automatically, which matters when you are stuck on a dark highway at night and not sure of the mile marker. Once you request service, USAA dispatches a network provider to your location. Do not call a random tow truck and expect full reimbursement. USAA has a network, and using an out-of-network provider can mean partial or no reimbursement. If you are in an area with poor cell service and need to call a local truck yourself, save your receipt and call USAA afterward to discuss reimbursement.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does USAA roadside assistance have a limit on how many tows per year?**
A: Yes. Most USAA ERS plans limit you to four service calls per year per vehicle. Using more than that in a 12-month period may result in the benefit being declined or the add-on being reviewed at renewal.

**Q: Can I use USAA roadside assistance for a car that belongs to someone else?**
A: No. USAA ERS is tied to the vehicle listed on your policy, not to you personally as a driver. If you are driving a rental or a friend's car that breaks down, your USAA ERS will not apply.

**Q: What if I pay for the tow out of pocket and want USAA to reimburse me?**
A: USAA does allow reimbursement claims for out-of-pocket roadside expenses, but you need the original receipt and the service must qualify under your ERS benefit. Submit through the app or online. Reimbursement is typically capped at what USAA would have paid a network provider.

**Q: Does USAA ERS cover towing for a flat tire if I have no spare?**
A: Yes, if you have no spare or the spare is unsafe, a tow to the nearest tire shop falls under ERS. If you just need the flat changed and have a usable spare, ERS covers the tire change instead. See [flat tire no spare no roadside assistance](/flat-tire-no-spare-no-roadside-assistance-options/) for what to do if your coverage is not active yet.

**Q: Does USAA roadside assistance work in all 50 states?**
A: Yes. USAA ERS operates nationwide through a dispatch network. Response times vary by location, and rural areas may take longer. Always confirm an ETA from the dispatcher so you know how long to wait safely off the road.
