> **Quick Answer:** AAA's free tow distance depends on your membership tier. Classic usually covers up to 5 miles, Plus up to 100 miles, and Premier up to 200 miles. Exact limits vary by regional club, so check your card or app. Past your limit, you pay a per-mile fee. You can tow to any shop or your home, not just the nearest one.

## How Far Will AAA Tow My Car for Free?

It depends on your tier, and the number printed on your membership card or in the AAA app is the one that counts. The standard national structure looks like this:

| Plan | Typical free tow distance |
|---|---|
| Classic | Up to 5 miles |
| Plus | Up to 100 miles |
| Premier | Up to 200 miles |

Regional AAA clubs set their own terms, so a few clubs run slightly different numbers or add conditions. If you can't find your card, open the app or log in on the AAA site and look at your plan benefits. Don't guess on the phone with a dispatcher. Ask them to confirm your covered mileage before the truck is sent.

## What Happens If My Car Needs to Go Farther Than My Limit?

You pay for the extra miles, usually at a per-mile rate set by the tow company, and AAA covers the portion up to your limit. Ask the dispatcher for the rate before you agree, and ask whether the overage is billed to you directly or collected by the driver on site.

Two ways to avoid a surprise bill:

- **Pick a closer destination.** A shop 4 miles away on a Classic plan costs you nothing. A dealership 15 miles away costs you for 10 miles.
- **Split the trip.** Tow to a nearby shop for diagnosis. If the car needs a specialist later, move it from there.

The distance is measured from where the car sits to the drop-off point, not from your home. A breakdown near your driveway stretches a Classic plan much further than one 40 miles out on the highway.

## Can I Choose Where AAA Tows My Car?

Yes. You can pick any repair shop, your home, or a dealership, as long as it falls within your covered mileage. AAA will also tow to a shop of your choice if you already have a mechanic you trust. Tell the dispatcher the full street address and confirm the shop is open, because a tow to a closed lot leaves your car sitting outside overnight.

If you're not sure where to go, the nearest AAA Approved Auto Repair facility is a reasonable default. You're not required to use it.

Your plan also caps the number of service calls per year, commonly four. A fifth tow in the same membership year gets billed.

## Is It Worth Upgrading to Plus or Premier?

If you drive highways or live more than 5 miles from your regular mechanic, yes. A Classic tow of 5 miles barely clears a city block in many metros. Most real breakdowns happen on highways, where the nearest shop might be 10 to 20 miles off, and a Plus plan's 100-mile reach covers that easily.

Do the math before you break down, not after. Compare your annual membership upgrade cost to what a single overage tow would run you. ## Common Questions

**Q: Does AAA tow free if I don't have a membership?**
A: No. Without an active membership, AAA won't dispatch a covered tow. You can join on the spot in many cases, but there is often a waiting period or a fee for new members calling mid-emergency.

**Q: Is AAA's free tow distance one way?**
A: Yes. The mileage covers the trip from the breakdown location to your chosen drop-off point, not a round trip back.

**Q: Can AAA tow my car to another state for free?**
A: Only if the destination stays within your covered mileage. A Premier plan's 200 miles can cross a state line, but anything past 200 miles is billed as overage.

**Q: How many free tows does AAA give you per year?**
A: Most plans cap service calls at around four per membership year, though the exact number depends on your regional club. Check your plan details or the app.

**Q: How long will I wait for AAA to arrive?**
A: Wait times swing widely with weather, time of day, and location. The dispatcher gives you an estimate when you call, and the app shows live tracking. For realistic numbers, see [roadside assistance wait times](/roadside-assistance-how-long-do-you-have-to-wait/).
