## How Much Does an After-Hours Tow Cost in Dallas Right Now?

Expect to pay $125 to $250 for a local after-hours tow in Dallas, covering roughly 5 to 10 miles. Most Dallas tow companies charge a base hookup fee of $75 to $100 during the day, then layer a night or after-hours surcharge of $50 to $100 on top of that, plus a per-mile rate of $3 to $7 per mile after the first few.

If you're stuck on I-35E near the Mixmaster interchange downtown, or broken down on the LBJ Freeway (I-635) in Farmers Branch at 2 a.m., you're almost certainly looking at the higher end of that range. Operators in high-traffic corridors know demand is non-negotiable at night, and their pricing reflects it.

A flatbed, which most shops require for AWD vehicles, luxury cars, and low-clearance sports cars, typically costs $25 to $50 more than a standard wheel-lift truck. For full details on that cost difference, see [Towing Cost in Dallas Texas Hook and Chain vs Flatbed](/towing-cost-in-dallas-texas-hook-and-chain-vs-flatbed/).

## What Exactly Makes After-Hours Towing More Expensive?

After-hours pricing is more expensive because tow companies pay drivers overtime, face lower truck availability, and absorb higher operating risk at night. Those costs get passed to you.

The specific factors that push your bill up:

- **Time of call.** Most Dallas operators define "after hours" as anything after 6 or 7 p.m. through 6 or 7 a.m. Some add a separate "late night" surcharge for calls between midnight and 5 a.m.
- **Day of week.** Friday and Saturday nights are the most expensive. Holidays like July 4th and Labor Day are treated the same as weekend nights by most local operators.
- **Location.** A call from Deep Ellum or Uptown is easy to reach. A call from the far end of I-20 near Duncanville or from SH-161 in Irving is a longer deadhead drive, and some companies charge for that repositioning time.
- **Vehicle type.** Heavy trucks, SUVs, and AWD vehicles require specific equipment and cost more regardless of time of day.

## Should I Call My Insurance or Pay Out of Pocket?

Call your insurance or roadside assistance provider first, every time. Out-of-pocket after-hours towing in Dallas will almost always cost more than your deductible or annual roadside add-on fee.

Check your phone right now before you call a tow company directly:

1. Open your insurance app or flip to the roadside assistance card in your glove box.
2. Call the number on the card. Do not call a random tow company from a Google search while stressed at night.
3. Confirm what's covered. Many policies cover a tow up to a set dollar amount or mileage limit, and some include after-hours calls at no extra cost to you.

AAA members are covered around the clock with no after-hours surcharge billed to them directly. State Farm, GEICO, and Allstate roadside plans vary. ## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged by an After-Hours Tow Truck in Dallas?

Get a firm quote before the driver hooks up your vehicle. Texas law requires tow companies to provide a written or verbal rate disclosure before towing, and you have the right to ask.

The questions to ask before you agree:

- What is your base hookup fee tonight?
- Is there an after-hours surcharge, and how much is it?
- What is your per-mile rate, and how many miles to my destination?
- Is there a storage fee if my car goes to your yard instead of my mechanic?

That last point matters. If your chosen shop is closed and the driver takes your car to a tow yard, storage fees of $40 to $75 per day start accruing immediately. Give the driver a 24-hour mechanic address or a trusted driveway to drop the car if you can. ## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average after-hours tow truck cost in Dallas for a 10-mile tow?**
A: Plan on $150 to $225 for a 10-mile after-hours tow in Dallas. That includes a base hookup fee around $85 to $100, a night surcharge of $50 to $75, and per-mile charges for the distance beyond the first few included miles.

**Q: Do Dallas tow companies charge more on weekends than weeknights?**
A: Yes, most do. Friday and Saturday nights typically carry the highest surcharges. Some operators treat Sunday mornings before 8 a.m. the same as a Saturday night call. Always confirm the rate before the driver moves your vehicle.

**Q: How long does an after-hours tow truck take to arrive in Dallas?**
A: Typical wait times run 45 minutes to 90 minutes for after-hours calls in Dallas. High-demand nights like holiday weekends or bad weather can push that past two hours. If you're in a dangerous spot on a freeway, call 911 first and let HERO operators know your location.

**Q: Can I get a flatbed tow truck after midnight in Dallas?**
A: Yes, flatbed trucks operate 24 hours in Dallas, but availability is tighter after midnight. Call ahead and confirm the operator has a flatbed available before committing to a company, especially if your car is an AWD or has low ground clearance.

**Q: Does AAA cover after-hours towing in Dallas with no extra charge to me?**
A: AAA members pay no after-hours surcharge directly. The service is included in your membership tier. Classic members get up to 5 miles towed free per call. Plus and Premier tiers cover 100 and 200 miles respectively. The tow company still charges AAA the after-hours rate, but that cost does not get passed to you at the time of service.
