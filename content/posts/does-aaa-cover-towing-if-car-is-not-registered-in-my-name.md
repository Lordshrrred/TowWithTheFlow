> **Quick Answer:** Usually yes. Most AAA memberships cover the member, not a specific vehicle, so you're typically covered in any eligible car you're driving or riding in, including one registered to someone else. The catches are vehicle type, tow distance limits tied to your membership tier, and rules that vary by regional club. Call the number on your card and confirm before you commit to a tow.

## Does AAA Cover Me in a Car That Isn't Registered to Me?

Yes, in most cases. AAA roadside coverage is attached to the person, not the license plate. If you're a member and you break down in a friend's, parent's, or partner's car, you can generally call for help as a driver or passenger.

The car still has to qualify. Standard passenger vehicles, SUVs, and light trucks are the normal fit. Rental cars are usually fine for the member's roadside needs, but check the details first. Commercial vehicles, heavy-duty trucks, and anything towing a trailer can fall outside standard coverage.

One more point: AAA is a federation of regional clubs, and each sets its own fine print. Don't assume the rule you read online applies to your club. Your membership card or app has the real terms.

## What Do I Say When I Call AAA From Someone Else's Car?

Give them your membership number and tell them you're in a vehicle that belongs to someone else. Be honest and specific about the car: year, make, model, color, and the plate number if you can read it.

Do not claim the car is yours. The dispatcher doesn't need the registration papers, but misrepresenting ownership can cause problems later if there's a dispute or a damage claim.

Before you hang up, get three things: a confirmation number, an estimated arrival time, and the destination the truck will take the car to. If the car's owner is nearby, have them tell you where they want it towed. A tow to the owner's regular shop or home is often smarter than the closest lot.

## How Far Will AAA Tow a Car That Isn't Mine?

The tow distance depends on your membership tier, not on who owns the car. Basic memberships commonly cover a short tow, around 5 miles. Plus and Premier tiers extend that, often to 100 miles on the top tier, though your club's numbers may differ.

Go past the limit and you pay the per-mile overage yourself. That matters more with a borrowed car, because the owner may live far from where you broke down. Ask the dispatcher for the exact covered distance before the driver loads the vehicle, then compare it to the distance to the shop.

If the tow will run over, you have two options: pay the overage, or have the truck go to the closest reputable shop and arrange the rest later. ## What Do I Do While I Wait for the Truck?

Get the car off the travel lane if you can, turn on the hazards, and stay out of the vehicle on high-speed roads. On an interstate, stand well behind a guardrail, not beside the car.

If the wait drags on, don't just sit there hoping. ## Common Questions

**Q: Does AAA cover towing in a rental car?**
A: Often yes, because coverage follows the member. Rentals can have their own roadside program, so check your rental agreement first, since calling AAA and the rental company can create conflicting instructions.

**Q: Does AAA cover a car with no insurance or expired registration?**
A: AAA doesn't verify insurance or registration at dispatch, so the service call usually goes through. Be aware that a tow driver or impound lot may ask for proof of ownership at drop-off.

**Q: Can I use my AAA membership on a friend's car if my friend isn't there?**
A: Usually yes, as long as you're the member and you're with the vehicle. The member's presence is what matters, not the owner's.

**Q: Does AAA cover motorcycles, RVs, or trailers I don't own?**
A: Not automatically. Standard memberships cover cars and light trucks. Motorcycles, RVs, and towed trailers typically need an upgraded tier or a specific add-on.

**Q: What if the tow goes past my AAA mileage limit in someone else's car?**
A: You pay the overage per mile. Ask the dispatcher for the covered distance before loading, and consider towing to the nearest shop instead.
