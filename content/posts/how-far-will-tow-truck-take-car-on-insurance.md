> **Quick Answer:** Most insurance-based roadside assistance plans cover towing to the nearest qualified repair facility, typically 5 to 25 miles depending on your insurer and plan tier. Some policies cap the dollar amount instead of the mileage. If the shop you want is farther than your coverage allows, you pay the difference per mile out of pocket. Check your declarations page before you call for a tow.

## How Far Will My Insurance Actually Tow My Car?

Your coverage depends entirely on your policy type. Basic roadside assistance add-ons from insurers like GEICO, Progressive, and State Farm usually cover the nearest shop or a set mileage limit, commonly 10 to 25 miles. More premium tiers or standalone roadside plans sometimes go up to 100 miles. The phrase "nearest qualified facility" is the key one to watch: your insurer gets to define "qualified," and that may not be your preferred mechanic across town.

Some policies cap the benefit at a dollar amount instead of a mileage limit. Progressive's basic roadside add-on, for example, caps per-incident towing reimbursement at a fixed dollar figure regardless of how far the truck drives. If the tow runs over that cap, you owe the driver the balance at drop-off. See the full breakdown in our article on [Progressive Insurance towing coverage limits per incident](/progressive-insurance-towing-coverage-limit-per-incident/).

## What Happens If My Shop Is Farther Than My Coverage Limit?

You pay the overage. Most tow companies charge a per-mile rate after the hook-up fee, typically $3 to $7 per mile on top of a base charge of $50 to $100. If your policy covers 15 miles and your shop is 40 miles away, you are paying out of pocket for 25 miles. At $5 per mile that is an extra $125 on the spot.

Your options: have the car towed to the nearest covered shop for diagnosis first, then arrange a second tow once you know the situation. Or call a private tow company, pay the full bill yourself, and submit for reimbursement if your policy allows it. Keep the receipt either way.

## Does Insurance Cover Towing After a Breakdown Versus After an Accident?

These are handled differently. Roadside assistance add-ons typically cover mechanical breakdowns, dead batteries, flat tires, and lockouts. Collision or comprehensive claims handle tows resulting from an accident, and the coverage limit is often different, sometimes higher, because the tow is bundled with the overall claim.

If your car is not driveable after a crash, the towing cost usually falls under your collision or comprehensive deductible situation rather than your roadside add-on. Whether a breakdown tow is covered at all depends on whether you added roadside assistance to your policy. It is not automatic. Read more about how this works in [does insurance cover towing if your car breaks down](/does-insurance-cover-towing-if-car-breaks-down-not-accident/).

- **AAA Premier:** 200 miles per tow.

## What Should I Tell the Tow Driver Before They Hook Up?

Tell them your destination upfront and ask for the total estimated cost if the destination exceeds your coverage. Do not wait until the car is already on the flatbed. Once it is loaded, you have limited negotiating power.

Have your insurance card or roadside assistance member number ready. If your insurer dispatches the tow directly, the driver is paid by the insurer up to your limit and will collect the balance from you. If you called a tow company independently, you pay the full amount and submit for reimbursement yourself, which can take weeks.

One practical move: use your insurer's app or dispatch line to request the tow rather than calling a company yourself. Direct dispatch usually means the billing is handled cleanly at the coverage cap, with no surprise invoices later.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does insurance pay the tow driver directly or do I pay and get reimbursed?**
A: It depends on how the tow was arranged. If your insurer dispatched the truck, they usually pay the company directly up to your limit and you pay any overage on the spot. If you called a company yourself, you typically pay the full amount and submit a receipt for reimbursement.

**Q: Can I choose which shop my car gets towed to?**
A: Usually yes, but only up to your covered mileage or dollar limit. Beyond that limit, the extra distance comes out of your pocket. Your insurer may push you toward a "nearest facility," but you have the right to direct the tow elsewhere and pay the difference.

**Q: What if I need my car towed more than 100 miles?**
A: Only AAA Plus and Premier plans routinely cover tows that long. Standard insurance roadside add-ons will not get you there for free. For a long-distance tow, expect to pay $3 to $7 per mile beyond your coverage limit. Our guide on [how much it costs to tow a car 100 miles on the interstate](/how-much-to-tow-car-100-miles-interstate/) has current pricing.

**Q: Does my roadside assistance reset after each use or is there an annual cap?**
A: Most policies allow a set number of tows per year, commonly 3 to 4. AAA tracks this per membership year. Insurance-based roadside add-ons often cap at 3 incidents per policy period. Once you hit the limit, you pay for any additional tows out of pocket.

**Q: If I have both AAA and insurance roadside assistance, which one should I use?**
A: Use whichever gives you the longer tow for your situation. AAA Plus at 100 miles will almost always beat a basic insurance add-on. Using AAA also means your insurance claim count stays clean, which matters at renewal. Save insurance roadside assistance for situations where your AAA uses are exhausted.
