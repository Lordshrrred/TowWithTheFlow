> **Quick Answer:** It depends on your policy. Many insurers cap roadside towing at 5 to 15 miles, some tow to the nearest qualified repair shop regardless of distance, and others set a dollar limit instead of a mileage limit. Check your policy or app before the truck is dispatched, and tell the dispatcher your destination upfront so you know what you owe.

## How Far Will Car Insurance Actually Tow My Car?

Most auto insurance roadside add-ons tow you somewhere between 5 and 15 miles. Some cover up to 25 or 50 miles. Others skip mileage entirely and pay a flat dollar amount, often $50 to $100 per tow. The number is in your policy, not in a general rule.

The limit comes in one of three forms:

- **Mileage cap:** Tow covered up to a set number of miles, you pay the overage.
- **Dollar cap:** Insurer pays up to a set amount, and a long tow can exceed it fast.
- **Nearest qualified shop:** Insurer covers the tow to the closest facility that can handle the repair, which can be shorter than your preferred shop.

If you only carry standard liability and collision, you may have no towing coverage at all. Roadside coverage is usually a separate add-on. See [does car insurance cover roadside assistance if I add it](/does-car-insurance-cover-roadside-assistance-if-i-add-it/) for how that works.

## What Do I Do Right Now If I'm Stranded?

Get safe first, then check coverage before you agree to anything. If you can't coast off the road, stay in the vehicle with your seatbelt on and hazards flashing, and call 911 if you're in a live lane. If you're on a highway, the left lane is the most dangerous spot to be stuck, so read [car broke down in the left lane of the highway](/car-broke-down-in-left-lane-of-highway-what-to-do/) if that's you.

Once you're safe:

1. Open your insurer's app or pull up your policy and find the roadside section.
2. Look for the towing limit: miles, dollars, or "nearest facility."
3. Request service through the insurer so the tow is billed correctly.
4. Tell the dispatcher your destination before the truck rolls.

Calling a random tow company yourself usually means you pay upfront and file for reimbursement later. Reimbursement often caps at the same limit, and sometimes lower.

## What Happens If My Shop Is Farther Than My Coverage Allows?

You pay the difference, usually per mile past the cap. Tow rates vary widely by region, so the overage can run from a few dollars to several dollars per mile. ## Does the Type of Breakdown Change How Far I Get Towed?

Yes, it can change the type of truck, and sometimes the coverage. All-wheel-drive vehicles, low-clearance cars, and anything with a failed transmission usually need a flatbed. A broken axle or seized drivetrain can't be dragged on wheel-lift equipment. If you're unsure which you need, check [how to know if my car needs a flatbed tow](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/) before you call, because a flatbed can cost more and some policies treat it differently.

Accident-related tows are a different claim path entirely. If the car isn't drivable because of a collision, towing usually falls under your collision coverage rather than roadside, and the limits change. That's a separate question from a mechanical breakdown.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does my insurance tow me to my own mechanic or just the nearest shop?**
A: It depends on the policy. Many cover a tow to your chosen shop only within the mileage cap, otherwise they default to the nearest qualified facility. Ask the dispatcher which applies before the truck leaves.

**Q: What if I call a tow company myself instead of my insurer?**
A: You'll typically pay upfront and submit a receipt for reimbursement, which may be capped lower than what you paid. Calling through the insurer avoids that gap.

**Q: Is there a difference between towing coverage and roadside assistance?**
A: Often yes. Roadside assistance usually bundles towing with jump starts, lockouts, and fuel delivery. Some policies offer only towing, and the limits differ. Read your declarations page for the exact wording.

**Q: Does using my roadside coverage raise my premium?**
A: Roadside claims are generally treated differently from collision or liability claims, but practices vary by insurer. Ask your agent directly if you're worried about it, and check usage limits like those in [GEICO roadside assistance: how many times per year](/geico-roadside-assistance-how-many-times-per-year/).

**Q: What if I don't have any towing coverage?**
A: You pay the full bill out of pocket. Get the rate and total estimate before the driver hooks up, and if the charge looks inflated afterward, see [how to dispute a towing charge if overcharged](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-if-overcharged/).
