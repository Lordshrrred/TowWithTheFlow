> **Quick Answer:** Who pays depends on what coverage you have active. The rental company's own roadside plan covers you if you bought it at the counter. Your personal auto insurance may extend to rentals. Many travel credit cards include rental car roadside assistance as a cardholder benefit. Call the rental company first: they have a 24/7 line and will direct the response while you sort out reimbursement later.

## Who Actually Pays for Roadside Assistance on a Rental Car?

The rental company pays if you purchased their roadside assistance or collision damage waiver package at pickup. If you declined that and you have personal auto insurance with roadside coverage, your own policy steps in. If neither applies, your credit card is the third option. You will rarely be stuck with zero coverage, but you need to know which pocket to reach into before you make the call.

The order of responsibility typically shakes out like this:

1. Rental company's own plan (purchased at counter)
2. Your personal auto insurance roadside add-on
3. Credit card benefit (Visa Signature, Mastercard World, Chase Sapphire, Amex Platinum all have some form of this)
4. AAA membership, which covers you in any vehicle you are driving

If none of those apply, you pay out of pocket and the cost runs $75 to $150 for a basic tow, more for longer distances. See [what a tow actually costs per mile](/how-much-to-tow-car-100-miles-interstate/) if you need a number to plan around.

## What Does the Rental Company's Roadside Plan Actually Cover?

It covers the specific breakdown services the rental company defines in their contract, which is usually towing to the nearest authorized dealer or repair facility, flat tire change, jump start, lockout service, and fuel delivery. Enterprise, Hertz, Avis, and Budget all bundle this into their "Roadside Assistance Protection" product, which typically runs $5 to $15 per day added to your rental rate.

What it does not cover: damage you caused by running the car out of oil, driving through a flooded road, or any mechanical failure you contributed to through negligence. Those costs come back to you under a separate damage clause.

## Does My Personal Auto Insurance Cover a Rental Car Breakdown?

Usually yes, if your policy includes a roadside assistance rider. Most personal policies extend liability and collision coverage to rental vehicles, and the roadside benefit follows the same logic. Call your insurer's roadside line, give them your policy number, and tell them you are in a rental. They dispatch a truck just as they would for your own car.

The catch: your deductible still applies to any mechanical damage claim, and if your insurer pays for a tow, it may count against your annual roadside assistance usage limit. [USAA, for example, limits how many roadside calls you can make per year](/usaa-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-how-many-times/), and the same is true at most carriers. Check that before assuming you have unlimited calls.

## Will I Get a Replacement Rental Car While Mine Is Being Fixed?

Most rental companies will provide a replacement vehicle if the breakdown is not your fault and you are still within your rental period, but this is not automatic. You have to ask, and availability depends on the location. If you are in a small market or at an airport counter with limited inventory, you may wait hours.

If you purchased the rental company's roadside plan, replacement vehicle coverage is often included. If you relied on your credit card benefit, check the card's benefit guide: cards like the Chase Sapphire Reserve include trip interruption protection that can reimburse alternative transportation costs, but you need to file a claim afterward with receipts.

## Common Questions

**Q: If I didn't buy the rental company's roadside plan, am I just on my own?**
A: Not necessarily. Check your personal auto insurance for a roadside rider, your credit card benefits guide, and your AAA membership if you have one. One of those usually covers it.

**Q: Can I call any tow truck I want when broken down in a rental car?**
A: No. Call the rental company first. If you hire your own tow without their approval, you may be billed for the tow, storage fees, and any unauthorized repair attempts. The rental company controls where the vehicle goes.

**Q: Does AAA cover me in a rental car?**
A: Yes. AAA membership covers the member in any vehicle they are driving, including rentals. Call the AAA member line and give your location. They dispatch a truck the same way they would for your personal car.

**Q: What if the rental car breaks down in another state or far from a city?**
A: The rental company's emergency line handles out-of-area breakdowns. Give them your exact mile marker and the highway number. Response times in rural areas can be longer, so note that in your call and ask for an estimated arrival window.

**Q: Will a rental car breakdown affect my personal insurance rates?**
A: If you file a roadside-only claim (tow, jump start, lockout), it typically does not affect your premium the way a collision claim would. But if there is a damage dispute with the rental company that flows through your liability coverage, that is treated more like a standard claim.
