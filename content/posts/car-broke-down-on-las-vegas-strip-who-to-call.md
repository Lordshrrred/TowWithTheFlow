> **Quick Answer:** Call 911 if you're blocking traffic or in danger. If you're safely off the road, call your roadside assistance provider first (AAA, your insurer, or your automaker's app). If you have none, call a licensed tow company directly. On the Strip, expect to pay $75 to $150 for a local tow. Do not accept a ride from anyone who approaches your car uninvited.

## Who Do I Actually Call First When My Car Dies on the Strip?

Your first call depends on where you're sitting. If your car is stopped in a travel lane on Las Vegas Boulevard between Sahara Avenue and Russell Road, that's an active traffic situation. Call 911, tell them your location and that your vehicle is disabled, and get out of the car on the sidewalk side. Metro Police will respond and can help manage traffic while you wait.

If you're already in a casino parking structure, a side street, or you've managed to coast to the curb, skip 911. Call your roadside assistance provider instead. That's AAA, your insurance company's roadside line, or the roadside program through your automaker (GM's Roadside Assistance, Ram Care, etc.). Give them your exact address or drop a pin. The Strip has landmarks every hundred feet, so telling them "south side of Flamingo Road on Las Vegas Boulevard, in front of the Horseshoe" is enough.

No roadside coverage at all? Call a tow company directly. Search "tow truck Las Vegas" and call two or three for a quote before you commit. Prices vary and the Strip is a high-demand zone, especially on weekends. See [what towing rates look like in the Strip area](/towing-cost-las-vegas-nevada-strip-area-rates/) before you agree to anything.

## Is It Safe to Stay in My Car on Las Vegas Boulevard?

No. If your car is on the main roadway, get out immediately and move to the sidewalk. Las Vegas Boulevard through the resort corridor carries heavy tourist traffic at all hours, and the lanes are narrow near major intersections like Flamingo, Tropicana, and Spring Mountain Road. Staying inside gives you almost no protection if another driver doesn't see you.

If you're in a casino driveway or valet lane and your car won't move, stay with the vehicle, turn on your hazards, and tell a nearby valet or security guard. Casino staff deal with breakdowns regularly and can help coordinate a tow or at minimum keep other cars from boxing you in.

At night, wear something light-colored if you have it and stay well away from the travel lanes. The Strip is lit, but that doesn't mean drivers are paying attention.

## Will Metro Police or a Tow Truck Show Up on Their Own?

Metro Police may dispatch if someone else calls in a disabled vehicle blocking traffic, but don't count on it. On a busy Friday or Saturday night near the LINQ or MGM Grand, response to a non-injury breakdown call can take a while.

If police do respond, they may call a tow truck from their rotation, which is a contracted tow. That tow is not automatically cheaper, and you do not have to use it. You have the right to call your own tow company. Politely tell the officer you've already arranged a tow or you're calling one now. The rotation tow is a convenience, not a requirement.

One thing that speeds everything up: know your exact cross street before you call anyone. "Las Vegas Boulevard and Harmon Avenue" gets help to you faster than "near the Cosmopolitan."

## What If My Car Broke Down Because of the Heat?

Las Vegas heat causes real mechanical failures, not just overheating gauges. If your temperature warning light is on and steam is coming from the hood, pull over immediately, shut off the engine, and do not open the radiator cap. Let it sit at least 30 minutes before anyone touches the coolant system. A pressure-released cap on a hot radiator will burn you.

If your battery died, summer heat degrades lead-acid batteries faster than cold does. A jump start may get you moving temporarily, but if the battery is more than three or four years old, it likely won't hold a charge through the summer. Carry a portable jump starter if you're driving in Las Vegas between May and September. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I just leave my broken-down car on the Strip and come back later?**
A: No. Clark County and LVMPD will have it towed within a short time, especially in the resort corridor. You will then owe both the private tow fee and storage fees. Stay with the car or arrange a tow before you walk away.

**Q: Does AAA work on the Las Vegas Strip?**
A: Yes. AAA dispatches to the Strip and covers the full resort corridor. Classic members get a free tow up to 5 miles, Plus up to 100 miles. Wait times on busy weekend nights can run 45 to 90 minutes, so call as soon as the car stops moving.

**Q: What if my car breaks down in a casino parking garage on the Strip?**
A: Tell casino security immediately. They can keep traffic moving around you and may have a preferred tow company on call. You are still free to call your own tow provider. Most Strip casino garages are private property, so Metro will not typically respond unless asked.

**Q: Can I get a jump start on the Strip without calling a tow truck?**
A: Yes. AAA, your insurer's roadside line, and several app-based services (Urgently, Honk) offer jump starts as a standalone service, usually cheaper than a full tow. If another driver offers to jump you, make sure they know what they're doing. A wrong connection on a modern vehicle with a battery management system can damage the electrical system.

**Q: What's the fastest way to find a reputable tow company near the Strip right now?**
A: Search "tow truck Las Vegas" and call two or three companies for a quote before you agree. Ask for the total price including hookup fee and per-mile rate before they dispatch. Avoid any company that won't give you a price over the phone.
