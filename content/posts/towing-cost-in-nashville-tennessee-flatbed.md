> **Quick Answer:** A local flatbed tow in Nashville usually costs $100 to $250, depending on distance, time of day, and your vehicle. Expect a base hookup fee plus a per-mile charge after the first few miles. Nights, weekends, and interstate pickups push the price up. Get the total quoted before the truck rolls, and ask if it's all-in.

## How Much Does a Flatbed Tow Cost in Nashville?

Most local flatbed tows in Nashville run $100 to $250 all-in. Rates vary by company, and I won't hand you a made-up average, so treat this as a working range and confirm with whoever you call.

Here's how the bill is usually built:

- **Hookup fee:** the flat charge to load your car, often covering the first few miles.
- **Per-mile charge:** added after the included distance. Check the [Nashville per-mile breakdown](/towing-cost-in-nashville-tennessee-per-mile/) for how that stacks up.
- **After-hours surcharge:** nights, weekends, and holidays often cost more.
- **Extras:** winching a car out of a ditch, tight access, or a locked wheel can add labor charges.

For a wider look at what companies charge here, see [flatbed rates in Nashville](/towing-cost-nashville-tennessee-flatbed-rates/).

## Why Would I Need a Flatbed Instead of a Regular Tow Truck?

You need a flatbed when your car can't safely roll on its own wheels. A flatbed carries the whole vehicle off the ground, so nothing drags or spins.

Insist on a flatbed if your car is:

- **All-wheel drive or four-wheel drive.** Towing these with two wheels down can damage the drivetrain. Many Subarus, Audis, and most 4x4 trucks fall in this group.
- **Low to the ground,** like a sports car or anything with a front splitter.
- **Damaged in the drivetrain,** with a transmission that won't shift or an axle that's broken. See [how to know if your car needs a flatbed](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/).
- **Wrecked or stuck,** with bent wheels or flat tires.

If a driver shows up with a wheel-lift truck for an AWD car, don't let them load it. Send them back or call another company.

## What Do I Do If My Car Breaks Down on I-40 or I-65 in Nashville?

Get the car as far onto the shoulder as you can, turn on your hazards, and get everyone out and away from traffic if you can do it safely. Interstate shoulders around Nashville are tight, especially on I-40 near the downtown loop and I-65 through the Briley Parkway and Rosa Parks Boulevard stretch.

Then do this:

1. **Stay out of the car if the shoulder is narrow.** Stand well behind the guardrail when one is available.
2. **Call 911 if you feel unsafe** or traffic is passing very close. For road conditions, 511 and the TDOT SmartWay app help you see what's ahead.
3. **Call roadside assistance first if you have it.** Your insurer or AAA may cover the tow, which can drop your cost to zero. See [how far insurance will tow your car](/how-far-will-insurance-tow-my-car-after-a-breakdown/).

## What Should I Ask Before Agreeing to a Flatbed Tow?

Ask for the total price, in full, before the truck leaves the lot. Vague quotes are where overcharges start.

Ask these questions:

- "What's the total, including hookup, mileage, and any fees?"
- "Is that price good for after-hours?"
- "Do you take cards, or is it cash only?"
- "How long until you arrive?"
- "Where are you taking the car, and is there a storage fee?"

If they quote a number, get it again by text when you can. A written record helps if the final bill doesn't match, and you can use it if you need to [dispute an overcharge](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-if-overcharged/).

One tip that saves people money: if your car is going to a specific shop, tell the driver the shop's address when you call, not after loading. Mileage is billed from pickup to drop-off, and a shop near Nolensville Pike versus one across the county line can change the total by a lot.

## What If I Don't Have Insurance or Roadside Coverage?

You pay out of pocket, but you still have options and some control over the price. Call two or three companies and compare quotes while you wait in a safe spot.

Don't flag down a random truck that just shows up. Tow operators that patrol and arrive uninvited are the ones most likely to quote high or push you toward their own storage lot. Call the company yourself.

Having a card ready helps, since many drivers want payment on the spot. For a full breakdown of uninsured costs, read [flatbed towing in Nashville with no insurance](/towing-cost-in-nashville-tennessee-flatbed-no-insurance/).

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a flatbed tow cost per mile in Nashville?**
A: After the included hookup distance, most companies charge a per-mile rate on top of the base fee. Rates differ by company, so ask for the per-mile number and the total for your exact route.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive than a regular tow?**
A: Often slightly, because flatbed trucks cost more to run. For AWD, 4x4, low, or damaged vehicles, the extra cost is worth it because it prevents drivetrain damage.

**Q: Will my insurance pay for a flatbed tow in Nashville?**
A: Only if your policy includes roadside or towing coverage, and it usually has a mileage or dollar limit. Check your policy or call your insurer before you pay out of pocket.

**Q: Can I get a flatbed tow in Nashville at night or on a weekend?**
A: Yes, many companies run 24 hours, but after-hours and holiday calls often cost more. Ask whether the quote already includes the surcharge.

**Q: What if the driver shows up with the wrong truck for my car?**
A: Don't let them load it. If you drive AWD, 4x4, or a low car, refuse a wheel-lift and ask for a flatbed. Call another company if needed.
