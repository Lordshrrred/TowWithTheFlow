> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in San Diego usually runs $100 to $250 total, made up of a hookup fee of roughly $65 to $125 plus $4 to $7 per mile. Flatbeds, nights, weekends, and freeway pickups push the price up. Always get a total quote before the driver hooks up, and ask whether it includes mileage.

## How Much Does a Tow Truck Cost in San Diego?

A typical San Diego tow costs $100 to $250 for a local trip of 5 to 15 miles. These are estimates, and prices vary by company, so treat them as a sanity check rather than a guarantee.

Most tow companies charge in two parts:

- **Hookup fee:** about $65 to $125, charged no matter how short the tow.
- **Mileage:** about $4 to $7 per mile after the first few miles, which are sometimes included.

A breakdown near Mission Valley that gets towed to a shop in Kearny Mesa is a short haul. A tow from Chula Vista to Oceanside is a long one, and the per-mile charge will dominate the bill. For the mileage math, see [Towing Cost in San Diego California Per Mile Breakdown](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-per-mile-breakdown/).

## What Makes a San Diego Tow Cost More?

Flatbed service, after-hours calls, and hard recovery situations are what push a tow past the base rate. Here are the usual add-ons:

- **Flatbed vs. wheel-lift:** Flatbeds often cost $25 to $75 more. All-wheel-drive cars, lowered cars, and anything with transmission trouble need one. See [Towing Cost in San Diego California Flatbed vs Wheel Lift](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/).
- **Time of day:** Nights, weekends, and holidays commonly add a surcharge.
- **Location:** A car on the shoulder of I-5 or I-805 is a simple hookup. A car in a ditch, off a canyon road in the East County, or stuck in a tight underground garage downtown needs winching, which is billed extra.
- **Distance:** Anything beyond the included miles adds up quickly. Compare options in [Towing Cost in San Diego California Local vs Long Distance](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-local-vs-long-distance/).
- **Wait and storage fees:** If the shop is closed when you arrive, the car may go to the tow yard, where daily storage fees start.

## What Should I Do Right Now If My Car Breaks Down in San Diego?

Get the car as far off the road as you safely can, turn on your hazards, and call for a tow only after you're out of traffic. Then follow these steps:

1. **On a freeway (I-5, I-8, I-15, I-805, SR-163, SR-52):** Pull onto the right shoulder, exit the car on the passenger side, and stand well away from traffic. San Diego freeways are fast and crowded, so the shoulder is not a safe place to sit inside the car.
2. **Check your coverage first.** AAA, your insurance, or even your credit card or new-car warranty may include free towing. A call that takes two minutes can save you $150.
3. **Get a total price in writing.** Ask for the hookup fee, the per-mile rate, whether mileage is included, and any surcharges. A text message is enough.

## What If I Don't Have Insurance or Roadside Assistance?

You pay out of pocket, but you still have leverage: get two quotes, ask about cash discounts, and tow only as far as you need to. Many shops in neighborhoods like Clairemont, City Heights, and Pacific Beach will accept a vehicle after hours and let you sort out the repair the next day, which can be cheaper than towing a long distance to a preferred shop.

Pay with a card when you can so you have a dispute option. Be wary of any driver who arrives unrequested at a breakdown scene and quotes a high price on the spot. If you didn't call them, you don't have to use them. For more on this scenario, read [Towing Cost in San Diego California No Insurance](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-no-insurance/).

## How Can I Avoid Getting Overcharged on a Tow?

Never agree to a tow without a total price, and never let a driver hook up first and discuss money later. Watch for these warning signs:

- A quote that's only the hookup fee, with mileage "to be determined"
- Pressure to decide immediately
- A cash-only demand with no receipt
- A destination that suddenly changes to a yard you didn't pick

Keep your receipt and the driver's company name. If the final bill is higher than the quote, dispute it right away rather than weeks later. For a broader look at pricing in this area, see [Towing Cost in San Diego California](/towing-cost-san-diego-california/).

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a short tow cost in San Diego?**
A: A tow of a few miles typically runs $100 to $175, mostly made up of the hookup fee. Distance adds roughly $4 to $7 per mile beyond that.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive than a regular tow in San Diego?**
A: Usually yes, by about $25 to $75, though some companies charge the same rate. Flatbeds are the right choice for AWD vehicles, low cars, and anything that can't roll freely.

**Q: Will AAA cover my tow in San Diego?**
A: Yes, if you have an active membership, but coverage is limited by tier. The basic level typically covers about 5 miles, and higher tiers cover longer distances. Check your plan's limit before you call.

**Q: Can I be charged extra for being on the freeway?**
A: Some companies add a freeway or recovery surcharge, especially if the pickup is difficult or dangerous. Ask for the total quote before they dispatch.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive in San Diego?**
A: Often 30 to 60 minutes, longer during rush hour on I-5 and I-805 or in bad weather. Ask for an ETA when you call, and call a second company if the wait seems unreasonable.
