Your car just died inside a tunnel. Traffic is still moving. There's no shoulder, maybe no cell signal, and almost no room to maneuver. This is one of the most dangerous breakdown spots possible, and how you handle the next 60 seconds matters.

> **Quick Answer:** Put your hazard lights on immediately. If the car is still rolling, coast to the tunnel exit or the nearest emergency lay-by. If you're completely stopped, stay in the car with your seatbelt on, call 911 or the tunnel emergency line using any available signal, and do not stand behind or beside your vehicle. Tunnel operators monitor cameras and will respond fast.

## What Do I Do the Moment My Car Stops in a Tunnel?

Turn on your hazard lights before you do anything else. That single action buys you time by warning approaching drivers. If the car still has any momentum, use it: steer toward the tunnel wall or an emergency lay-by if one is marked, and get as far out of the travel lane as physically possible.

Once stopped, keep your seatbelt fastened and stay inside the vehicle. The instinct to get out and look under the hood is dangerous here. In a confined tunnel, a car door swinging open into moving traffic can cause a secondary crash. The only reason to exit is if you smell smoke or see flames, in which case you leave through the door closest to the wall, stay against the wall, and walk to the nearest emergency exit.

## How Do I Call for Help When There's No Cell Signal?

Most tunnels have emergency call boxes mounted at regular intervals along the wall, typically every 150 to 300 feet. These are hardwired phones that connect directly to tunnel operations or emergency dispatch regardless of cell coverage. Look for orange or yellow boxes with a phone symbol.

If you do have a cell signal, call 911 first and give them your direction of travel, the tunnel name, and your approximate position inside it. Tunnel operators watch camera feeds continuously and will already be aware of a stopped vehicle within minutes. Do not call a tow company before you call 911. In a tunnel, emergency services coordinate the tow, and sending a truck in without authorization creates additional hazards.

If your engine stopped suddenly with a flashing check engine light and shaking before the breakdown, read [Check Engine Light Flashing and Car Shaking: Pull Over Now](/check-engine-light-flashing-car-shaking-pull-over-now/) for what that sequence usually means mechanically.

## Is It Safe to Wait Inside the Car or Should I Get Out?

Stay in the car with your seatbelt on unless there is fire or smoke. This is the standard guidance from tunnel safety agencies and it runs counter to what most people feel like doing. A stopped car with hazard lights on is visible. A person standing in a tunnel lane or even on a narrow walkway is not.

If the tunnel has a designated pedestrian walkway separated from traffic by a solid barrier, you can move there while waiting. But a narrow painted line on the road surface is not a safe refuge. Sit tight, keep the hazards blinking, and wait for tunnel staff or emergency responders. Response times inside monitored tunnels are fast, often under five minutes.

## What Happens When Help Arrives: Will They Tow Me Right Away?

Tunnel responders will push or tow your vehicle to the nearest exit first, then figure out the mechanical situation. They will not do a repair inside the tunnel. Once you are clear of the tunnel, a flatbed or wheel-lift tow truck takes over for transport to a shop.

## What Warning Signs Should Have Told Me This Was Coming?

A sudden stall in a tunnel rarely comes from nowhere. Fuel pump failure is one of the more common causes: the engine sputters under load, then cuts out completely. A serpentine belt snap will kill your alternator and power steering simultaneously and cause immediate stall if your engine has an accessory-dependent design. Overheating that you ignored on the approach can trigger a shutdown too.

The honest answer is that most tunnel breakdowns were preventable. If your car was running rough, throwing warning lights, or losing power before you entered, you should have pulled over before going in. Tunnels offer zero margin. If you notice overheating symptoms approaching a tunnel, treat it the same as [Car Overheating on Highway: Should I Pull Over Immediately?](/car-overheating-on-highway-should-i-pull-over-immediately-2/): yes, immediately, before you enter.

## Common Questions

**Q: Should I put out emergency triangles or flares if my car breaks down in a tunnel?**
A: No. Do not set up flares or triangles in a tunnel. Flares are a fire hazard in enclosed spaces, and placing triangles requires you to walk into active traffic lanes with almost no clearance. Hazard lights are your only safe warning device inside a tunnel.

**Q: What if my car breaks down in a tunnel and I have kids or pets with me?**
A: Keep children buckled and in the car. If you must exit due to fire, take them with you and move immediately to the tunnel wall, then walk to the nearest marked emergency exit. Do not try to cross lanes with children.

**Q: Will the tunnel operator call a tow truck for me, or do I have to arrange it myself?**
A: Tunnel operations staff will initiate the tow to clear the lane. You may have the option to choose your own tow company once you are outside the tunnel, but inside, their contractor or incident response team handles it first.

**Q: Can I try to restart the car and drive out of the tunnel myself?**
A: If the engine restarts cleanly and the car drives normally, yes, proceed slowly to the exit. Do not restart and drive if you are losing coolant, smelling burning oil, or the engine is making knocking sounds. Driving a failing engine deeper into a tunnel or stalling again mid-exit creates a worse situation.

**Q: How long will I be stuck if my car breaks down in a high-traffic tunnel?**
A: Monitored tunnels with camera systems typically get a response vehicle to you within 5 to 10 minutes. The vehicle will be pushed or towed out quickly because tunnel operators prioritize clearing the lane. Expect to be outside the tunnel within 15 to 30 minutes in most cases.
