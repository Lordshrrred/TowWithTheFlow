> **Quick Answer:** Stay in your locked car with hazards on. Call 911 if you feel threatened, then call a tow truck. Do not get out to look under the hood, do not accept help from strangers, and do not leave your vehicle unattended. Your car can be replaced. Get yourself to safety first, vehicle second.

## What Do I Do the Moment My Car Breaks Down in an Unsafe Area?

Keep moving if you can. Even on a flat tire or overheating engine, rolling another block or two to a lit, busy area, a gas station, a fast food restaurant, anywhere with people and light, is worth more than stopping exactly where you broke down. If the car is completely dead, put it in park, turn on your hazard lights, and lock every door.

Do not pop the hood. Do not get out to check the tire. Standing outside your car in an unfamiliar neighborhood at night makes you a target. Stay inside, stay low if needed, and keep your phone in your hand.

If you have roadside assistance through AAA, your insurance, or a standalone app like Urgently, call them now. Give them your exact cross streets, not just a general area. If you are not sure of your location, drop a pin in Google Maps and read the coordinates or street names off the screen.

## Is It Safe to Stay in the Car or Should I Leave and Find Help?

Stay in the car unless there is a physical threat to your safety inside it. A locked vehicle is a barrier. Walking an unfamiliar neighborhood alone at night removes that barrier.

The exception: if someone is trying to break in or you feel an immediate threat, get out and move toward any lit public space, a store, a restaurant, anywhere with witnesses and cameras. Leave the car. It can be towed. Call 911 as you move.

If smoke is coming from under the hood, that changes the calculation. Get out and move away from the car, then call for help. A fire risk inside the vehicle outweighs the risk outside. For more on that scenario, see [Car Smoking Under Hood on Side of Road: What to Do Right Now](/car-smoking-under-hood-on-side-of-road-what-to-do/).

## Who Should I Call First, 911 or a Tow Truck?

Call 911 first if you feel personally threatened or unsafe. Police can respond faster than a tow truck and will stay with you until the situation is resolved. In many cities, police dispatchers will also contact a tow for you.

If you feel physically safe but just need to get out of the area, call your tow truck or roadside service first. Be direct with the dispatcher: tell them the neighborhood or intersection and that you want priority response because of the location. Most dispatch services can note an urgency flag on your call.

While you wait, keep the doors locked, keep the hazards running, and keep your phone charged. If your battery is low, a car phone charger or portable battery pack buys you critical time. Sit in the front seat so you can see approaching vehicles clearly through the windshield.

## What Should I Do If Someone Approaches My Car?

Do not roll the window down. Crack it an inch at most if you need to communicate. A brief "I already have help on the way, thanks" through a cracked window is enough.

## What Happens to My Car If I Have to Leave It Behind?

If you have to leave the car for your personal safety, call a tow service as soon as you reach a safe location and tell them where it is. Most tow operators can pick up an unattended vehicle as long as you provide the location, your name, and a callback number.

Check whether you have a police report filed. If you called 911 and an officer responded, there will be a record of the breakdown, which protects you if the car is ticketed or towed by the city before your service arrives. ## Common Questions

**Q: Should I call 911 just because I broke down in a rough area, even if nothing has happened yet?**
A: Yes, you can. 911 is not only for active crimes. Many dispatchers will send a welfare check or have an officer swing by when you explain you are stranded alone in an unsafe area. It costs you nothing to call.

**Q: What if my phone is almost dead and I have to choose one call?**
A: Call 911. They can contact a tow on your behalf and will stay on the line with you. A tow company cannot dispatch police if things escalate.

**Q: Can I leave my hazard lights on without draining the battery while I wait?**
A: Hazards draw relatively low current, but if your car is completely dead, they will not work at all. If the battery is weak but the car still has some power, run the hazards. A dead battery means you need a tow anyway, so the drain is not your biggest concern at that point.

**Q: What if I broke down at night and I cannot read the street signs to give my location?**
A: Open Google Maps or Apple Maps on your phone. Even without data, GPS will place a dot on the map showing your position and the nearest intersection. Screenshot it so you have it ready when you call for help.

**Q: Will my insurance company tow my car from a bad neighborhood, or will they refuse the location?**
A: Licensed tow operators dispatch to the address you give, period. Your insurance's roadside partner cannot refuse a pickup based on neighborhood. If a dispatcher gives you pushback, ask to escalate or call a local tow company directly and submit for reimbursement afterward.
