Flatbed service, after-hours calls, and anything requiring a winch will push you toward the top of that range or past it. If you have no insurance or roadside coverage, expect to pay out of pocket at pickup.

## What Does a Local Tow Actually Cost in Portland Right Now?

A standard wheel-lift tow within Portland city limits costs $75 to $125 for the hookup fee, plus $3 to $5 per mile. A 10-mile haul from a breakdown near the Interstate Bridge on I-5 to a shop in North Portland will typically total $105 to $175. That's the honest number for a daytime, straightforward call with no complications.

A few things push the price up fast:

- **Flatbed service:** Add $25 to $50 over a standard wheel-lift. All-wheel-drive vehicles, low-clearance cars, and anything with a locked-up drivetrain require a flatbed. See the full breakdown in our [Portland flatbed towing cost guide](/towing-cost-portland-oregon-flatbed/).
- **After-hours calls:** Expect a $25 to $50 surcharge for calls between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m.
- **Winch-out:** If your car is off the road surface, such as a ditch on Highway 26 near Sylvan Hill, winching starts around $50 to $100 on top of the tow fee.
- **Storage and impound:** If your car gets towed to a lot rather than a shop of your choosing, daily storage fees in Portland typically run $40 to $65 per day.

## Does Location in Portland Change What I Pay?

Yes, and it matters more than most people think. Distance is the main cost driver, but where you break down also affects response time and whether the dispatcher sends a driver who charges premium rates.

Breaking down on I-84 near the Gateway Transit Center means you're close to multiple shops in the 82nd Avenue corridor, so your total mileage bill stays low. Breaking down on US-26 west of the Sunset Tunnel or on I-205 near Clackamas Town Center puts you farther from the shop-dense parts of the city, which adds miles and cost. Tows from Outer East Portland or Gresham into inner Southeast or Northwest Portland can easily hit 15 to 20 miles, pushing your total to $150 to $225.

If you're on the Burnside Bridge or near the Steel Bridge during peak traffic, response times also stretch, and some companies charge a congestion or standby fee if the driver idles waiting for traffic to clear.

## How Do I Keep from Overpaying on a Portland Tow?

Get the total price confirmed before the driver hooks up your car. This is non-negotiable. Ask for the hookup fee and the per-mile rate, then do the math yourself based on the distance to your destination.

A few specific tips:

1. **Name your destination up front.** Tell the dispatcher exactly where the car is going. A shop in the Pearl District costs more to reach from a breakdown in Lents than a shop on Foster Road does.
2. **Check your credit card.** Visa Signature and some World Mastercard benefits include free towing up to a certain distance. Call the number on the back of your card before paying out of pocket.
3. **Compare two quotes if you have time.** If you're broken down in a safe spot, such as a MAX light rail park-and-ride or a Fred Meyer parking lot, a two-minute call to a second company can save you $30 to $50.
4. **Rainy weather adds complexity.** Portland's wet season brings more calls and stretched dispatch queues. Our [Portland rainy weather towing cost post](/towing-cost-portland-oregon-rainy-weather/) covers what that does to pricing and wait times.

## Common Questions

**Q: What's the average tow truck cost for a short local tow in Portland?**
A: For a tow under 5 miles, expect $85 to $115 total. The hookup fee is usually $75 to $95, and a few miles of mileage adds $15 to $25 on top of that.

**Q: Do Portland tow companies charge more at night or on weekends?**
A: Most do. After-hours and weekend surcharges of $25 to $50 are standard. Always ask the dispatcher whether the quoted rate includes any time-of-day premium before they send a driver.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call AAA than pay a Portland tow company directly?**
A: If you have AAA, yes. Basic membership covers the first 5 miles of towing free, with a per-mile charge after that. For a typical Portland in-city tow, that covers most or all of the mileage cost. Whether [AAA membership is worth the annual cost](/aaa-membership-worth-it-single-tow-cost-comparison/) depends on how often you use it.

**Q: Can I negotiate the tow price with a Portland driver?**
A: Sometimes, especially with independent operators. Established companies usually have fixed rate cards. If you're being quoted something that sounds high, ask for an itemized breakdown. That alone often brings the number down.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive in Portland?**
A: On a normal day in central Portland, 20 to 40 minutes is typical. On I-5 near the Rose Quarter during rush hour or on a rainy evening when calls spike, budget 45 to 75 minutes. Move your car as far off the travel lane as possible and stay behind the guardrail while you wait.
