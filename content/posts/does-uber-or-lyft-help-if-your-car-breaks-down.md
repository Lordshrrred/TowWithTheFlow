Your car just died on the side of the road and you're wondering if you can just call an Uber or Lyft and be done with it. Here's the real answer.

> **Quick Answer:** Uber and Lyft will not tow your car, jump your battery, change your tire, or send any mechanical help. What they will do is give you a ride away from the breakdown scene, which is genuinely useful when you need to get somewhere safe or reach a mechanic. For your actual car, you still need a tow truck or roadside assistance service.

## Will Uber or Lyft Actually Send Help to My Breakdown?

No. Neither app dispatches anything except a driver to pick you up as a passenger. There is no roadside assistance arm, no tow service, no emergency mechanical support. If you open the Uber or Lyft app right now and request a ride, a driver will come to your location and take you where you need to go. Your car stays exactly where it is.

That said, getting yourself out of a dangerous spot is a legitimate use of both apps. If your car breaks down on a highway shoulder or in an unfamiliar area, getting into a safe vehicle and off the roadside is a smart first move while you arrange a tow.

## So What Can I Actually Use Uber or Lyft For During a Breakdown?

You can use them to get yourself to safety, to a mechanic, to a parts store, or to somewhere you can wait comfortably. That is genuinely valuable. If your car dies on I-70 in the middle of nowhere, calling a rideshare to get to the nearest town while a tow truck handles the vehicle is a reasonable plan.

One practical move: once you are in the rideshare, call your tow company or roadside assistance from the backseat. You can give the driver your car's exact location (drop a pin before you leave) and coordinate pickup without standing on a shoulder waiting. For tips on staying safe while waiting for a tow, see [how to stay safe waiting for a tow truck on the highway](/how-to-stay-safe-waiting-for-tow-truck-on-highway/).

## What Should I Do About My Actual Car?

Call a tow truck or roadside assistance first, then call your rideshare if you need to leave the scene. Do not leave before you have a tow arranged and confirmed, or your car could sit there for hours getting ticketed or impounded.

- **Auto insurance roadside assistance.** Many policies include it. Check your app or call your insurer.

- **Credit card roadside assistance.** Some Visa and Mastercard products include this benefit. Check the back of your card or the cardholder portal.
- **Pay out of pocket.** A local tow typically runs $75 to $150 for

## Does Uber Assist or Any App Actually Send Mechanical Help?

There have been various "on-demand roadside assistance" apps over the years, like Honk, Urgently, and Allstate's app-based service. These are not Uber or Lyft. They connect you with local tow operators and roadside technicians for a fee, similar to AAA but without an annual membership. Urgently in particular is used as the backend by several insurance companies and automakers for their roadside programs.

If you want app-based help for the car itself, search for Urgently or Honk in your app store. Expect to pay $50 to $150 depending on what you need and where you are. These services are not free and response times vary by location.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I request an Uber to wait while I figure out what's wrong with my car?**
A: You can ask, but drivers are not required to wait and the meter runs. A standard rideshare is not designed for this. If you need someone on-scene, call a roadside assistance service instead.

**Q: What if my breakdown is in a spot where Uber or Lyft can't pick me up, like a highway median?**
A: Get yourself to a legal pickup location first, like a nearby exit ramp or rest area. Drivers cannot legally stop on most highway shoulders. Walk to the nearest safe spot before requesting the ride.

**Q: Is there any situation where Uber or Lyft is the right first call during a breakdown?**
A: Yes, if you are in immediate danger, getting away from the scene is the priority. Call 911 first if needed, then use a rideshare to reach safety. Arrange the tow once you are out of harm's way.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover the rideshare ride I took after breaking down?**
A: No. Auto insurance covers towing and sometimes rental cars, not rideshare rides. A rideshare cost comes out of your own pocket.

**Q: Can I leave my car on the highway shoulder while I take a rideshare to get help?**
A: For a short time, yes, but you need to know your state's rules. Most states allow a disabled vehicle on a shoulder temporarily, but police can call a tow after a certain window, often one to two hours on interstates.
