> **Quick Answer:** Yes, roadside assistance covers flat tires, not just towing. Most plans send a technician to put your spare on, which counts as a covered service call. If you have no usable spare, or the tire is too damaged, the plan usually falls back to a tow. Plans do not cover buying a new tire or the repair itself.

## Does Roadside Assistance Actually Cover a Flat Tire?

Yes. Nearly every roadside plan, including AAA, insurance add-ons, and credit card or manufacturer programs, lists flat tire service as a standard benefit. A technician arrives, removes the flat, and mounts your spare.

The labor and dispatch are covered. The tire is not. If the flat is shredded or the sidewall is split, you still pay for a repair or replacement at a tire shop afterward.

Check your plan's wording for "tire change" or "flat tire service." If it only says "towing," call and ask before you assume.

## What Happens If I Don't Have a Spare?

Without a usable spare, the plan treats it as a tow. The technician can't change a tire that doesn't exist, so they load the car and haul it to a tire shop, within your plan's mileage limit.

Check this now, because it catches people off guard:

- Many newer cars ship with a tire inflator kit and sealant instead of a spare. The sealant only handles small tread punctures, not sidewall damage.
- Some vehicles carry a compact "donut" spare, which is fine for the swap but limits you to about 50 mph and roughly 50 miles.
- Run-flat tires let you drive a short distance on a deflated tire, but they usually can't be repaired once driven flat.

If you're unsure what your car has, look under the cargo floor or beneath the vehicle. You do not want to learn this on the shoulder.

## What Does a Flat Tire Call Not Cover?

Roadside plans cover the service call, not the parts or the fix. Expect to pay for the tire repair or replacement, any shop labor beyond the roadside swap, and mounting and balancing a new tire.

Other limits to know:

- Call limits per year. Many plans cap you at three or four calls, so a flat is one of them. If you're on a carrier plan, see [how many times per year GEICO roadside assistance can be used](/geico-roadside-assistance-how-many-times-per-year/) for a real example of how those caps work.
- Towing distance caps. Standard coverage often stops at a set mileage, and anything beyond that is billed to you at a per-mile rate.
- Locked lug nuts. If you lost the wheel lock key, the technician may not be able to remove the wheel at all.
- Registration and membership rules. Some programs follow the member, others follow the vehicle. See [whether AAA covers towing if the car isn't registered in your name](/does-aaa-cover-towing-if-car-is-not-registered-in-my-name/).

## What Should I Do Right Now With a Flat Tire?

Get the car out of traffic first, then call. A flat is a fix-

it-later problem, and your safety is the priority.

1. Pull as far right as possible and put your hazards on. If you're on a divided highway, drive slowly on the rim to the next exit rather than stopping in a live lane. A wrecked rim is cheaper than a collision.
2. Stay out of the car if you're on a busy shoulder, standing well away from traffic and behind the guardrail when you can.
3. Call your plan and say "flat tire, I need a tire change." If you say "I need a tow," that's what you'll get, and it may count against a tow-specific limit.
4. Tell them whether you have a spare. It changes what truck they send.
5. Don't change it yourself on a narrow shoulder with fast traffic. Waiting for a technician is the right call there.

## What If I Have No Roadside Plan?

You pay out of pocket, and prices vary by market. A tire swap from an independent operator is typically cheaper than a tow, so ask specifically for a "tire change" and confirm the price before they dispatch.

If the flat is bad enough that the car needs hauling, per-mile rates apply. For a sense of what that looks like, see [towing cost in Columbus, Ohio per mile](/towing-cost-in-columbus-ohio-per-mile/).

Before you hire anyone, ask for a total price including the hook-up fee, not just the per-mile rate.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does AAA change a flat tire or just tow?**
A: AAA will change a flat with your spare if you have one. If there's no spare or the tire can't be mounted, they tow you to a shop within your membership's mileage limit.

**Q: Will roadside assistance pay for a new tire?**
A: No. Plans cover the dispatch and the labor of swapping the spare or towing. The replacement tire and any shop labor are your cost.

**Q: Does a flat tire use up one of my roadside calls?**
A: Yes. Any dispatched service counts toward your annual limit, whether it's a flat, a jump start, or a tow.

**Q: Can I use roadside assistance for a tire I can't remove because of a wheel lock?**
A: The technician usually can't remove a wheel with a locked lug nut and no key. They'll most likely tow you instead, so keep the key in the glovebox, not buried in the trunk.

**Q: How long does a roadside tire change take to arrive?**
A: It depends on your location and demand. Expect anywhere from 30 minutes to a couple of hours, and ask the dispatcher for an estimate when you call. For realistic timing, see [how long it takes a tow truck to arrive on the highway](/how-long-does-it-take-for-a-tow-truck-to-arrive-on-highway/).
