> **Quick Answer:** A flatbed tow out of Las Vegas across state lines is priced by the mile, not as a flat fee. Expect a hookup fee plus roughly $3 to $7 per loaded mile, so a run to Southern California or Phoenix can land in the several-hundred-dollar range. Rates vary by company and time of day. Get the total price in writing before the driver loads your car.

## How Much Does a Flatbed Tow Out of State from Las Vegas Cost?

Most long-distance flatbed tows from Las Vegas are quoted as a hookup fee plus a per-mile rate, and the per-mile rate is usually lower than the local rate. Local tows often charge a high rate for the first few miles. Long-haul tows spread cost over distance, so the per-mile number drops, but the total climbs fast.

Exact prices change by company, season, and demand, so treat any figure you read online, including mine, as a starting point. Call at least two companies and compare. What you want from each:

- The hookup or base fee
- The per-mile rate and whether it counts loaded miles only
- Any after-hours, weekend, or holiday surcharge
- Fuel surcharge
- Total estimated price for your exact destination

## Why Does Distance to Another State Change the Price So Much?

Distance changes the price because the driver and truck are tied up for the whole round trip, and the company has to cover deadhead miles (the empty drive back to Las Vegas). That is why a 270-mile run toward Los Angeles costs far more than a 15-mile run to a shop off Tropicana.

Common out-of-state destinations from Las Vegas, and what the route looks like:

- **Southern California:** I-15 south through Primm, across Mountain Pass, and down through Barstow. Roughly 270 miles to Los Angeles.
- **Phoenix:** US-93 south through Boulder City, over the Hoover Dam bypass bridge, then down toward Kingman and Wickenburg. Roughly 300 miles.
- **Utah:** I-15 north through the Virgin River Gorge toward St. George. Roughly 120 miles to St. George.

Summer matters too. A heavy tow across the Mojave in July puts strain on the truck and driver, and some companies price that in. Our [flatbed summer heat pricing guide](/towing-cost-in-las-vegas-nevada-flatbed-summer-heat/) covers it.

## Do I Need a Flatbed for an Out-of-State Tow?

For a long-distance haul, a flatbed is almost always the right call, and many companies will refuse to wheel-lift a vehicle for 200-plus miles. Wheel-lift towing drags two wheels on the road for the entire trip, which adds wear and risk over that distance.

Insist on a flatbed if your car is all-wheel drive, four-wheel drive, a low-clearance or luxury vehicle, an EV, or has any transmission or drivetrain damage. Towing an AWD vehicle on two wheels can damage the drivetrain. If you're unsure, run through [how to know if your car needs a flatbed tow](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/).

## How Do I Get Towed Out of State Without Getting Overcharged?

Get the full price in writing (text or email) before the driver touches your car, and confirm it is a total, not just

 a per-mile rate. Long-distance tows are where surprise bills happen, because "per mile" sounds cheap until it is multiplied by 270.

Do this in order:

1. **Get your exact location.** If you're stuck on I-15 near the Spaghetti Bowl or off the Strip, note the nearest exit or cross streets so quotes are accurate.
2. **Call two or three flatbed companies** and ask for an out-the-door total to your destination.
3. **Ask if they are licensed for interstate towing.** A company hauling across state lines should hold the proper authority. Legit operators answer this without hesitation.
4. **Confirm the drop-off.** Know the exact address and that someone can receive the car, or that the lot is open when you arrive.
5. **Photograph your car** from all sides before loading, and keep the receipt.

## Is It Cheaper to Tow Locally and Repair in Las Vegas?

Often, yes. If the car is not drivable, a local tow to a Las Vegas shop costs a small fraction of a 300-mile haul. Out-of-state towing makes sense when the car is worth the cost, you have a trusted shop at home, or you are relocating anyway.

Compare the total. A tow to a shop in the Southwest Valley or near the I-15 and Sunset Road area may cost under a couple hundred dollars, while a haul to Phoenix could cost several times that. Get a diagnosis first if you can. A dead battery or a bad alternator is a very different decision than a failed transmission.

Check your coverage before paying out of pocket. Some insurers and roadside plans cap towing at a set mileage, often well under 100 miles, and charge you per mile beyond it.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a flatbed tow from Las Vegas to Los Angeles cost?**
A: It varies by company and demand, but expect a base fee plus a per-mile charge over roughly 270 miles, which lands in the several-hundred-dollar range. Get a written, out-the-door total from at least two companies before agreeing.

**Q: Will AAA tow my car out of state from Las Vegas?**
A: AAA covers towing up to a mileage limit set by your membership level, and anything beyond that you pay per mile. Check your plan's tow distance before you call, because a cross-state haul usually exceeds the base limit.

**Q: Can a Las Vegas tow company legally tow my car to another state?**
A: Yes, if the company is properly licensed for interstate towing. Ask directly whether they hold interstate authority, and get the quote and destination in writing.

**Q: Is it cheaper to rent a car hauler or trailer instead?**
A: Sometimes, but only if your vehicle can be safely loaded and you have a truck rated to pull it. If the car won't roll, steer, or run, a professional flatbed is safer and often worth the cost.

**Q: Do tow companies charge extra for nights and weekends out of state?**
A: Many do. After-hours, weekend, and holiday surcharges are common, so ask about them when you get your quote. See our guide on [emergency towing cost on weekends](/how-much-does-emergency-towing-cost-on-weekends/).
