> **Quick Answer:** Your comprehensive auto insurance typically covers towing after an ice-related accident if you have collision coverage. Roadside assistance through insurance or AAA may also apply. Without coverage, you pay out of pocket, usually $75-200 depending on distance and complexity.

## What To Do

1. **Call 911 if injured** or blocking traffic
2. **Contact your insurance company** immediately to report the incident
3. **Check your roadside assistance coverage** before calling a tow truck
4. **Ask the tow operator about payment** before they hook up your vehicle
5. **Take photos** of the scene and vehicle damage for insurance claims
6. **Get a receipt** from the towing company regardless of who pays

## Insurance Coverage for Ice Accidents

**Collision coverage** pays for towing when your car slides off the road due to ice. This applies even if no other vehicles are involved. A single-car slide-off on a frozen back road counts as a collision claim the same way a fender bender does. Your deductible still applies, so if your deductible is $500 and the tow costs $150, you are paying that out of pocket anyway. Factor that in before filing a claim, since a small claim can affect your rates.

**Roadside assistance** through your insurance may cover the tow if you have this add-on. Maximum coverage is usually $100-150 per incident. Crucially, roadside assistance does not require you to file a collision claim, so using it will not trigger a rate increase the way a collision claim might. If the tow falls within that limit, this is almost always the smarter option.

**Comprehensive coverage alone** typically won't cover ice slide-offs unless you hit an animal or object. Ice on the road is not a covered peril under comprehensive. Do not assume you are covered just because you pay for comprehensive. Check your declarations page or call your agent and ask specifically about single-vehicle collision events.

One important edge case: if a municipality was negligent in treating a known ice hazard, you may have grounds to pursue the local government for reimbursement. This is rare and slow, but worth noting if the conditions were extreme and documented.

## Roadside Assistance Options

- **AAA membership**: Covers towing up to certain mileage limits
- **Credit card benefits**: Some cards include roadside assistance
- **Vehicle manufacturer programs**: Check if your car has built-in coverage
- **Cell phone carrier plans**: Some include roadside help

AAA Classic covers towing up to 5 miles per incident, while Plus and Premier tiers extend that to 100 miles or more. If you are on a rural road and the nearest lot is 30 miles out, your tier matters a lot. Credit card benefits are frequently overlooked and can cover $50-100 per use, though they often require you to pay first and submit for reimbursement. Manufacturer programs like Ford Roadside Assistance or Toyota's connected services are worth a quick check in your glove box paperwork before you assume you have nothing.

## What It Might Cost

- **Short distance tow**: $75-125
- **Highway or long distance**: $125-200+
- **Winching from ditch**: Additional $50-100
- **After-hours service**: 25-50% surcharge

Winching is where costs climb fast. If your front wheels are buried in a ditch and the truck has to set up a pulley system or use a second vehicle for a recovery, you can be looking at $200-350 before the actual tow even starts. Winter tows from rural areas at 2 a.m. on a weekend can easily reach $400 total when you stack the after-hours surcharge on top of distance and recovery fees. Get a firm quote before they attach anything to your vehicle.

## When You Pay Out of Pocket

You'll pay directly if you have:
- Liability-only insurance
- No roadside assistance coverage
- Exceeded your annual roadside limits
- Used a non-approved towing service

Some insurers require you to use a preferred tow provider or they will only reimburse a set rate. If you call a random operator and they charge $250 but your insurer caps reimbursement at $100, you absorb the difference. Always ask your insurer for a preferred provider before the truck arrives when possible.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will filing a tow claim after sliding on ice raise my insurance rates?**
A: It depends on how you file. Using a standalone roadside assistance benefit typically does not affect your rates. Filing a collision claim might, depending on your insurer and your state's regulations. Call your agent and ask before deciding which route to take.

**Q: What if I slid off the road but the car is still drivable?**
A: If the car drives safely and you can get it back on the road without a tow, you do not need to file any claim at all. Only call for a tow if the vehicle is stuck, damaged, or unsafe to drive. Unnecessary claims are never worth the potential rate impact.

**Q: Does AAA cover winching, or just towing?**
A: AAA covers winching as part of their standard towing service in most situations, as long as the vehicle is accessible from a public road. Severe off-road recovery or situations requiring special equipment may fall outside standard coverage, so confirm with AAA dispatch before they send a truck.

## Stay Safe

- Turn on hazard lights immediately
- Exit vehicle away from traffic if safe
- Stay visible with reflectors or phone flashlight
- Do not attempt to drive out if wheels are spinning
- Wait inside vehicle if on a busy road
