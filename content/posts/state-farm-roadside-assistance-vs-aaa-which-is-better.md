> **Quick Answer:** AAA wins for dedicated roadside coverage, especially if you drive a lot or travel long distances. State Farm roadside assistance is cheaper to add onto an existing policy but comes with dollar caps that can leave you short on a long tow. If you already have State Farm auto insurance, check your towing dollar limit before assuming you're covered. If you want unlimited-distance towing and more services, AAA is the stronger standalone choice.

## What Does Each Plan Actually Cover When I'm Stranded?

Both plans cover the basics: towing, jump starts, flat tire changes, fuel delivery, and lockout service. The difference is depth. AAA Classic, the entry-level tier, tows you up to 5 miles per call. AAA Plus bumps that to 100 miles, and AAA Premier covers up to 200 miles per tow. State Farm's roadside assistance is an add-on to your auto policy, and it works on a reimbursement-or-dispatch model with a per-incident dollar cap, typically $75 to $100 depending on your state and policy. A tow across town or a long haul after a highway breakdown can easily run past that cap. For specific details on how that dollar limit plays out, see our breakdown of [State Farm roadside assistance dollar limits for towing](/state-farm-roadside-assistance-dollar-limit-towing/).

## Which One Saves Me More Money Over a Year?

State Farm roadside assistance costs roughly $5 to $10 per vehicle per year added to an existing policy. That's hard to beat on price alone. AAA Classic runs about $60 to $80 per year depending on region, with AAA Plus around $100 to $120. If you have a reliable car and rarely break down, State Farm's add-on is cheaper and probably enough. The math shifts if you drive a high-mileage vehicle, live in a rural area, or travel frequently. One tow over State Farm's dollar cap can cost you $150 to $300 out of pocket, which wipes out years of premium savings in a single call.

## What Happens If I Need a Tow More Than 5 or 10 Miles?

State Farm's roadside add-on doesn't cap by distance. It caps by dollar amount per incident. So if your tow costs $200 and your policy cap is $75, you pay the $125 difference out of pocket. AAA Classic's 5-mile tow limit works differently: beyond 5 miles, they'll still tow you, but you pay per additional mile. With AAA Plus at 100 miles covered, most breakdowns, including highway blowouts or engine failures, are covered completely. If you drive interstates regularly, AAA Plus is the safer plan. For how towing distance limits work with insurance add-ons more broadly, the [GEICO roadside assistance towing distance limit](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-distance-limit/) article covers the same mechanic and is worth reading for comparison.

## Which Should I Pick If I Have State Farm Insurance Already?

Keep the State Farm add-on only if your car is reliable, your drives are short, and you have a credit card to cover gaps. If your vehicle has over 100,000 miles or you regularly drive more than 50 miles from home, add AAA Plus on top. The combined cost is still under $150 a year for most people. Having both is not redundant, because AAA's dispatch network is faster and broader in most regions, and its towing coverage won't leave you holding a bill halfway through. State Farm's add-on also covers only listed vehicles on the policy. AAA membership covers you as a person in any vehicle, including rentals and friends' cars, which is a real difference if you're a frequent traveler or a family with multiple drivers.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I use State Farm roadside assistance on someone else's car?**
A: No. State Farm's roadside add-on is tied to the specific vehicle listed on your policy. AAA membership follows you as a person, so it works in any car you're riding in or driving, including rentals.

**Q: Does AAA cover towing after an accident, or only mechanical breakdowns?**
A: AAA covers towing after accidents in most cases, but it depends on your membership tier and local club rules. Check your specific AAA club's terms. For a deeper look at accident towing coverage, see our article on [does AAA cover towing after an accident or only breakdowns](/does-aaa-cover-towing-after-accident-or-only-breakdowns/).

**Q: How fast does AAA actually respond compared to State Farm's dispatch?**
A: AAA has its own network of contracted towers with tracked ETAs. State Farm dispatches through third-party networks, and response times vary more by region. In urban areas both are usually under an hour. In rural areas, AAA's larger network often reaches you faster.

**Q: What if my State Farm roadside call costs more than my dollar limit?**
A: You pay the difference directly to the tow company. State Farm either reimburses up to the cap or dispatches and pays the driver up to the cap, depending on your policy. Always confirm your per-incident limit before you need it so you're not surprised on the side of the road.

**Q: Is it worth having both AAA and State Farm roadside at the same time?**
A: Usually yes, if you travel often or have an older vehicle. Use AAA as your primary for dispatch and long tows, and the State Farm add-on costs so little that dropping it saves almost nothing. Having both just means more options and no out-of-pocket surprises.
