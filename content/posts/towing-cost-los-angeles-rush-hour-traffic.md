> **Quick Answer:** Towing during Los Angeles rush hour costs $150-$300 for the base tow, plus $50-$100 in traffic delay fees. Morning rush (7-9 AM) and evening rush (4-7 PM) add 30-45 minutes to response times. Freeway breakdowns cost more than surface streets due to CHP requirements and safety equipment.

## What To Do

1. **Call 911 if you're on a freeway** - CHP must clear the scene before tow trucks can work safely in traffic lanes. Expect CHP to arrive within 10-20 minutes on major freeways like the 405, 101, or 10. Until they do, no tow operator will approach your lane-side vehicle. That wait alone can push your total roadside time past an hour during peak traffic.

2. **Get to the shoulder immediately** - Even if your car is barely running, move as far right as possible. Traffic delays cost you money. A car sitting in a travel lane triggers CHP mandatory response, which adds $50-$80 in incident management fees on top of your tow bill. A car already on the shoulder lets the tow truck go straight to work.

3. **Call your insurance roadside assistance first** - [Progressive insurance towing reimbursement](/progressive-insurance-towing-reimbursement-amount/) and other major carriers often cover $100-$150, which helps with rush hour surcharges. Call them before you call a private tow company. Private operators who arrive without authorization from your insurer may not be reimbursable.

4. **Request ETA and traffic fee upfront** - Ask the dispatcher: "What's your rush hour traffic charge?" Don't get surprised by a $75 delay fee. Also confirm whether the quoted price is door-to-door or hook-to-destination, since some LA operators charge separately for winching a disabled car onto the flatbed.

5. **Specify your exact location** - "405 North at Wilshire exit" gets you faster service than "somewhere on the 405." Precision matters in heavy traffic. Include visible landmarks, the direction of travel, and whether you're before or after an on-ramp. Dispatchers in LA juggle dozens of calls during rush hour and a vague location adds 10-15 minutes just in routing.

6. **Stay in your vehicle only if safe** - On surface streets, stay inside. On freeways, exit passenger side and get behind the barrier if possible. If there is no barrier and you are on the right shoulder with traffic passing close, stay belted in the passenger seat rather than standing outside.

## What It Might Cost

**Base towing rates in LA:**
- Local tow (5-10 miles): $125-$200
- [Freeway breakdown](/towing-cost-los-angeles-freeway-breakdown/): $150-$250
- Long distance (20+ miles): $4-$6 per mile after first 5 miles

**Rush hour surcharges:**
- Traffic delay fee: $50-$100
- Extended response time (over 45 minutes): Additional $25-$50
- Freeway safety equipment: $25-$40 extra

**Peak traffic times with highest costs:**
- Monday-Friday 7:00-9:30 AM
- Monday-Friday 4:00-7:30 PM
- Friday evenings until 8:30 PM

Expect total costs of $200-$400 for rush hour towing. [Towing cost from highway to nearest exit](/towing-cost-from-highway-to-nearest-exit/) is often your cheapest option if your car can be safely moved off the freeway first. A short tow from the 405 to a gas station at the next exit might run $100-$150, versus a full tow to a shop across town at $300-$400. Get dropped at a safe lot, then arrange the longer tow during off-peak hours if cost is a concern.

The day of the week also matters. Friday afternoon on the 10 freeway westbound near downtown is among the worst scenarios in LA. Response times there during 4-7 PM regularly exceed 60 minutes, and you may see a $90-$100 delay fee tacked onto the base rate. Tuesday or Wednesday midday, the same tow could be half the wait and no surcharge at all.

**Payment:** Most LA tow companies require cash or card payment on scene. Credit cards add 3-4% processing fees. Some operators do not carry change for cash, so carry small bills or plan to pay by card and absorb the fee.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I refuse a tow company the CHP calls and wait for my own?**
A: Yes. If CHP calls a tow from their rotation, you are not required to use it. Politely tell the officer you want to call your own provider or insurance roadside assistance. You may have to wait longer, but CHP rotation tows in LA often charge $300-$500 for a basic freeway tow.

**Q: Do rush hour towing fees show up on the invoice or get added verbally at the end?**
A: Reputable companies itemize every charge on the invoice. Ask for a written or texted quote before the truck hooks up, and confirm the final amount before you sign. If a driver quotes one price on the phone and a higher one on arrival, you have the right to refuse service before hookup.

**Q: Is AAA faster than calling a private tow company during LA rush hour?**
A: Not always. AAA contracts with local providers in LA, so response time depends on who is available in your zone. During peak hours, AAA estimated wait times can reach 60-90 minutes. Their main advantage is cost coverage, not speed. If you are in immediate danger, call 911 first regardless of membership.

## Stay Safe

- Never exit your vehicle into traffic lanes
- Turn on hazard lights immediately
- Call someone to let them know your location
- Keep phone charged for GPS tracking updates to tow truck
- Stay hydrated - LA traffic delays can stretch towing waits to 90+ minutes
- If on surface streets, stay inside your locked vehicle
- On freeways, exit passenger side only if there's a safe barrier area
