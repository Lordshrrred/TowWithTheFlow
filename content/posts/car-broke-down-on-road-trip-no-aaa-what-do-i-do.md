## What Do I Do First When My Car Breaks Down in the Middle of a Road Trip?

Get the car off the road and get your hazards on, that's the immediate priority. If you can still roll, aim for an exit, a rest area, or at minimum a wide, flat shoulder well clear of the travel lane. Do not stop on a bridge, in a curve, or near a highway interchange where visibility is low.

Once stopped, stay inside the vehicle with your seatbelt on if you're on a high-speed road. A car on the shoulder of an interstate is a sitting target. Only get out if there's smoke or fire. Set up road flares or emergency triangles at least 100 feet behind the car if you have them.

## Do I Really Need AAA, or Are There Other Ways to Get a Tow?

You do not need AAA to get towed. AAA is a paid membership service. Plenty of independent tow companies operate in every corridor of the country and take direct payment by card.

Here's how to find one without a membership:

- **Google Maps:** Search "tow truck near me" and call the top-rated local result directly. Read the one-star reviews to spot price gougers before you call.
- **Your auto insurance app:** Open it now. Companies like State Farm, GEICO, and Progressive often include roadside assistance as an add-on. Many drivers have it and don't realize it. [Does State Farm roadside assistance cover towing?](/does-state-farm-roadside-assistance-cover-towing-cost/) Worth checking before you pay out of pocket.
- **Your credit card:** Visa Signature, certain Chase cards, and American Express cards offer roadside dispatch as a card benefit. Check your card's benefits page.
- **Motor clubs:** Allstate Motor Club and Better World Club offer pay-per-use options, but in a breakdown moment, calling a tow truck directly is usually faster.

If you're somewhere remote with no cell signal, read this: [what to do if your car broke down with no cell service on a highway](/car-broke-down-no-cell-service-on-highway-what-to-do/).

## How Much Will a Tow Cost Me Without a Membership?

A local tow of 5 to 10 miles typically runs $75 to $150 out of pocket. Most companies charge a $50 to $75 hook-up fee plus $3 to $7 per mile after the first few.

If you've broken down far from your destination, the math gets painful fast. A 50-mile tow can easily run $250 to $400. A long-distance tow across multiple states is a different situation entirely. See [how much a long-distance tow truck costs per mile](/how-much-does-a-long-distance-tow-truck-cost-per-mile/) before you authorize anything.

One thing to know: tow companies on or near interstates sometimes charge more than companies a few miles off the highway. If you have time to make two calls, get a quote from a shop in the nearest town, not just the first company that answers.

## Where Should I Get the Car Towed To?

Tow to the nearest reputable repair shop that can actually diagnose what you have. On a road trip, "nearest" matters more than "my usual mechanic back home."

## What If It's a Minor Problem I Can Handle Roadside?

Check the obvious stuff before you call for a tow and spend $100. A flat tire, dead battery, or overheating engine all have potential roadside fixes if you have the right tools or help.

- **Flat tire:** If you have a spare and a jack, you can change it. No spare? [Here's what to do when you blew a tire with no spare](/blew-a-tire-on-highway-no-spare-what-to-do-now/).
- **Dead battery:** Anyone with jumper cables and a running car can help. Ask another driver or a trucker at a rest stop. Most people will stop.
- **Overheating:** Pull over immediately, shut the engine off, and do not open the radiator cap when the engine is hot. The coolant system runs under pressure and can cause serious burns. Wait 30 minutes minimum before touching anything. [Car overheating on the highway](/car-overheating-on-highway-do-i-need-a-tow/) usually means low coolant or a failed water pump. Neither is driveable.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I call a tow truck without AAA on a road trip?**
A: Yes, absolutely. AAA is optional. Search "tow truck near me" on Google Maps, call directly, and pay by card. You'll get the same tow truck, just without the membership discount.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover a tow if I break down on vacation?**
A: It depends on your policy. If you added roadside assistance as an endorsement, yes. Check your insurance app or call your insurer's claims line before paying out of pocket. Many drivers pay for this coverage and never use it.

**Q: How long will I have to wait on the side of the road for a tow truck?**
A: In most metro areas and along major interstates, 30 to 60 minutes is typical. In rural stretches, waits of 90 minutes to 2 hours are common. Ask the estimated arrival time when you call, and call 911 if you feel unsafe while you wait.

**Q: Should I accept a tow from a truck that just shows up without me calling?**
A: Be cautious. Predatory towing does happen near interstates. If a truck pulls up unsolicited, ask for the company name, get a written estimate before authorizing anything, and confirm where they plan to take the car. You have the right to choose your own tow destination in most states.

**Q: What if I can't afford the tow right now?**
A: Most tow companies take credit cards. If you genuinely have no way to pay, call 911 and explain the situation. State police can sometimes connect you with resources, or will at least document your location for safety. Some states also operate free motorist assistance patrols on certain interstates during peak travel periods.
