> **Quick Answer:** Stay calm and don't brake in panic. Pump the pedal hard and fast, since it may build pressure. Downshift to lower gears, then pull the parking brake slowly and steadily. Turn on hazard lights, honk, and steer toward an escape route: an uphill grade, a runaway ramp, or grass. Once stopped, shut the engine off and call for a tow. Don't drive it again.

## What Do I Do Right Now If My Brakes Stop Working?

Pump the brake pedal hard and fast several times, then start shedding speed with the transmission and parking brake. Do this in order:

1. **Pump the pedal.** If a line has a slow leak, pumping can build enough pressure for partial braking. If the pedal sinks to the floor and stays there, move on.
2. **Hazards on, horn on.** Tell traffic something is wrong.
3. **Downshift.** In an automatic, move to L, 2, or the manual shift mode and step down one gear at a time. In a manual, drop gears progressively. Engine braking is your strongest tool now.
4. **Apply the parking brake slowly.** Pull or press it gradually. Yanking it at highway speed can lock the rear wheels and spin the car. If it's an electronic parking brake, hold the switch and the car will usually brake in stages.
5. **Pick your exit.** Look for an uphill road, a runaway truck ramp, a wide grass shoulder, or an empty lot. Gravity and soft ground stop cars better than anything else you have left.

Keep both hands on the wheel and keep your eyes up, not on the pedal.

## What If I'm on a Highway and Can't Slow Down?

Head for the right shoulder or an exit ramp, signaling and moving gradually, while using engine braking to bleed speed. Avoid hard swerves, which can cause a rollover at speed.

If traffic is stopped ahead and you can't slow enough, scrape the car along a guardrail or concrete barrier. It's ugly and expensive, but sliding your fender against a barrier stops you far more gently than hitting another vehicle head-on. Trade the paint, protect the people.

On long downgrades in mountain country, look for runaway truck ramps. Those gravel beds are built for exactly this, and they work on passenger cars too. Aim straight down the middle.

Don't turn the engine off while still rolling. You'll lose power steering and, in many cars, the brake booster assist, and some steering columns can lock.

## What Just Caused My Brakes to Fail?

Most sudden brake failures trace back to a few causes, and knowing which one hit you helps your mechanic. Common culprits:

- **Fluid loss.** A ruptured brake line, a blown caliper seal, or a failed master cylinder lets fluid escape and the pedal goes soft or sinks to the floor.
- **Brake fade.** Long downhill drives overheat pads and rotors. The pedal feels firm, but the car won't slow. The fix is to stop and let everything cool for at least 30 to 45 minutes.
- **Vacuum booster failure.** The pedal turns rock-hard and takes serious leg force. You still have brakes, just with far more effort required. Stand on the pedal with your whole leg.
- **Air in the lines.** Often from a recent repair. The pedal feels spongy before it fails.
- **Worn-out pads.** A metal-on-metal grinding noise for weeks beforehand is the classic warning most people ignore.

## Is It Safe to Drive After the Brakes Come Back?

No. Even if the pedal firms up and the car slows normally, treat it as a failed system until a shop inspects it. A fade that "fixed itself" will fade again at the next hill. A slow fluid leak can return you to a floor-pedal the next time you press hard.

Once you've stopped, shut the engine off, set the parking brake, and move everyone away from the road. Then arrange a tow. Do not limp it to the shop on your own power. ## Common Questions

**Q: Should I put the car in park to stop it?**
A: No. Shifting into park at speed can destroy the transmission and lock the drivetrain unpredictably. Use lower gears for engine braking instead, and only select park once you're fully stopped.

**Q: Can I use the emergency brake at highway speed?**
A: Yes, but pull it slowly and progressively. A sudden full pull can lock the rear wheels and cause a spin. Hold the release button if your handle has one so you can ease off if the back end gets loose.

**Q: Will the car stop if I turn the engine off?**
A: Not quickly, and it makes things worse. You'll lose power steering and likely the brake booster. Leave the engine running until you're fully stopped.

**Q: What if the pedal goes to the floor but the car still slows a little?**
A: That's a hydraulic leak, probably a line, caliper, or master cylinder. Pump the pedal to build pressure, combine it with downshifting and the parking brake, and plan to get towed.

**Q: How do I know if brake fade is the problem and not a real failure?**
A: Fade follows long downhill braking, comes with a hot, burning smell, and the pedal stays firm. Stop somewhere safe, let the brakes cool at least 30 to 45 minutes, and still have a shop check them before you drive on.
