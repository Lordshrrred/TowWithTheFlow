Expect to pay $75 to $150 for a local tow of 5 to 10 miles, and $3 to $7 per mile beyond that. If you have roadside assistance through your insurer or AAA, this is exactly what it covers. ## My Car Died on the Highway. Is It the Fuel Pump?

A sudden loss of power at highway speed with no warning lights is the classic fuel pump failure signature. The engine does not sputter and struggle. It quits. You lose throttle response, the RPMs drop, and the car coasts to a stop. Cranking the starter after that produces no start because the injectors are getting no fuel pressure. A failed fuel pump relay or a clogged fuel filter can mimic this exactly, but the fix and the tow situation are the same either way.

If you heard a high-pitched whine from the rear of the car in the days before this happened, that was the pump telling you it was dying. The fuel pump sits inside the gas tank on most modern vehicles, and when the motor inside it burns out, you are done driving until it is replaced.

## What Do I Do Right Now If I'm Stuck on the Highway?

Get to the shoulder immediately using whatever momentum you have left. Turn on your hazard lights before you even start braking. If you can reach the far right shoulder or, better, an off-ramp, do it. Once stopped, stay in the car if traffic is moving fast nearby. If you are on a busy interstate with no real shoulder clearance, get out the passenger-side door and stand behind the guardrail.

Place emergency triangles or flares at least 200 feet behind your vehicle to give approaching drivers time to react. For a deeper look at positioning yourself safely on a live highway, [this guide on broken down on the highway at night](/broken-down-on-highway-at-night-safety-tips-what-to-do/) applies even in daylight.

Then call for a tow. Do not attempt to push-start the car or try to coast to the next exit if you have already stopped. A dead fuel pump will not respond to any of that.

## What Will It Cost to Tow My Car with a Failed Fuel Pump?

Most local tows in the 5 to 10 mile range run $75 to $150, with a hook-up fee of $50 to $75 baked in. After the first 5 to 10 miles, add $3 to $7 per mile. A 30-mile tow to a dealership or repair shop can easily run $175 to $275 out of pocket if you are paying cash.

Your fuel pump itself will cost $200 to $600 in parts depending on the make and model, plus 1 to 3 hours of labor, since the tank usually has to drop to access it. Plan for a $400 to $900 total repair bill at an independent shop, more at a dealership.

If you have AAA, your membership tier determines how many free tow miles you get. Classic covers 5 miles, Plus covers 100 miles, and Premier covers 200. If your insurer offers roadside assistance, check your policy now while you wait. USAA, State Farm, and GEICO all offer towing coverage as an add-on. ## Should I Tow to a Dealer or an Independent Shop?

Tow to wherever you can get the repair done fastest at a price you can verify. An independent shop with good reviews will charge less for a fuel pump replacement than a dealership, and the quality of the part and work is often identical. Wix, Delphi, and Airtex are common aftermarket fuel pump brands that mechanics use every day without issue.

If your car is under a powertrain warranty, the dealer matters. Otherwise, call two shops while you wait for the tow truck, describe the symptoms, ask for a ballpark on a fuel pump replacement for your year, make, and model, and pick the one that sounds reasonable and can get you in today or tomorrow. Waiting three days at a dealership costs you more in rental car fees than the labor savings.

## What If I Can't Afford the Tow Right Now?

You have a few options that do not involve leaving your car on the shoulder. If your car insurance has a roadside assistance rider, this is a covered tow regardless of what caused the breakdown. Call your insurer's roadside line first.

If you are paying out of pocket, some tow companies will take a credit card over the phone before they dispatch. Others require payment on delivery. If cash is the issue, ask the dispatcher directly about payment options before they roll. You can also check whether the breakdown is covered under a credit card benefit: Visa Signature, certain Chase cards, and American Express cards often include roadside dispatch as a cardholder perk at no extra charge per use.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I add fuel and drive if my fuel pump failed?**
A: No. A failed fuel pump motor cannot push fuel to the engine regardless of how much is in the tank. Adding fuel will not help. You need a tow.

**Q: How long does it take to replace a fuel pump on the side of the road?**
A: It cannot be done roadside. The repair requires dropping the fuel tank, which takes a lift, proper tools, and a safe environment. This is a shop job only.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover the tow for a fuel pump failure?**
A: Only if you have roadside assistance coverage added to your policy. Comprehensive or collision coverage does not apply here. Check your declarations page or call your insurer directly.

**Q: How do I know it's the fuel pump and not the fuel filter or relay?**
A: You likely cannot tell for certain without a fuel pressure test. A shop will check pressure at the fuel rail first. A clogged filter or bad relay can cause identical symptoms, and both are cheaper fixes, so it is worth letting the mechanic diagnose before assuming the pump itself is the problem.

**Q: Is it safe to wait in my car on the highway shoulder for the tow truck?**
A: Staying in the car is safer than standing in traffic lanes, but if you are on a narrow shoulder with fast-moving trucks passing close, exit from the passenger side and stand behind the guardrail. Turn hazard lights on and leave them on the entire time you wait.
