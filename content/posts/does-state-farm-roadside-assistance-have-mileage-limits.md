> **Quick Answer:** Yes. State Farm Emergency Road Service typically limits towing by distance or by a dollar cap, and the exact limit depends on your state and your policy. Many policies cover a tow to the nearest qualified repair facility rather than to a shop of your choice. Check your declarations page or the State Farm app now, then confirm with the dispatcher before the truck hooks up.

## Does State Farm Roadside Assistance Really Have Mileage Limits?

Yes, though State Farm doesn't publish one universal number. Emergency Road Service is an optional add-on to your auto policy, and what it pays for varies by state and by the version of coverage you bought. The limit usually shows up in one of two forms: a maximum towing distance, or a maximum dollar amount per disablement.

Some policies tow you to the nearest qualified repair facility. Others reimburse up to a set dollar figure, and you pay the rest. Either way, a tow to a shop across town or in another city can blow past the cap.

I won't quote you a mileage figure here, because the number on your policy is the only one that counts. Guessing wrong means a surprise bill.

## How Do I Find My Exact Towing Limit Right Now?

Open your declarations page. It lists every coverage you carry, and if Emergency Road Service isn't on it, you don't have it. You can pull the declarations page from the State Farm app, your online account, or the policy documents emailed to you.

If you're stranded and can't dig through paperwork:

1. Open the State Farm app and check your coverages.
2. If you have coverage, request service through the app or the number on your insurance card.
3. Ask the dispatcher directly: "What is my towing limit, and where will you take the car?"
4. Get the answer before the driver loads your vehicle.

Asking costs nothing. Not asking can cost you $100 or more for extra miles.

## What Happens If My Tow Goes Over the Limit?

You pay the difference. If your coverage tows you 15 miles and your preferred shop is 35 miles away, the extra distance is billed to you, usually at a per-mile rate set by the tow company. Rates vary by market, so ask the driver for the per-mile figure before you agree to anything.

Two ways to avoid overage:

- **Tow to the nearest shop, then move the car later.** A second short tow beats paying for one long one in some cases, but only if the nearest shop isn't going to charge you storage while you decide.
- **Pay the extra miles only after getting a quote.** Never let a driver say "we'll sort it out later."

Storage fees are the other trap. If the car sits at a shop or yard for several days, daily fees add up fast. See [how much an impound lot charges per day](/how-much-does-impound-lot-charge-per-day/) for the typical ranges.

Call a local tow company directly and ask for a flat quote, including hookup fee and per-mile charge. Cash-paying customers can sometimes negotiate, especially on longer hauls. [Can you negotiate tow truck price](/can-you-negotiate-tow-truck-price/) covers what works.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does State Farm roadside assistance come with every auto policy?**
A: No. Emergency Road Service is an optional coverage you add to your policy, and it isn't available identically in every state. Check your declarations page to see if you have it.

**Q: Will State Farm tow me to my own mechanic?**
A: Only if the distance falls within your coverage limit. Many policies tow to the nearest qualified repair facility, so a mechanic farther away may mean paying for the extra miles yourself.

**Q: Is there a limit on how many times I can use State Farm roadside assistance?**
A: Some policies limit the number of service calls or the dollar amount per event. Ask State Farm or review your policy language to see if a usage cap applies to your coverage.

**Q: Does State Farm roadside assistance cover a dead battery or flat tire?**
A: Typically yes, services like jump-starts, tire changes, fuel delivery, and lockouts are part of Emergency Road Service, subject to dollar limits. See [whether roadside assistance covers a dead battery jumpstart](/does-roadside-assistance-cover-dead-battery-jumpstart/) for how this generally works.

**Q: What should I do if the tow truck arrives and I'm unsure about the limit?**
A: Stop before the car is loaded. Call State Farm, confirm your limit and destination, and get the per-mile overage rate from the driver in writing or by text.
