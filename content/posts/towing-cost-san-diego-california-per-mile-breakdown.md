> **Quick Answer:** In San Diego, most tow companies charge a hook-up fee of $75 to $125, then $3 to $7 per mile on top of that. A short local tow of 5 miles runs $90 to $160. A longer haul from the I-8 corridor out to El Cajon or from Mission Valley up to Mira Mesa can push $150 to $250 or more. Always get the per-mile rate confirmed before the truck hooks up.

## What Does Per-Mile Towing Actually Cost in San Diego?

The per-mile rate in San Diego typically runs $3 to $7 per mile, but that number means nothing without the hook-up fee added in. Most operators here charge a flat hook-up or dispatch fee of $75 to $125 just to show up and connect to your car. The mileage meter starts after that.

Here is what that looks like in practice:

| Tow Distance | Hook-Up Fee | Mileage Cost | Estimated Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 miles (local, e.g. downtown to Hillcrest) | $85 | $12-$21 | $97-$106 |
| 7 miles (e.g. Kearny Mesa to Mission Valley) | $95 | $28-$49 | $123-$144 |
| 15 miles (e.g. La Jolla to Chula Vista) | $100 | $60-$105 | $160-$205 |
| 25 miles (e.g. Oceanside to downtown SD) | $110 | $100-$175 | $210-$285 |

These are private-company rates. If Caltrans dispatches a Freeway Service Patrol truck on I-5 or I-15, that first push to the nearest safe shoulder or call box area is free, but the secondary tow to a shop is not.

## How Much More Does It Cost to Get Towed Off the Freeway in San Diego?

Breaking down on I-5 near the I-805 interchange or stalling on I-8 heading toward Alpine costs more than a neighborhood call, for two reasons. First, many companies charge a higher dispatch fee for freeway responses because of the safety setup involved. Expect the hook-up fee to run $100 to $150 instead of the low end. Second, the tow distance from a freeway breakdown is often longer than it looks, because the driver cannot always take the nearest exit. They have to reach a legal drop point.

If you break down near the I-15 and Miramar Road interchange, for example, the shop you want might be 8 miles away but the driver routes 11 miles to get there safely. You pay for the actual miles driven, not the straight-line distance.

After-hours towing (evenings, weekends, holidays) adds $25 to $75 to the base fee at most San Diego operators. Always ask when you call.

## Does It Cost More If I Need a Flatbed vs. a Wheel-Lift Truck?

Yes, flatbeds run higher. Flatbed rates in San Diego typically add $20 to $50 to the base hook-up fee compared to a wheel-lift or hook-and-chain truck. The per-mile rate is sometimes the same, sometimes $1 to $2 higher per mile.

AWD and 4WD vehicles, low-clearance sports cars, and anything with a damaged drivetrain need a flatbed. If you call and ask for the cheapest truck, you may get a wheel-lift that cannot legally or safely move your car. ## How Do I Make Sure I'm Not Overcharged on a Per-Mile Tow?

Ask for three numbers before you agree to anything: the dispatch or hook-up fee, the per-mile rate, and the estimated mileage to your destination. Write them down or take a screenshot. Confirm who is paying for the drive back if the tow yard is nowhere near your destination.

San Diego has a mix of reputable regional operators and smaller outfits that quote low on the phone and add fees on arrival. "Administrative fees," "fuel surcharges," and "after-hours premiums" are all real line items, but they should be disclosed before hookup, not after. California law requires towing companies to post their rates. If a driver will not give you numbers before connecting to your car, call a different company.

## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average towing cost per mile in San Diego?**
A: San Diego tow companies typically charge $3 to $7 per mile, on top of a hook-up fee of $75 to $125. A 10-mile tow usually lands between $130 and $195 total depending on the operator and time of day.

**Q: Is there a free tow option if I break down on the freeway in San Diego?**
A: Caltrans Freeway Service Patrol covers several San Diego corridors including I-5, I-8, and I-15 during peak hours and will push your car to a safe location at no charge. That service does not cover the tow to a repair shop, which you arrange and pay for separately.

**Q: Do tow companies in San Diego charge more at night?**
A: Most do. After-hours premiums of $25 to $75 are common, applied to the base hook-up fee. Always ask when you call if there is a different rate for evenings or weekends.

**Q: Can I negotiate the per-mile tow rate in San Diego?**
A: Rarely, and not in an emergency situation. You have more leverage if you call multiple companies and compare quotes before agreeing. Some larger companies have fixed published rates, which at least gives you something to verify against.

**Q: How long will it take for a tow truck to reach me in San Diego?**
A: In most San Diego neighborhoods and along major corridors like I-5 or I-15, expect 30 to 60 minutes. Congested periods on the 163 near Balboa Park or on I-8 near the Mission Valley merge can stretch that. For a broader look at wait times, see [How Long Does It Take for a Tow Truck to Arrive](/how-long-does-it-take-for-a-tow-truck-to-arrive/).
