Your State Farm roadside assistance policy has a towing coverage limit, and if you're broken down right now, you need to know that number before the truck shows up.

> **Quick Answer:** State Farm roadside assistance typically covers towing up to the nearest qualified repair facility, but the dollar cap depends on which coverage you have. The Emergency Road Service add-on usually reimburses up to $80 per disablement, though some policies carry higher limits. Tows beyond that distance or over the cap come out of your pocket. Call the number on your insurance card or log into the State Farm app to confirm your exact limit before the tow truck moves your car.

## What Is State Farm's Actual Towing Coverage Limit?

State Farm's Emergency Road Service (ERS) coverage caps towing reimbursement at a set dollar amount per disablement event, commonly around $80, though your specific policy may be higher. That cap is not a distance limit in miles. It is a dollar ceiling. A short tow in a city might fall under $80. A longer haul on a rural highway almost certainly will not.

The ERS benefit is an optional add-on to your auto policy, not automatic. If you never selected it, you have no towing coverage through State Farm at all. Check your declarations page or the State Farm mobile app right now to confirm whether ERS is even on your policy before you authorize a tow.

Some State Farm policies are bundled with third-party roadside networks that dispatch a tow and bill State Farm directly up to the cap, covering the overage separately with you. Others require you to pay the full tow upfront and submit a reimbursement claim afterward. Know which system your policy uses, because it changes how you handle payment at the scene.

## How Far Will State Farm Tow My Car?

State Farm's towing benefit is designed to get your car to the nearest qualified repair facility, not to a shop across town that you prefer. There is no published mileage limit written into the ERS add-on the way GEICO structures theirs. Instead, the dollar cap does the limiting for you. A tow to a shop five miles away might be fully covered. A tow 40 miles to a dealer you trust will almost certainly exceed the reimbursement ceiling.

If your breakdown happens somewhere remote, for example on a rural two-lane with no repair shop nearby, the nearest qualified facility could legitimately be 30 or 40 miles out. State Farm will still cover up to the cap. You pay the rest. Get a quote from the tow operator before they hook up your car so you know exactly what the overage will be.

## What Happens When the Tow Cost Exceeds My Coverage?

You pay the difference out of pocket, either at the scene or when the bill comes. There is no grace period and the tow operator has no obligation to wait on State Farm to settle before releasing your car at the shop.

A few things that help: First, negotiate the per-mile rate before the truck moves. Tow rates are not fixed by law in most states, and some operators will work with you, especially if you show them your insurance card and explain your situation. Second, if you pay the full amount yourself, keep every receipt. State Farm's reimbursement claim process requires documentation, and they will only reimburse up to your policy limit regardless of what you paid. Third, if the tow is going to be expensive and you have AAA, compare which benefit pays more before you call. ## Does State Farm Roadside Cover Anything Besides Towing?

Yes. The Emergency Road Service add-on typically also covers battery jump-starts, flat tire changes using your spare, lockout service, fuel delivery (usually up to a few gallons), and winching if your car is stuck in a ditch or shallow mud. Each of these is subject to the same per-disablement dollar cap as towing. A lockout or jump-start usually runs well under $80, so those tend to be fully covered. Winching on a soft shoulder or after sliding off a road in a snowstorm can push costs above the cap quickly, especially if a second truck is needed.

One disablement event equals one use of the benefit. If you call for a jump and the battery dies again an hour later and you need a tow, that is typically treated as a second disablement and a second claim.

## What Do I Do Right Now If I'm Waiting on a Tow?

Call the number on your State Farm insurance card or open the State Farm app and request roadside assistance through there. Both will connect you to their dispatch network. While you wait, get off the road if possible, turn on your hazard lights, and stay behind the guardrail if you are on a highway shoulder. Do not stand between your car and live traffic lanes.

If you are in a dangerous spot, for example on a blind curve or a high-speed merge lane, call 911 first. Your safety is not a coverage question.

When the tow truck arrives, confirm the destination and ask for a written quote before they attach to your vehicle. Once a car is on the truck, you have very little leverage on price. ## Common Questions

**Q: Does State Farm roadside assistance cover towing to any shop I choose?**
A: Not automatically. The policy language covers towing to the nearest qualified repair facility. If you want the car towed to a specific dealer or preferred shop that is farther away, State Farm will reimburse up to your cap, and you pay any overage for the extra distance.

**Q: How do I file a reimbursement claim with State Farm for a tow I already paid for?**
A: Log into your State Farm account online or through the app, go to claims, and select roadside reimbursement. Attach your itemized receipt from the tow company. State Farm will reimburse up to your policy's per-disablement cap. The process typically takes a few business days.

**Q: Is State Farm roadside assistance included automatically with full coverage?**
A: No. Emergency Road Service is an add-on endorsement that costs a few dollars per vehicle per policy period. Full coverage means you have comprehensive and collision, not roadside. You need to have specifically added ERS to your policy for towing to be covered.

**Q: What if State Farm's dispatch is slow and I need a tow faster?**
A: You can call any tow company directly, pay out of pocket, and submit for reimbursement up to your coverage cap afterward. Keep the receipt and make sure it is itemized with the pick-up address, drop-off address, and mileage.

**Q: Does State Farm roadside cover my car if someone else is driving it?**
A: State Farm's ERS coverage typically follows the vehicle, not the driver. If a permissive driver (someone you gave permission to use the car) breaks down in your insured vehicle, the roadside benefit should apply. Confirm this with your agent, because policy language can vary.
