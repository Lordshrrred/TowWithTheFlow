> **Quick Answer:** Night towing in Denver typically runs $100 to $175 for a local tow of up to 5 miles, compared to $75 to $150 during daytime hours. Most companies charge an after-hours premium of $25 to $50 on top of their base hook-up fee. If you're stuck on I-25 or I-70, expect the higher end of that range due to highway access fees and longer dispatch times.

## How Much More Does a Night Tow Cost in Denver Compared to Daytime?

Plan on paying $25 to $50 more for a night tow than you would during business hours. Denver tow companies define "after hours" differently, but most treat anything from 9 or 10 p.m. through 6 or 7 a.m. as a night rate. That premium gets added to a base hook-up fee that already runs $75 to $125 depending on the company and truck type.

Here's what the typical night tow structure looks like in Denver:

- **Hook-up fee (after hours):** $100 to $130
- **Per-mile charge:** $3 to $5 per mile after the first 5
- **Flatbed upcharge:** Add $20 to $40 over a wheel-lift truck
- **Highway access or scene fee:** $25 to $50 on major corridors

A wheel-lift tow from the Colfax and Colorado Boulevard area to a shop in the Baker neighborhood at 1 a.m. might cost $115 to $140 all-in. The same tow at noon might run $85 to $110.

## What Does a Night Tow Cost If I Break Down on I-25 or I-70?

Highway tows at night in Denver are the most expensive scenario you'll face. On I-25 near the I-70 interchange (also called the mousetrap) or on I-70 heading toward the Colfax exit, tow companies routinely add a highway or "scene" fee because their drivers are working in live traffic with rotating amber lights, which increases their liability and slow-down time.

Expect $150 to $200 for a highway tow of 5 to 7 miles after dark. If you're on I-70 west of C-470 heading toward the foothills, distance charges stack fast and a 15-mile tow to a Denver shop can hit $200 to $275 at night. If you're stranded on the highway, your first call should go to 911 if there's any safety concern, and then to a tow company or your roadside assistance provider.

## Does My Insurance or Roadside Assistance Cover Night Towing?

Yes, roadside assistance through your insurer or AAA covers night tows the same as daytime tows. The coverage doesn't change based on the clock. What changes is what the tow company charges, and whether your plan's reimbursement cap is high enough to cover the full bill.

AAA Plus and AAA Premier members get up to 100 and 200 miles of towing covered per incident, which is plenty for any Denver metro tow. Basic AAA caps out at 5 miles, which may leave you paying out of pocket for the overage on a highway tow. State Farm and Allstate roadside riders typically reimburse up to $75 to $100 per tow, which might not fully cover an after-hours highway call.

Call your provider first, confirm the per-tow limit, and ask specifically whether they have a contracted tow company dispatching in Denver or whether you arrange it yourself and submit for reimbursement. The difference matters when you're waiting at 2 a.m. on the shoulder of I-25 near the Broadway exit. ## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged for a Night Tow in Denver?

Ask for the total price before the truck moves your car. That's the single most effective move. Colorado does not cap private tow rates for consensual tows, meaning

 a company you call yourself can charge whatever the market bears. Once the car is hooked and moving, your leverage is gone.

Before you confirm, ask these specific questions:

1. What is the hook-up or base fee?
2. What is the per-mile rate and when does it start?
3. Is there an after-hours surcharge?
4. Is there a highway or scene fee for where I'm located?
5. Does the rate change if I need a flatbed instead of a wheel-lift?

Getting answers to all five takes 90 seconds and can save you $50 to $100. If the dispatcher won't give you a clear answer, call a different company. Most reputable Denver operators will quote you a full estimated price over the phone before dispatch.

## Is It Safe to Wait for a Tow Truck at Night in Denver?

Get off the roadway and stay visible. If you break down on I-25, I-70, or US-36, pull as far right as possible onto the shoulder. Turn on your hazard lights immediately. If you have road flares or LED triangles, place them 100 to 200 feet behind your car. Then get out of the vehicle on the passenger side, away from traffic, and stand behind the guard rail if one is present.

Do not sit in your car on a live highway shoulder at night. Rear-end collisions into stopped vehicles on Denver-area highways happen regularly, especially on I-70 near the Pecos Street corridor and on I-25 through downtown. The car can be replaced. Get clear of it.

If you're on a surface street like Colfax, Broadway, or Federal Boulevard, staying inside the locked car with hazards on is generally safer than standing outside in an unfamiliar area at night.

## Common Questions

**Q: What time does after-hours towing pricing start in Denver?**
A: Most Denver tow companies switch to night rates between 9 p.m. and 10 p.m. and return to standard rates at 6 a.m. or 7 a.m. Always confirm the specific window when you call.

**Q: Can I negotiate the night tow rate with a Denver tow company?**
A: Sometimes. If you're a AAA member or have roadside through your insurer, the contracted rate is already set. For a cash or card call, you can ask if they'll waive the after-hours fee, especially if the tow is long distance and worth more to them. Some will, most won't.

**Q: How long does a night tow truck take to arrive in Denver?**
A: Expect 30 to 60 minutes on most Denver surface streets at night. On I-25 or I-70 during late-night hours, response can be faster since traffic is lighter, but availability of trucks matters. [Tow Truck Response Time Denver Colorado](/tow-truck-response-time-denver-colorado/) has specific expectations by area.

**Q: Will a tow company drop my car at a closed repair shop at night?**
A: Yes. Most Denver tow operators will leave your car in a shop's after-hours lot as long as there's a safe place to set it down. Confirm the drop location with the shop first, and make sure there's room. Some shops on tight lots in Capitol Hill or LoHi won't accept overnight drops without prior arrangement.

**Q: Does night towing cost more if I'm in the mountains west of Denver?**
A: Yes, significantly. A tow from Evergreen or Idaho Springs to a Denver shop at night can run $300 to $450 once you factor in distance, mountain road fees, and the after-hours premium. See [Towing Cost in Denver Colorado Mountains](/towing-cost-denver-colorado-mountains/) for exact figures.
