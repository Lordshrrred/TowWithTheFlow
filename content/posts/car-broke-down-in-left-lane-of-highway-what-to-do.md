> **Quick Answer:** Turn on your hazard lights, keep rolling toward the left shoulder, and steer onto it if you can reach it. If you can't, stay buckled in with your foot on the brake and call 911. The left lane is the most dangerous place on the highway to be stopped. Only get out if you can reach a safe spot behind a barrier.

## What Do I Do the Second My Car Dies in the Left Lane?

Hit your hazard lights immediately and steer toward the left shoulder while you still have momentum. Don't brake hard or you'll lose the distance you need.

Most cars will coast a surprisingly long way. If the engine quits, you lose power assist on the brakes and steering, but both still work with more effort. Push the brake pedal firmly and turn the wheel with both hands. Don't pump the pedal or yank the wheel.

Shift to neutral if the engine is dead and you're rolling. That lets you try restarting without the car lurching. If it fires back up, drive to the nearest exit or shoulder and stop.

## Should I Stay in My Car or Get Out?

Stay in the car, belted in, unless you have a barrier between you and traffic or the car is on fire. A stopped vehicle in a fast left lane gets hit from behind far more often than people expect, and a parked car is a heavier, safer place to be than standing beside it.

Here's the decision rule:

- **Car stuck in a live lane with no shoulder:** Stay belted in. Foot on the brake so your brake lights stay on. Hazards on. Call 911.
- **Car on the left shoulder next to a concrete median barrier:** Staying in the car is still usually safest. Left shoulders are narrow, and passing vehicles are a few feet from your door.
- **Smoke, fire, or fuel smell:** Get out immediately. Exit on the side away from traffic, which on a left shoulder is often the passenger side, then move well past the barrier or guardrail.

If you do get out, never stand between your car and the lane. Never try to push the car yourself in a live lane.

## Who Do I Call When I'm Stuck in a Live Lane?

Call 911 first if your car is blocking a lane or you're in any danger. A stalled car in a high-speed lane is an emergency, not an inconvenience. Dispatchers can send highway patrol, who can close the lane or block behind you with a patrol car and flashing lights. Say your highway, direction of travel, nearest exit or mile marker, and that you're in the left lane.

Use the nearest mile marker or exit number. The small green mile marker signs along the right edge of the road are easy to miss from the left lane. Your phone's map app can also give you coordinates.

Once the scene is secure, call your roadside provider or a tow company. ## Can I Try to Fix It Myself Before Help Arrives?

Only if the car is already off the road and you can do it from inside or on the far side of a barrier. Restarting the engine from the driver's seat is fine. Popping the hood in a live lane is not.

Quick checks you can do from the seat:

1. Check the fuel gauge. Running dry is a common left-lane stall.
2. Look at the dash. A battery light points to an alternator failure ([see what that means](/alternator-died-while-driving-can-i-make-it-home/)). A flashing check engine light with shaking points to a misfire.
3. Try one restart. If it cranks but won't catch, stop. Repeated cranking drains the battery you'll need for hazards.

Save your phone battery. Keep hazards running, since flashers draw little power compared with cranking.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is it illegal to stop in the left lane of a highway?**
A: Stopping in a lane is generally illegal unless your vehicle is disabled and you can't move it. A genuine breakdown is the exception. Get as far left or right as you can safely and keep your hazards on.

**Q: Should I put out flares or reflective triangles?**
A: Only if you can place them safely, which usually means well behind the car and off the road. Never walk into a live lane to set them. If you can't do it from a protected spot, skip it and rely on hazards.

**Q: What if I can't get my car to the shoulder and traffic is coming?**
A: Stay belted in with hazards on and your foot on the brake pedal, and call 911. Do not stand outside the car. A buckled seat and a running brake light are your best protection until an officer arrives.

**Q: Can I call a regular tow company instead of 911?**
A: If the car is blocking a lane, call 911 first. A regular tow truck cannot safely work in a live lane without police protection. Once the lane is secure, call your tow company or roadside provider.

**Q: What if my car just won't restart after a stall on the highway?**
A: Try once with the car in neutral or park. If it won't start, don't keep cranking. A dead battery, failed alternator, or fuel pump problem is likely, and you should wait for a tow.
