## Who Do I Actually Call When My Car Breaks Down in Denver at Night?

Your first call is to a 24-hour towing or roadside assistance company. Search "24-hour tow truck Denver" right now and call the first local result, not a national dispatch aggregator that adds time and markup. If you have AAA, USAA roadside, or insurance-included roadside assistance, call that line directly. Your second call, if you're on a highway, is to Colorado State Patrol by dialing 511 and selecting the roadside assistance option, or simply dial 911 if you're in immediate danger.

Don't wait and hope someone stops. Denver nights on I-25 near the 6th Avenue interchange or on I-70 through the Mousetrap get fast and unpredictable traffic. Get a tow moving before you figure out the mechanic.

## Where Should I Wait and Is It Safe to Stay in My Car?

If you're off the road with room on the shoulder, stay inside the car with your seatbelt on and doors locked. That is the safest position in most situations. A moving car hitting a parked one at the roadside will push your car forward, and being inside is safer than standing behind it.

If you're in a neighborhood like Five Points, Sun Valley, or along Colfax at night and feel unsafe, it's reasonable to walk to a lit business or stay visible near a streetlight rather than sitting alone in a dark car. Use your judgment. On a highway, never stand behind or beside the car in the travel lane area. Move as far onto the shoulder or grass as possible.

Turn on your hazard lights the moment you stop. If you have road flares or LED triangles in the trunk, set them 100 to 200 feet behind the car. Most drivers don't carry these, but they matter, especially on unlit stretches of I-70 east of Quebec Street.

## How Long Will a Tow Truck Take to Get to Me at Night in Denver?

## What If I'm Stranded on a Highway Like I-25 or I-70 in Denver?

Get completely onto the right shoulder, as far right as you can go without dropping off the pavement edge. Turn on hazards immediately. If your car is in a lane or partially blocking traffic, that is an emergency situation. Call 911 first, not a tow company.

Colorado State Patrol monitors major corridors including I-25 through downtown, I-70 through the Mousetrap (the I-25/I-70 interchange, one of the busiest and most dangerous in the state at night), and I-225 through Aurora. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I call 911 if my car broke down in Denver at night?**
A: Yes. If you're on a highway, feel unsafe, or your car is blocking traffic, 911 is the right call. They'll dispatch Colorado State Patrol. For a straightforward breakdown in a safe location, skip 911 and call a tow company directly.

**Q: What if I don't have roadside assistance and can't afford a tow?**
A: Call around to two or three local tow companies and ask for their base rate before agreeing to anything. Some accept credit cards at the scene. If you're truly stuck with no funds, call a family member to come wait with you and sort out payment. Don't accept a tow without knowing the price first.

**Q: Is it safe to wait alone in my car on Colfax or Federal at night in Denver?**
A: Keep doors locked, windows up, and don't open the door for strangers. If you feel threatened, call 911 and stay on the line. These corridors have high foot traffic at night, and a lit, locked car is generally fine for a 30 to 45-minute wait.

**Q: Will police tow my car if I leave it on the street overnight in Denver?**
A: Denver can tag and tow vehicles left on arterials or with hazard lights off after a certain window, often within a few hours on major roads. If you can't get a tow that night, leave your hazards on, put a note on the dash with your phone number, and contact a shop first thing in the morning.

**Q: What information should I have ready when I call a tow truck?**
A: Your exact location (cross streets or highway mile marker), your car's make, model, and year, whether it's drivable or needs to be winched, and where you want it towed. Having the destination shop's address ready saves time and avoids getting towed somewhere inconvenient.
