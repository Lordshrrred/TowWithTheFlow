> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in San Diego costs $75 to $150 for the first five miles, then $3 to $7 per mile after that. A flatbed runs higher, usually $125 to $250 for a short haul. If you break down on I-5, I-8, or I-15 and the California Highway Patrol calls a tow, expect a CHP Incident Management Team truck, which may carry a higher dispatch rate billed directly to you.

## What Does a Tow Truck Actually Cost in San Diego Right Now?

Most local tows in San Diego land between $75 and $200 depending on distance and truck type. The standard hook-up fee (the flat charge just to show up and attach your car) runs $50 to $100, and mileage adds $3 to $7 per mile on top of that. A five-mile tow from somewhere like the Morena District to a shop near Kearny Mesa typically comes out to $90 to $130. A longer haul, say from Chula Vista up to Escondido, could hit $175 to $250 before any extras.

If the CHP dispatches a tow on a San Diego freeway, that truck operates under a rotational tow program with rates set by Caltrans. Those rates are regulated, but they are not cheap, and storage fees start accruing the same day.

For a full per-mile breakdown with sample route costs, see [Towing Cost in San Diego California Per Mile Breakdown](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-per-mile-breakdown/).

## Does the Type of Tow Truck Change What I Pay?

Yes, significantly. A wheel-lift truck is cheaper because it is faster to hook up and simpler to operate. A flatbed costs more because of the equipment and the time required to load your vehicle. In San Diego, expect to pay $30 to $75 more for a flatbed on the same distance.

When does a flatbed actually matter? If your car is all-wheel drive, has a broken axle, or has been in a front-end collision, a flatbed is not optional. Running a disabled AWD vehicle on a wheel-lift can destroy the transfer case. Low-clearance cars, modified suspensions, and exotics also need flatbeds. If you are unsure which type you need, [this guide on flatbed vs wheel-lift towing costs in San Diego](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/) covers the real-world difference.

## What Happens to the Price If I Break Down on the Freeway?

Freeway breakdowns in San Diego almost always cost more. On I-5 near the I-805 interchange in Mission Valley, on I-8 heading toward El Cajon, or anywhere on I-15 through Mira Mesa, CHP patrol officers will contact the rotational tow company if you cannot move your vehicle. You do not get to pick the company at that moment.

Rotational tow rates in California are set per county, but San Diego County rates allow for a hook-up fee, per-mile charge, and additional fees for things like a gate fee if the yard closes before you retrieve your car. Storage at a tow yard runs $50 to $75 per day. If you leave your car overnight, that cost stacks fast.

## What If I Do Not Have Insurance or Roadside Assistance?

You pay out of pocket, and that means negotiating upfront. Without AAA, without roadside coverage through your insurer, and without a motor club, you are a cash customer. Call at least two local tow companies before committing. Get a price quoted before they hook up, not after.

A short tow in San Diego paying cash typically runs $100 to $175. Prices tend to run higher at night, on weekends, and during peak beach traffic hours in the summer when Pacific Beach and Mission Beach corridors are jammed and response times stretch. For a detailed look at what to expect without coverage, see [Towing Cost in San Diego California No Insurance](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-no-insurance/).

One concrete way to reduce exposure: download the AAA app before you ever need it. A basic AAA membership costs around $60 to $80 per year and covers four tows up to five miles each. One tow pays for it.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow truck in San Diego for a short distance?**
A: A short tow of one to three miles in San Diego typically costs $75 to $110. That includes the hook-up fee and the mileage. Cash prices from private companies may be negotiable.

**Q: Will the CHP call a tow truck for me on the freeway and what does that cost?**
A: Yes, CHP will call a tow from the county rotational list if your vehicle is blocking or endangering traffic. Rates are regulated by San Diego County but still run $150 to $300 or more for a short haul, and storage fees begin immediately if you cannot retrieve the car same day.

**Q: Does AAA cover towing in San Diego?**
A: Basic AAA covers tows up to five miles at no additional charge, up to four times per year. Beyond five miles, you pay the per-mile rate. Classic Plus and Premier tiers extend that free mileage to 100 miles per tow.

**Q: How long does it take for a tow truck to arrive in San Diego?**
A: Most private tow companies in San Diego quote 30 to 60 minutes. During rush hour on I-5 or I-15, or during a major accident closure, it can stretch to 90 minutes. CHP dispatch is usually faster on active freeway incidents.

**Q: Is it cheaper to use local San Diego tow companies or a national service like AAA?**
A: If you already have AAA or roadside coverage through your insurer, use it. The per-incident cost is effectively zero within your coverage limits. If you do not, local San Diego tow companies will generally quote lower cash rates than app-based national dispatch services, which add a margin on top of the local company's rate.
