> **Quick Answer:** A tow from the Las Vegas Strip area typically runs $95 to $175 for a local haul, but expect $150 to $225 or more on weekend nights, holidays, or if your car is on Las Vegas Boulevard itself. Hook-up fees, mileage charges, and after-hours premiums all stack fast here. Get the total price confirmed before you authorize anything.

## What Does a Tow Actually Cost on the Las Vegas Strip Right Now?

A standard local tow on or near Las Vegas Boulevard runs $95 to $175 during normal business hours. After 10 p.m. on a Friday or Saturday, that same tow regularly hits $175 to $225, and some operators charge more during major events like New Year's Eve or fight weekends at T-Mobile Arena.

Here is how the charges break down:

- **Hook-up fee:** $65 to $100 just to dispatch and connect your vehicle
- **Per-mile rate:** $4 to $7 per mile after the first few miles
- **After-hours surcharge:** $25 to $50 added automatically after 8 or 10 p.m., depending on the company
- **Flatbed upcharge:** $20 to $40 more if your vehicle needs a flatbed (all-wheel-drive, lowered car, or any car where wheel-lift risks driveline damage)

A short tow from the Bellagio or Caesars Palace area to a shop in the auto row on South Decatur Boulevard is roughly 4 to 6 miles. At standard rates that is $85 to $130 in mileage plus the hook-up fee, so $150 to $200 total. For more on flatbed-specific pricing in the city, see [Towing Cost in Las Vegas Nevada Flatbed Rate: What You'll Pay Right Now](/towing-cost-in-las-vegas-nevada-flatbed-rate/).

## Why Are Strip Area Tow Prices Higher Than the Rest of Las Vegas?

Access is the main driver. Las Vegas Boulevard between Tropicana Avenue and Sahara Avenue is one of the most congested corridors in the country on weekends. A tow truck driver may sit in traffic for 20 to 40 minutes just to reach your car, and operators price for that time.

Demand spikes are also real. On a Saturday night when three headliner shows let out simultaneously, every tow truck in the zip code is busy. Scarcity pushes prices up fast. Some operators also add a "resort corridor fee" of $15 to $30 that they bury in fine print on the invoice.

Additionally, if your car is disabled in a casino parking structure, the casino may already have a preferred tow contract in place. That company may not offer the lowest rate in the city. You have the right to request a different provider, but expect a longer wait. ## What Should I Do the Moment My Car Breaks Down on or Near the Strip?

Get your car out of traffic first, then make calls. If you stall on Las Vegas Boulevard, turn on your hazards and attempt to coast or push to the nearest curb or lot. Do not leave the car in an active lane.

If you are on I-15 near the Tropicana interchange or on US-95 near the Spaghetti Bowl, pull to the right shoulder as far as possible, exit the vehicle on the passenger side, and stand behind the guardrail if one is available. Call 911 if you are in a dangerous position. NDOT's 511 service can tell you if a freeway assistance truck is in the area.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged by a Strip Area Tow Operator?

Ask three things before you agree to anything: the hook-up fee, the per-mile rate, and whether there are any surcharges for the time of day or location. A legitimate operator will give you a straight answer in 30 seconds.

A few other protections:

- **Nevada law requires written authorization** before towing. Do not let anyone hook up your vehicle without signing something or confirming a total price.
- **Photograph your car** before the tow truck arrives and after it is loaded. Document any pre-existing damage.
- **Know where your car is going.** Confirm the destination address before you authorize the tow. An unscrupulous operator can take your car to a high-fee storage lot and charge $75 to $150 per day while you sort it out.
- **If a tow truck shows up without you calling one**, that is a flag. [Learn what that situation usually means](/car-accident-tow-truck-showed-up-without-being-called-scam/) before you hand over your keys.

If you negotiated poorly in the moment or feel the charge was unfair, you can dispute towing charges with the Nevada Transportation Authority, which licenses tow operators in Clark County.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a tow from the Las Vegas Strip to Henderson cost?**
A: Henderson is roughly 10 to 15 miles from mid-Strip depending on your destination. Expect $130 to $200 during the day and $175 to $250 at night, including the hook-up fee.

**Q: Will my insurance cover a tow on the Strip?**
A: If you have roadside assistance on your auto policy, yes, usually with no out-of-pocket cost. Call your insurer's roadside line first. If you only have liability coverage, you are paying out of pocket.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive on the Strip on a Friday night?**
A: Budget 45 to 90 minutes on a busy weekend night. Traffic on Las Vegas Boulevard and high demand from the resort corridor both slow response times significantly.

**Q: Is a flatbed required for AWD or 4WD vehicles on the Strip?**
A: Yes. If your vehicle has all-wheel drive or four-wheel drive and cannot be put in neutral, a flatbed is required to avoid driveline damage. Make sure the company you call has one available before they dispatch.

**Q: Can I negotiate the tow price after the fact if it seems too high?**
A: Sometimes. If the operator inflated charges or added fees not disclosed upfront, dispute it directly and cite the original quote. Some companies will adjust to avoid a Nevada Transportation Authority complaint.
