> **Quick Answer:** Stop driving immediately when your transmission slips. A slipping transmission can fail completely without warning, leaving you stranded or causing an accident. Pull over safely, turn on hazards, and call for a tow truck. Continuing to drive risks total transmission failure and expensive damage.

## What To Do

1. **Pull over immediately** when you feel the transmission slip (engine revs but car doesn't accelerate, delayed shifting, or rough gear changes).

2. **Turn on hazard lights** and move to the rightmost lane or shoulder if safe to do so.

3. **Put the car in park** and turn off the engine. Do not attempt to drive further.

4. **Call for a tow truck** or your roadside assistance provider. Your car needs professional diagnosis and likely major repair.

5. **Stay in your vehicle** if you're on a busy road or highway. If you must exit, stay away from traffic and keep your phone charged.

6. **Document the problem** by noting exactly when the slipping started, what you felt, and any warning lights on your dashboard.

When your transmission slips, you're dealing with internal mechanical failure. The transmission can't properly transfer power from your engine to your wheels. This creates unpredictable acceleration and can cause you to lose control suddenly. Don't risk it by trying to limp home.

The most common cause is low or burnt transmission fluid. Healthy fluid is bright red and slightly sweet-smelling. If yours looks dark brown or smells scorched, the fluid has broken down and is no longer protecting the clutch packs and bands inside the transmission. Once those components start slipping under load, continued driving generates heat that accelerates the damage fast. A transmission that might have been saved with a fluid change and minor repair can become a full rebuild job within 20 to 30 miles of continued driving.

Other causes include a worn torque converter, failing solenoids, or a slipping clutch band. Automatic and manual transmissions both slip, but the feel is slightly different. In an automatic, you'll notice the engine RPM jumping to 3,000 or 4,000 while your speed barely changes. In a manual, you'll feel the engine pull away from the drivetrain mid-gear, especially under hard acceleration or on a hill. Either way, the car is telling you it cannot reliably put power to the ground.

Transmission fluid leaks often precede slipping. If you noticed [transmission fluid leaking](/transmission-fluid-leaking-can-i-drive-to-mechanic/) before the slipping started, the damage may already be severe. Low fluid levels cause internal components to overheat and fail.

Slipping is also more dangerous than most drivers realize. At highway speeds, a sudden loss of drive engagement can cause you to slow sharply in fast-moving traffic. On an incline, it can leave you rolling backward before the transmission re-engages. These are not minor inconveniences. They are crash scenarios.

## What It Might Cost

Towing for transmission failure typically costs $75-150 for local distances (under 10 miles). Longer distances add $2-4 per mile. If you're on a highway, expect to pay highway rates which can reach $200-300 for a 20-mile tow.

Many insurance companies cover emergency towing. [Check if your insurance deductible applies to towing costs](/insurance-deductible-applies-to-towing-costs-when/) before calling a private company.

On the repair side, costs vary widely by what the tech finds. A solenoid replacement might run $150-400. A full fluid flush and filter service is $100-250. But if the clutch packs or planetary gears are damaged from running dry or overheated, you're looking at a rebuilt or remanufactured transmission, which typically runs $1,800-4,500 installed depending on your vehicle and region. A brand-new OEM transmission on a truck or SUV can push past $6,000. Driving another 10 miles on a slipping transmission is not worth the gamble.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive just a short distance to get off the highway or reach a parking lot?**
A: Moving the car a few hundred feet to get off an active travel lane is reasonable for safety. Beyond that, stop. Even a half-mile of driving under load on a slipping transmission can turn a repairable problem into a full replacement.

**Q: Will my car insurance or warranty cover transmission towing and repair?**
A: Standard auto insurance covers towing only if you have roadside assistance added to your policy, not collision or liability coverage alone. An extended warranty or powertrain warranty may cover the repair itself, so check your paperwork before authorizing any work at the shop.

**Q: My transmission only slips occasionally. Do I still need a tow?**
A: Yes. Intermittent slipping means the problem is already present and will worsen under heat and load. Many transmissions that slip only occasionally fail completely within days. Get it diagnosed before it strands you somewhere worse than a safe shoulder.

## Stay Safe

- Never try to "baby" a slipping transmission to your destination
- Avoid hills and highway speeds where transmission failure could cause accidents
- If the car won't move at all, you need [emergency roadside assistance](/car-wont-shift-out-of-park-towing-options/) immediately
- Keep your doors locked while waiting for the tow truck
- Have your registration, insurance, and driver's license ready for the driver

Transmission repair costs range from $1,500-4,000 or more. A tow truck bill of $100-200 is minor compared to the engine damage you could cause by continuing to drive with a failing transmission.
