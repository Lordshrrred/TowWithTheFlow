## How Much Does a Flatbed Tow Actually Cost in Memphis?

A standard flatbed tow within Memphis city limits costs $95 to $175 for the first 5 to 10 miles, with a per-mile rate of $3.50 to $5 after that. The hook-up fee (sometimes called the dispatch or drop fee) is usually baked into that base price, but confirm before the truck rolls. Some operators charge it as a separate $50 to $75 line item, which inflates the final bill if you're not watching.

If you're moving a vehicle across Shelby County, say from Cordova near Germantown Parkway out to a shop on Summer Avenue in Midtown, you're looking at 15 to 20 miles total and a bill in the $145 to $225 range depending on the company.

After-hours calls, including nights and weekends, typically add $25 to $50 on top of the base rate. If you broke down late on a Friday night on I-55 near the Crump Boulevard exit, budget for that premium.

## Why Does My Car Need a Flatbed Instead of a Regular Tow Truck?

A flatbed is required when your wheels cannot roll freely, the vehicle is all-wheel drive or four-wheel drive, it's severely damaged after an accident, or the car sits too low to clear the wheel-lift arms without scraping. Front-wheel-drive cars with a dead transmission also need a flatbed because dragging the driven wheels will destroy the transmission further. If you're unsure, [check this guide on whether your car needs a flatbed tow](/how-do-i-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/) before calling.

Flatbeds take longer to load and cost more to operate than wheel-lift trucks, which is why the rate is higher. That's not price gouging; that's the actual equipment cost.

## Where in Memphis Are Flatbed Tows Most Expensive?

The priciest situations are highway breakdowns and tight or hard-to-access locations. On I-40 near the I-240 split in East Memphis, tow trucks often sit in stopped traffic before they even reach you, and some operators charge a waiting or staging fee of $1 to $2 per minute beyond 15 minutes on scene. The same applies to breakdowns on the Hernando DeSoto Bridge (I-40 over the Mississippi), where TDOT may require a specific contracted operator, limiting your options.

Breakdowns in South Memphis near the Lamar Avenue corridor or on US-61 through Whitehaven tend to get faster response because there are more independent towing companies operating in that zone, which keeps prices more competitive. Midtown and Downtown Memphis are similarly well-served.

If you're stranded on I-269 out near Collierville, you're at the edge of Shelby County and some Memphis-based companies will charge a mileage surcharge just to get the truck to you. That adds $30 to $60 before the actual tow begins.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a flatbed tow cost in Memphis for a truck or SUV?**
A: Larger vehicles like full-size pickups or SUVs typically cost $15 to $30 more than a standard car tow because of weight and longer load time. Expect $115 to $200 for a local haul under 10 miles.

**Q: Can any tow truck in Memphis handle an AWD vehicle?**
A: No. AWD and 4WD vehicles must go on a flatbed. A wheel-lift tow on an AWD car will damage the transfer case or rear differential. Always tell the dispatcher your drivetrain type when you call.

**Q: How long does it take a flatbed tow truck to arrive in Memphis?**
A: Most companies quote 30 to 60 minutes for Memphis proper. During rush hour on I-240 or after a major accident that backs up multiple routes, wait times can stretch to 75 to 90 minutes. Call two companies and take whoever arrives first.

**Q: Will a tow company in Memphis charge more if my car is in a ditch or off-road?**
A: Yes. Recovery from a ditch, parking lot curb, or off-pavement position usually adds a winching fee of $75 to $150 on top of the standard flatbed rate. That fee is separate from the tow and is legitimate.

**Q: Is it cheaper to use the tow company recommended by the police at the scene?**
A: Not usually. Police-dispatched tow companies in Memphis rotate on a contract list and are not required to be the cheapest option. You have the right to call your own company. The police-dispatched truck may already be hooked up before you realize you had a choice, so act fast.
