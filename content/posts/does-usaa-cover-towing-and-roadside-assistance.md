> **Quick Answer:** USAA covers towing and roadside assistance only if you added the optional roadside coverage to your auto policy. It's not automatic. If you have it, it typically covers towing, jump-starts, flat tire changes, lockouts, and fuel delivery, up to policy limits. Check your declarations page or the USAA app to confirm before you call a tow truck.

## Does USAA Automatically Cover Towing?

No. Basic liability, collision, and comprehensive coverage don't pay for a breakdown tow. Roadside assistance is an add-on to your USAA auto policy, and it's usually cheap compared to one out-of-pocket tow.

The one exception: if your car is towed because of a covered accident or comprehensive loss, towing can be reimbursed under that claim. That's a different coverage path than roadside assistance. If you're dealing with a wreck, see [who pays for towing after a car accident that wasn't your fault](/who-pays-for-towing-after-a-car-accident-not-my-fault/).

## How Do I Find Out If I Have USAA Roadside Assistance?

Open the USAA app or log in on the website and look at your auto policy details. Your declarations page lists every coverage on the policy, and roadside assistance will appear by name if you have it.

If you're stranded and can't find it, call the USAA number printed on the back of your member card or in the app. Have your policy number or member number ready. Don't pay a tow driver on the spot and hope to get reimbursed until you've confirmed coverage. Reimbursement rules vary, and a dispatched USAA provider is the cleanest route.

## What Does USAA Roadside Assistance Actually Cover?

When it's on your policy, roadside assistance generally covers the common breakdown problems:

- **Towing** to a repair facility, within mileage and dollar limits
- **Jump-starts** for a dead battery
- **Flat tire changes** using your spare
- **Lockout service** if keys are locked inside
- **Fuel delivery** when you run out of gas (the fuel itself is usually on you)

Two details trip people up. First, a flat tire service only swaps on your spare. If you don't have a usable one, or you've got a run-flat that's destroyed, you're likely looking at a tow instead. Second, fuel delivery brings gas to you but doesn't pay for it. More on that in [does roadside assistance cover fuel delivery when you run out of gas](/does-roadside-assistance-cover-fuel-delivery-ran-out-of-gas/).

Exact dollar and mileage caps depend on your state and policy, so confirm yours in the app instead of assuming.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is USAA roadside assistance free for members?**
A: No. It's an optional add-on with a small added premium on your auto policy. Once it's on your policy, covered services are typically provided without a separate per-call fee, up to your limits.

**Q: Can I add USAA roadside assistance after my car already broke down?**
A: Don't count on it. Coverage generally isn't retroactive and may not apply to an event that already happened. Add it before you need it.

**Q: Does USAA roadside assistance cover me in someone else's car?**
A: It depends on how the coverage is written. Many policies tie it to the insured vehicle, not the driver. Check your policy language or ask USAA, since rules differ from carrier to carrier. Our [AAA article on someone else driving your car](/does-aaa-cover-towing-for-someone-else-driving-my-car/) shows how this issue plays out with a membership-based plan.

**Q: Will using roadside assistance raise my USAA premium?**
A: Roadside assistance is generally not treated like an at-fault claim, but practices can vary. Ask USAA directly if you're worried, and don't skip a needed tow over this fear.

**Q: Does USAA tow after an accident, or only for breakdowns?**
A: After an accident, towing usually runs through your collision or comprehensive claim, not the roadside add-on. Report the accident to USAA and ask which coverage applies to the tow.
