> **Quick Answer:** Neither Uber nor Lyft includes standard roadside assistance for drivers. Their insurance covers certain accidents and liability while you're on a trip, but it does not pay for tows, jump-starts, flat tires, or lockouts. Some programs offer driver discounts or paid add-ons through partners. If you're stuck now, get safe first, then use your personal insurance, a membership like AAA, or a local tow company.

## Does Uber or Lyft Give Drivers Free Roadside Assistance?

No. Neither company provides free towing, jump-starts, tire changes, fuel delivery, or lockout service as a built-in driver benefit. You are an independent contractor, and the car's mechanical condition is your responsibility.

What they do provide is insurance, and it's narrower than most drivers assume. Coverage depends on your app status:

- **App off:** Nothing from the platform. Only your personal policy applies.
- **App on, waiting for a ride request:** Mostly liability coverage, not repairs to your car.
- **Passenger in the car or en route to pickup:** Higher liability limits, plus collision and comprehensive for your car if you carry them on your personal policy (the platform's coverage typically sits on top of that, with a deductible).

None of those tiers pay for a tow because your alternator died.

## Are There Any Driver Perks That Help With Breakdowns?

Sometimes, but they're discounts, not coverage. Both platforms have offered driver rewards or partner programs over the years, such as savings on maintenance, tires, and fuel, and occasionally discounted roadside plans through a third party. These change often and vary by city and driver tier, so check the Driver app's rewards or earnings section for what's currently listed. Don't assume a perk exists because a forum post said so.

Treat any offer as a way to buy roadside coverage cheaper, not as coverage you already have.

## What Should I Do If My Car Breaks Down During a Ride?

Get everyone safe first, then handle the trip. Turn on hazards, coast to the shoulder or a parking lot if you can, and get passengers out on the side away from traffic. On a highway, they should stand well off the road behind a barrier or guardrail, not next to the car.

Then:

1. **End or pause the trip in the app** and tell the passenger you'll help them reach another ride. Most apps let you report a vehicle issue so you aren't penalized unfairly.
2. **Request a replacement ride for them** if the app allows it, or make sure they have a safe place to wait.
3. **Call for help yourself.** If you're on a busy road and feel unsafe, call 911 before anything else.
4. **Document it.** Photos of the location and a screenshot of the trip status help if anything is disputed later.

## How Can I Avoid Getting Stranded Without Coverage?

Set up coverage before you need it, because trying to buy it from the shoulder doesn't work. Spend ten minutes now to:

- Add a roadside plan that explicitly covers rideshare use.
- Save a local tow company's number in your phone, so you aren't searching with a dead-battery panic.
- Keep a charged power bank and a jump pack in the trunk. A dead battery is among the most common reasons a high-mileage rideshare car stops, and a jump pack fixes it in two minutes.
- Check tire pressure and the 12V battery age regularly. Rideshare cars rack up miles fast, and the battery usually gives warning signs like slow cranking before it quits.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does Uber pay for my tow if my car breaks down during a trip?**
A: No. Uber's insurance covers liability and, in certain trip phases, collision and comprehensive damage to your car. It does not pay for a tow due to a mechanical breakdown or a dead battery.

**Q: Does Lyft provide roadside assistance to drivers?**
A: Not as a standard benefit. Lyft may list discounts or partner offers in the driver app, but those reduce what you pay. They don't give you free towing or repair help.

**Q: Will my AAA or insurance roadside plan cover me if I drive for Uber or Lyft?**
A: It depends on the plan. Some personal policies exclude vehicles used for commercial purposes, so call and ask directly before relying on it.

**Q: Who pays if my passenger is injured when my car breaks down?**
A: Liability coverage from the platform may apply depending on the trip phase and what happened. A breakdown alone usually isn't a claim, but an accident during one is. Report it in the app and contact your own insurer.

**Q: What if I'm stuck on the highway and can't afford a tow right now?**
A: Call 911 if you're in a dangerous spot, since police can help secure the scene. Your state's DOT may run a free highway service patrol on major roads, so check your state DOT's traffic app by name.
