> **Quick Answer:** If your car overheats on a Phoenix highway, pull onto the shoulder, shut the engine off, and call for a tow. Don't open the radiator cap. A typical tow in the Phoenix metro runs about $100 to $250 depending on distance, vehicle type, and time of day. Highway pickups on the I-10 or Loop 101 can cost more than surface-street calls.

## Should I Keep Driving If My Car Is Overheating on the Highway?

No. Pull over as soon as you can safely do it, because driving a hot engine for even a few more minutes can warp the cylinder head or blow the head gasket, turning a $150 hose repair into a $2,000-plus engine job.

Here's the order of operations:

1. Turn on your hazards and signal toward the right shoulder. Don't brake hard.
2. Turn the A/C off and the cabin heat to full blast. It pulls some heat off the engine for the last few hundred yards.
3. Stop, shut the engine off, and pop the hood latch from inside. Don't stand next to the hood while it's pressurized.
4. Get out on the passenger side, away from traffic, and stay well off the pavement.

Phoenix summer pavement radiates heat, so if it's 110 degrees out, don't stand on the shoulder for long. Wait in the car with the doors cracked, or stand behind the guardrail if there is one.

## How Much Does a Tow Cost for an Overheated Car in Phoenix?

Most overheating tows in the Phoenix area cost $100 to $250 all-in, with a hookup fee of roughly $60 to $100 plus a per-mile charge after the first few miles. Exact pricing varies by company, and you should always ask for the total before the truck rolls.

What pushes the price up:

- **Freeway pickups.** A stop on I-10, I-17, or the Loop 202 near the Broadway Curve can add a surcharge because of the traffic risk to the driver.
- **Distance.** Getting from the shoulder of the US-60 in Mesa to a shop in north Scottsdale is a long haul. Per-mile rates add up fast.
- **After-hours or holiday calls.** See [Phoenix after-hours towing rates](/towing-cost-phoenix-arizona-after-hours/) for what to expect.
- **Flatbed requirement.** Many overheated cars should go on a flatbed, which costs a bit more. More in [flatbed towing costs in Phoenix](/towing-cost-in-phoenix-arizona-flatbed/).

## Can I Add Coolant or Water and Keep Going?

Only after the engine has cooled for at least 30 to 45 minutes, and even then it's a short-term gamble. In Phoenix heat that can take longer, so be patient.

If you carry coolant or water:

1. Wait until the upper radiator hose is no longer too hot to squeeze by hand and the temp gauge reads cold.
2. Use a thick rag over the radiator cap or coolant reservoir cap and turn it slowly. Steam can burn you badly.
3. Add a 50/50 coolant mix if you have it. Plain water works in an emergency.

## Is It Safe to Wait on a Phoenix Freeway Shoulder for the Tow Truck?

It's safer than driving a cooked engine, but the shoulder is still dangerous, so limit your time outside the car and stay far from traffic. Phoenix freeway speeds run 65 to 75 mph, and shoulders on stretches like I-17 north of the Black Canyon are narrow.

Keep these in mind:

- Stay in the car with your seatbelt on if the shoulder is tight and traffic is close. Get out only if you smell fuel or see fire.
- Call 911 if you can't get fully off the roadway or feel unwell from the heat. Heat exhaustion comes fast at 110 degrees.
- Give the dispatcher your freeway, direction, and nearest exit or mile marker. Arizona's 511 service and the ADOT traffic app can help you confirm where you are.
- Keep water on hand. A parked car in Phoenix summer sun can top 130 degrees inside within minutes, so cracked windows matter.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a tow cost in Phoenix if my car overheats on the freeway?**
A: Plan on roughly $100 to $250 for most metro-area tows. Long distances, freeway pickups, and after-hours calls push it higher. Get the total quoted before the driver hooks up.

**Q: Does AAA cover an overheated car in Phoenix?**
A: Yes, AAA covers towing for breakdowns, including overheating, up to your plan's mileage limit. Basic plans cover a short tow, while Plus and Premier extend that distance. Check your tier before you call.

**Q: How long should I let my engine cool before touching the radiator cap?**
A: Wait at least 30 to 45 minutes, longer in Phoenix summer heat. Never open the cap while the engine is hot, because pressurized coolant can spray out and cause serious burns.

**Q: Can an overheated engine be fixed or is it done?**
A: Often it's fixable if you stop quickly. A bad thermostat, hose, or radiator is a modest repair. Driving on after the gauge hits red can warp the head or blow the head gasket, which costs far more.

**Q: Should I request a flatbed or a regular tow truck for an overheated car?**
A: Ask for a flatbed if the engine may be damaged or if your car is all-wheel drive. A flatbed avoids putting extra strain on the drivetrain and is the safer default.
