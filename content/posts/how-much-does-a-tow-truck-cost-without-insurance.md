> **Quick Answer:** Without insurance, expect to pay $75 to $125 for a standard local tow under 5 miles, plus $3 to $7 per mile after that. A 10-mile tow typically runs $100 to $200. A long-distance tow of 50 miles or more can reach $400 to $600. After-hours calls, flatbed requirements, and urban markets all push prices higher. Get the rate confirmed before they hook up your car.

## What Does a Tow Truck Actually Cost If I'm Paying Out of Pocket?

Most tow companies charge a hook-up fee plus a per-mile rate. The hook-up fee, also called a base rate or dispatch fee, usually runs $50 to $100. Per-mile rates typically fall between $3 and $7, depending on your location and the type of truck required.

Here is a realistic breakdown by distance for someone paying cash or card with no coverage:

| Distance | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|
| Under 5 miles | $75 to $125 |
| 5 to 10 miles | $100 to $200 |
| 10 to 25 miles | $150 to $275 |
| 25 to 50 miles | $250 to $400 |
| 50+ miles | $400 to $600+ |

These are national averages. High-cost metros like New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago sit at the top of those ranges. Smaller cities and rural areas tend to be cheaper, but rural tows sometimes cost more per mile because the driver is traveling farther to reach you.

Flatbed service adds $50 to $100 over a standard wheel-lift tow. If you drive an all-wheel drive vehicle, an electric vehicle, or a low-clearance sports car, a flatbed is usually required. [Towing a car on a flatbed in Seattle runs $125 to $300 for a local tow](/towing-cost-seattle-washington-flatbed/), which gives you a sense of what major West Coast metro pricing looks like.

## What Makes the Price Go Higher Than Expected?

After-hours calls are the biggest surprise. Evening, weekend, and holiday service typically adds $25 to $75 on top of the base rate. If your car breaks down on a Friday night, you are paying a premium.

Other factors that drive the price up:

- **Vehicle size.** Trucks, SUVs, and vans cost more to tow than sedans.
- **Winching.** If your car is in a ditch or off the pavement, most companies charge a separate winch fee of $50 to $150 on top of the tow. A [car stuck in a snow ditch](/car-stuck-in-snow-ditch-tow-truck-cost/) is a common situation where this applies.
- **Storage fees.** If your car goes to an impound or a tow yard and sits overnight, daily storage fees of $35 to $75 start accumulating fast.
- **Long wait for dispatch.** Some services charge a waiting fee if they arrive and the situation is complicated.

One detail worth knowing: accident scenes on controlled-access highways often involve police-directed tows. If the police call the tow truck, you may not get to choose the company, and those rates can be higher than what you would negotiate yourself. ## Can I Negotiate the Price or Is It Fixed?

You can often negotiate, and it is worth trying. The rate is not regulated the way a taxi fare is in most states. The best time to push back is before they hook up your vehicle. Once the car is on the truck, your leverage disappears.

## Is There a Cheaper Alternative to Calling a Random Tow Company?

Yes. If you are not in immediate danger and the car can wait, you have options.

AAA membership costs $60 to $130 per year depending on the tier. A single tow without coverage costs $100 to $300. If you break down even once a year, the membership pays for itself. [The cost comparison breaks down clearly here.](/aaa-membership-worth-it-single-tow-cost-comparison/)

Motor club apps like Honk, Urgently, or Blink are newer alternatives. You book through the app, see the price upfront, and a vetted driver shows up. Pricing is often more transparent than calling a random dispatch line.

Your auto insurance may cover towing even if you did not realize it. Roadside assistance riders are cheap add-ons, sometimes $5 to $15 per year. Check your policy declarations page right now if you are not sure. Progressive and State Farm both offer this coverage, and it can cover towing costs you would otherwise pay out of pocket.

## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average tow truck cost without insurance for a 10-mile tow?**
A: Plan on $125 to $200 for a 10-mile tow with no insurance coverage. That includes a $75 to $100 hook-up fee plus $5 to $7 per mile. After-hours or flatbed service pushes it toward the higher end.

**Q: Do tow truck companies charge more at night?**
A: Most do. After-hours rates typically add $25 to $75 on top of the standard base fee. Some companies have a flat after-hours surcharge; others just quote you a higher rate without naming it separately.

**Q: Can I refuse to pay if the tow truck already took my car?**
A: Not without consequences. Tow companies in most states have lien rights on your vehicle. If you refuse to pay, they can hold the car and charge daily storage fees until you settle up. That $150 tow becomes much more expensive sitting in a yard for a week.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive than a wheel-lift?**
A: Yes, typically $50 to $100 more. Flatbeds are required for all-wheel drive cars, EVs, and vehicles with front-end damage. If your car can roll and steer, a wheel-lift is usually fine and costs less.

**Q: Does calling 911 get me a free tow?**
A: No. Police can call a tow truck to clear a hazard, but you still pay for the tow. In some cases, a police-dispatched company charges more than one you called yourself, and you have less ability to shop around.
