> **Quick Answer:** Usually no, not by itself. A collision damage waiver (CDW/LDW) covers damage to the rental, not a breakdown tow. Towing is typically handled by the rental company's own roadside program, and many include it for mechanical failure. Call the number on your rental agreement or key tag before you call any tow truck, or you may end up paying out of pocket.

## Does Rental Car Insurance Actually Cover Towing?

Rental car insurance products mostly do not pay for a tow, but the rental company usually arranges one for free when the car fails mechanically. The two things get confused all the time.

A collision damage waiver or loss damage waiver is not insurance. It is the rental company agreeing not to bill you for damage or theft of the car. It does not pay a tow operator for hauling a car with a dead alternator.

What often does cover a breakdown tow is the rental company's built-in roadside assistance. Most major companies run a 24-hour line and will dispatch their own contracted tow or send a replacement vehicle. Some include basic roadside in the rate. Others sell it as an add-on, often a few dollars a day.

Check your rental agreement now. Look for the phrases "roadside assistance," "emergency assistance," or "breakdown." The phone number is usually on the contract, the key tag, or a sticker inside the driver's door jamb or glove box.

## Who Pays If I Break Down Versus Get in an Accident?

For a mechanical breakdown, the rental company typically pays. For an accident or driver-caused problem, you or your insurance usually pays.

Mechanical failure (dead battery, failed alternator, overheating, transmission problems) is the company's responsibility because they own the car. They should cover the tow and swap you into another vehicle.

Driver-caused trouble is different. Running out of fuel, losing the key, a flat you caused by hitting a curb, or putting the wrong fuel in the tank can shift the tow bill to you. Accident towing falls under liability and collision coverage, not roadside programs. If you accepted the CDW, it may waive the car's damage, but the tow often still gets billed separately depending on the contract. For how accident towing generally works with auto policies, see [who pays for towing after a car accident that wasn't your fault](/who-pays-for-towing-after-a-car-accident-not-my-fault/).

## What Should I Do Before I Call a Tow Truck?

Call the rental company's roadside number first. Calling your own tow truck can void your reimbursement.

Here's the order that protects your wallet:

1. Get safe. Pull as far off the road as you can, hazards on, and get everyone out and away from traffic if you're on a highway shoulder.
2. Call the roadside number on your rental agreement or key tag. Give them your location and plate number.
3. Ask them directly: "Is this tow covered, and will you send your own provider?" Get a reference or case number.
4. If they tell you to arrange your own tow, ask for authorization in writing (text or email) and the maximum they'll reimburse.
5. Photograph the dash warning lights, odometer, and the car's condition before it's hooked up.

## Can My Own Car Insurance or Credit Card Cover the Tow?

Possibly, but only for the right situation, and it depends on what you carry.

Your personal auto policy may extend liability to a rental, but roadside and towing coverage is a separate add-on that often does not follow you into a rental car. Read your declarations page, or call your insurer. For how those add-ons typically work, see [does car insurance cover roadside assistance if I add it](/does-car-insurance-cover-roadside-assistance-if-i-add-it/).

A membership like AAA is tied to you as a member, not to a specific car, so it can often cover a tow in a rental. Mileage limits depend on your plan, covered in [how far will AAA tow my car for free](/how-far-will-aaa-tow-my-car-for-free/).

Many credit cards that offer rental coverage focus on collision and theft. Some include roadside dispatch, usually pay-per-use rather than free. Check your card's benefits guide before you count on it. More on that in [does my credit card cover roadside assistance towing](/does-my-credit-card-cover-roadside-assistance-towing/).

## What If I Already Paid for a Tow Myself?

Keep every document and file a reimbursement claim with the rental company right away.

Save the itemized tow invoice, your location and timestamps, the case number from your roadside call, and photos. Submit them to the rental company's customer service, not the counter at the airport. If they refuse a breakdown tow that was clearly mechanical, escalate and cite your agreement. A disputed charge can also be contested with your card issuer if it was billed to you improperly.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does the collision damage waiver cover towing?**
A: Generally no. A CDW or LDW waives your responsibility for damage or theft of the rental. A tow for a breakdown is handled by the company's roadside program, not the waiver, though some contracts bundle them differently.

**Q: Who pays if my rental car breaks down on the highway?**
A: The rental company normally pays for a mechanical breakdown tow and should send a replacement car. Call their roadside line first so the tow is authorized and documented.

**Q: Will my personal auto insurance cover towing a rental car?**
A: Only if your policy includes towing or roadside coverage that extends to rentals, which many do not. Check your declarations page or call your insurer before you rely on it.

**Q: What if I run out of gas in a rental car?**
A: Expect to pay. Running out of fuel is treated as driver error, so the tow or fuel delivery often gets billed to you, sometimes with a service fee on top. See [does roadside assistance cover fuel delivery when you run out of gas](/does-roadside-assistance-cover-fuel-delivery-ran-out-of-gas/).

**Q: Can I use AAA in a rental car?**
A: Yes, in most cases. AAA coverage follows the member, not the vehicle, so you can call them for a tow while driving a rental. Check your plan's tow mileage limit first.
