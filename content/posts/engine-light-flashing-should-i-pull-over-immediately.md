> **Quick Answer:** Yes, pull over immediately. A flashing check engine light is not the same as a steady one. It signals an active misfire severe enough to damage your catalytic converter within minutes of continued driving. Get off the road, shut the engine off, and call for a tow. Do not drive to a shop unless the light goes steady and the car feels normal.

## Is a Flashing Engine Light Actually an Emergency, or Can I Keep Driving?

It is an emergency. A flashing light means the engine is misfiring right now, meaning unburned fuel is passing into the exhaust and cooking your catalytic converter. A catalytic converter replacement runs $1,000 to $2,500 on most vehicles. A few more minutes of driving can turn a $200 ignition coil problem into a $2,000 repair.

A steady check engine light is different. That means a fault has been logged but conditions are currently stable. You can drive to a shop that day with a steady light. A flashing light gives you no such grace period.

## What Do I Do Right Now If My Engine Light Is Flashing?

Signal, get to the right shoulder or the nearest exit, and stop. Turn the engine off. Do not restart it to "see if it clears." If you are on an interstate, stay in the car with your seatbelt on and hazard lights running until you are safely out of the travel lanes. Then call for a tow.

If the light is flashing and you also notice rough idling, a shaking sensation, or a smell like rotten eggs from the exhaust, those are signs the catalytic converter is already under stress. At that point you want a flatbed, not a wheel-lift, especially on all-wheel-drive vehicles. If you need a reference on what to do when your engine light is flashing and the car is shaking at the same time, the steps at [Check Engine Light Flashing and Car Shaking: Pull Over Now](/check-engine-light-flashing-car-shaking-pull-over-now/) cover that combination in detail.

Set out reflective triangles or flares behind the vehicle if you have them and traffic is close. For guidance on which warning devices work best on a highway shoulder, see [Emergency Triangle vs Flares: Which Is Safer on the Highway?](/emergency-triangle-vs-flares-which-is-safer-on-highway/)

## What Is Actually Causing My Engine Light to Flash?

The most common cause is a failed ignition coil or a fouled spark plug causing a cylinder misfire. On a four-cylinder engine, one dead cylinder means 25% of combustion is dumping raw fuel into the exhaust. V6 and V8 engines are slightly more forgiving for a few seconds, but not enough to justify driving.

Other causes include a failing fuel injector, a cracked spark plug wire (common on older engines still using distributor-style ignition), or a lean fuel condition caused by a vacuum leak. None of these are safe to drive through when the light is actively flashing. The ECU flashes the light, rather than keeping it steady, specifically because it detects a condition that causes immediate hardware damage.

Get a tow. The distance does not matter much here. Even a mile of driving with a severe misfire is enough to

## What Will This Cost Me, and Will Roadside Assistance Cover the Tow?

A local tow to a shop typically runs $75 to $150 for the first five miles, with most companies charging $3 to $7 per mile after that. If you have AAA, your insurer's roadside add-on, or a manufacturer roadside program (Ford, Toyota, and GM all include roadside for newer vehicles under warranty), that tow may cost you nothing out of pocket.

The repair cost depends on what caused the misfire. A single ignition coil is $80 to $200 in parts and labor on most domestic vehicles. A full set of coils and plugs on a V6 can run $400 to $600. If you drove long enough to damage the catalytic converter before stopping, add $1,000 or more on top of that. Stopping now is always cheaper than stopping later.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive with a flashing check engine light if I go slow?**
A: No. Speed does not change the damage happening inside the exhaust system. The catalytic converter is being damaged by unburned fuel regardless of how fast you are going. Pull over and tow it.

**Q: What is the difference between a flashing and a solid check engine light?**
A: A solid light means a fault is stored but conditions are currently stable enough to drive to a shop. A flashing light means active damage is occurring right now and you should stop immediately.

**Q: My check engine light was flashing but stopped. Is it safe to drive now?**
A: Only if the car idles smoothly, has no shaking, and the light stays steady or off for several minutes. Even then, get it scanned the same day. A misfire that clears itself can return, and the underlying cause is still present.

**Q: Will my car fail if I turn it off and back on with a flashing check engine light?**
A: It might restart and run, especially if the misfire is intermittent. But restarting does not fix the problem. If the light flashes again after restart, do not drive it. Call for a tow.

**Q: How do I know if my catalytic converter is already damaged from the misfire?**
A: A sulfur or rotten egg smell from the exhaust is a strong sign. You may also notice a rattling sound from under the car when you accelerate, which is the converter substrate breaking apart. At that point the repair cost goes up significantly.
