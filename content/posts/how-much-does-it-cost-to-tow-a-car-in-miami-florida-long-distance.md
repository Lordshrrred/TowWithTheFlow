> **Quick Answer:** A long distance tow from Miami typically costs a hook-up fee of $75 to $125 plus roughly $3 to $7 per mile after the first 5 to 10 miles. A 100-mile tow to Orlando often lands between $350 and $700. Flatbeds, tolls, after-hours calls, and storm demand push it higher. Always get an all-in quote before the truck leaves.

## What Does a Long Distance Tow Out of Miami Actually Cost?

Expect a flat hook-up fee of $75 to $125 plus $3 to $7 for every mile beyond the included distance. Prices vary by company and by the day, so treat these as ranges, not promises.

Here is what that looks like in practice:

| Trip from Miami | Approx. Miles | Typical Range |
|---|---|---|
| Fort Lauderdale | 30 | $150 to $300 |
| West Palm Beach | 70 | $250 to $500 |
| Orlando | 235 | $600 to $1,400 |
| Tampa | 280 | $800 to $1,700 |

Once you pass 100 miles, many companies drop the per-mile rate, so a quote beats multiplying numbers yourself. ## What Makes a Long Distance Tow More Expensive?

The biggest price drivers are vehicle type, tow method, timing, and where you are stuck. Here is what moves the number:

- **Flatbed vs. wheel-lift.** Flatbeds cost more, but they are the right call for AWD, lowered, electric, and damaged cars. Details are in [towing cost in Miami Florida flatbed](/towing-cost-in-miami-florida-flatbed/).
- **Tolls.** Heading north on the Florida Turnpike or toward Alligator Alley (I-75) adds toll charges. Ask whether they are included in the quote.
- **Time of day.** Nights, weekends, and holidays often add $50 to $150.
- **Location.** A car stuck in the I-95 express lanes or on the Dolphin Expressway (SR 836) near the I-395 interchange can cost more for recovery.
- **Storms.** Demand spikes before and after tropical systems. See [towing cost in Miami Florida during hurricane season](/towing-cost-miami-florida-hurricane-season/).

## How Do I Get an Accurate Quote Without Getting Burned?

Call two or three companies and ask for a total, not a rate. A per-mile number alone tells you nothing about the final bill.

Ask these exact questions:

1. What is the total price to my destination, including hook-up, mileage, tolls, and fuel surcharge?
2. Will you send a flatbed, and is that included?
3. What is the dispatch fee if I cancel?

## Is There a Cheaper Way to Move My Car a Long Distance?

Yes, sometimes. If the car is not an emergency, tow it locally to a shop or storage lot first, then arrange the long haul separately.

Your options, from cheapest to most expensive:

- **Roadside coverage.** AAA Plus and Premier include extended tow distances, often 100 miles or more depending on the tier. Check your own plan limits before you pay out of pocket.
- **Auto transport carrier.** For a non-urgent move out of state, a car hauler is often cheaper than a dedicated tow past 200 miles, but it takes days.
- **Repair shop near home.** Towing 5 miles to a Miami shop and repairing there can cost less than a 150-mile tow to your regular mechanic.

If you have no coverage at all, read [tow truck cost in Miami Florida without insurance](/tow-truck-cost-in-miami-florida-without-insurance/) for realistic numbers and ways to cut the bill.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a 100-mile tow cost in Florida?**
A: Plan on roughly $300 to $700 for a 100-mile tow, depending on the company, truck type, and timing. Flatbeds and after-hours calls sit at the high end.

**Q: Is it cheaper to tow a car or ship it?**
A: For emergencies and distances under about 200 miles, towing is usually faster and competitive. Past that, a car hauler is often cheaper but takes several days.

**Q: Do tow companies charge by the mile or a flat rate?**
A: Most charge a flat hook-up fee plus a per-mile rate beyond a base distance. Long hauls are often quoted as a single flat total, so ask for that.

**Q: Will AAA cover a long distance tow from Miami?**
A: Only up to your plan's tow limit, which varies by membership level. Miles beyond the limit are billed to you, so confirm your tier before dispatch.

**Q: Should I pay the tow driver upfront?**
A: Usually you pay on delivery or at pickup, but never agree to anything without a written or texted total price first. Get a receipt itemizing every charge.
