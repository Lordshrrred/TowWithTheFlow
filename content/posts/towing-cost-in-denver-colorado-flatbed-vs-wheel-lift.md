## Flatbed vs. Wheel Lift: The Real Difference

A wheel lift tow truck hooks under your front or rear axle and lifts two wheels off the ground while the other two roll. It is faster to hook up and costs less. A flatbed drives your whole car onto a tilting platform so all four wheels leave the ground completely.

That distinction matters a lot in Denver, where AWD and 4WD vehicles are extremely common. If all four wheels drive, and you lift only two, the drivetrain keeps spinning on the wheels still touching the road. On a Subaru, a Jeep, or any full-time AWD car, that does real damage. Always request a flatbed for AWD vehicles, even for a short tow.

Wheel lift is fine for a standard front-wheel-drive or rear-wheel-drive car that rolls freely and has enough ground clearance to accept the cradle.

## When to Insist on a Flatbed

Demand a flatbed in these situations, no exceptions:

- Your vehicle is AWD or 4WD (Subaru, Toyota 4Runner, Ford F-150 4x4, Jeep, most modern SUVs)
- The car has been in a collision and the steering or suspension is compromised
- A wheel is completely flat or the rim is bent and it cannot roll
- The vehicle is a rear-engine car (Porsche 911, older VW Beetle) where lifting the rear means lifting the drive wheels
- Low-clearance vehicles, sports cars, or heavily modified lifts that a wheel cradle cannot safely grip
- The transmission is locked out of neutral and the wheels will not roll freely. If you are dealing with that specific situation, check [car won't shift out of park towing options](/car-wont-shift-out-of-park-towing-options/) before the truck arrives.

If a dispatcher tries to send a wheel lift for your AWD vehicle to save time or money, push back. The extra $30 to $50 for a flatbed is nothing compared to a transfer case rebuild.

## Stay Safe

- Pull as far off the road as possible before calling. On I-25 or I-70, get behind the white line or onto an exit ramp if you can reach one.
- Turn on hazard lights immediately. Stay in the car if you are on a busy highway. Get out and behind a barrier if the shoulder is narrow.
- Tell the dispatcher your exact vehicle make, model, and drivetrain so they send the right truck the first time.
- Get a price confirmed over the phone before the truck rolls. Reputable Denver operators will give you a hook-up fee and per-mile rate upfront.
- If you carry roadside assistance through your insurance, call them first. Some policies cap reimbursement at flat rates that do not cover flatbed premiums. Know your limit before you authorize the tow.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can a wheel lift tow truck handle my Subaru Outback?**
A: No, the Outback uses full-time all-wheel drive, so lifting only two wheels will spin the drivetrain on the wheels still touching the road and can damage the transfer case or transmission. Always request a flatbed for any AWD or 4WD vehicle.

**Q: How much extra will I pay if I break down late at night in Denver?**
A: Most Denver towing companies add an after-hours surcharge of $25 to $50 for calls between 9 p.m. and 6 a.m., with weekend late-night calls sometimes reaching $75 on top of the base rate. Budget toward the higher end of the price ranges if you are calling after dark.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover the difference between a wheel lift and a flatbed tow?**
A: It depends on your policy. Some roadside assistance coverage caps reimbursement at a flat dollar amount that may not fully cover flatbed pricing, so confirm your limit with your insurer before authorizing the tow to avoid an unexpected out-of-pocket charge.
