> **Quick Answer:** Turn on your hazard lights, call 911, and tell the dispatcher you're on the median. Stay in the car with your seatbelt on unless there is fire or smoke. Never try to cross traffic on foot. Median stops are more dangerous than they look, so ask for police or a highway patrol unit, not just a tow truck.

## Should I Stay in My Car on the Interstate Median?

Yes. Stay in the car with your seatbelt fastened and your hazard lights on, unless you smell smoke, see flames, or the car is sitting in the travel lane.

The median feels safer than the shoulder because you're away from the right lane. It isn't. Drivers drift left when they're tired or distracted, and a stopped car on the inside shoulder gets hit from behind at highway speed. Your seatbelt and the car's structure protect you better than standing outside it.

If you do have to get out for fire or smoke, exit on the side facing away from traffic, move well away from the car, and stand behind a guardrail or cable barrier if one exists. Do not stand between your car and the travel lanes.

## Who Do I Call When I'm Stopped on the Median?

Call 911 first, and say "I'm on the median" along with your direction of travel and the nearest mile marker or exit.

A median stop is a legitimate safety emergency, and police or highway patrol can put a patrol car behind you with lights on. A tow truck cannot do that. Many tow companies are also reluctant or legally restricted from working the inside shoulder, so dispatch often has to send an officer first.

Give the dispatcher these details:

- Highway number and direction (for example, northbound I-25)
- Last exit you passed and the next one coming
- Nearest mile marker, which is on the small green posts along the edge
- Your car's color and make, so officers can spot you
- Whether anyone is hurt, and whether kids or pets are with you

If you have no signal, see [what to do if your car breaks down with no phone](/what-to-do-if-car-breaks-down-with-no-phone/). Your state's 511 service or DOT traffic app can also help you confirm your location.

## Can I Cross the Interstate on Foot to Get Help?

No. Do not walk across interstate lanes under any circumstances.

Cars at 70 mph cover the length of a football field in about three seconds, and drivers are not watching for pedestrians. Crossing four or six lanes with a median in the middle is how a breakdown becomes a fatality. The same goes for walking along the median toward an exit. Help is coming to you. Wait.

If you're in a car with other people, everyone stays buckled. If someone absolutely must leave the vehicle for a medical or fire reason, they exit away from traffic and stand clear of the car, not behind it.

## Can I Drive the Car Out of the Median?

## How Long Will I Wait and What Will the Tow Cost?

Expect a wait anywhere from 20 minutes to over an hour depending on traffic, time of day, and weather, and the tow itself is a separate matter once you're safe.

Police usually arrive first. The tow often comes after, and the company may need law enforcement to block a lane before it can hook up. Some states run free highway assistance patrols that help with fuel, a jump, or a tire. Others dispatch a rotation tow company. ## Common Questions

**Q: Is it legal to stop on the interstate median?**
A: Stopping for a genuine emergency or breakdown is allowed in most states. Parking there for any other reason is not. Get the car out of the median as soon as it's safe, and let police direct you.

**Q: Should I keep my hazard lights on or turn them off?**
A: Keep them on. If the battery is dying, keep them on as long as possible and avoid draining it with the radio or climate control.

**Q: What if my car is partly in the travel lane?**
A: Get out and move well away from the car and traffic, behind a barrier if possible, then call 911. A car in a live lane is a much bigger hazard than one fully on the median.

**Q: Will my insurance or roadside plan cover a median tow?**
A: Usually, yes, if you have roadside assistance or towing coverage. Call your provider after you're safe, but call 911 first. See [whether AAA covers towing if the car isn't in your name](/does-aaa-cover-towing-if-car-is-not-registered-in-my-name/) if registration is a concern.

**Q: Should I put out flares or a warning triangle?**
A: Only if you can place them without stepping into traffic. On a median, setting triangles behind your car often means walking toward moving lanes, so skip it if that's the case. Hazard lights and staying belted are safer.
