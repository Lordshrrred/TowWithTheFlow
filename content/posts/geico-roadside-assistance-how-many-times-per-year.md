> **Quick Answer:** GEICO's Emergency Road Service add-on generally does not advertise a fixed number of calls per year. The limits that matter are per event: a towing distance cap and a dollar limit on service calls. Excessive claims can still draw scrutiny from the insurer. Your exact terms are in your policy declarations page, so check that before assuming you're covered.

## Does GEICO Limit How Many Times Per Year I Can Use Roadside Assistance?

GEICO does not typically publish a hard yearly cap on roadside calls, but your policy has per-event limits that work like a cap. Those limits are usually a maximum towing distance and a maximum amount for labor or service at the scene.

The exact wording depends on your state, your policy, and when you bought it. Don't trust a blog post, including this one, over your own declarations page. Log into your GEICO account or app, open your policy documents, and look for "Emergency Road Service." That page tells you what you actually bought.

If you're standing on a shoulder right now, make the call first and sort out the fine print while you wait. A denied claim is better to learn about after you're safe.

## What Does GEICO Roadside Assistance Actually Cover Per Call?

Each call is typically covered up to a set limit, and the usual services are towing, jump-starts, flat tire changes, lockout help, and a small delivery of fuel. You generally pay for the fuel itself.

Here's how those usually break down:

- **Towing:** Capped by distance or dollar amount. Anything beyond the limit comes out of your pocket.
- **Jump-start:** Usually covered. If the battery is dead for good, you'll need a new one at your cost.
- **Flat tire:** They'll put on your spare. They won't supply a tire. If you have no spare or a run-flat that's destroyed, expect a tow instead.
- **Lockout:** Covered up to a service-call limit. A locksmith job on a high-security key can run past it.
- **Fuel delivery:** The service call is covered. The gas isn't free.

A tow over your mileage cap is the most common surprise. If your shop is 40 miles away and your cap is 15, you owe the difference to the driver, often per mile.

## Can Too Many Roadside Calls Get My Coverage Cancelled or Raise My Rate?

Frequent claims can lead to questions at renewal, though roadside calls usually weigh far less than collision claims. Some insurers treat roadside as a separate service benefit, but policies and state rules differ, and I can't tell you GEICO's internal handling.

What I can tell you is the mechanical truth. If you're calling roadside four times a year, you have a car problem, not a coverage problem. A weak battery, a failing alternator, or a tire with a slow leak will keep stranding you until it's fixed. Read [Alternator Died While Driving: Signs and What to Do](/alternator-died-while-driving-signs-and-what-to-do/) if your car keeps dying right after jump-starts. A repeat battery call usually means the charging system is the real culprit.

## What Should I Do While I Wait for the Tow Truck?

Get out of the traffic lane and keep your hazards on. Move to the shoulder as far right as you can, or off the road entirely. If you can't safely stand outside, stay buckled in with your seatbelt on and the car in park.

If it's a freeway, exit the vehicle on the passenger side and wait behind the guardrail when one exists. Don't stand between your car and traffic. The shoulder on a busy interstate is one of the most dangerous places to be.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is GEICO roadside assistance unlimited?**
A: Not exactly. There's usually no advertised cap on the number of calls, but each call has limits on towing distance and service costs. Check your declarations page for the exact figures on your policy.

**Q: Does using GEICO roadside assistance raise my insurance rates?**
A: Roadside calls are generally treated differently from collision or liability claims, and they typically carry less weight. Policies and state rules vary, so call GEICO if you want a definite answer for your account.

**Q: How far will GEICO tow my car?**
A: It depends on the limit written in your Emergency Road Service coverage. If your destination is farther than the cap, you pay the extra mileage to the tow company.

**Q: Can I use GEICO roadside assistance if I'm not in my own car?**
A: Often the coverage follows the insured vehicle listed on the policy, not the driver. Some policies extend to the named insured in other vehicles, so confirm in your policy documents or with GEICO.

**Q: What if I don't have Emergency Road Service on my GEICO policy?**
A: You pay out of pocket for the tow or service. Compare the call-out price to your options, and check whether your credit card or a motor club membership covers you instead.
