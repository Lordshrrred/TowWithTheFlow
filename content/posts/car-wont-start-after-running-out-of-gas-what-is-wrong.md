> **Quick Answer:** Running a fuel tank dry can air-lock the fuel lines, clog the fuel filter with sediment that settled at the bottom of the tank, or burn out the fuel pump, which needs fuel around it to stay cool. Add at least two gallons, wait two minutes, then try cranking in short five-second bursts. If it still won't catch after three or four tries, stop cranking. You likely have a bigger problem than an empty tank.

## Why Won't My Car Start Even After I Added Gas?

The fuel pump is almost always the reason. When you run the tank completely dry, the pump sucks in air instead of fuel, and it runs hot with nothing to cool it. Even a short dry-run can overheat and damage the pump's internal components. On top of that, sediment and rust that sits at the bottom of any fuel tank gets stirred up and pulled straight into the fuel filter or injectors the moment the pump starts drawing again.

A clogged fuel filter means fuel can't reach the engine at normal pressure, so the engine cranks but never fires. A damaged pump means fuel pressure is too low or nonexistent. Either way, the symptom looks the same: the engine turns over fine but refuses to start.

## What Do I Do Right Now to Try to Get It Running?

Add at least two gallons, not just enough to move the needle. Less than that and the pump pickup tube may still be pulling in air. Once you've added fuel, do this:

1. Turn the key to the "on" position (not crank, just on) for five seconds. This primes the fuel system.
2. Turn it off. Repeat that prime cycle two or three times.
3. Now crank the engine in five-second bursts, with ten seconds of rest between each attempt.
4. If it catches and dies immediately, the fuel filter is likely clogged and starving the engine.
5. If it never catches at all after four or five attempts, stop cranking. You risk killing a weak battery and flooding the situation further.

Prolonged cranking with a damaged pump won't fix anything and will drain your battery. At that point you need a tow, not more tries.

## How Do I Know If the Fuel Pump Is Fried?

Turn the key to the "on" position and listen at the fuel filler cap or near the rear of the car. A working fuel pump makes a faint whirring or humming sound for about two seconds as it pressurizes the lines. If you hear nothing, the pump is not priming. That silence is a strong indicator the pump failed during the dry run.

On most fuel-injected cars built after the mid-1990s, the fuel pump lives inside the gas tank and relies on gasoline for lubrication and cooling. Running it dry even briefly can seize or burn the brushes in an electric pump. This is especially common on vehicles with more than 80,000 miles, because the pump was already worn.

If the pump is confirmed dead, the car needs to be towed. Driving with a failed pump is not possible and attempting to keep cranking risks draining your battery on top of the pump problem. ## Is There Anything I Can Do on the Side of the Road Before Calling a Tow?

A few things are worth trying before you make the call. First, if you have access to a can of starting fluid, a short spray into the air intake can confirm whether the engine itself is fine and the problem is fuel delivery only. If it fires briefly on starting fluid and then dies, fuel is not reaching the engine.

Second, check your inertia fuel shutoff switch. Ford vehicles, in particular, have a reset button, usually located in the trunk, behind a panel, or near the spare tire. A hard jolt, like bumping a curb when you coasted in, can trip it. Push the button and try again.

Third, if the car starts but immediately sputters and dies, drive very slowly if you can. A partially clogged fuel filter may pass just enough fuel for low-rpm movement. Get off the road and to a safe spot rather than sitting in a travel lane.

If none of this works, call for a tow. Do not keep cranking. A dead battery on top of a pump problem means you are now dealing with two separate breakdowns. ## Common Questions

**Q: How much gas do I need to add to get it started after running out?**
A: Add at least two gallons. Less than that and the fuel pump pickup tube may not be submerged enough to draw liquid fuel reliably, especially on a slight incline.

**Q: Can running out of gas ruin my fuel pump permanently?**
A: Yes. Running dry overheats electric fuel pumps because gasoline is what cools and lubricates them. One dry-run on a high-mileage pump can finish it off. On a newer vehicle with low miles, you may get lucky.

**Q: My car started after I added gas but then died after a few seconds. What does that mean?**
A: That usually means a partially clogged fuel filter is restricting flow. The engine got just enough pressure to fire but can't sustain it. The filter needs to be replaced before the car will run normally.

**Q: How long should I crank the engine before giving up and calling a tow?**
A: Limit yourself to four attempts of five seconds each, with rest in between. Beyond that you risk killing the battery and overheating the starter motor. If it hasn't started by then, you need a professional diagnosis.

**Q: Will my check engine light come on after running out of gas?**
A: Possibly. Low fuel pressure caused by a starved pump can trigger codes related to the fuel system, like P0087 (fuel rail pressure too low). If the light stays on after the car is running normally, a scan tool will tell you exactly what was tripped.
