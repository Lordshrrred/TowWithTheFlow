> **Quick Answer:** Get to the shoulder, turn on your hazards, and call 911 if you're in a dangerous spot. If you're safely off the road, call your roadside assistance provider first (AAA, your insurer, or a direct tow service). Save insurance for after the tow is arranged. The order matters: safety, then tow, then insurance.

## What Do I Do the Moment My Car Dies on the Interstate?

Steer to the right shoulder immediately, even if the car is coasting on fumes of momentum. Get as far right as you can, ideally past the white fog line. Turn on your hazard lights the second you realize something is wrong, not after you've stopped. Once you're stopped, if you have road flares or reflective triangles, set them 100 to 300 feet behind the car. Exit from the passenger side door, away from traffic, and stand behind the guardrail if one is present. Do not stand between your car and moving traffic.

If you stopped in a lane, on a ramp, or anywhere that puts you or other drivers in immediate danger, call 911 before anything else. Dispatchers can send highway patrol to block traffic or push you to safety. For a clean shoulder stop, you can skip 911 and go straight to your tow service.

## Who Do I Actually Call First After Getting to the Shoulder?

Call your roadside assistance provider first, whether that is AAA, a plan through your auto insurer like [State Farm roadside assistance](/state-farm-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-limit/) or [GEICO](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-distance-limit/), or a standalone service. These calls get a truck rolling faster than calling a random tow company because the dispatcher already has your vehicle info and a network of drivers. If you have no roadside coverage, search for a local tow company using Google Maps. Pick one with recent reviews and a listed address, not just a phone number.

Call your insurance company after the tow is arranged, not before. The tow situation is time-sensitive. Your insurance claim is not going anywhere.

## Should I Call 911 or a Tow Truck First?

It depends on where you stopped. Call 911 first if you are stopped in a live lane, on a bridge with no shoulder, in a tunnel, or if anyone is injured. Highway patrol can arrive faster than a tow truck and has the authority to manage traffic around you. In many states, a trooper will also stay with you until the tow arrives.

If you are safely on the shoulder with clearance from traffic, skip 911 and call your tow service. Do not tie up an emergency line for a non-emergency. Most state DOTs also operate highway service patrols on major interstates, marked trucks that drive the highway looking for disabled vehicles. On heavily traveled corridors like I-95, I-10, or I-80, you may see one within 20 minutes without calling anyone.

## How Long Will I Wait and What Do I Do While I Wait?

Tow truck response times on interstates typically run 30 to 90 minutes depending on your location and time of day. Rural stretches of interstate can push that wait past two hours. Stay in your car with your seatbelt on if you are safely on the shoulder and far from traffic. A stationary car on a shoulder is hit by inattentive drivers more often than people expect. If your car is close to the travel lane, get over the guardrail entirely and wait there.

## Will My Insurance Cover This Tow, or Am I Paying Out of Pocket?

Most standard auto insurance policies do not include towing unless you added roadside assistance as a rider. Check your declarations page or your insurer's app right now, not later. If you have it, you are typically covered for a tow up to a set mileage limit, often 15 to 100 miles depending on the plan and tier. If you have no coverage, expect to pay $75 to $150 for a short local tow and more for longer distances on rural stretches of highway. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I just wait for a state highway patrol truck to find me?**
A: Yes, on most major interstates, highway service patrol trucks actively drive routes looking for disabled vehicles. However, response times vary and are not guaranteed. Call for a tow at the same time so you are not waiting with no backup plan.

**Q: What if my phone is about to die and I can only make one call?**
A: Call 911. They can relay your location to highway patrol and contact a tow on your behalf. Give them your exact mile marker and highway number immediately.

**Q: How do I know my exact location on the interstate to give the tow dispatcher?**
A: Look for green mile marker signs posted on the right shoulder. Give the dispatcher your state, highway number, direction of travel, and the nearest mile marker number. That is more useful than a street address.

**Q: Can a tow truck driver tell me what's wrong with my car?**
A: Most drivers will give you an honest read if they see something obvious, like a blown tire or a belt dangling under the hood. They are not mechanics and are not liable for a diagnosis, but most will tell you what they notice. Do not make repair decisions based solely on what they say.

**Q: Is it safe to accept a tow from a truck that pulls up without being called?**
A: Be cautious. Some unsolicited tow trucks at breakdowns are legitimate highway service patrols, but others are predatory operators who overcharge and take your car to a lot you did not choose. If a truck shows up unbidden, ask where they will take the car and get the price in writing before agreeing.
