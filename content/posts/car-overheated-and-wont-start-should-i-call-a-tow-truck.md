> **Quick Answer:** Yes, call a tow truck. If your car overheated and now won't start, do not keep cranking it. A hot engine that refuses to turn over can mean a failed coolant system, seized components, or a damaged head gasket. Repeated starting attempts can turn a repair into a rebuild. Get to a safe spot, let it cool, and have it towed unless the fix is obvious and simple.

## Should I Call a Tow Truck If My Car Overheated and Won't Start?

Yes, call one. An engine that overheated and then refuses to start is not a car you should keep trying to coax back to life.

Overheating can warp the cylinder head, blow the head gasket, or in the worst case seize the engine. If it cranks slowly, clicks, or does nothing, you may already be past the "add coolant and go" stage. Every extra crank on an engine with a damaged head gasket can push coolant into the cylinders or scrape metal on metal.

The exception: if the engine cranked fine, started, then you shut it off at the first warning, it may only need to cool and be topped off. That is a different situation from "won't start."

## What Should I Do Right Now Before the Tow Arrives?

Stop trying to start it, get everyone to safety, and wait. Do these in order:

1. **Turn the key off.** Do not crank it again.
2. **Get out of traffic.** If you are on a highway shoulder, exit on the passenger side and stand well away from the road, behind the guardrail if there is one.
3. **Hazards on, hood latched.** Do not pop the hood into a hot engine bay.
4. **Never open the radiator cap or coolant reservoir cap while hot.** The system is pressurized and can spray scalding coolant. Wait at least 30 minutes after the engine cools completely.
5. **Look underneath, from a safe angle.** Green, orange, or pink fluid pooling under the front means a leak. Sweet smell, white steam, or a temperature gauge pinned in the red all point the same direction.

If you smell burning or see smoke, stay clear. The same goes for any fluid on the ground that might be oil. For related symptoms, see [car engine smoking on highway](/car-engine-smoking-on-highway-safe-to-keep-driving/).

## Can I Just Wait for It to Cool and Try Again?

You can wait, but one cool-down and one careful check is all you get. After the engine is fully cool (30 to 60 minutes), you can check the coolant level in the overflow reservoir.

If the reservoir is empty, you have a leak or a boil-over. Adding coolant or water may let the engine start, but it will not fix the cause, and the car will overheat again. This is covered in detail in [car leaking coolant on side of road: can I add water and drive](/car-leaking-coolant-on-side-of-road-can-i-add-water-and-drive/).

If it still will not start after cooling and refilling, stop. That points to something beyond low coolant, such as a hydrolocked cylinder, a failed starter heat-soaked from the bay temperature, or internal damage. Call for the tow.

## Should I Ask for a Flatbed or a Regular Tow?

Ask for a flatbed. An overheated car with a possible internal problem should not be dragged on its drive wheels if you can avoid it. A flatbed carries the car with all four wheels off the ground, which protects the transmission and drivetrain. Front-wheel-drive cars towed on a wheel-lift put stress on the front end. ## Common Questions

**Q: How long should I wait before trying to start an overheated car again?**
A: Wait at least 30 to 60 minutes until the engine is cool to the touch around the radiator hose and hood. Try once, briefly. If it cranks but will not catch, or does not crank at all, stop and call for a tow.

**Q: Can I pour cold water into an overheated engine?**
A: Only after it has fully cooled, and only into the coolant reservoir or radiator. Cold water on a hot engine block can crack it. Water is a short-term fix, not a repair.

**Q: Why won't my car start after overheating?**
A: Common causes include a blown head gasket letting coolant into the cylinders, a seized engine, a heat-soaked starter, or a failed sensor. A mechanic needs to diagnose it.

**Q: Is it safe to drive a car that overheated once but now runs?**
A: Not far. Low coolant and a damaged gasket can fail again quickly. Drive only to the nearest shop with the heater on full blast to pull heat from the engine, and pull over at the first gauge spike.

**Q: Will my insurance cover a tow if my car overheated?**
A: It depends on whether you have roadside or towing coverage.
