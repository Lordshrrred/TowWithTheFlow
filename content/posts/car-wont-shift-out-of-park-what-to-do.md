> **Quick Answer:** Your car is probably stuck in park because the brake-shift interlock isn't releasing. Press the brake pedal firmly and try again. If that fails, find the shift lock override slot near your shifter (check your owner's manual for its location), insert a key or small flathead screwdriver, press down, and you can manually release the shifter. Common causes include a blown fuse, a faulty brake light switch, or low battery voltage.

## Why Won't My Car Shift Out of Park?

The most common reason is a failed brake-shift interlock system. Every automatic transmission vehicle made after 1993 is required by federal safety standard FMVSS 114 to have this system, which prevents the shifter from moving unless the brake pedal is depressed. When the interlock fails, the shifter locks regardless of what you do.

The three most likely culprits:

- **Brake light switch failure.** This switch tells the interlock solenoid that you've pressed the brake. If it fails, the solenoid never gets the signal. A quick check: if your brake lights aren't working, the switch is almost certainly the problem.
- **Dead or weak battery.** The interlock solenoid needs power to release. A battery too low to start the car is sometimes still too low to energize the solenoid, even if the dashboard lights up.
- **Blown fuse.** The interlock circuit runs through a fuse. On most vehicles it's labeled "stop lamp," "brake," or "shift interlock" in the fuse box.

## How Do I Get My Car to Shift Out of Park Right Now?

Use the shift lock override. This is a manual bypass built into every automatic-transmission vehicle for exactly this situation.

1. Set the parking brake if it isn't already on.
2. Turn the ignition to the "On" or "Acc" position (engine off is fine).
3. Locate the shift lock override slot. It's almost always within an inch or two of the shifter, often covered by a small plastic cap you can pop off with a coin or flathead screwdriver.
4. Insert a key, flathead screwdriver, or similar narrow object and press down while depressing the brake pedal.
5. While holding both, move the shifter to Neutral.
6. In Neutral, you can start the car normally and drive to a shop.

If you're on a slope and worried about rolling, have someone else hold the regular brake while you work the override. Do not attempt this without the parking brake set or someone on the brake pedal.

## Is This Safe to Drive, or Do I Need a Tow?

If you get the car into gear using the override and it drives normally, you can drive it to a shop. The interlock is a safety feature, not a core transmission component. Getting the car into Neutral and then starting it is not going to damage anything mechanically.

That said, do not ignore it. The underlying cause, whether a dead brake light switch, a bad solenoid, or a fuse, needs to be diagnosed and fixed. A brake light switch that isn't working means drivers behind you have no brake light warning, which is a real hazard on any highway. Get it looked at the same day if possible.

If the override doesn't work, or if the car shifts into Neutral but won't start, you're likely dealing with a battery or electrical issue beyond just the interlock. At that point, a tow makes more sense than troubleshooting on the side of the road. ## What Does It Cost to Fix a Car That's Stuck in Park?

The fix is usually cheap. A brake light switch runs $15 to $40 for the part and takes a mechanic less than 30 minutes to swap. Total repair cost is typically $50 to $120 at a shop. A blown fuse costs almost nothing. A faulty shift interlock solenoid is a bit more, usually $100 to $250 parts and labor depending on how buried it is in your center console.

If the root cause turns out to be a dying battery that's causing voltage drops across multiple systems, a new battery runs $120 to $200 installed. Compare that to a tow, which without roadside coverage can run $75 to $150 or more for a local haul. ## Could This Be a Transmission Problem?

Rarely, but it's possible. If you've already verified the brake lights work, the battery is fully charged, and the shift lock override also refuses to budge the shifter, the mechanical detent inside the shifter assembly itself may be seized, or there's a deeper transmission linkage problem.

A seized shifter detent or broken cable is a different animal than an interlock issue. You'll need a tow at that point. Do not force the shifter. Yanking hard enough to break the shift cable or crack the shifter assembly will make the repair significantly more expensive. If the car is also showing other signs like slipping, shuddering, or refusal to engage any gear once it's moving, read through the details on [transmission slipping on the highway](/transmission-slipping-on-highway-do-i-need-a-tow/) to get a clearer picture of what you're dealing with.

## Common Questions

**Q: My car won't shift out of park but the brake lights work fine. What else could it be?**
A: Check the shift interlock fuse first. It's a separate fuse from the brake lights on many vehicles. If the fuse is good, the shift interlock solenoid itself may have failed and will need to be replaced.

**Q: Can I drive my car after using the shift lock override?**
A: Yes. Using the override does not damage the transmission or any mechanical component. Just get the root cause diagnosed promptly, especially if your brake lights are out.

**Q: My battery is dead and my car is stuck in park. How do I deal with both at once?**
A: Jump-start or charge the battery first. Once the battery has enough voltage, the interlock should release normally when you press the brake. If it still won't release after jumping, use the shift lock override.

**Q: Where exactly is the shift lock override on my car?**
A: It's almost always adjacent to the shifter, often under a small plastic cap near the base of the lever or on the shifter console itself. Check your owner's manual under "shift lock release" or "emergency shift" for the exact location on your specific make and model.

**Q: Will a mechanic be able to tell me why my car got stuck in park?**
A: Yes. A scan tool will usually show brake switch faults immediately, and checking fuses and solenoid function is straightforward. This is not a difficult diagnosis. Most shops can pinpoint the cause in under 30 minutes.
