> **Quick Answer:** AAA is usually better if you want coverage that follows you, not your car, plus longer tow distances on higher tiers. Allstate Roadside Services is usually better if you already insure with Allstate and want a cheap add-on tied to one vehicle. Plan terms and prices vary by state and policy, so check your current documents before you rely on either.

## Which Is Better, AAA or Allstate Roadside Assistance?

For most drivers, AAA is the stronger product and Allstate is the cheaper one. They work differently, and that difference matters more than the price tag.

AAA is a membership. It covers you as a person, so it works in your car, a friend's car, or a rental, depending on your membership level. Allstate Roadside Services is an add-on to an Allstate auto policy and attaches to the vehicle listed on that policy.

Pick AAA if you drive multiple cars, ride with others, or want the option of a longer tow. Pick Allstate if you're already an Allstate customer, own one car, and mostly want basic help like jump-starts and short tows without a second subscription.

## How Do the Towing Distances Compare?

AAA's tow distance depends on your tier, and the higher tiers cover substantially longer tows than the basic one. Allstate's tow distance is set by the coverage you add to your policy and is typically a modest limit.

Both companies change terms by state and by year, so I won't quote you a mileage number I can't verify. Here is what to check:

- **AAA:** Look at your membership tier on the card or app. The basic tier covers a short tow. Higher tiers extend it. Mileage past the limit is billed per mile.
- **Allstate:** Pull up your declarations page and find the roadside line. The limit is spelled out there.

The practical test is this: if you commute 30 miles each way, a short-tow plan may not get you to your own mechanic. For a deeper look at how distance caps work across providers, see [how far insurance will tow your car for free](/how-far-will-insurance-tow-my-car-for-free/).

## Who Is Covered, the Driver or the Car?

AAA covers the member. Allstate covers the vehicle on the policy. This is the biggest practical gap between the two.

With AAA, a member who breaks down as a passenger in someone else's car can generally still call for help under their own membership. The details depend on the tier. If your spouse is driving your car and you're not in it, coverage questions come up, and we cover that exact scenario in [whether AAA covers towing when someone else is driving your car](/does-aaa-cover-towing-for-someone-else-driving-my-car/).

With Allstate, the car is the unit of coverage. If it's listed on your policy with the roadside add-on, it's covered no matter who's behind the wheel. If you borrow a friend's car, your Allstate add-on won't follow you.

If your household shares cars or you rent often, AAA fits better. If one person owns one car and nobody else touches it, either works.

## What Does Each One Cost?

## Is Either One Worth It If My Insurer Already Covers Towing?

Maybe not, and check this before paying for anything. Many auto policies include or offer towing. If you already have it, a second plan may duplicate coverage you're paying for.

Check your policy for towing and labor language, then read the limits. If you're stuck right now with no plan, don't wait on a decision. Pay the tow out of pocket, keep the receipt, and sort out coverage afterward. Some plans reimburse in certain cases.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does AAA cover a tow if the car is in someone else's name?**
A: It depends on the tier and whose membership is being used. The member is generally the one covered, but vehicle and registration rules vary, so read your tier's terms or see [AAA coverage when the car isn't registered in your name](/does-aaa-cover-towing-if-car-is-not-registered-in-my-name/).

**Q: Can I have both AAA and Allstate roadside assistance?**
A: Yes, nothing prevents it, but you'd be paying for overlapping coverage. Most people are better off choosing one primary plan and checking whether their auto insurer already includes a basic tow.

**Q: Which one covers a flat tire or a dead battery?**
A: Both typically offer basic services like tire changes, jump-starts, and lockouts, with limits on frequency and service calls. Confirm your plan's specifics, and see [whether roadside assistance covers a flat tire or only towing](/does-roadside-assistance-cover-flat-tire-or-just-towing/).

**Q: Will either plan cover towing after an accident?**
A: It varies by plan and by how the plan defines a covered event. Some memberships cover accident tows and some route them through your collision coverage instead, so check your terms before assuming.

**Q: Is AAA or Allstate faster to respond?**
A: Neither company publishes a guaranteed arrival time, and response depends on your location, time of day, and local contractor availability. Both dispatch through local tow operators, so rural roads and bad weather slow either one down.
