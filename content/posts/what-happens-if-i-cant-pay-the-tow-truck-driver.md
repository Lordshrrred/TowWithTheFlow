> **Quick Answer:** If you can't pay the tow truck driver on the spot, the driver can legally hold your car at their lot until you pay. Most private tow companies accept credit cards and some allow deferred payment, but they are not required to release your vehicle without payment. Storage fees start accumulating immediately, often $40 to $75 per day, so the longer you wait, the more you owe.

## What Can the Tow Truck Driver Actually Do If I Don't Pay?

The driver can refuse to release your car and take it to their storage lot. This is called a possessory lien, and it's legal in every state. The tow company has the right to hold your vehicle as collateral against the unpaid bill. They cannot sell your car immediately, but after a state-mandated waiting period (usually 10 to 30 days depending on your state), they can initiate a lien sale to recover what you owe.

What they cannot legally do: physically block you from leaving, threaten you, or charge fees that weren't disclosed before the tow. If a driver is aggressive or refuses to show you a rate sheet, note the company name and truck number and report it to your state's motor vehicle or consumer protection office.

## Will They Take My Car to an Impound Lot If I Can't Pay?

Yes, if you can't pay at the scene, the driver will take your vehicle to their storage yard. This is where the situation can get expensive fast. A basic tow that costs $125 at the roadside becomes $275 after three days of storage. The tow company is required to notify your local law enforcement or DMV when they store a vehicle, and in most states they must mail you a notice within a set number of days (commonly 3 to 5 business days). If you've seen how [impound lot release fees stack up](/tow-truck-impound-lot-release-fee-who-covers-that/), you already know that waiting is the worst thing you can do financially.

## What Are My Options When I Have No Cash and No Card?

- **Digital payment.** Venmo, Zelle, and Cash App are accepted by a growing number of independent operators. Just ask.
- **Call your insurance company.** If you have roadside assistance through your insurer, they can often pay the driver directly or reimburse you. - **AAA.** If you're a member, the tow is covered with no cash transaction at the scene at all.

## How Fast Do Storage Fees Add Up, and Can I Dispute Them?

Storage fees at tow yards typically run $40 to $75 per day, and many lots charge a gate or administrative fee of $25 to $50 on top of the base tow. So if your tow was $150 and you wait a week to pay, you could owe $450 to $675 total. Some states cap these fees by law. California, Texas, and Florida all have regulatory limits on what a tow yard can charge per day, but the caps are not always low.

You can dispute a charge if the company failed to disclose rates before towing, added fees not listed on their rate sheet, or towed you without authorization. Document everything: take photos of the rate sheet, get a written receipt, and note the time the tow started. If the numbers don't add up, your state's consumer protection office or the police department that authorized the tow (for non-consent tows) is your first call.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can a tow truck driver hold my car hostage until I pay?**
A: Yes, legally. A tow company has a possessory lien on your vehicle until the bill is paid. They can hold it at their storage yard, but they must follow state notice requirements and cannot simply sell it without giving you time to pay.

**Q: What if I just leave my car and walk away without paying?**
A: The tow company will store the vehicle and begin a lien process. Storage fees will accumulate daily, and after the state's waiting period (often 10 to 30 days), they can sell the car at auction. You will also likely owe any shortfall if the car sells for less than what's owed.

**Q: Can I call police if the tow truck driver won't release my car after I pay?**
A: Yes. Once you've paid in full and the driver refuses to release the vehicle, that can constitute unlawful detention of property. Call local police and show your payment receipt.

**Q: Does my car insurance cover towing if I have no cash on me?**
A: If your policy includes roadside assistance, yes, many insurers will pay the tow company directly or reimburse you. Call your insurer from the roadside before the tow happens so they can coordinate payment with the driver.

**Q: Can I get my belongings out of the car if I can't pay and they take it to the lot?**
A: In most states, tow companies are required to allow you access to retrieve personal property from the vehicle even if you haven't paid the tow bill. Call the lot before showing up, bring your ID, and get that in writing when you arrive.
