> **Quick Answer:** Yes, pull over now. A flashing check engine light is not the same as a steady one. It means your engine is misfiring badly enough to damage the catalytic converter in real time. Every mile you drive risks turning a $300 repair into a $1,500 one. Get off the highway at the nearest exit or onto the shoulder, shut the engine off, and assess.

## What Does a Flashing Check Engine Light Actually Mean?

A flashing check engine light means active misfiring. The engine is firing unevenly, dumping raw unburned fuel into the exhaust, and that fuel is overheating your catalytic converter. A steady check engine light is a "get it checked soon" warning. A flashing one is a "stop the car" warning. These are not the same signal, and treating them the same is one of the more expensive mistakes a driver can make.

## Should I Pull Over Immediately or Can I Drive to the Next Town?

Pull over as soon as you can do it safely, which usually means the next exit or the nearest wide shoulder. Do not push it to the next town if that is 20 or 30 miles out. The catalytic converter on most modern vehicles sits in the exhaust stream and runs at roughly 1,200 to 1,600 degrees Fahrenheit under normal conditions. A severe misfire can push that temperature high enough to melt the honeycomb substrate inside the converter. Once that happens, you need a new cat on top of whatever caused the misfire in the first place.

If you make it to a safe stop, turn the engine off. Do not let it idle on the shoulder hoping the light will stop flashing.

## What Are the Most Common Causes of a Flashing Check Engine Light?

The most common cause is a failed ignition coil or a bad spark plug. When one cylinder stops firing, the engine stumbles and raw fuel exits through the exhaust. Other causes include a clogged fuel injector stuck closed on one cylinder, a vacuum leak causing a lean misfire, or a failing crankshaft position sensor that throws off ignition timing across multiple cylinders. In older vehicles, a cracked distributor cap can cause the same pattern.

None of these are drivable conditions when the light is flashing. Some, like a single bad coil pack, are relatively cheap fixes if you catch them before they destroy the catalytic converter. If your engine is also making a knocking sound alongside the flashing light, that is a different and more serious problem. See [engine making knocking noise at highway speed](/engine-knocking-noise-highway-speed-tow-or-drive/) for that situation.

## Do I Need a Tow, or Can I Drive It to a Shop?

You need a tow if the light is still flashing after you restart the engine. If you pull over, let the engine cool for 10 minutes, restart it, and the light is now steady instead of flashing, you may be able to drive carefully to a nearby shop at low speeds with no hard acceleration. But if it starts flashing again within a few minutes, stop again and call for a tow.

## How Do I Stay Safe While Broken Down on the Highway?

Get as far off the travel lane as possible. If you can reach an exit, take it. If you are on the shoulder, pull as far right as the shoulder allows, turn on your hazard lights, and get out of the vehicle on the passenger side, away from traffic. Move behind the guardrail if one is present. Do not stand behind or directly beside the car. Call 911 if you are in a dangerous position. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive with a flashing check engine light if the car seems to run okay?**
A: No. The car may feel drivable, but the catalytic converter is being damaged with every mile. The misfire may not be severe enough to feel through the seat, especially at highway speeds, but the damage is still happening.

**Q: What if the light flashes for a few seconds and then goes steady?**
A: A light that flashes briefly and then goes steady is a borderline situation. The misfire may have been a single event rather than ongoing. Pull off when safe, restart the engine after a few minutes, and if the light stays steady and the engine feels normal, drive carefully to a shop. Do not resume highway driving until it has been diagnosed.

**Q: Will my insurance cover the tow if my engine light comes on?**
A: It depends on your policy. If you have roadside assistance through your insurer, a breakdown tow is typically covered up to a mileage limit. Comprehensive coverage does not cover breakdowns, only physical damage from external events. Check your declarations page or call your insurer before assuming you are covered.

**Q: How much will it cost to fix the cause of a flashing check engine light?**
A: A single bad ignition coil costs $80 to $200 in parts and an hour of labor. A set of spark plugs runs $100 to $250 depending on the engine. If the catalytic converter is already damaged, add $800 to $2,000 or more for that repair on top of the original problem.

**Q: Can a bad gas cap cause the check engine light to flash?**
A: No. A loose or missing gas cap triggers a steady check engine light related to the EVAP system, never a flashing one. A flashing light is always a misfire code. Do not waste time checking the gas cap if the light is flashing.
