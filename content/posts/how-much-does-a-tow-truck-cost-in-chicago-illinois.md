> **Quick Answer:** A tow truck in Chicago costs $75 to $150 for a short local haul under 5 miles. Cross-town tows from Wicker Park to the South Loop or beyond run $150 to $300. Add $50 to $100 for nights, weekends, or winter calls. Flatbed service costs more than a wheel-lift. If you have no insurance or roadside plan, expect to pay out of pocket at the higher end of every range.

## How Much Does a Tow Truck Actually Cost in Chicago?

A standard local tow in Chicago runs $75 to $150 for the first 5 miles, with a per-mile rate of $3 to $7 after that. A longer haul, say from O'Hare on the Northwest Side down to Hyde Park, can push $250 to $350 depending on the company. Hook-up fees, also called dispatch or service fees, add $50 to $75 on top before the truck even moves an inch.

Flatbed towing costs more than a wheel-lift because the equipment is more expensive to operate. If your car has all-wheel drive, a low front bumper, or fresh body damage, you need a flatbed. Expect to pay $20 to $50 more per job. For a full breakdown of [flatbed rates in Chicago](/towing-cost-chicago-illinois-flatbed-rates/), those numbers are covered in detail separately.

## Does It Cost More to Get Towed on a Chicago Expressway?

Yes, expressway tows in Chicago often cost more and move faster, and both facts matter. The city contracts with rotating tow companies for expressway incidents on the Dan Ryan (I-90/94), the Kennedy (I-90/94 northwest), the Eisenhower (I-290), and the Stevenson (I-55). These contracted trucks are dispatched by IDOT and Illinois State Police, and the rates they charge are regulated, but they are not free.

If you break down on the Dan Ryan near the 31st Street ramp and a contracted truck responds, you are getting their service whether you called them or not. Regulated rates for these IDOT-contracted companies are posted and reviewed periodically, but they still include hook-up fees and per-mile charges. If you want to understand your full situation when you are stuck on an expressway, read [what to do if your car breaks down on a Chicago expressway](/car-broke-down-on-chicago-expressway-what-to-do/) before the truck arrives.

## What Factors Push the Cost Higher in Chicago Specifically?

Traffic, distance, time of day, and vehicle type are the four main variables, and all four work against you in Chicago.

Distance is the biggest driver. A tow from a parking garage in River North to a shop in Lincoln Square is 6 to 8 miles through city traffic. That same trip in a suburban market might be a flat $75. In Chicago, you are paying per mile through congested streets, and the driver's time is factored in.

Time of day adds a real surcharge. Night calls, typically anything after 9 or 10 p.m., cost $50 to $100 more. Weekend rates are a separate tier. [Weekend towing in Chicago](/towing-cost-in-chicago-illinois-weekend-rate/) runs noticeably higher than weekday calls, and Sunday is the most expensive day to need a tow.

Winter is its own category. Ice events, snow emergencies, and sub-zero cold all spike demand and push rates up. A tow during a January storm on the Kennedy near the I-190 split is going to cost more than the same tow in October, both because of surge demand and because recovery from a snowy ditch takes more equipment and labor.

## What If I Don't Have Insurance or a Roadside Plan?

You pay full retail, no discounts. Without AAA, a manufacturer roadside program, or an insurance add-on, a Chicago tow company has no reason to offer you a reduced rate. Private tow companies set their own prices outside of the IDOT expressway contract system.

Call at least two companies before you commit if you have time and are safely off the road. Ask for the hook-up fee and the per-mile rate separately. Some companies quote a bundled "short haul flat rate" that sounds cheap but excludes mileage beyond 3 miles. Get the total, not the starting number.

If you are paying cash with no coverage, a local Chicago tow with no complications runs $100 to $175 for most in-city moves. If you are on the expressway and a contracted truck responds without you calling, you have less negotiating room but you are still entitled to a written invoice.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a tow truck cost in Chicago at night?**
A: Night tows in Chicago typically add $50 to $100 to the base rate. A $100 daytime tow becomes $150 to $200 after 9 p.m. Rates vary by company, but after-hours surcharges are standard across the city.

**Q: How much does it cost to tow a car 10 miles in Chicago?**
A: A 10-mile tow in Chicago runs roughly $125 to $200. You are paying a hook-up fee of $50 to $75 plus $5 to $7 per mile, depending on the company and time of day.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover the tow truck cost in Chicago?**
A: Only if you have roadside assistance added to your policy. Standard liability or collision coverage does not pay for towing unless the tow is part of a covered accident claim. Check your declarations page or call your insurer before assuming you are covered.

**Q: What is the cheapest way to get towed in Chicago?**
A: AAA membership is the cheapest option if you already have it, since basic members get up to 5 miles free per tow. If you do not have a plan, call two or three private companies and compare total costs before the truck rolls. Avoid using the first number you find under stress without asking for a full quote.

**Q: Are Chicago tow truck rates regulated or can companies charge whatever they want?**
A: IDOT-contracted expressway tow companies operate under regulated rate schedules. Private tow companies called directly by the driver are not subject to the same caps and can set their own rates. Always ask for the total cost before authorizing a private tow.
