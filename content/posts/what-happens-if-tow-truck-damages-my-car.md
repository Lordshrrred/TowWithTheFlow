> **Quick Answer:** The tow company is legally responsible for damage it causes while your car is in its care. Photograph everything before you sign anything, note the damage on the paperwork, and file a claim with the company's insurer. Reputable companies carry cargo or "garagekeepers" coverage for exactly this. If they refuse, your own comprehensive coverage and a state complaint are your backups.

## Who Is Responsible If a Tow Truck Damages My Car?

The towing company is responsible. Once they hook up or load your car, it is in their custody, and they owe you reasonable care. Licensed operators typically carry garagekeepers or on-hook coverage that pays for damage during towing and storage.

The key phrase is "damage they caused." Pre-existing dents, a dragging bumper from the original breakdown, or a failing transmission don't count. That's why documentation matters more than anything else you do next.

## What Should I Do Right Now If I Notice Damage?

Photograph the damage before you sign, pay, or let the driver leave. Do this while the truck and driver are still there.

1. **Don't sign a "no damage" release.** Many drop-off forms have a line saying the vehicle was received in good condition. If you see new damage, write it on the form, or refuse to sign until it's noted.
2. **Take wide and close photos.** Shoot all four sides, the wheels, the bumpers, and the underside if you can. Include the truck, its company name, and its plates in at least one shot.
3. **Get the driver's name and the company's insurance information.** You're entitled to ask. Get the dispatch ticket or invoice too.
4. **Check the common damage spots.** Scraped front lips, scuffed rocker panels, bent wheels, and cracked bumper covers are the usual culprits when a car is winched onto a flatbed too aggressively.
5. **Photograph your car at the pickup spot if you still can.** Dated photos from before the tow are your best evidence. If you're still stranded, take them now. ## How Do Tow Truck Companies Usually Damage Cars?

Most damage comes from the wrong equipment or rushed loading, and both are preventable. The common causes:

- **Wheel-lift towing on the wrong drivetrain.** Towing an all-wheel-drive car or a front-wheel-drive car with the wrong wheels on the ground can wreck the transmission or center differential. Flatbeds avoid this.
- **Chains or hooks on plastic.** Hooking a cheap strap to a bumper cover instead of the factory tow loop cracks it.
- **Low front ends.** Sports cars and lowered vehicles scrape the front lip on steep ramp angles unless the driver uses a slide-back deck or wood blocks.
- **Dropped or unsecured loads.** Rare, but straps that slip can cause real body damage.

If your car is AWD, electric, or low to the ground, say so on the phone and ask for a flatbed. Flatbed rates differ from standard tow rates, and you can check typical numbers in our [Nashville flatbed towing cost guide](/towing-cost-in-nashville-tennessee-flatbed-no-insurance/) to see the range.

If they cooperate, their insurer sends an adjuster or asks for an estimate. If they stall, deny it, or go silent, escalate:

- **File through your own insurance.** If you carry comprehensive or collision, your insurer can pay for repairs and then pursue the tow company (called subrogation). You may owe a deductible, which is often refunded if they recover the money. Compare this against how your policy treats towing: our post on [whether car insurance covers towing](/does-my-car-insurance-cover-towing-if-i-cause-the-accident/) explains what's included.
- **Complain to your state regulator.** Many states license towing operators through the DOT, public utilities commission, or consumer affairs office. A complaint can pressure a company that ignores you.
- **Small claims court.** For damage under your state's small claims limit, you don't need a lawyer. Photos, the signed form, and a written estimate carry real weight.
- **Check the insurance first.** If the operator has no coverage, a licensing complaint becomes more important, since uninsured operators risk losing their license.

Keep every receipt, email, and photo in one folder. Claims drag when evidence is scattered.

## Can I Refuse to Pay a Tow Bill If They Damaged My Car?

Probably not, and refusing is risky. A tow company can hold your vehicle and charge daily storage fees until the bill is paid, and unpaid storage can escalate fast.

The safer play: pay the towing bill under protest, write "paid under protest, damage noted" on the receipt, and pursue the damage claim separately. That keeps your car from sitting on a lot racking up charges while the claim plays out. ## Common Questions

**Q: Will my insurance cover damage from a tow truck?**
A: If you have comprehensive or collision coverage, yes, though you'll pay your deductible up front. The tow company's insurer or your own insurer can then pursue reimbursement from the responsible party.

**Q: How long do I have to report tow truck damage?**
A: Report it immediately, ideally at the scene, and file the written claim within a few days. Some company policies and state rules set short windows, and delays make it easier for them to blame earlier damage.

**Q: What if the tow truck damaged my car and the company went out of business?**
A: Your own comprehensive or collision insurance is the fallback, since the company's coverage may no longer be reachable. You can also file a state complaint and check whether the company's insurer still holds records of the policy.

**Q: Can a tow truck driver be held personally responsible?**
A: Usually the company is liable, not the individual driver. The driver acts as the company's employee, so claims go to the business and its insurer.

**Q: Is it the tow company's fault if my transmission failed after being towed?**
A: Only if the method of towing caused it, such as pulling an AWD car on its wheels. If the transmission was already failing, you'll need a mechanic's written opinion to prove which one it was.
