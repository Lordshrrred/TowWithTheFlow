> **Quick Answer:** Most credit card roadside assistance covers towing costs up to $50-100 per incident, with annual limits of $500-1,000. Premium cards offer higher limits. Coverage typically includes towing to the nearest qualified repair facility within a specified mileage radius.

## What To Do

1. **Check your card benefits immediately** - Call the number on the back of your card or check your card's app for roadside assistance details

2. **Verify your eligibility** - Most programs require you to be the primary cardholder and the breakdown must involve a vehicle you own or are legally driving

3. **Call the roadside assistance number** - Don't call a random tow truck first, as you may not get reimbursed

4. **Document everything** - Keep receipts, take photos of your vehicle's location, and note the tow truck company details

5. **Understand the limits** - Towing is usually covered to the nearest repair shop, not your preferred mechanic across town

6. **Know what's excluded** - Off-road recovery, impound fees, and storage costs typically aren't covered

## What It Might Cost

Credit card towing coverage varies by card type:

- **Basic cards**: $50-75 per tow, $300-500 annual limit
- **Premium cards**: $100-200 per tow, $1,000+ annual limit
- **Distance limits**: Usually 5-10 miles to nearest shop
- **Out-of-pocket**: Pay upfront, get reimbursed within 2-6 weeks

Popular card coverage examples:
- Chase Sapphire: Up to $50 per incident
- American Express Platinum: Up to $100 per incident
- Capital One Venture: Up to $50 per incident

A few things worth knowing about the numbers. The $50-75 range on basic cards sounds reasonable until you're stranded somewhere rural and the nearest shop is 20 miles out. Average tow rates run $75-125 for the hook-up fee alone, then $3-7 per mile after that. On a 20-mile tow at $5 per mile, you're looking at $175-225 total, meaning you could easily be $100-150 out of pocket even after your card's reimbursement kicks in.

The reimbursement timeline matters too. Most programs say 2-6 weeks, but disputed claims or missing documentation can push that past 8 weeks. If you paid on a tight budget, that gap hurts. Always ask the tow operator for an itemized receipt on the spot, not just a total. Some reimbursement programs reject claims that don't show a line-by-line breakdown of hook-up fee versus mileage charge.

One scenario people miss: if you break down late at night and the card's dispatch network can't locate an available driver within 45-60 minutes, the operator may tell you to arrange your own tow and submit for reimbursement. That's when documentation becomes critical. Get the dispatch call reference number, screenshot the app confirmation if there is one, and keep the entire paper trail together.

Also check whether your card counts each service call as one incident or bills by the calendar year. Some cards cap you at four incidents per year regardless of dollar amount. Others reset annually on your card membership date, not January 1st. Read the fine print before you need it, not during a breakdown.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I use my credit card roadside assistance if I'm driving someone else's car?**
A: It depends on your card's specific terms. Most programs cover any vehicle you are legally operating at the time of the breakdown, but a few restrict coverage to vehicles registered in your name. Check your benefits guide before you need it, because the answer varies even between cards from the same bank.

**Q: What happens if the tow costs more than my card's per-incident limit?**
A: You pay the difference out of pocket at the time of the tow. For example, if your card covers $75 and the tow bill is $180, you owe the driver $105 on the spot. Budget for this possibility if you're driving in a remote area where longer tows are likely.

**Q: Will using my credit card roadside assistance raise my rates or count as an insurance claim?**
A: No. Credit card roadside assistance is a card benefit, not an insurance policy. Using it has no connection to your auto insurance record and will not affect your premiums. It is completely separate from any AAA membership or auto insurance roadside add-on you may also carry.

## Stay Safe

- Pull completely off the road before calling
- Turn on hazard lights and use flares if available
- Stay inside your vehicle if on a busy highway
- Don't accept help from random strangers
- Verify the tow truck driver's credentials when they arrive
- Never leave valuables in a vehicle being towed
