> **Quick Answer:** Jump start first, tow only if the jump fails or something else is wrong. A dead battery in a parking garage is usually fixable on the spot with jumper cables or a portable jump starter. Call your roadside assistance, a mobile battery service, or a good Samaritan with cables. If the car still won't start after two solid jump attempts, then it's time to call a tow.

## Should I Try to Jump Start My Car Before Calling a Tow Truck?

Yes, always try a jump start first. A dead battery is one of the most fixable roadside problems there is, and calling a tow truck when you don't need one costs you $75 to $150 you didn't have to spend. If your lights are dim, your dash is dead, or you heard a single click when you turned the key, that points directly at the battery, not the engine.

The one exception: if you smell burning plastic, see corrosion so heavy it's crumbling off the terminals, or the battery is cracked or leaking, skip the jump and call for help instead.

## How Do I Get a Jump Start Inside a Parking Garage?

You have three realistic options. First, check if the garage has an attendant on duty. Many airport and hospital garages keep a jump pack at the booth specifically for this situation. Ask before assuming.

Second, call your roadside assistance provider. AAA, GEICO, State Farm, and most others dispatch a truck with a jump pack. Response times inside a parking structure vary, but most urban areas see help within 30 to 45 minutes. If you have coverage, this costs you nothing out of pocket. For details on what your policy actually covers, the article on [GEICO roadside assistance towing distance limits](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-distance-limit/) breaks down a typical policy clearly.

Third, flag down another driver. Connect the red clamp to the positive terminal on your dead battery, the other red clamp to the positive terminal on the good battery, the black clamp to the negative terminal on the good battery, and the last black clamp to an unpainted metal surface on your car, not the dead battery's negative terminal. Start the good car, wait two minutes, then try yours.

If the car starts, drive it for at least 20 to 30 minutes on open road to give the alternator time to recharge the battery. Idling in the garage won't cut it.

## What If My Car Won't Start Even After a Jump?

If two full jump attempts fail, you're likely dealing with more than a dead battery. Common culprits are a battery that's completely failed and won't hold a charge (common in batteries over four years old), a bad alternator that isn't charging the battery while the engine runs, or a parasitic draw that drained the battery and will do it again immediately.

At that point, you need a tow. A mobile mechanic is worth considering if you want to avoid towing cost entirely. Some services will come to a parking garage, test the battery with a load tester on the spot, and swap in a new one within an hour. Interstate, AutoZone, and O'Reilly all have mobile battery replacement programs in most metro areas worth checking before you commit to a tow.

## Are There Any Safety Risks to Jumping a Car Inside a Garage?

The risk is low but real. A battery can release hydrogen gas when it's charging or being jumped. In an open parking structure with airflow, this disperses fast and isn't a concern. In a fully enclosed, poorly ventilated underground level, keep the jump brief and don't linger near the battery with an open flame or sparks.

Modern sealed lead-acid batteries and AGM batteries (the flat kind found in many newer vehicles) produce far less gas than older flooded batteries, so the risk is minimal in most cases. Don't let this stop you from attempting a jump. Just don't stand directly over the battery the entire time.

Also, if the other driver helping you has a newer vehicle, especially a hybrid or electric, check your owner's manuals before connecting cables. Some hybrid 12-volt systems can't be used as a jump source. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can a tow truck get into a parking garage to tow my car?**
A: Most flatbed tow trucks cannot fit in a standard parking garage due to height restrictions, which are typically 6 to 7 feet. A wheel-lift or hook-and-chain truck has a lower profile and is more likely to fit. Tell the dispatcher you're in a parking structure when you call so they send the right equipment.

**Q: How much does a jump start service cost if I don't have roadside assistance?**
A: Expect to pay $50 to $80 for a jump start from a tow company or mobile service, called out privately. Some services charge a flat rate, others charge a dispatch fee plus a per-mile rate. It's almost always cheaper than a tow.

**Q: My battery died but the car started after a jump. Do I still need to see a mechanic?**
A: Yes, if the battery is more than three to four years old or has died more than once recently. A shop can run a free battery load test in about five minutes. A battery that passed a jump start can still fail the next morning in the cold.

**Q: Will the parking garage call a tow truck for me if I ask?**
A: Some will, especially airport and hotel garages. They often have a preferred towing vendor on call. The downside is you don't get to choose the company, and rates can be higher. It's worth asking, but also call your own roadside assistance at the same time.

**Q: How long does it take for roadside assistance to reach a parking garage?**
A: Typically 30 to 60 minutes in a metro area, though it varies by time of day and how busy service is. Give the dispatcher the exact level and section of the garage when you call. Dispatchers need a specific location to find you, not just the garage name.
