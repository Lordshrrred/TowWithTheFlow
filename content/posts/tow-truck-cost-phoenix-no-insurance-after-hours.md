You broke down in Phoenix at night, you have no roadside coverage, and you need a tow. Here is what it is actually going to cost you and how to handle it without getting taken.

> **Quick Answer:** A private-pay tow in Phoenix after hours runs $95 to $135 for the hookup fee plus $4 to $7 per mile. A 10-mile tow to a repair shop lands around $135 to $205. After-hours calls, typically 9 PM to 7 AM, add a surcharge of $25 to $75 on top of that. Flatbed service costs more than wheel-lift. Get a price confirmed before they hook up your car.

## What To Do

1. **Get off the road and stay visible.** Pull as far right as possible, turn on your hazard lights, and if you have them, set out reflective triangles or flares. Phoenix freeways like I-10, I-17, and the Loop 101 move fast at night even after midnight. Do not stand between your car and traffic.

2. **Call ADOT Freeway Service Patrol first.** If you broke down on a Phoenix-area freeway, the Arizona Department of Transportation runs free patrols on major corridors during certain hours. They can push your car to a safe spot or help with minor issues. Check 511az.gov or call 511. Their coverage is limited by time and location, but it costs you nothing if they are available.

3. **Call at least two tow companies and get quotes.** Do not call one company and wait. In Phoenix, private tow rates are not state-regulated the way impound tows are. One company might quote $150 and another $240 for the same job. Ask specifically: "What is the hookup fee, what is the per-mile rate, and is there an after-hours charge?" Get a dollar amount before you say yes.

4. **Confirm the destination before they hook up.** Standard tows drop you at the nearest shop or wherever you specify within the quoted distance. If your mechanic is 18 miles away and you approved a quote assuming 10 miles, you will pay more. Know the mileage before they roll.

5. **Pay by card, not cash.** Most Phoenix tow operators accept credit cards. If you are worried about payment flexibility, see how [tow trucks near you accept credit cards](/tow-truck-near-me-accepting-credit-card/) so you are not scrambling for ATMs at 2 AM.

6. **Photograph the hookup.** Before they tow it, take photos of all four corners of your vehicle. Disputes over pre-existing damage are common. This protects you.

7. **Ask about storage fees.** If the shop is closed and your car sits in their lot overnight, some operators charge $35 to $75 per day in storage. Ask upfront whether the drop-off location charges storage and when it starts.

## Stay Safe

- Do not accept a tow from a driver who shows up unsolicited. Predatory "bandit" tow operators patrol Phoenix freeways, especially at night.
- If a driver arrives before the company you called, confirm they are actually from that company before you authorize anything.
- If you are on a dark stretch of road and feel unsafe waiting in or near the car, call 911 and ask for a DPS trooper to stay with you. They will.
- Never leave your car running while you wait outside. In Phoenix heat, staying cool matters, but leave the doors unlocked and stay aware of traffic.
- If you have kids in the car, staying inside with the AC running is the right call. [Car broke down with kids in car](/car-broke-down-with-kids-in-car-safety-steps/) covers the specific steps for that situation.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I negotiate the tow price with the driver who shows up?**
A: You can try, but it is more effective to negotiate before you commit by calling multiple companies and getting competing quotes over the phone. Once a driver has already hooked up your vehicle, your leverage drops significantly.

**Q: What happens if I refuse to pay the tow company when they drop off my car?**
A: In Arizona, tow operators can place a lien on your vehicle and store it until the bill is settled, with daily storage fees adding up quickly. Disputing a charge is better handled by paying under protest and then filing a complaint with the Arizona Attorney General than by refusing payment at the drop-off.

**Q: Will a regular mechanic shop be open to receive my car if I get towed after midnight?**
A: Most shops will not have staff available, but many allow after-hours drop-offs in a secure lot. Confirm this with your destination shop before the tow, and ask the tow operator whether the lot charges storage fees for vehicles left overnight.
