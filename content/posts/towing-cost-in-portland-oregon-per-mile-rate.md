Expect roughly $75 to $150 to hook up and about $3 to $7 per mile after the first few miles. Flatbeds, nights, weekends, and rain push it higher. ## How Much Does Towing Cost Per Mile in Portland?

Expect a hook-up fee of roughly $75 to $150 plus about $3 to $7 for every mile after the included distance. Rates vary by company and are not set by any citywide schedule, so treat these as ballpark figures and confirm the total on the phone.

A typical bill breaks down like this:

- **Hook-up fee:** covers the call-out, loading, and often the first few miles.
- **Per-mile charge:** applies from the pickup point to your drop-off.
- **Add-ons:** winching, after-hours surcharge, storage, and gate fees if you're dropped at a locked yard.

A 10-mile tow from the Lloyd District to a shop in Beaverton might land between $130 and $220. The same tow at 2 a.m. on a Sunday can run noticeably more.

## What Makes a Portland Tow Cost More?

Flatbeds, after-hours calls, bad weather, and awkward recovery locations all add to the bill. Ask the dispatcher which of these apply before you say yes.

- **Flatbed vs. wheel-lift:** All-wheel-drive cars, low-clearance cars, and anything with transmission trouble should go on a flatbed. Flatbeds cost more. See [flatbed towing costs in Portland](/towing-cost-portland-oregon-flatbed/) and the [flatbed vs. wheel-lift breakdown](/towing-cost-portland-oregon-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/).
- **Rain:** Portland's wet season means more breakdowns and longer waits, and some companies add a surcharge. More in [towing cost in Portland during rainy weather](/towing-cost-portland-oregon-rainy-weather/).
- **Time of day:** Nights, weekends, and holidays usually cost extra.
- **Recovery work:** A car stuck in a ditch off Highway 26 or down a ravine near the West Hills needs a winch. That's a separate charge.
- **Distance:** A cross-river tow over the Marquam Bridge on I-5 or the Fremont Bridge on I-405 during rush hour burns driver time, and some companies price for it.

## What Do I Do Right Now If My Car Breaks Down on I-5 or I-84?

Get to the shoulder, turn on your hazards, and call 911 if you can't get fully clear of traffic. Highway shoulders in the Portland metro are narrow in spots, especially on I-5 through the Rose Quarter and on I-84 near the Banfield. Staying inside with your seatbelt on is often safer than standing outside.

1. Pull as far right as possible and turn wheels away from traffic.
2. Hazards on. Dome light on at night.
3. Call 911 if you're in a live lane or on a blind curve.
4. Check 511 (TripCheck is Oregon's road-conditions service) before you sit and wait, so you know if a closure will slow help.
5. Call a tow company and get the price quote, the arrival estimate, and the truck type before you agree.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged on a Tow in Portland?

Get the full price in writing or by text before the truck loads your car. The most common overcharges come from vague quotes that balloon with "mileage" and "fees" at the end.

Ask these exact questions:

- What's the hook-up fee, and how many miles does it include?
- What's the per-mile rate after that?
- Is there any after-hours, weather, or winching surcharge?
- Is the total cash, card, or both?
- Where exactly are you taking the car, and is there a storage fee if it sits overnight?

A practical tip: tell the driver your drop-off address and ask for the total to that address. A "per mile" quote with no destination isn't a real price.

## Should I Call AAA or My Insurance First?

Check your coverage first, because a covered tow can cost you nothing. Many auto policies and AAA memberships include a set towing distance, often measured in miles, so a short tow may be fully free while a longer one costs the difference.

Look at the card in your wallet, your insurer's app, or your credit card benefits. Some cards include roadside help. If your plan covers only a few miles, ask the tow company to take you to the nearest shop first, then arrange the longer trip later.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a tow cost per mile in Portland?**
A: Most companies charge about $3 to $7 per mile after the hook-up fee covers the first stretch. Rates differ by company, time of day, and truck type, so confirm before you agree.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive than a regular tow in Portland?**
A: Usually yes. Flatbeds cost more but protect AWD, low, and damaged-drivetrain cars. If your car has transmission or axle trouble, insist on a flatbed.

**Q: Can I choose where my car gets towed in Portland?**
A: Yes, on a private-property or voluntary tow. You pick the shop or your home. If police or ODOT dispatch the tow after a crash or hazard, the destination may be set for you.

**Q: Do Portland tow companies charge extra for rain?**
A: Some do, especially during heavy fall and winter storms when demand spikes. Ask if any weather or after-hours surcharge applies before the truck is dispatched.

**Q: What if I can't afford the tow?**
A: Ask the company about payment options, check whether your insurance or credit card includes roadside coverage, and call a few companies to compare quotes. Don't let a car sit at a yard, because storage fees add up daily.
