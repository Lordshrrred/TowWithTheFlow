## What Does a Tow Actually Cost in Indianapolis Without Insurance?

A local tow inside Marion County runs $75 to $125 for the hookup fee, plus $3 to $5 per loaded mile. Most breakdowns within the city limits cost $100 to $175 out of pocket. If you're further out, say stranded on I-70 near the Plainfield interchange or on I-465 at the Southport Road exit, add $5 to $10 per additional mile beyond the driver's standard zone.

Wheel-lift tows are cheaper. Flatbed tows cost $25 to $50 more because of the equipment. If your car is all-wheel drive, has a low clearance, or won't roll, you need a flatbed. See [towing cost in Indianapolis on a flatbed](/towing-cost-indianapolis-indiana-flatbed/) for a full breakdown on that specific pricing.

After-hours calls add another $25 to $75 on top of the base rate. Indianapolis tow companies consider "after hours" anything from roughly 10 PM to 6 AM, and some start the clock at 8 PM. If you're calling at midnight after a breakdown on I-865, expect the higher end.

## How Do I Actually Pay When I Have No Insurance?

You pay the tow driver directly before they unhook your car. No insurance claim, no third-party billing, no reimbursement process. Most Indianapolis operators accept cash, debit, or credit card. Some smaller independents are cash only, so ask before they hook up.

Get a written or texted quote before you agree to anything. Reputable companies will give you a price over the phone: hookup fee plus per-mile rate to your destination. If a driver shows up and names a price that is significantly higher than the quote, you can push back or call another company. You are not obligated to accept service until they've actually moved the vehicle.

If money is genuinely tight, ask the dispatcher upfront whether they offer any payment flexibility. Some do, most don't, but it costs nothing to ask.

## Are There Any Cheaper Options Besides Calling a Random Tow Company?

Yes, two worth knowing about. First, AAA. A basic AAA Classic membership costs around $60 to $75 per year and covers up to 5 miles of towing per call, with four calls per year. If you broke down today and you're not a member, you can join right now on your phone and use the benefit within the hour in most cases, though some plans have a waiting period for the first use. Check the membership terms before you count on it.

## What If I Got Towed Without Asking, Like After an Accident?

If your car was towed by a company you didn't call, you could be dealing with a predatory "consent tow" situation. This happens at accident scenes on I-70, I-65, and the Downtown interstate merge areas where drivers sometimes show up uninvited. Indiana law gives you the right to choose your own tow provider at an accident scene if you are present and capable of making that decision.

If your car is already at an impound lot, you owe the tow fee plus daily storage. Indianapolis impound storage fees typically run $30 to $50 per day. Get the car out fast. Every day it sits costs you more money, and you don't have insurance to absorb any of it.

Check out [who pays tow truck cost when not at fault in an accident](/who-pays-tow-truck-cost-when-not-at-fault-accident/) if another driver caused the incident. You may be able to recover that cost from their liability coverage even if you carry no insurance yourself.

## Common Questions

**Q: What's the cheapest tow I can get in Indianapolis without insurance?**
A: The floor is roughly $75 to $100 for a very short local tow, hookup fee included, if you're within 3 to 5 miles of the shop and calling during business hours. Anything further, after hours, or requiring a flatbed will cost more.

**Q: Can a tow company refuse to release my car until I pay?**
A: Yes. In Indiana, a tow company has a lien on your vehicle until the bill is paid. They can hold the car and charge daily storage until you settle up. Do not let the car sit at the lot.

**Q: Will a tow company give me a price over the phone before they come?**
A: Any reputable Indianapolis operator will. If they refuse to quote a hookup fee and per-mile rate before dispatch, call someone else. That reluctance is a red flag.

**Q: Does it cost more to get towed on the interstate versus a surface street?**
A: The mileage rate is the same, but interstate breakdowns sometimes carry an additional "highway response" fee from certain companies, usually $15 to $25. Ask specifically when you call.

**Q: If I'm towed to a shop that can't fix my car the same day, does it cost extra to move it again?**
A: Yes, every tow is a separate charge. If you think the first shop might not have the parts or capacity, ask before you commit to a destination. Moving a car twice without insurance will cost you $200 or more in tow fees alone.
