> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in Jacksonville usually costs $75 to $175 total: a hookup fee of roughly $50 to $100 plus a per-mile charge after the first few miles. Nights, weekends, and highway pickups on I-95 or I-295 push the price up. Get the total quoted before the driver arrives, and confirm whether your insurance or credit card already covers it.

## How Much Does a Local Tow Cost in Jacksonville?

A local tow in Jacksonville typically runs $75 to $175 for a short trip, and most of that is the hookup fee. Prices vary by company, so treat these as working ranges, not guarantees.

- **Hookup fee:** about $50 to $100, charged no matter how short the ride
- **Per-mile rate:** roughly $3 to $7 per mile after the included distance
- **Flatbed surcharge:** often $25 to $50 more than a standard wheel-lift tow
- **After-hours or holiday:** commonly adds $25 to $75

A tow from a Southside apartment complex to a shop on Beach Boulevard, maybe 8 miles, will land near the bottom of that range. A breakdown on the I-295 beltway to a shop across town climbs fast. For the mileage math, see [towing cost in Jacksonville per mile](/towing-cost-in-jacksonville-florida-per-mile-rate/).

## What Makes a Jacksonville Tow Cost More?

Distance, time of day, vehicle type, and where you're stuck all move the price. Jacksonville is one of the largest cities by land area in the lower 48, so distance matters more here than in most metros.

- **Distance:** Getting from Orange Park to the Northside or Mayport is a long haul. Mileage adds up quickly.
- **Location:** A pickup on I-95 near the Fuller Warren Bridge or the I-10 and I-95 interchange is harder and riskier than a parking lot pickup. Some companies charge more for it.
- **Vehicle:** AWD cars, lowered cars, EVs, and trucks usually need a flatbed. Dragging an AWD vehicle or an EV on its drive wheels can damage it, so don't let a driver talk you out of a flatbed.
- **Timing:** Friday evening traffic on the Mathews Bridge and Hart Bridge approaches slows arrival, and some dispatchers price for it.
- **Storm season:** After a heavy Florida downpour, demand spikes and prices follow.

## What Should I Ask the Tow Company Before They Come?

Ask for the total price, not just the hookup fee. A good dispatcher can give you a number in under a minute.

1. "What's the total to tow from [exact location] to [exact destination]?"
2. "Is that a flatbed, and is that price included?"
3. "Do you charge extra for a highway pickup or after hours?"
4. "What forms of payment do you take?"

Write down the company name and the quoted price. When the truck shows up, confirm the name on the truck matches. Predatory operators sometimes circle breakdowns, especially on I-95, and show up unrequested. If you didn't call them, don't let them hook up. ## Does My Insurance or Membership Already Cover the Tow?

Possibly, and checking takes two minutes. Many drivers pay for towing coverage and never use it.

- **Auto insurance:** If you added roadside assistance or towing to your policy, it may cover a set distance or dollar amount. It usually covers breakdowns, not accident claims.
- **AAA:** Basic membership typically includes a short tow. Check your tier, because the included mileage varies.
- **Credit card or new-car warranty:** Some cards and manufacturer warranties include roadside towing.

If you caused a collision, towing coverage is a separate question. See [whether your insurance covers towing when you cause the accident](/does-my-car-insurance-cover-towing-if-i-cause-the-accident/). Even if you're covered, ask the insurer's dispatcher for an arrival estimate. If it's long and you're sitting on the shoulder, safety comes first.

## What Should I Do While I Wait for the Tow Truck?

Get the car as far off the road as you safely can, turn on your hazards, and stay out of the travel lanes. On I-95 or I-295, exit the vehicle on the passenger side and wait behind the guardrail if one is available.

- Stay off the shoulder side of the car with your back to traffic.
- Keep your phone charged and your location shared with the dispatcher.
- Photograph your car's condition and odometer before the driver loads it.
- Don't pay cash on the roadside without a written receipt.

If the wait drags on, you have options. See [what to do when the tow truck is taking too long](/tow-truck-taking-too-long-what-are-my-options-roadside/).

## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow truck in Jacksonville for a short distance?**
A: A short local tow of under 10 miles typically costs $75 to $150 in total. Most of that is the hookup fee, so a short distance doesn't save as much as people expect.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow company directly than go through insurance?**
A: Not always. If your policy includes roadside coverage, the tow may cost you nothing out of pocket. Check your coverage first, then compare to a direct quote.

**Q: Do tow trucks in Jacksonville charge extra to tow from the highway?**
A: Some do, especially on I-95 and I-295 where pickups are more dangerous. Ask for the total price up front and confirm the highway pickup is included.

**Q: Can I ride in the tow truck?**
A: Most companies allow one or two passengers in the cab, but policies vary. Ask the dispatcher when you call, especially if you're stranded with kids or at night.

**Q: Where does the tow truck take my car if I don't pick a shop?**
A: Usually to the tow company's own lot, where storage fees can start the next day. Pick a destination shop before the driver arrives, or confirm the storage rate.
