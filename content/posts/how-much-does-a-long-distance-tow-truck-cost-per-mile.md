> **Quick Answer:** Long distance towing typically runs $2 to $4 per mile, plus a hookup fee of $35 to $100. A 100-mile tow lands around $250 to $500 before extras. A 300-mile tow can easily hit $700 to $1,300. Flatbed transport, oversized vehicles, and remote pickup locations all push the number higher. Get at least two quotes before you commit.

## What Does a Long Distance Tow Actually Cost Per Mile?

The going rate is $2 to $4 per mile for most passenger vehicles on a flatbed, with the hookup fee added on top. Local tows often bill a flat rate for the first 5 to 10 miles, but once you cross roughly 50 miles, most operators switch to a straight per-mile calculation.

Here's a realistic range by distance:

| Distance | Low End | High End |
|---|---|---|
| 50 miles | $135 | $300 |
| 100 miles | $235 | $500 |
| 200 miles | $435 | $900 |
| 300 miles | $635 | $1,300 |
| 500 miles | $1,035 | $2,100 |

These figures assume a standard sedan or compact SUV on a flatbed, one driver, normal road access. Specialty rigs, dual-rear-wheel trucks, and vehicles that need wheel-lift dollies because they're all-wheel drive can add $50 to $200 or more to the total.

## What Makes the Per-Mile Rate Go Up?

Several factors push you toward the $4-per-mile end or beyond.

**Vehicle size and type.** A Ford F-350 dually or a lifted 4x4 may not fit a standard flatbed. The operator has to use a larger rig, and the rate goes up accordingly. Motorcycles, on the other hand, often cost less to move.

**AWD and 4WD vehicles.** These can't be dragged on two wheels without damaging the drivetrain. They need a flatbed or four-wheel dollies. Most reputable operators use flatbeds standard, but confirm this before you agree to anything. See how [a seized engine situation affects your towing options and costs](/engine-seized-while-driving-towing-cost-insurance/) for a real-world example of why the wrong tow method makes a bad day worse.

**Time of day and day of week.** Nights, weekends, and holidays add a surcharge. Some operators charge 15 to 25 percent more after 6 p.m. or on Sundays.

**Remote pickup location.** If you break down 20 miles off the interstate on a rural highway, the driver charges for the dead miles to reach you. That can add $40 to $80 before your car is even on the truck.

**State lines.** Crossing state lines sometimes triggers a different rate because of licensing and permit requirements. Ask explicitly if your tow crosses a state line.

## Does Insurance or a Membership Cover Long Distance Towing?

Most standard roadside assistance plans do not cover long distance towing at full cost. That's the critical thing to know before you assume you're covered.

AAA Classic, for example, covers up to 5 miles per tow. AAA Plus covers up to 100 miles, and AAA Premier covers up to 200 miles. If you need 300 miles, even Premier members pay out of pocket for the overage. ## How Do You Avoid Getting Overcharged?

Call at least two companies and ask for a total estimate including hookup fee, mileage rate, and any fuel surcharges. Get the number in writing via text or email before the truck moves.

A few things to watch for:

- **Per-mile rate with no mention of a hookup fee** usually means the fee appears on the final invoice. Ask directly.
- **"We'll figure it out when we get there"** is a red flag. Legitimate operators quote before they hook up.
- **Fuel surcharges** are legitimate but should be disclosed upfront, not added after the fact.
- If the final bill feels wrong, you have real recourse. [Disputing a towing charge after a roadside emergency](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-after-roadside-emergency/) walks through how to push back effectively.

If you have time and the car is safe where it sits, use that time to shop. A 200-mile tow can vary by $200 to $400 between operators in the same market.

## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average cost to tow a car 100 miles?**
A: Expect $235 to $500 for a standard passenger vehicle, including the hookup fee. The per-mile rate is typically $2 to $4, and most operators add $35 to $100 to hook up the vehicle.

**Q: Is it cheaper to tow a car long distance or ship it on a transport carrier?**
A: For distances over 300 miles, an enclosed or open auto transport carrier is often cheaper than a tow truck and puts less wear on the tow equipment. A tow truck makes more sense when the car is not driveable and you need it moved quickly without waiting for a carrier's schedule.

**Q: Will AAA pay for a 200-mile tow?**
A: AAA Plus covers up to 100 miles per tow. AAA Premier covers up to 200 miles. Classic covers only 5 miles. Anything beyond your tier's limit is billed directly to you at the operator's per-mile rate.

**Q: Does my car insurance pay for a long distance tow after a breakdown?**
A: Usually not in full. Most auto insurance roadside assistance riders cap reimbursement at $50 to $100 per incident, which is far below the cost of a 100-plus-mile tow. Comprehensive or collision coverage may pay for towing after an accident, but not a mechanical breakdown.

**Q: Can I negotiate the per-mile rate with a tow company?**
A: Yes, especially for planned long-distance moves where the car is safely parked. Operators prefer steady work over dead time, so calling on a weekday morning and offering flexibility on pickup time gives you real leverage. Asking for a flat total rather than a per-mile calculation also works in your favor sometimes.
