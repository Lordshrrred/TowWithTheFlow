> **Quick Answer:** Sunday night tow truck service for a flat tire costs $125-250 depending on distance and location. If you just need a tire change, roadside assistance runs $75-150. Always call multiple companies and ask about flat tire service before requesting a full tow.

## What To Do

1. **Check if you really need towing.** Many tow companies offer tire changing services for less money than a full tow. If your spare is in good shape and properly inflated (most spares need 60 PSI), a roadside tech can swap it in 15-20 minutes and you're back on the road without paying tow fees.

2. **Call your insurance company first.** Many auto policies include roadside assistance that covers Sunday nights at no extra charge. Look on the back of your insurance card for a 24/7 number. Progressive, State Farm, and Geico all offer this add-on for roughly $5-10 per month, and it pays for itself the first time you use it.

3. **Try AAA or roadside apps.** If you have membership, use it. Non-members can often pay on the spot for $100-150. Apps like Urgently or HONK connect you to a local provider and show upfront pricing before you commit, which is useful late at night when you cannot easily comparison shop.

4. **Get quotes from multiple tow companies.** Sunday night rates vary wildly between companies. Call at least three. A company that charges $95 base on weekdays may charge $160 on Sunday nights. Those surcharges are legal and common, so you need to ask directly.

5. **Ask specifically about tire change service.** Say "I have a flat tire and need roadside help" instead of "I need a tow." Dispatchers route calls differently based on what you request. Asking for a tow gets you a tow truck driver; asking for roadside service may get you a lighter-duty tech who charges less.

6. **Confirm the total cost upfront.** Get mileage rates, base fees, and Sunday surcharges in writing via text. If the dispatcher won't quote a total, ask them to text you the per-mile rate and base hookup fee. A 10-mile tow at $6 per mile plus a $100 base fee adds up fast, especially with a $50 Sunday surcharge on top.

7. **Have your spare tire accessible.** If it's locked under the truck bed or buried under cargo, move it before the truck arrives. On trucks with a crank-down spare underneath the bed, you need the key or the special socket tool from your trunk kit. If that tool is missing, call ahead and mention it so the tech brings the right equipment.

## What It Might Cost

**Tire Change Service:**
- Base rate: $60-100
- Sunday/holiday surcharge: $15-50
- Total: $75-150

**Full Tow Service:**
- Base hook-up: $75-125
- Per-mile rate: $3-7
- Sunday surcharge: $25-75
- 5-mile tow total: $125-250

Keep in mind that a longer tow changes the math significantly. A 20-mile tow at $6 per mile plus a $100 base and $50 surcharge runs $270 before tip. If the flat happened near a 24-hour tire shop, it may be worth towing there directly rather than back to your regular shop, since you avoid a second trip later.

**Insurance/AAA:**
- Often covered completely
- May have small deductible ($25-50)

If you are filing through insurance, note the provider's response time. On Sunday nights in rural areas, even covered service can take 60-90 minutes. In dense urban areas, response is usually 30-45 minutes. Knowing this helps you decide whether to wait or pay out of pocket for a faster local company.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I use my spare tire if it's one of those small donut spares?**
A: Yes, but keep your speed under 50 mph and drive no more than 50-70 miles on it. Donut spares are not rated for highway speeds or extended use, so get to a tire shop the next morning before driving any distance.

**Q: What if I don't have a spare tire at all?**
A: You will need a full tow to a tire shop or your home. Some roadside providers carry a portable tire inflator and plug kit, which can handle a simple puncture in the tread for around $20-40 extra, but a blowout or sidewall damage cannot be plugged and requires a tow.

**Q: Is it safe to wait in my car for the tow truck on a Sunday night?**
A: If you are on a highway shoulder, stay inside with your seatbelt on and doors locked. If you are on a side street with no moving traffic, waiting outside the vehicle away from the road is reasonable. Either way, keep your hazard lights running the entire time.

## Stay Safe

- Pull completely off the roadway before calling
- Turn on hazard lights and use flares if available
- Stay inside your vehicle if on a busy road
- Have your license, registration, and payment ready
- Verify the tow truck company name matches who you called
