> **Quick Answer:** A flatbed tow in Dallas with no insurance coverage runs $100 to $175 for a short local haul of 5 to 10 miles. Longer trips cost more: expect $200 to $350 for 20 to 30 miles. You pay the full invoice out of pocket. Prices spike after hours, on weekends, and if you're stranded on I-635 or I-35E where hookup is harder. Get at least two quotes before agreeing.

## How Much Does a Flatbed Tow Cost in Dallas If I Have No Insurance?

Without roadside coverage, you're paying full retail. Most Dallas tow companies charge a hookup fee of $65 to $100, then add a per-mile rate of $3 to $5 per mile after that. A 7-mile flatbed tow, say from a breakdown near the Mockingbird Lane exit on US-75 to a shop in East Dallas, will run you $90 to $135. Push that out to 25 miles and you're looking at $175 to $225.

Flatbeds cost more than wheel-lift trucks because they're safer for your car's drivetrain and required for all-wheel-drive and low-clearance vehicles. That price difference is real, usually $20 to $40 more per trip, and worth it to avoid transmission damage.

For a full picture of how flatbed compares to hook-and-chain pricing locally, see [Towing Cost in Dallas Texas Hook and Chain vs Flatbed](/towing-cost-in-dallas-texas-hook-and-chain-vs-flatbed/).

## Where You Break Down in Dallas Changes the Price. Why?

Location affects both response time and cost. Breaking down on the shoulder of I-35E near the Oak Cliff split or on the upper deck of I-30 near downtown creates a harder, slower hookup. Some companies charge a congestion or difficulty fee of $20 to $50 for high-traffic locations where the driver has to work fast in tight conditions.

Break down in a quiet neighborhood like Lake Highlands or Lakewood, and the hookup is clean, no premium. Get stuck in the Mixmaster interchange at rush hour, and you may pay extra just because it takes longer to reach you safely.

Time of day matters too. Dallas flatbed rates after 8 p.m. and on weekends typically run 15% to 25% higher than standard daytime rates. If you're stranded late, check out [Tow Truck Cost in Dallas Texas After Business Hours](/tow-truck-cost-dallas-texas-after-business-hours/) before you call.

## Can I Negotiate the Price or Are Tow Rates Fixed in Dallas?

You can negotiate, and you should. Texas does not set mandatory rate caps for private tow companies on non-police-ordered tows. That means the price is whatever a company decides to charge, and it varies widely between operators.

Call at least two companies before committing. Tell them your vehicle type, exact location, and destination. Get the hookup fee and per-mile rate stated clearly before they roll a truck. If a company quotes you over the phone but suddenly adds fees at pickup, you have the right to refuse service before signing anything. Don't let urgency pressure you into a bad deal.

One tactic that works: mention you've already gotten a quote from another company. Most dispatchers will match or get closer.

## Common Questions

**Q: What's the average flatbed tow cost in Dallas with no insurance for a 10-mile trip?**
A: Expect $110 to $160 for a 10-mile flatbed tow in Dallas, including the hookup fee. Distance, time of day, and your exact location all affect the final number.

**Q: Do I have to use a flatbed, or can I save money with a different truck type?**
A: Only if your car is two-wheel-drive, not damaged, and not a low-clearance vehicle. All-wheel-drive and 4WD vehicles need a flatbed to avoid drivetrain damage. Wheel-lift tows run $20 to $40 cheaper but aren't appropriate for every car.

**Q: Can police order a tow in Dallas and how much does that cost?**
A: Yes. If your car is blocking traffic or is abandoned, Dallas PD can authorize a tow. Those police-ordered tows go through contracted city operators and often include impound fees starting around $150 to $200 on top of the tow cost itself. Get your car out fast if it's in a dangerous spot.

**Q: Is there a cheaper way to get a car towed in Dallas without roadside coverage?**
A: Shop around by phone before anyone rolls a truck. Rates vary enough in Dallas that calling three companies can save you $40 to $60. Some operators on the outskirts, like in Duncanville or Farmers Branch, charge less than downtown-adjacent companies.

**Q: Does no-fault insurance or liability-only coverage pay for a tow in Texas?**
A: No. Liability-only coverage pays for damage you cause to others. It does not cover your own vehicle, towing, or roadside help. You need comprehensive coverage or a separate roadside assistance add-on to get tow reimbursement.
