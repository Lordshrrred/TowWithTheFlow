> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in Los Angeles usually costs $100 to $250 total: a hookup fee of roughly $75 to $125 plus $4 to $8 per mile. Flatbeds, rush hour, nights, and freeway pickups push the price up. Heavy-duty or long-distance tows can run several hundred dollars. Ask for the total price before you agree to anything.

## How Much Does a Tow Truck Cost in Los Angeles?

Expect $100 to $250 for a typical local tow in Los Angeles, with the base hookup fee making up most of it. These are typical market ranges, not fixed rates, and they vary by company and situation.

- **Hookup fee:** about $75 to $125
- **Mileage:** about $4 to $8 per mile after the first few miles
- **Flatbed surcharge:** often $25 to $75 extra
- **After hours, weekends, holidays:** commonly 25 to 50 percent higher

A 5-mile tow from Koreatown to a shop in Mid-City might land near $120 to $180. A 25-mile haul from the Valley over the Sepulveda Pass will cost far more because of mileage and time in traffic. For a deeper per-mile breakdown, see [towing cost per mile in Los Angeles](/towing-cost-per-mile-los-angeles/).

## What Makes a Tow More Expensive in Los Angeles?

Time of day and location move the price more than distance does. LA traffic means the driver burns billable time just reaching you.

- **Rush hour:** A breakdown on the 405 near the 10 interchange at 5 p.m. can take a driver an hour to reach. Some companies price that in. More on that in [towing cost in Los Angeles during rush hour traffic](/towing-cost-los-angeles-rush-hour-traffic/).
- **Flatbed required:** All-wheel-drive cars, low-clearance cars, EVs, and anything with transmission damage generally need a flatbed. Not sure yours does? Check [how to know if your car needs a flatbed tow](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/).
- **Freeway pickups:** Safety risk and tight access can add cost, but on most freeways you may not pay at all (see below).
- **Wait and winch time:** Cars stuck in a tight underground garage in Downtown LA or Santa Monica may trigger extra labor charges.

## Is Freeway Towing Free in Los Angeles?

Sometimes. On many Los Angeles freeways, the Freeway Service Patrol (FSP), a Caltrans and CHP program, runs roving trucks during peak hours that can help with a flat, a jump, a little fuel, or a short tow to a safe spot off the freeway. The service is free to the driver, but coverage depends on the freeway segment and time of day, so don't count on it showing up.

If a CHP officer arrives, they may call a rotation tow company to move your car. That tow is not free, and you generally have the right to ask for a different tow company or have your own provider come, if that's safe and practical. Ask the officer. Don't assume you have no say.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged on a Tow?

Get the total price, in writing or by text, before the truck is hooked up. Predatory tow operators exist, especially around crashes and freeway shoulders.

1. Ask: "What is the total cost to tow to [exact address]?" Not just the hookup fee.
2. Ask if the quote includes mileage, flatbed, and any storage or gate fees.
3. Confirm payment methods before they arrive. Some drivers push cash only.
4. Photograph your car, the truck, and the driver's company name and license plate before they hook up.
5. Never let an unsolicited tow truck that just "happened by" take your car.

Pick the destination yourself. A tow to your own mechanic or home is usually cheaper than a tow to the driver's yard, where storage fees start the next day.

## Does Insurance or Roadside Assistance Cover the Tow?

Many policies do, up to a limit. AAA basic memberships typically cover a short tow (often about 5 to 7 miles) and higher tiers extend that, but check your actual plan. Your auto insurer may include roadside coverage as a cheap add-on, often a few dollars a month.

If you have neither, you're paying out of pocket. See [towing cost in Los Angeles with no insurance](/towing-cost-in-los-angeles-california-no-insurance/) for what to expect and how to cut the bill.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow truck per mile in Los Angeles?**
A: Typically $4 to $8 per mile after the first few miles, on top of a hookup fee. Some companies include the first 5 to 10 miles in the base price, so always ask for the all-in total.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive in LA?**
A: Usually yes, by about $25 to $75 over a standard wheel-lift tow. If your car is AWD, an EV, or has drivetrain damage, the flatbed is not optional, and the extra cost is worth it.

**Q: Can I get a tow truck in Los Angeles at night?**
A: Yes, many companies run 24 hours, but expect after-hours pricing, often 25 to 50 percent higher. Get the total quote before dispatch.

**Q: Does the CHP tow my car for free if I break down on the freeway?**
A: No. CHP may call a rotation tow company to clear a hazard, and you pay that company. The free service is the Freeway Service Patrol, which only helps with minor fixes or short moves to safety, where it operates.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive in LA?**
A: Often 30 to 60 minutes, and longer during rush hour or bad weather. If you're on a freeway shoulder, stay out of the car on the passenger side, away from traffic, while you wait.
