> **Quick Answer:** Don't stand next to your car fumbling with your phone. Move to a lit, populated spot nearby, stay on the phone with someone, and call a locksmith or roadside assistance immediately. If you feel threatened at any point, call 911. Your car can be dealt with later. You cannot.

## What Should I Do First If I'm Locked Out in a Bad Area?

Get away from your vehicle and get somewhere safer before you do anything else. A dark parking lot, a quiet side street, or an unfamiliar neighborhood at night is not the place to stand alone staring at your locked door.

Here's the order of operations:

1. **Move to better lighting.** A convenience store, a restaurant, a gas station, anywhere with people and cameras. Even 50 feet of distance can change your exposure level.
2. **Call someone.** A friend, family member, anyone. Tell them exactly where you are and stay on the phone. This matters both for your safety and for your own mental state.
3. **Call for help.** Locksmith, roadside assistance, or your insurance company's emergency line. Give them your cross streets, not just an address.
4. **Stay inside if possible.** If the area felt unsafe before you stepped out, and you can safely re-enter a nearby building, do it and wait there.

If you feel actively threatened, do not wait. Call 911 immediately. Police will assist with a lockout situation if there is a safety concern, and most departments will.

## Who Do I Call to Unlock My Car When I'm Stranded Somewhere Unsafe?

A local locksmith is usually your fastest option. They carry slim jim tools and air wedge kits and can open most vehicles without damage in under 10 minutes. Response times in urban areas run 20 to 45 minutes depending on time of day.

Roadside assistance is the other path. AAA, your auto insurance roadside add-on (State Farm, Geico, Progressive, and most others offer this), and manufacturer programs like Ford Roadside Assistance or OnStar all dispatch locksmiths or their own technicians. If you have any of these, use them first because the service is already paid for. If you don't, a private locksmith will run you $50 to $150 for a basic lockout, sometimes more late at night. See [locked keys in car: roadside assistance or locksmith, which is cheaper](/locked-keys-in-car-roadside-assistance-or-locksmith-which-is-cheaper/) for a full cost breakdown.

When you call, give them your exact location using cross streets and a landmark. "I'm outside the CVS at 5th and Main, facing south" is more useful than a street address alone when you're stressed and they're navigating.

## Is It Safe to Wait by My Car for the Locksmith to Arrive?

Not if the area genuinely feels dangerous. Wait inside a business or a well-lit public space instead, and give the locksmith a callback number so they can reach you when they arrive.

## What If My Keys Are Locked Inside With the Car Running?

Get help faster. A running engine means a depleted fuel tank eventually, and if it's hot outside it also means you may have a child or pet inside, which changes everything from a locked-out inconvenience to an emergency.

Call 911 immediately if there is a person or animal inside the vehicle. Dispatchers will send police or fire, and officers carry slim jim tools for exactly this reason. Do not try to break the window yourself unless the situation is life-threatening, because you risk injury from glass and you are liable for the repair cost.

If it is just the engine running with no passengers, still call a locksmith right away. A running engine in a parked car in a rough neighborhood also attracts attention. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I call 911 just because I'm locked out of my car in a scary area?**
A: Yes. If you feel unsafe, that is enough reason. Police respond to lockouts regularly, especially when a safety concern is involved. Be honest with the dispatcher about why you're calling and where you are.

**Q: How long will a locksmith take to reach me at night?**
A: In most cities, 30 to 60 minutes is realistic for a late-night call. In a dense metro area you might get someone in 20 minutes. Call immediately, give your exact location, and ask for an ETA so you can plan where to wait.

**Q: Will a locksmith come to a bad neighborhood?**
A: Most will. They do this work across the city every night. If you're concerned, mention your location when you call and ask directly. Some dispatchers can tell you the technician's ETA and a general description of who to expect so you are not approached by someone you cannot verify.

**Q: What if I have no phone battery and I'm locked out in an unsafe area?**
A: Walk to the nearest open business and ask to use their phone or charger. Convenience stores, gas stations, and fast food restaurants are your best bets at night. Do not approach strangers on the street for help with your phone.

**Q: Can I break my own car window to get back in?**
A: You can, it is your car. But it is a last resort. A side window costs $150 to $400 to replace, glass goes everywhere inside the cabin, and the cut risk is real. Only break a window if there is a person or animal trapped inside in dangerous heat or cold, or if you face an immediate physical threat and need to drive away.
