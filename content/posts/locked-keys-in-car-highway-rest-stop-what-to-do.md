> **Quick Answer:** Call your roadside assistance provider first. AAA, your insurance, or a credit card benefit will dispatch a locksmith to your location at no or low out-of-pocket cost. If you have none of those, call a local locksmith directly. A lockout at a rest stop is not a 911 situation unless someone is trapped inside the vehicle. Expect 30 to 60 minutes for help to arrive.

## What Are My First Steps Right Now If I'm Locked Out at a Rest Stop?

Check every door and the trunk before you call anyone. People miss unlocked rear doors more often than you would expect, especially on SUVs where the liftgate has a separate lock. If everything is locked, your fastest path forward is roadside assistance. Call AAA, your insurance company's roadside line, or check your credit card benefits app. Give them your exact rest stop location: the highway number, direction of travel, and state. That information speeds up dispatch considerably.

Do not break a window. A replacement side window runs $200 to $400 installed, and a locksmith will cost a fraction of that.

## Who Should I Call, and What Will It Cost Me?

A locksmith dispatched through roadside assistance will usually cost you nothing out of pocket, or a flat service fee in the $50 to $75 range if you have a copay plan. If you call a locksmith directly without any coverage, expect $75 to $150 for a standard lockout at a rest stop. Prices are higher at night or on weekends.

Your coverage options, in order of priority:

- **AAA membership.** Covers lockouts with no per-incident fee depending on your membership tier. [AAA towing and service miles vary by plan](/aaa-towing-coverage-miles-per-year-limit/), so know your tier.
- **Your auto insurance.** Many policies include roadside assistance as an add-on. State Farm, USAA, Progressive, and GEICO all offer it. Check your insurance app or the declarations page in your glove box.
- **Credit card benefits.** Visa Signature and certain Mastercard World cards include roadside assistance. [Credit card roadside coverage varies more than people think](/does-credit-card-roadside-assistance-cover-towing-cost/), so read the terms before you rely on it.
- **Your car dealership plan.** Some new vehicles come with complimentary roadside assistance through the manufacturer for the first few years. Check your owner's manual or the manufacturer's app.

## How Do I Find My Exact Location at a Rest Stop?

Tell the dispatcher the highway number, mile marker if visible, direction of travel (northbound, southbound, eastbound, westbound), and the state. Most rest stops have posted signage near the entrance with the facility name and sometimes a mile marker. On interstates, mile markers increase as you travel north or east, so if you passed mile marker 142 before pulling in, you are somewhere around 142.

Your phone's GPS is the most reliable backup. Open Google Maps or Apple Maps and drop a pin on your current location. You can share that pin directly with a dispatcher or read off the coordinates.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can a locksmith get into any car at a rest stop, or are some too hard to unlock?**
A: A professional locksmith can open virtually any passenger car or truck. Modern cars with laser-cut keys and transponder chips take a bit longer, but the lockout process itself does not require programming a new key. The locksmith uses a physical tool to release the door lock mechanically.

**Q: What if I have a baby or pet locked inside the car?**
A: Call 911 immediately. This is the one situation where emergency services, not a locksmith, should respond. In hot weather, a car interior can reach dangerous temperatures within minutes. Do not wait for a roadside assistance ETA.

**Q: Will my insurance rate go up if I use roadside assistance for a lockout?**
A: No. Roadside assistance claims are service calls, not at-fault incidents. They do not affect your collision or liability history and will not trigger a rate increase.

**Q: How long will a locksmith actually take to reach a rest stop?**
A: In a metro area or near a major highway corridor, 20 to 45 minutes is typical. In a rural area on a remote stretch of interstate, it can be 60 to 90 minutes. Give the dispatcher your location precisely and ask for an estimated arrival time so you know what you are working with.

**Q: What if I have no roadside assistance and can't afford a locksmith right now?**
A: State police or highway patrol can sometimes help you contact a local locksmith and may know which services are closest to your rest stop. Do not call 911 for this unless there is a safety emergency. Most rest stops have posted information boards or a phone near the entrance. Some truckers carry slim-jims and will offer to help, but again, the risk of vehicle damage from an amateur attempt is real.
