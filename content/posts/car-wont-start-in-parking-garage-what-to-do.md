Your car won't start in a parking garage. You're stuck in a concrete box with low ceilings, bad cell reception, and people trying to get around you. Here's what to actually do.

> **Quick Answer:** First, turn on your hazard lights. Try to determine if it's a dead battery (clicking, dim lights) or something else. Call roadside assistance or a tow service immediately. Parking garages create two real problems: poor cell signal and low clearance that blocks standard tow trucks. You may need a flatbed or specialized tow, and you need to request that upfront when you call.

## What's Most Likely Wrong When a Car Won't Start in a Garage?

A dead battery causes the majority of no-start situations in parking garages, especially if you left lights on or the car sat for several days. Here's how to narrow it down fast:

- **Clicking once or rapid clicking:** Dead or severely weak battery. See [Car Won't Start, Clicks Once and Nothing Else](/car-wont-start-clicks-once-and-nothing-else/) for what that sound means and your options.
- **No sound at all, dash goes dark:** Battery is completely dead or a main fuse has blown.
- **Engine cranks but won't fire:** Fuel delivery problem, bad spark, or an immobilizer issue.
- **Starts briefly then dies:** Could be a fuel pressure problem or a failing crankshaft position sensor.

Don't keep cranking. If it won't start after two or three solid attempts, you're burning up the starter motor and potentially flooding the engine. Stop, assess, call for help.

## Can a Regular Tow Truck Get Into a Parking Garage?

Not always, and this is the most important thing to know before you call. Most multi-level parking garages have clearance between 6 feet 2 inches and 7 feet. A standard flatbed tow truck is typically 11 to 13 feet tall with the vehicle loaded. It cannot physically enter the structure.

When you call for a tow, tell the dispatcher immediately:
1. You're inside a parking garage, not at the entrance.
2. The garage's clearance height if you can find it posted near the entrance.
3. What level you're on.

A reputable towing company will send a low-clearance wheel-lift truck or a specialized flatbed that can stay on street level while they roll your car to the exit. If you're on an upper level, they may need to jump-start the car first just to move it, then tow from outside. Know what you're asking for before they show up with the wrong equipment. [Understanding whether you need a flatbed](/how-do-i-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/) can help you ask the right questions.

## Who Do I Call When My Car Won't Start in a Parking Garage?

Call in this order based on what you have:

1. **Roadside assistance through your insurer or automaker.** If you have coverage through State Farm, GEICO, AAA, or your car's manufacturer program, call them first. Be explicit about the garage clearance issue.
2. **A local towing company directly.** Search "low clearance tow truck" plus your city. Generic dispatch centers sometimes send the wrong truck and you'll wait twice.
3. **The parking garage attendant.** Some garages, especially airport and downtown structures, keep jump-start equipment on site or have a security team that can help or at least control traffic around your car.
4. **911 only if** the situation becomes a safety issue, someone is blocking emergency access, or the garage is about to close and lock.

Cell signal is often weak inside concrete structures. If you can't get a signal, walk to the stairwell closest to an exterior wall, or go up to the roof level if there is one. That usually recovers enough signal to make a call.

## How Long Will I Wait for Help, and What Will It Cost?

In a dense urban area, roadside assistance response times run 30 to 60 minutes on average during normal hours. Peak times, bad weather, or nights can push that to 90 minutes or more. See [how long tow trucks actually take to arrive](/how-long-does-it-take-for-a-tow-truck-to-arrive/) for a realistic picture.

On cost: a jump-start call typically runs $50 to $100 if it's not covered by roadside assistance. A tow from inside a garage to a nearby shop usually falls between $85 and $175 for the first few miles, though it can go higher if a specialized low-clearance truck is required. If you don't have roadside assistance, expect the upper end of that range with no negotiation.

One practical tip: if a jump-start gets the car running, drive straight to an auto parts store. AutoZone and O'Reilly both do free battery and alternator tests. A battery that died once in a warm garage may have very little life left, and you don't want to find that out again tomorrow in the same spot.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I get a jump-start inside a parking garage?**
A: Yes, if another car can get close enough and the ceiling allows a truck or SUV to enter. Call roadside assistance and specify you're inside the structure. Some services carry portable lithium jump packs that don't require a second vehicle at all, which works perfectly in tight spaces.

**Q: What if my car is blocking a ramp or exit in the garage?**
A: Turn hazard lights on immediately and call the garage attendant first. They may have equipment to push or roll your car to a safe spot. If no attendant is available, call a tow company and explain the blockage. Most dispatchers will prioritize calls where a vehicle is obstructing traffic flow.

**Q: Will the parking garage charge me extra while I wait for a tow?**
A: Most do. Parking fees don't stop because your car broke down. Some garages will waive extra fees if you explain the breakdown situation to an attendant and get a time stamp. It's worth asking, especially in airport or event-center garages where daily max fees can be steep.

**Q: My car won't start after sitting in the garage for several days. What happened?**
A: Most likely the battery drained. Batteries lose charge over time, and some vehicles have parasitic draws from electronics that drain a battery in three to five days. A jump-start and a free battery test will tell you if the battery needs to be replaced or if there's a deeper electrical issue pulling current when the car is off.

**Q: Does roadside assistance cover tows from inside a parking structure?**
A: Generally yes. Roadside assistance through insurers like State Farm or GEICO typically covers towing from wherever the car is disabled, including garages. The catch is mileage limits, some policies only cover the first five to seven miles. Check your policy limit before you authorize a tow to a shop across town.
