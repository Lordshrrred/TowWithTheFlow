## What Towing Costs in Nashville

Nashville is a spread-out city. A breakdown on I-24 near Antioch sits a very different distance from a downtown shop than one near the Briley Parkway interchange. Distance is the biggest cost driver, but it is not the only one.

Here is what Nashville towing companies typically charge:

**Hookup fee:** $75 to $125. This is the flat charge just to send the truck and attach your vehicle. You pay it no matter how short the tow.

**Per-mile rate:** $3 to $7 per mile. Most local operators land around $4 to $5. Budget closer to $6 or $7 if you are calling a premium fleet or a busy dispatcher on a Friday night.

**Flatbed vs. wheel-lift:** Flatbed trucks run $20 to $40 more than wheel-lift. If you have a lowered car, AWD, or a vehicle with front-end damage, the shop will require a flatbed anyway.

**After-hours and weekend:** Expect a $25 to $50 surcharge for calls between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m., or any time on a holiday weekend. Nashville's bar district and Broadway corridor keep tow companies busy on weekends, which means dispatch times can stretch and some companies charge for the wait.

**Accident tow:** If police dispatch a tow from an accident scene, the company that responds is often on a city rotation list. You do not get to shop rates. Those tows typically run $150 to $300 just to get your car to an impound lot, and storage fees start the same day. If you are navigating that situation, check what your insurance covers before you authorize anything. ## Sample Nashville Tow Scenarios

**Broke down near downtown Nashville, towed 6 miles to a Midtown shop:** Hookup $95 + 6 miles at $5 = $125 total.

**Stalled on I-65 near Harding Place, towed 12 miles to a dealership in Brentwood:** Hookup $100 + 12 miles at $5 = $160.

**Late-night breakdown on Gallatin Pike, flatbed required, towed 8 miles:** Hookup $110 + flatbed surcharge $30 + 8 miles at $6 = $188.

**Long-distance tow from Nashville to Murfreesboro, roughly 35 miles:** Hookup $100 + 35 miles at $5 = $275. Some companies cap long-distance rates; ask before you agree.

## What To Do Right Now

1. **Get off the road safely.** If you are on I-40, I-65, or I-24, pull fully onto the shoulder. Turn on hazard lights immediately.
2. **Call your insurance first.** Check your phone app or your insurance card for a roadside assistance number before calling a random tow company. Many policies cover at least one tow per year.
3. **Get a quote before you authorize.** Ask the dispatcher: hookup fee, per-mile rate, and whether the destination requires a flatbed. Get a number, not a range.
4. **Know your destination.** Have the address of your mechanic or dealership ready. Tow companies charge to the drop-off location. If you do not know where you want to go, they will take you to their affiliated lot and storage fees start fast.
5. **Confirm wait time.** In heavy traffic on 440 or during a Titans or Predators event, response times can hit 45 to 90 minutes. If it is a safety concern, call 911 first.

## Stay Safe

- Stay in your vehicle if you are on a highway shoulder. Tennessee has a Move Over law and fast-moving traffic on its interstates is a real danger.
- If you must exit the vehicle, get past the guardrail and well away from the lane.
- At night, use your phone flashlight or a reflective vest if you have one.
- Do not accept help from strangers offering to tow your car for cash. Unlicensed operators sometimes work accidents and charge whatever they want with no recourse.
