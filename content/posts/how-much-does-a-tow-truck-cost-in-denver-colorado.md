> **Quick Answer:** A tow truck in Denver typically costs $75 to $125 for a local tow under 10 miles. Add $3 to $7 per mile beyond that. After-hours, weekend, and winter surcharges can push a basic call to $150 to $250 or more. If you broke down in the foothills or on I-70 in the mountains, expect the high end of that range or higher.

## What You'll Actually Pay: Denver Tow Truck Costs Broken Down

Denver towing operators charge a hook-up fee plus a per-mile rate. Here's how the math works on the ground:

- **Hook-up fee:** $75 to $100 for most flatbed or wheel-lift tows within the metro
- **Per-mile rate:** $3 to $7 per mile after the first few miles
- **After-hours or weekend premium:** $25 to $75 added to the base rate
- **Snow or ice surcharge:** $30 to $100 extra during active winter weather
- **Specialty tow (AWD, low-clearance, or disabled vehicle):** Add $50 to $150 depending on difficulty

A 5-mile tow from a Capitol Hill parking lot to a shop in the Highlands? Expect $90 to $130. A 20-mile tow from a breakdown on I-25 to a dealership in Littleton? Budget $150 to $200 in normal conditions.

Denver's elevation and frequent winter storms matter here. Tow operators running in snow or responding to mountain foothills routes charge more, and they're upfront about it. If your car went down on a canyon road or off I-70 near Morrison, see the breakdown of [towing cost in Denver Colorado mountains](/towing-cost-denver-colorado-mountains/) for what you can expect to pay on those specific routes.

## What To Do Right Now

1. **Get off the road if you can.** Pull to a shoulder, parking lot, or side street before you call. Tows from active lanes sometimes trigger traffic control fees.
2. **Know your location.** Give cross streets or a mile marker. "Near the Colfax Walgreens" helps dispatch find you faster than "on Colfax somewhere."
3. **Call your insurance or roadside plan first.** Many drivers have towing coverage they forget about. Check before you pay out of pocket.
4. **Ask for a price quote before they hook up.** Colorado doesn't cap towing rates, so get the hook-up fee and per-mile rate confirmed verbally or in writing.
5. **If it's a snow emergency, call early.** Denver tow trucks stack up fast during snowstorms. If you're waiting more than an hour, you're not alone. Read more about [tow truck costs during Denver snow emergencies](/tow-truck-cost-denver-snow-emergency/) and what response times look like.
6. **Take a photo of your car's position and any damage before the tow.** Useful if there's a dispute later.

## Stay Safe While You Wait

- Stay inside your car on highway shoulders whenever possible. A running car is more visible than a person standing outside.
- Turn on hazard lights immediately, even in daylight.
- Set out road flares or reflective triangles if you have them, 50 to 100 feet behind the vehicle.
- If you're on I-25, I-70, or C-470 during rush hour or a snowstorm, do not stand near the traffic side of your vehicle.
- Keep your phone charged. If the battery is low, conserve it by not streaming video while you wait.
