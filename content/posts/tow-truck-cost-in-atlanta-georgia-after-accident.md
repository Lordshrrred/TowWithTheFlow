> **Quick Answer:** After an accident in Atlanta, expect to pay $100 to $250 for a standard tow within the city. Add $50 to $100 more if your car needs a flatbed (common after a collision), you're stuck on I-285, or it's after midnight. If another driver caused the crash, their liability insurance typically covers the tow. If it's your fault, your collision or comprehensive coverage usually applies, depending on your policy.

## How Much Does a Tow Truck Cost in Atlanta After an Accident?

A post-accident tow in Atlanta typically runs $100 to $175 for a short in-city haul, say from a fender-bender on Peachtree Street to a shop in Midtown or Buckhead. If the car is disabled and needs a flatbed, budget $150 to $250. Add a hook-up fee of $50 to $75 on top of a per-mile rate that usually lands between $3 and $5 per mile. Longer hauls, like dragging a wrecked vehicle from the I-285/I-20 interchange out to a body shop in Smyrna or Decatur, push the total past $300 without much trouble.

Heavy-duty tows for trucks, SUVs, and AWD vehicles that must ride a flatbed to avoid drivetrain damage cost more. [If you're not sure whether your car needs a flatbed after an accident, this breakdown explains when it's required](/how-do-i-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/).

## Who Actually Pays the Tow Bill After a Crash in Atlanta?

The at-fault driver's liability insurance pays for your tow if they caused the accident. You call their insurer, file a claim, and get reimbursed. The catch: you often have to pay the tow company upfront on scene, then wait for reimbursement, which can take days to weeks.

If you caused the crash, your collision coverage handles it once you meet your deductible. Comprehensive coverage does not apply to collision-related towing, it only covers non-collision events like theft or hail. If you only carry liability (no collision), you pay the tow out of pocket. For the full insurance breakdown, [this article on whether car insurance covers towing after an accident](/does-car-insurance-cover-towing-after-accident/) lays out exactly what each coverage type pays.

Georgia law does not cap tow rates for accident scenes the way some states do, so rates vary by operator.

## Why Is the Tow More Expensive at an Accident Scene Than a Normal Breakdown?

Accident tows cost more for several real reasons. Police-dispatched tows on Atlanta's major corridors, I-75 through downtown, I-85 near the Spaghetti Junction interchange at I-285, or I-20 west of downtown, come from a rotation list managed by the Georgia Department of Transportation's NaviGAtor system. You do not get to shop around while traffic is backing up behind you. Rotation-list operators know this.

Additional charges that stack up fast after a crash:

- **Scene cleanup fee:** $25 to $75 for minor fluid or debris cleanup
- **Winching or recovery fee:** $100 to $300 if the car is off the road, in a ditch, or wedged against a barrier. The stretch of I-285 near the Chamblee-Tucker Road area is notorious for guardrail impacts that require full recovery rigs.
- **Storage fees:** Once the car hits the impound or tow yard, you're paying $35 to $75 per day. Get the car released or moved to your own shop as fast as possible.
- **After-hours premium:** Accidents that happen between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. often add $50 to $100 to the base rate.

## Does It Cost More to Tow a Car After an Accident in Atlanta Than a Normal Breakdown?

Yes, almost always. A standard breakdown tow is pre-planned: you call, you wait, you pick the provider. An accident tow is reactive, often police-dispatched, and involves a vehicle that may be undrivable in the middle of active traffic. All of that adds cost. For comparison, a regular breakdown tow in Atlanta from, say, Little Five Points to a shop in Decatur might run $85 to $125. The same trip post-accident, with a flatbed, police dispatch, and minor cleanup, easily doubles that. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I refuse the tow truck the police call and use my own?**
A: Yes, in most cases. If your car is not blocking traffic and the scene is safe, you can decline a police-dispatched rotation tow and call your own provider. Act fast, because once an officer orders a specific tow, it gets more complicated to cancel.

**Q: How long can my car sit at the tow yard before fees get out of control?**
A: Most Atlanta-area yards charge $35 to $75 per day starting the day after drop-off. After a week, you could owe $250 to $500 in storage alone. Contact your insurer the same day as the accident and get authorization to release the vehicle to a body shop quickly.

**Q: What if the other driver has no insurance and I can't get reimbursed?**
A: Your uninsured motorist property damage coverage, if you carry it, can cover the tow. If you don't have that coverage, you're paying out of pocket and would need to pursue the at-fault driver in small claims court. For details on towing without insurance coverage, [see the Atlanta no-insurance towing cost guide](/towing-cost-atlanta-georgia-no-insurance/).

**Q: Do tow trucks in Atlanta charge more on weekends or holidays?**
A: Most operators add a $25 to $75 surcharge for weekend nights and holidays. If your accident happens on a Friday night or a holiday weekend, assume you're in the higher range of any estimate you get.

**Q: Is a flatbed required after an accident, or can they use a wheel-lift?**
A: If the car has front-end or suspension damage, or if it's an AWD or 4WD vehicle, a flatbed is required to avoid additional drivetrain damage during transport. A wheel-lift on a damaged car can snap tie rods or bend control arms. Insist on a flatbed if there's any visible suspension or steering damage.
