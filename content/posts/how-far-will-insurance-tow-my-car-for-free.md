> **Quick Answer:** There is no single number. Insurance-based roadside towing commonly covers anywhere from 5 to 100 miles, or a flat dollar cap like $50 to $150, depending on your insurer and plan. Many policies have no roadside add-on at all. Check your policy documents or insurer app for your exact limit before the driver hooks up, because anything past it comes out of your pocket.

## How Far Will Insurance Actually Tow My Car for Free?

Most policies that include towing cover somewhere between 5 and 100 miles, but your policy sets the real number. Some insurers cap by distance, some cap by dollar amount, and some only cover towing to the nearest qualified repair shop.

The pattern looks like this:

- **Mileage cap:** "Up to 15 miles" or "up to 100 miles," common on bundled roadside plans.
- **Dollar cap:** "Up to $75 per disablement." This is common on basic add-ons, and the dollar cap is why a long tow in a pricey market gets cut short.
- **Nearest shop only:** Some plans tow to the closest repair facility, not the shop you prefer.

Most insurers also treat roadside towing as an optional add-on, not a default. If you never added it, you may have zero towing coverage. See [Does Car Insurance Cover Roadside Assistance If I Add It?](/does-car-insurance-cover-roadside-assistance-if-i-add-it/) for how that add-on works.

## How Do I Find My Exact Towing Limit Right Now?

Open your insurer's app or log in on your phone. Look for "roadside assistance" or "towing and labor" under your coverages. The limit is listed there.

If you can't get in, check your insurance card or the policy declarations page in your email. The line item reads "Towing and Labor" or "Roadside Assistance," followed by a mileage or dollar figure.

If you still can't find it, call the number on your insurance card and ask three things:

1. Do I have towing coverage on this policy?
2. Is the cap in miles or dollars?
3. Do I have to use your dispatched tow company, or can I choose?

That last question matters. Many insurers only reimburse for their own network. If you call your own tow truck, you may pay up front and fight for reimbursement later.

For carrier-specific details, read [Progressive Roadside Assistance Towing Coverage Limit](/progressive-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-limit/) or [Does GEICO Cover Towing and Roadside Assistance?](/does-geico-cover-towing-and-roadside-assistance/).

## What Happens If My Car Needs to Go Farther Than the Free Limit?

You pay the difference, usually per mile past the cap. Long-distance towing typically runs several dollars per mile on top of a base hookup fee, and flatbeds cost more than wheel-lift trucks.

Say your plan covers 15 miles and your preferred shop is 40 miles away. You owe the extra 25 miles. Before agreeing, ask the driver for the total cost in writing or by text, including the hookup fee and per-mile rate past your covered distance.

You have three ways to keep the bill down:

- **Tow to a closer shop**, then move the car later when it isn't an emergency.
- **Tow to your home** if the car is safe to sit there and the distance fits your cap.

## Does Insurance Towing Work Differently After an Accident?

Yes. After a collision, towing usually falls under your collision coverage or the at-fault driver's liability, not your roadside add-on. The mileage cap often doesn't apply the same way.

If another driver caused the crash, their insurer should pay for the tow. If you caused it, your collision coverage typically handles it, minus your deductible in some cases. Roadside add-ons often exclude accident tows entirely.

Read [Who Pays for Towing After a Car Accident That Wasn't Your Fault?](/who-pays-for-towing-after-a-car-accident-not-my-fault/) before you sign anything at the scene. Don't let a tow driver talk you into a yard you didn't choose without asking who's paying.

## Should I Call My Insurer or a Tow Company First?

Call your insurer first if you have roadside coverage. Their dispatcher confirms your limit and sends a covered truck, which protects you from surprise charges.

Call a tow company directly only when your insurer can't send help in a reasonable time or you've confirmed you have no coverage. If you're stuck on a highway shoulder, safety comes before the paperwork. Get the car off the travel lane if you can, turn on your hazards, and stay clear of traffic. ## Common Questions

**Q: Is towing always free with car insurance?**
A: No. Towing is typically a paid add-on, not a standard coverage. If you never added roadside assistance or towing and labor, your insurer won't pay for a breakdown tow.

**Q: Does my insurance tow me to my house or just to a shop?**
A: It depends on the plan. Some tow to any location within your mileage cap, others only to the nearest repair facility. Ask the dispatcher before the truck loads your car.

**Q: What if the tow truck driver says my limit is lower than I think?**
A: Pause the tow and call your insurer from the roadside to confirm. Get the covered distance or dollar amount from them directly, not from the driver.

**Q: Can I use my own tow company and get reimbursed?**
A: Sometimes, but many insurers only reimburse their network and only up to the cap. Keep the itemized receipt and confirm reimbursement rules before you pay.

**Q: Does AAA tow farther than my insurance does?**
A: Often yes. Standard AAA plans commonly cover about 5 to 7 miles and upgraded tiers cover 100 miles or more, but the exact figure depends on your membership level. Check your tier. See [Does AAA Cover Towing After an Accident or Only Breakdowns?](/does-aaa-cover-towing-cost-after-accident-or-only-breakdowns/) for the limits.
