> **Quick Answer:** Portland towing costs $75-150 for local hauls, but expect 20-50% surcharges during heavy rain. Weather delays, hazard pay, and increased demand drive prices up. Major storms can push emergency tows to $200+ within city limits.

## What To Do

1. **Call multiple tow companies** - Prices vary dramatically during bad weather
2. **Ask about weather surcharges upfront** - Many Portland companies add 25-40% for rain/ice conditions
3. **Check your insurance coverage** - Some policies cover weather-related towing at no extra cost
4. **Consider waiting** - If not blocking traffic and safe, prices often drop when weather clears
5. **Get exact quotes** - Distance, vehicle type, and storm severity all affect final cost
6. **Ask for ETA** - Rain causes delays that can double response times

On a typical dry Portland day, a tow truck might reach you in 20-30 minutes. During a heavy November rain event or an ice storm on the West Hills, that same company might quote you 60-90 minutes. Dispatch queues fill fast, especially after rush hour pile-ups on I-5 or I-205. If your car is drivable and you are in a safe pullout, waiting until the storm passes can save you both time and real money.

Always confirm whether the quote is a flat rate or a hook-up fee plus a per-mile charge. A $75 quote that hides a $4-per-mile fee turns into $135 before you reach a shop across town. Get the full number, not just the hook-up price.

## What It Might Cost

**Normal Portland rates:**
- Local tow (under 5 miles): $75-125
- Medium distance (5-15 miles): $100-175
- Accident/recovery: $150-300

**Rainy weather additions:**
- Weather surcharge: 20-50% extra
- Emergency/after-hours: Additional $25-75
- Hazardous conditions: Up to double base rate

**Real examples during storms:**
- Downtown to SE Portland: $180-220
- Highway accident recovery: $250-400
- Stuck in flooding: $300-500

A few things push that flooding number even higher. If your car has rolled into a ditch or is partially submerged, the tow operator needs specialized rigging equipment and often a second crew member for safety. That labor and gear costs extra. Expect a minimum of $350-450 for any water-recovery situation, and significantly more if the vehicle needs to be winched out of a steep embankment off Highway 26 or Skyline Boulevard.

Vehicle type also matters more than most people realize. A standard sedan tows for the base rate. An AWD SUV or a truck that needs all four wheels lifted off the ground requires a flatbed, which typically adds $30-60 to any quoted price. If you drive a lifted truck or a low-clearance sports car, tell dispatch before they roll a truck so they send the right equipment on the first trip.

Major companies like Dick Hannah, Beaverton Hyundai's service, and AAA-contracted services all adjust rates during Pacific Northwest storms.

AAA membership is worth running the numbers on. Standard membership runs roughly $60-80 per year and covers several free tows up to a set mileage limit. One storm-season tow in Portland can cost more than two years of that membership fee.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will my car insurance cover a tow during a Portland rainstorm?**
A: It depends on your policy. Comprehensive coverage does not typically include towing, but if you added roadside assistance as a rider, it usually covers weather-related tows up to a stated dollar amount, often $50-100. Call your insurer before you call a tow company so you know exactly what you are entitled to.

**Q: How long will I wait for a tow truck during a bad Portland storm?**
A: Plan for 60-90 minutes during heavy rain events, and potentially longer if ice is involved or a major accident has closed lanes on I-5 or I-84. Calling two or three companies simultaneously and taking whoever arrives first is a smart move when conditions are dangerous.

**Q: Can I negotiate the weather surcharge with a Portland tow company?**
A: Occasionally, yes. Independent operators have more flexibility than large fleet companies. If you are not blocking traffic and can wait, telling dispatch you will hold off for an hour often gets you closer to the base rate. During declared weather emergencies, however, most companies hold firm on surcharges because demand is high and their operating costs genuinely spike.

## Stay Safe

- Pull completely off roadway if possible
- Turn on hazard lights and use flares/triangles
- Stay inside vehicle unless absolutely necessary to exit
- Watch for hydroplaning traffic and reduced visibility
- Keep phone charged for multiple tow company calls
- Have cash ready as some drivers prefer it during emergencies
