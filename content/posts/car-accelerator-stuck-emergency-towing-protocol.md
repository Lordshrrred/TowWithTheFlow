> **Quick Answer:** Shift into neutral immediately, steer to the shoulder, and brake to a stop. Do not turn the engine off while moving at speed. Once stopped safely, turn it off. A stuck throttle means the car should not be driven again until a mechanic finds the cause. Call a tow truck. This is not a situation to diagnose roadside and drive to a shop.

## What Do I Do If My Accelerator Gets Stuck While Driving?

Shift into neutral. That single move breaks the connection between the engine and the wheels, no matter how high the RPMs climb. Steer toward the shoulder while you do it, then apply steady brake pressure to stop the car. Do not yank the key to off while you are still moving. On most modern vehicles with electronic power steering and power brakes, cutting the ignition kills your steering and brake boost, making a bad situation dangerous.

Once you are stopped and fully clear of traffic, turn the engine off. If you have a push-button start, press and hold it for three seconds. If the car has a keyed ignition, turn it to off.

Stay in the car with your seatbelt on until you are certain it is safe to exit. On a busy highway, a door swinging open into traffic is its own emergency. If you stopped on a highway shoulder, get out on the passenger side and move away from the road.

## Is It Safe to Drive After a Stuck Throttle, Even If It Seems Fixed?

No. Do not drive the car again until a mechanic has inspected it. A throttle that stuck once and then freed itself has a mechanical or electronic problem that has not gone away. It will happen again, possibly at higher speed or in heavier traffic.

The two most common causes are a physical obstruction under the floor mat jamming the pedal, or a faulty throttle body. On drive-by-wire systems, which have been standard on most vehicles since the early 2000s, a bad throttle position sensor or a failing electronic throttle control module can send incorrect signals and cause unintended acceleration. Neither of these is something you can confirm or fix on the shoulder.

Check under the floor mat quickly if you want to rule out the obvious before calling a tow, but do not restart the car and try to drive it.

## What Towing Protocol Should I Follow After a Stuck Accelerator?

Call a tow truck to the exact location where you stopped. Give the dispatcher your road name, the direction you were traveling, and the nearest mile marker or highway exit. For example: "Southbound I-40, just past Exit 140, right shoulder." This gets the truck to you without confusion.

A standard flatbed or wheel-lift tow to a shop is the right call here. The car does not need any special equipment for towing unless you also suspect drivetrain damage from sustained high-RPM operation before you could get it stopped.

If you have [roadside assistance through USAA](/usaa-towing-coverage-how-much-does-it-pay/) or another insurer, call them first. Most policies cover the full tow to the nearest qualified

## Could This Be a Brake Problem Too, or Just the Throttle?

It is just the throttle. Your brakes are a completely separate system and can overpower the engine even with the throttle wide open, assuming they are functioning normally. The panic in a stuck accelerator situation usually leads people to mash the brake and the gas together, which heats and fatigues the brakes fast, but a firm, sustained brake application will stop the car.

If you pressed the brake hard during the event and now notice a spongy pedal, a burning smell, or the car pulling to one side, those are separate concerns worth mentioning to the tow driver. A brake pedal that goes soft after heavy use can indicate overheated rotors or, in worse cases, compromised brake fluid. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I put my car in park to stop a runaway accelerator?**
A: Do not slam it into park at speed. Park locks the drivetrain mechanically and can snap the parking pawl, damage the transmission, or cause the rear wheels to lock. Shift to neutral first, stop the car, then move to park.

**Q: What causes a throttle to stick on a modern car?**
A: The most common causes are a floor mat riding up and pinning the pedal, a sticky or corroded throttle body, or a fault in the electronic throttle control system, including a bad throttle position sensor or wiring issue.

**Q: Will my insurance cover towing after a stuck accelerator if I did not crash?**
A: If you have roadside assistance or towing coverage on your policy, yes. This is a mechanical breakdown, not a collision claim, so it goes through your roadside or comprehensive coverage, not liability. Check your policy or call your insurer before you call the tow truck.

**Q: How much will it cost to fix a stuck accelerator?**
A: If the cause is a floor mat or debris, nothing beyond removing it. A throttle body cleaning runs $100 to $200 at most shops. Replacing a throttle body or electronic throttle control module typically runs $300 to $700 in parts and labor depending on the make and model.

**Q: Can I drive slowly to a shop after a stuck throttle if the car seems fine now?**
A: No. If the throttle stuck once, the underlying cause is still there. Drive-by-wire faults in particular can re-trigger without warning. The risk of a second stuck-throttle event in traffic is not worth the money you save on a tow.
