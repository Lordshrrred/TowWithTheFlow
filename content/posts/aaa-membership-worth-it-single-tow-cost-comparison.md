> **Quick Answer:** A single tow without coverage typically runs $100 to $200 for a local haul. AAA Classic membership costs around $60 to $75 per year. If you pay out of pocket even once, you've likely covered more than a year of membership. But whether it's worth it depends on what else you'd actually use, and whether one tow is really all you're comparing.

## Is One Tow Enough to Justify an AAA Membership?

Usually, yes. A standard local tow (5 to 7 miles, wheel-lift truck) runs $100 to $175 in most U.S. cities. AAA Classic membership runs roughly $60 to $75 per year for the primary member. That means a single tow out of pocket costs more than the membership itself. The math favors joining even if you only break down once every couple of years.

The catch is that "one tow" rarely stays one tow. Batteries die. Tires blow. You lock your keys in the car. AAA Classic covers up to four service calls per year, which changes the value calculation fast.

## What Does AAA Actually Cover, and What Are the Limits?

AAA Classic covers towing up to 5 miles per call. If your shop is 10 miles away, you pay the difference on the extra mileage, typically $3 to $6 per mile beyond the limit. For longer distances, you need AAA Plus (covers up to 100 miles) or AAA Premier (covers up to 200 miles).

Plus membership runs around $100 to $115 per year. Premier runs $120 to $165. If you regularly drive long stretches on rural highways or your nearest dealer is far from where you typically break down, the upgrade pays for itself quickly. A single 50-mile flatbed tow without coverage can run $250 to $400 or more depending on the market. [Flatbed towing in markets like Portland](https://towwiththeflow.com/towing-cost-portland-oregon-flatbed/) and Los Angeles consistently comes in at the higher end of that range.

## What Does a Single Tow Cost Without Any Coverage?

Out-of-pocket towing in most U.S. cities follows a hook-up fee plus a per-mile rate. Expect a $75 to $100 hook-up fee and $3 to $7 per mile after that. A 5-mile tow might cost $90 to $130. A 20-mile tow can run $150 to $240. After-hours calls add another $25 to $75 in most markets.

If you have no insurance roadside rider and no AAA, you're calling an independent tow company cold and paying whatever they quote. You have limited leverage, especially if you're stranded at night or on a highway. Understanding [whether you can negotiate that price after a breakdown](https://towwiththeflow.com/can-you-negotiate-tow-truck-price-after-breakdown/) helps, but your options are narrower without a pre-arranged plan.

## How Does AAA Compare to Adding Roadside Coverage Through Insurance?

Your insurance company's roadside add-on typically costs $10 to $25 per year per vehicle and covers a tow to the nearest qualified shop. That sounds cheaper than AAA, but the coverage is thinner. Most insurance roadside plans have a dollar cap (often $50 to $100 per incident) rather than a guaranteed mileage coverage. Once you hit the cap, you pay the rest.

State Farm's roadside add-on, for example, has a per-incident dollar limit that may not cover a full tow in a high-cost city. GEICO's roadside assistance also has a [tow

## Who Should Skip AAA and Just Pay Per Tow?

If you drive under 5,000 miles a year, have a new car under full factory warranty (which includes roadside), and your insurance already includes a solid roadside rider, stacking AAA on top might not add much. New vehicles from Toyota, Honda, and most domestic brands come with 24-month or 36-month roadside assistance built into the warranty. You'd be doubling up.

Also consider: AAA benefits are tied to the primary member, not the car. If your partner or teenager drives a separate vehicle, their tows don't count against your four-per-year limit if they're listed as associate members, but associate membership adds another $30 to $50 per year. Factor that in before assuming the household math is simple.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is AAA worth it if I only need one tow a year?**
A: Almost certainly. A single local tow without coverage costs more than a year of AAA Classic membership. Unless you have a factory warranty with roadside or a strong insurance rider already active, joining covers the tow and gives you battery service, lockout help, and fuel delivery on top of it.

**Q: Can I join AAA after my car breaks down and use it immediately?**
A: No. AAA has a 72-hour waiting period for service on pre-existing situations. If your car is already broken down, you cannot join and immediately claim a tow on that breakdown. You'd pay out of pocket for that call and use the membership going forward.

**Q: Does AAA cover towing after a car accident?**
A: Yes, AAA covers towing after accidents in most situations, not just mechanical breakdowns. The tow must be from the accident scene to a shop or safe location, and your mileage tier still applies. For more on exactly what's covered, [does AAA cover towing after an accident](https://towwiththeflow.com/does-aaa-cover-towing-after-accident-or-only-breakdowns/) explains the specifics.

**Q: What if my tow is longer than 5 miles with AAA Classic?**
A: Classic covers the first 5 miles. Beyond that, you pay the tow company's per-mile rate directly, typically $3 to $6 per mile. If you regularly need longer tows, AAA Plus (100-mile coverage) is worth the extra $35 to $45 per year.

**Q: Does AAA membership cover other drivers in my household?**
A: The primary membership covers only the named member. Household members need associate memberships, which cost an additional $30 to $50 each per year. Each associate member gets the same service call coverage independently.
