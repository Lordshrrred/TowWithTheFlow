## How Much Will a Tow Actually Cost Me in San Diego With No Insurance?

A standard wheel-lift tow inside San Diego city limits runs $85 to $150, with the average short local haul landing around $115. Flatbed tows, which are required for AWD vehicles, most sports cars, and anything with serious damage, start around $125 and often hit $175 to $200 for a 5 to 10 mile pull.

San Diego tow companies charge a hookup fee (also called a gate or base fee) plus a per-mile rate. Most local operators charge $4 to $7 per mile after the first few. A tow from Mission Valley to a shop near Kearny Mesa, about 6 miles, will typically cost $130 to $160 all in.

Towing from a freeway adds a hazard or freeway response fee on top of the base rate. If you're stranded on I-5 near the I-805 merge in La Jolla or stalled on I-8 near the Hotel Circle interchange, expect a $25 to $50 surcharge on many operators' invoices.

For a detailed side-by-side look at local versus long-distance rates, see [Towing Cost in San Diego California Local vs Long Distance](/towing-cost-san-diego-california-local-vs-long-distance/).

## Does San Diego Have Freeway Service Patrols That Could Help Me For Free?

Yes. Caltrans operates the Freeway Service Patrol (FSP) on major San Diego corridors including I-5, I-8, I-15, I-805, and SR-94 during peak commute hours, typically 6 to 9 a.m. and 3 to 7 p.m. on weekdays. FSP trucks will push or tow your car off the freeway to the nearest safe exit at no charge.

This won't get your car to a mechanic, but it can save you the $150 freeway hookup bill. If FSP gets you to the Rosecrans Street off-ramp or the Mission Gorge Road exit, you then call a local tower for the shorter, cheaper haul to your shop.

Check the San Diego 511 app or dial 511 for real-time info on which corridors have active FSP coverage before you assume help is coming.

## What Are the After-Hours and Weekend Rates I Should Expect?

After 6 p.m. and on weekends, most San Diego tow companies add a 20 to 30 percent surcharge. That base $115 tow becomes $145 to $150 at 9 p.m. on a Saturday. Some operators are upfront about it; others bury it in the invoice.

The key rule: ask before the truck rolls. Request a total estimate including any after-hours fees, the hookup charge, and the per-mile rate. A reputable company will give you a number. If they won't commit to a ballpark, call someone else.

Neighborhoods like Pacific Beach, Ocean Beach, and North Park see heavy weekend call volume. Dispatch times can run 45 to 75 minutes on Friday and Saturday nights, which sometimes means a different company (with different pricing) ends up responding.

## What's the Fastest Way to Get a Tow in San Diego If I Have No Coverage?

Call two or three local San Diego towers simultaneously and take the first confirmed ETA at a price you have in writing. Apps like Urgently and Honk connect you to vetted local operators and display pricing upfront, which removes most of the guesswork.

If cost is the biggest concern right now, San Diego has no shortage of small independent operators who charge less than the big fleet companies. A company dispatching out of National City or El Cajon will often run 15 to 20 dollars cheaper than one headquartered closer to La Jolla or Del Mar.

If you need coverage long-term, AAA membership costs around $70 to $120 per year and covers multiple tows up to a set mileage limit. One paid tow without coverage often costs more than a full year of AAA Basic. ## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average tow truck cost in San Diego without insurance?**
A: Most local San Diego tows without insurance run $100 to $175 for the first 5 to 7 miles. Add a freeway hookup fee or after-hours surcharge and you can hit $200 easily.

**Q: Can I get a free tow on the freeway in San Diego?**
A: Yes, Caltrans Freeway Service Patrol operates on I-5, I-8, I-15, I-805, and SR-94 during weekday peak hours and will tow you to the nearest exit at no charge. It only covers the freeway, not to your mechanic.

**Q: How do I avoid getting overcharged by a tow company in San Diego?**
A: Get a written estimate with the hookup fee and per-mile rate before they touch your car. California law requires it. If a company won't give you a number before hooking up, don't let them.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive in San Diego?**
A: During daytime weekday hours, most operators arrive in 30 to 45 minutes. Weekend nights in high-traffic areas like Mission Valley or downtown can stretch to an hour or more.

**Q: Is it cheaper to get AAA than to pay for one tow in San Diego?**
A: Usually yes. A single out-of-pocket tow in San Diego often costs $125 to $175. AAA Basic membership starts around $70 per year and covers tows up to 5 miles per incident. If you break down more than once, the math gets even more lopsided.
