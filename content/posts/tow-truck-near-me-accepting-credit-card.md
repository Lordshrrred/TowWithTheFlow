> **Quick Answer:** Most tow truck companies accept credit cards, but not all drivers carry a reader or the company may be cash-only. Call ahead and ask directly before they dispatch. If you have roadside assistance through insurance, your card may never come out at all. Apps like Urgent.ly and Honk connect you to verified operators and handle payment digitally.

## What To Do

1. **Search Google Maps for "tow truck near me"** and call the top two or three results. Do not assume they take cards. Ask: "Do you accept Visa or Mastercard on-site?" Some smaller independent operators are cash-only or Square-only, and you do not want that surprise when the driver shows up.

2. **Try a digital dispatch app.** Urgent.ly, Honk, and BEGAS are legitimate platforms that let you book a tow, see the price upfront, and pay through the app with any major card or digital wallet. This is your best option if you are in an unfamiliar area and cannot vet local companies quickly.

3. **Check your roadside assistance coverage.** If you have a policy through your auto insurance, AAA, or a credit card benefit, you may owe nothing out of pocket at the scene. Your card company handles billing separately. Pull up your insurance app or the number on your card and call before you book a stranger off Google. See [Does Your Car Insurance Deductible Apply to Towing Costs?](/car-insurance-deductible-applies-to-towing-cost/) for what you might still owe.

4. **Use Venmo or Zelle as a backup.** If a local operator does not carry a card reader but you trust them, ask about digital payment. Many independent drivers will accept it. Get a text confirmation of the amount before they hook up your car.

5. **If you have no payment options at all**, call a friend or family member to share their card number over the phone so you can read it to the driver. Most drivers can manually key a card number into a Square or Stripe terminal. It is not ideal, but it works.

6. **Do not hand cash to someone who approached you unsolicited.** If a tow truck shows up uninvited after a breakdown or accident and pushes for cash only, that is a red flag. Predatory towing is real. You have the right to decline and call your own tow.

## What It Might Cost

A local tow within 5 to 10 miles typically runs $75 to $150 in most U.S. cities. Hook-up fees alone can be $50 to $75 before mileage. Longer hauls add $3 to $7 per mile on top of that.

If you are uninsured or your policy does not cover towing, expect to pay the full amount at time of service. Some companies charge a processing fee of 3 to 5 percent for card payments. Ask before you agree.

## Stay Safe

- Pull as far off the road as possible before you start making calls. Hazard lights on, engine off.
- Stay inside the vehicle with your seatbelt on if you are on a highway shoulder. Get out only if you smell smoke or fuel. [Car Died on Highway Shoulder: Is It Safe to Wait for a Tow?](/car-died-on-highway-shoulder-safe-to-wait-for-tow/) covers this in detail.
- Do not give your card number to a driver before the tow is complete and you have confirmed the final charge.
- Screenshot or write down the company name, driver name, and agreed price before the car leaves the ground.
- If a company will not confirm they accept cards before dispatch, hang up and call the next one. You are the customer. You get to ask.
- Do not leave your car unlocked when you step away to talk to the driver.
