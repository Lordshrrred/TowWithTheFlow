> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in Denver costs $75 to $150 for the first 5 miles, plus $3 to $5 per mile after that. Flatbed trucks run $25 to $50 more than wheel-lift. After-hours, accident scenes, and mountain pickups all push the price higher. Without insurance or a membership, expect to pay out of pocket on the spot.

## What Does a Tow Actually Cost in Denver Right Now?

For a standard local tow within Denver city limits, you're looking at $75 to $150 as a base, with per-mile charges of $3 to $5 kicking in after the first few miles. A breakdown near Colorado Boulevard on I-70 towed to a shop in Glendale will typically land between $100 and $160 all in. A longer haul from the I-25 and I-70 interchange (the "mousetrap") out to Lakewood or Aurora adds another $20 to $40. These are real-world numbers, not the best-case quotes you get before they've seen your car.

Flatbed towing costs more because it protects all four wheels off the ground. If you're driving an AWD or 4WD vehicle, a flatbed isn't optional. See the full breakdown by truck type in this guide to [towing cost in Denver Colorado flatbed vs wheel lift](/towing-cost-in-denver-colorado-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/).

## What Factors Push the Price Up in Denver?

Several things change the final number fast.

**Time of day.** After-hours and weekend calls add $25 to $75 on top of the base rate. Most companies define after-hours as anything after 8 or 9 p.m.

**Location.** A tow from a highway shoulder on I-25 near the Broadway exit is straightforward. A tow from a parking garage in LoDo or a tight alley off Colfax takes longer to rig and costs more. Mountain calls, anything west of C-470 heading toward Evergreen or Idaho Springs on US-6 or I-70, can cost $200 to $400 because the driver is logging significant mileage just getting to you.

**Condition of the vehicle.** Car in a ditch, on its side, or locked up solid (seized engine, locked brakes) requires winching. Winch fees run $50 to $150 on top of the tow.

**Accident scenes.** If police have dispatched a rotation tow from the city's vendor list, you don't get to negotiate the price on the spot. Those tows frequently run $150 to $250 and up, and the car goes to a storage lot, not your preferred shop. Storage fees in Denver typically start around $35 to $50 per day. ## Does My Insurance or AAA Cover the Tow?

It depends on your policy, but many drivers don't check until they're already stranded. If you have roadside assistance added to your auto policy, your insurer usually covers one tow per incident up to a mileage limit, often 10 to 25 miles. Anything over that limit comes out of your pocket at the per-mile rate.

AAA covers towing up to 5 miles on the Classic plan, 100 miles on Plus, and 200 miles on Premier. If your car breaks down near the I-225 and Parker Road interchange and your shop is in Centennial, do the math before you assume it's covered.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged?

Get a price before they hook up. Any reputable company will quote you a base rate and a per-mile rate over the phone before dispatch. If they won't, call someone else.

Ask specifically: base fee, per-mile rate, and whether there are hookup fees or fuel surcharges. Some Denver operators add a $15 to $30 fuel surcharge. That's not automatically a scam, but it should be disclosed upfront.

If you're on a highway and a tow truck shows up uninvited before you've called anyone, you are not required to use them. Predatory "bandit towers" do operate in Denver. You have the right to call your own company. The exception is if law enforcement has formally requested a tow, in which case the rotation tow has legal authority to move your vehicle.

Know where your car is going before they load it. The driver should tell you the destination and confirm before leaving. Once it's on the truck and delivered to a lot you didn't choose, getting it moved costs another tow.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a tow truck cost in Denver for a short local trip?**
A: A local tow of 5 miles or less in Denver typically runs $75 to $125. The base hookup fee covers the first few miles, and per-mile charges apply after that.

**Q: Do tow truck drivers in Denver take credit cards?**
A: Most do, but not all. Confirm payment method when you call for dispatch. Some independent operators are cash-only, especially late at night.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive in Denver?**
A: During normal hours, 30 to 45 minutes is typical inside the city. During snowstorms or rush hour on I-25 or I-70, wait times can stretch to 90 minutes or longer. For current expectations, see [tow truck response time Denver Colorado](/tow-truck-response-time-denver-colorado/).

**Q: What happens if I can't afford to pay for the tow after an accident?**
A: The tow company can legally hold your vehicle until payment is made. Storage fees accumulate daily. Contact your insurance company immediately and ask if they can pay the tow company directly.

**Q: Is towing more expensive in winter in Denver?**
A: Yes. Snow emergencies, icy roads, and increased call volume all drive prices up. Companies may charge surge pricing, and wait times extend significantly during major storms. Budget an extra $50 to $100 during winter events.
