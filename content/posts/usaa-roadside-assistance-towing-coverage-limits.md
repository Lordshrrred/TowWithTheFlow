> **Quick Answer:** USAA roadside assistance covers up to $100 per incident for towing with no annual usage limit. You get unlimited service calls per year, but each tow is capped at $100 reimbursement. Coverage includes 15 miles of towing, with additional miles at your expense beyond that distance.

## What To Do

1. **Check your USAA policy documents** for your specific coverage level. Some policies include enhanced roadside assistance with higher limits.

2. **Call USAA roadside assistance first** at 1-800-531-USAA (8722) before arranging private towing. Using their network keeps costs predictable.

3. **Ask the tow truck driver for the total distance** before they hook up your car. USAA covers the first 15 miles, so know what you'll pay beyond that.

4. **Keep all towing receipts** if you pay out of pocket. USAA will reimburse up to $100 per incident when you submit proper documentation.

5. **Get a written estimate** if the tow will exceed $100. You'll pay the difference, and knowing the total cost prevents surprises.

USAA's standard roadside assistance differs from competitors. While [State Farm roadside assistance towing limits](/state-farm-roadside-assistance-towing-limit-miles/) vary by coverage level, USAA keeps it simple with a flat $100 per incident cap.

The 15-mile coverage distance applies to each towing incident. If your car breaks down 30 miles from the nearest shop, you'll pay for the extra 15 miles at the tow company's standard rate, typically $3-7 per mile. On a rural interstate where the nearest authorized shop is 40 miles out, that extra 25 miles could cost you $75-175 on top of the base hook-up fee, which itself often runs $50-75 before any mileage is counted.

Unlike some insurers, USAA doesn't limit how many times you can use roadside assistance per year. However, each incident still maxes out at $100 coverage. Multiple breakdowns mean multiple $100 limits, not one annual pool. If you break down twice in one month, you have $100 available each time, not $100 split between both incidents.

One detail many USAA members miss: the $100 cap covers the entire towing invoice, including the hook-up fee. Hook-up fees alone can run $50-75, which means the mileage coverage you actually get from your remaining balance may only stretch 4-8 miles in practice. Always confirm with the dispatcher what the base fee is before the truck rolls, so you know exactly what distance the remaining coverage will buy you.

Also worth knowing: reimbursement claims require you to submit receipts within a specific window, typically 60-90 days from the incident. Missing that deadline means you absorb the full cost yourself. Photograph your receipt immediately and submit it through the USAA mobile app while you're still at the scene if possible.

## What It Might Cost

Beyond USAA's $100 coverage, expect these costs:

- **Extra mileage**: $3-7 per mile beyond 15 miles
- **Specialty towing**: Motorcycles, RVs, or luxury vehicles often exceed the $100 limit
- **After-hours fees**: 20-50% surcharge for nights, weekends, holidays
- **Hook-up fees**: $50-75 base charge that counts against your $100 limit before mileage is factored in
- **Winching or recovery**: If your vehicle is off-road, in a ditch, or stuck in mud, expect an additional $75-150 recovery fee that the standard tow coverage may not fully address

A typical 25-mile tow costs $120-180 total. USAA covers $100, leaving you with $20-80 out of pocket. A longer 40-mile tow to a dealership on a Sunday night, factoring in an after-hours surcharge, can easily hit $275-350, leaving you responsible for $175-250.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does USAA roadside assistance cover flatbed towing, or only standard tow trucks?**
A: USAA's $100 reimbursement applies to the towing invoice regardless of truck type, so flatbed towing is covered up to the same $100 limit. The problem is flatbeds typically cost more, so you will hit the cap faster and pay more out of pocket.

**Q: Can I use a tow company that is not in USAA's network and still get reimbursed?**
A: Yes. If you arrange your own tow, pay the driver, and keep the itemized receipt, USAA will reimburse you up to $100 per incident when you submit the documentation. Using an out-of-network provider does not void your claim, but you take on the billing risk yourself.

**Q: Does the 15-mile limit reset if I need a second tow on the same breakdown, say from the roadside to a shop and then to another shop?**
A: No. USAA treats each dispatch call as a separate incident, so a second tow truck called for the same vehicle that same day would technically be a new incident with a fresh $100 limit. Confirm this with the USAA representative when you call, because dispatcher notes and timing can affect how claims are categorized.

## Stay Safe

- Call 911 first if you're in immediate danger
- Move to a safe location away from traffic before calling USAA
- Turn on hazard lights and use reflective triangles if available
- Wait inside your vehicle only if it's safely off the roadway
- Never accept rides from strangers while waiting for the tow truck

USAA's coverage works nationwide, similar to how [GEICO roadside assistance towing limits](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-limits-how-many-miles/) operate across state lines. The $100 limit and 15-mile distance apply whether you break down in your home state or across the country.
