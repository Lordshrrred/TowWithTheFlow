> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in Austin typically runs $100 to $200 for a hook-up fee plus the first few miles. Past that, expect roughly $4 to $7 per additional mile. Flatbeds, nights, weekends, and highway recoveries on I-35 or MoPac push the total higher. Always get the full price quoted before the driver hooks up your car.

## How Much Does a Tow Truck Cost in Austin Texas?

Most Austin drivers pay between $100 and $200 for a standard local tow, and that number can climb past $300 for a long haul or a complicated recovery. Rates vary by company, so treat these as working ranges, not guarantees.

Here is how the bill usually breaks down:

- **Hook-up fee:** $60 to $125, often including the first 5 to 10 miles.
- **Per-mile rate after that:** roughly $4 to $7 per mile.
- **Flatbed surcharge:** some companies add $25 to $75 over a wheel-lift tow.
- **After-hours or holiday surcharge:** often $30 to $100 on top.
- **Winching or recovery:** extra if you are in a ditch, off an embankment, or wedged in a tight spot.

A tow from a Mueller driveway to a shop in Hyde Park is a short, cheap job. A tow from Dripping Springs to a dealership in North Austin is not. For a deeper per-scenario breakdown, see [towing cost in Austin Texas](/towing-cost-austin-texas/).

## What Makes a Tow More Expensive on I-35 or MoPac?

Highway location, traffic, and time of day are the biggest price multipliers in Austin. I-35 through downtown is one of the most congested stretches in Texas, and a driver stuck in that traffic is losing paid hours getting to you.

What bumps the price:

1. **Rush hour.** A breakdown on MoPac (Loop 1) near the 2222 interchange at 5 p.m. costs more than the same breakdown at 10 a.m.
2. **Night and weekend calls.** The Sixth Street and Rainey Street crowds mean demand spikes Friday and Saturday night. Dispatchers know it. See [how much emergency towing costs on weekends](/how-much-does-emergency-towing-cost-on-weekends/) for what to expect.
3. **Toll roads.** SH 130, SH 45, and SH 71 east toward the airport have their own access rules. Read [what to do if you break down on a toll road](/car-broke-down-on-toll-road-who-do-i-call/) before you call anyone.
4. **Vehicle type.** AWD cars, lowered cars, trucks, and anything with a dead transmission usually need a flatbed.

## Do I Need a Flatbed or a Regular Tow Truck?

If your car is all-wheel drive, has a failed transmission, has flat tires on the drive wheels, or sits low to the ground, you need a flatbed. A wheel-lift truck drags two wheels on the pavement, and on the wrong car that causes real damage.

Tell the dispatcher your drivetrain up front. "Front-wheel drive Corolla" and "AWD Subaru" get very different trucks. If you are unsure, use [this flatbed checklist](/how-to-know-if-my-car-needs-a-flatbed-tow/) before you call.

Paying the extra $25 to $75 for a flatbed is cheaper than a $2,000 transmission repair caused by the wrong truck.

Ask for the total price, in writing or by text, before the driver touches your car. That one habit prevents most tow disputes.

Questions to ask the dispatcher:

- What is the hook-up fee, and how many miles does it include?
- What is the per-mile rate after that?
- Are there after-hours, flatbed, or fuel surcharges?
- What is the all-in estimate to my destination?
- Do you take cards, and is there a card fee?

Be careful with unsolicited trucks that pull up on I-35 or Highway 183 shoulders. Cars on the shoulder get targeted by drivers nobody dispatched. If you did not call them, do not let them hook up. Texas regulates towing, and a legitimate operator will show a company name on the truck and give you an itemized receipt.

## What If I Don't Have Insurance or Roadside Assistance in Austin?

You pay out of pocket, usually $100 to $250 for a local tow, and the tow company will typically want payment when the car is dropped. Have a card ready. Some shops release a car only after the tow bill is settled.

Ways to cut the cost:

- Tow to a shop close to you instead of your preferred mechanic across town. You can move the car later, or have the shop arrange it.
- Check whether your credit card, auto loan, or phone plan includes roadside benefits. Many people already have coverage and never use it.
- Call two or three companies. Prices vary more than people expect.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow truck per mile in Austin?**
A: Most Austin companies charge about $4 to $7 per mile after the included distance in the hook-up fee. Long hauls to San Antonio or Waco are often quoted as a flat rate instead.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow company directly or use roadside assistance?**
A: If you have AAA, an insurance add-on, or a credit card benefit, roadside assistance is usually free up to a mileage cap. Without coverage, calling a local company directly is the normal route.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take to arrive in Austin?**
A: Expect 30 to 60 minutes in normal conditions. Rush hour on I-35 or a weekend night can stretch that past an hour, so ask the dispatcher for an honest ETA.

**Q: Can I ride in the tow truck?**
A: Usually yes, one passenger fits in most cabs. Confirm with the dispatcher, since policies vary by company and some trucks only seat the driver.

**Q: Do tow companies in Austin take credit cards?**
A: Most do, and some charge a processing fee. Ask when you call so you are not stuck at the shop with a bill and no way to pay it.
