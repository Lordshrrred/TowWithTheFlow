> **Quick Answer:** Call 911 first, especially if you're on a highway or if anyone is hurt. Then call a tow truck. A car missing a wheel cannot be driven, period. The wheel hub, brake rotor, and sometimes the CV axle are likely damaged. You need a flatbed tow to a shop. Check your insurance or AAA for towing coverage before you pay out of pocket.

## What Do I Do the Moment My Wheel Falls Off While Driving?

Grip the steering wheel firmly, take your foot off the gas, and steer to the shoulder. Do not brake hard. The corner of the car where the wheel came off will drop and drag, and the vehicle will pull hard in that direction. Keep your hands steady and let the car slow naturally while you guide it as far off the road as possible.

Once stopped, turn on your hazard lights immediately. If you have road flares or reflective triangles, place them 100 to 200 feet behind the car. Get every passenger out of the vehicle and away from traffic, over the guardrail if there is one. A stopped car on the shoulder gets struck by other vehicles more often than most people expect.

## Who Should I Call After My Wheel Came Off?

Call 911 first if you are on a highway, an active travel lane, or anyone in the vehicle is injured. Police can block traffic and keep the scene safe. A loose wheel is also a road hazard to other drivers, and officers can help locate it before it causes a second crash.

After 911, call a tow truck. Your car is not driveable. The wheel stud pattern, the lug nuts, the hub, and often the brake rotor are compromised or destroyed. In many cases the CV axle snaps when the wheel separates, especially on front-wheel-drive vehicles. That means the car needs a flatbed, not a wheel-lift tow. Tell the dispatcher exactly what happened so they send the right equipment.

If you have AAA or roadside assistance through your insurance, call that number next. [Your comprehensive or roadside coverage may pay the tow cost entirely](/does-comprehensive-insurance-cover-towing-cost/), which can save you $100 or more depending on distance.

## Is It Safe to Wait in the Car for a Tow?

No, not if you are on a highway or high-speed road. Get out of the car on the passenger side, away from traffic, and wait behind the guardrail or up a bank if there is one. If you are on a low-speed surface street and completely off the travel lane, staying in the car with your seatbelt on is acceptable. Use your judgment based on how exposed the car is.

Do not attempt to reattach the wheel yourself on the roadside. Even if you can find it, you likely do not have all five lug nuts, and the hub flange may be bent or sheared. Driving on an improperly secured wheel can cause it to separate again at speed, which is how this situation becomes fatal.

For more on how long it is safe to leave a disabled vehicle in place while you wait, read [how long you can leave a broken-down car on the highway](/how-long-can-you-leave-broken-down-car-on-highway/).

## Why Did My Wheel Fall Off and What

Damage Am I Looking At?

Wheels fall off for a handful of reasons: lug nuts that were never properly torqued after a tire rotation or wheel installation, a worn or failed wheel bearing that allowed the hub to separate, or a broken axle that allowed the wheel assembly to come free. The most common cause after a recent tire service is undertorqued lug nuts. The correct torque for most passenger cars is between 80 and 120 foot-pounds, and it should be checked with a torque wrench, not just an impact gun.

Damage typically includes the wheel itself, the brake rotor, and the wheel hub assembly. If the CV axle snapped, that is an additional repair. You are looking at $800 to $2,500 in parts and labor at minimum, more if the knuckle or control arm took impact damage from the car dragging on the road.

If the wheel was recently serviced by a shop, document everything with photos before the tow. You may have a legitimate claim against that shop. This is also worth a conversation with your insurance adjuster. [Your insurer may cover some or all of the towing costs](/car-accident-towing-fee-who-pays-insurance/) depending on how the damage is classified.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive my car with a missing wheel to get it off the road?**
A: No. Even moving it 20 feet will destroy the brake rotor, the hub, and possibly the knuckle. The metal scraping the pavement can also throw sparks and cause a fire. Stay put and wait for a flatbed.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover a tow if my wheel fell off?**
A: If you have roadside assistance or comprehensive coverage, yes, most policies will cover the tow. Call your insurer directly and ask before you pay a tow company out of pocket. [See what Progressive typically pays for towing](/progressive-insurance-covers-towing-how-much/) as one example of how coverage limits work.

**Q: Who is liable if my wheel fell off after a recent tire rotation?**
A: The shop that last touched the wheels is likely liable if improper torque caused the failure. Get a police report, photograph everything at the scene, keep the failed wheel and all lug nuts you can find, and contact the shop before authorizing repairs elsewhere.

**Q: What type of tow truck do I need when a wheel has fallen off?**
A: You need a flatbed rollback, not a standard wheel-lift truck. A wheel-lift tow requires all four wheels to roll or at least be present. A flatbed loads the car onto the deck without rolling it at all. Be specific when you call for a tow.

**Q: How long will I be waiting on the side of the road for a tow truck?**
A: On a busy highway during business hours, 30 to 60 minutes is typical. At night or in a rural area, plan for 60 to 90 minutes or longer. Tell the dispatcher your exact location using mile markers or cross streets, not just a general highway name, to speed up dispatch.
