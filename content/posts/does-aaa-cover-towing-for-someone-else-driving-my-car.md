> **Quick Answer:** Usually not, if you are not in the vehicle. Standard AAA coverage follows the member, not the car. If a friend breaks down in your car and you are not riding along, the driver generally cannot use your membership unless they are also a member, though some plans and regional clubs differ. Call your local AAA club's member line to confirm before you pay out of pocket.

## Does AAA Cover Towing When Someone Else Is Driving My Car?

Generally no, because AAA roadside service is tied to the person who holds the membership, not the license plate. If you are a passenger in your own car, you are covered. If a friend, kid, or coworker is driving it alone, they are not the member and the service request can be denied.

Two things change the answer. First, regional AAA clubs run their own rules, so the fine print varies by club. Second, some plans, such as AAA Plus or Premier, include family or associate memberships, and the driver may be one of them. Check the member card or the app before assuming you are out of luck.

## What Can I Do If My Friend Is Stranded in My Car?

Get yourself added to the call, because the member can often request service on the driver's behalf. Here is the fastest sequence:

1. The driver gets to a safe spot and turns on hazards. If they are on a highway shoulder, they should exit on the passenger side and stand well away from traffic.
2. You call AAA from your own phone or open the app, signed in as the member.
3. Give the dispatcher the driver's exact location. A dropped pin from the driver's phone beats a verbal description every time.
4. Ask whether service can be authorized for a vehicle you are not in. Some clubs will do it, some require the member present.
5. If AAA says no, ask what it would cost to add the driver as an associate member on the spot. Many clubs allow this and activate coverage fast, though a waiting period can apply to some service types.

Keep the driver's phone charged and the call going until the truck arrives.

## What If AAA Won't Cover It and the Driver Needs a Tow Now?

## Does It Matter Whose Name the Car Is Registered In?

Less than people think, but it can matter. AAA is built around the person, so the car's registration usually is not the deciding factor. That said, a car registered to someone else, such as a parent or a leasing company, can complicate a claim. ## Could My Car Insurance Cover the Tow Instead?

Yes, and this is the backup most people forget. If your auto policy includes roadside assistance or towing coverage, it often follows the car, so a permitted driver may be covered even when AAA is not. Look at your declarations page for a roadside or towing line, or add it. Towing limits on these policies are often modest, so confirm the mileage cap before the truck loads the car.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can my teenager use my AAA membership to get a tow in my car?**
A: Only if they are listed as an associate member on your plan or you are in the car. Otherwise the dispatcher may refuse service. Adding a teen driver as an associate is usually cheap and worth doing.

**Q: If I call AAA for the driver, will they send a truck?**
A: Sometimes. Some clubs let the member authorize service remotely, others require the member to be present. Call and ask directly, since policies vary by region.

**Q: Can the driver pay and get reimbursed by AAA later?**
A: Possibly, if you are the member and the club allows reimbursement for non-AAA tows. Keep the itemized receipt and submit it quickly. Reimbursement is capped and not guaranteed.

**Q: Does AAA cover my car if I am a passenger but a friend is driving?**
A: Yes, in most cases. When the member is in the vehicle, service typically applies regardless of who is behind the wheel.

**Q: What if the car is a rental or borrowed from a friend, and I'm the AAA member driving it?**
A: As the member you are usually covered even in a borrowed or rental vehicle, since coverage follows you. Rental agreements sometimes require you to use their roadside line first, so check the contract.
