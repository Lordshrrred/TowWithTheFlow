> **Quick Answer:** A local tow in Portland, typically under 10 to 15 miles, usually runs $100 to $250 as a hook-up fee plus a few miles. Long distance towing, like Portland to Salem or Eugene, is billed per mile on top of the hook-up and often lands between $300 and $800 or more. Prices vary by company, vehicle, and time of day, so get a total quote before you agree.

## What Counts as a Local Tow vs a Long Distance Tow in Portland?

Most Portland tow companies treat anything inside the metro area, roughly 10 to 15 miles, as local. Beyond that, you're quoted by the mile.

A breakdown on I-84 near the Lloyd District, towed to a shop in Southeast Portland, is local. A breakdown on I-5 near Wilsonville, towed to your mechanic in Gresham, is stretching the line. Portland to Salem, Vancouver WA, or Eugene is firmly long distance. Crossing the Columbia into Washington also adds complications, so confirm the company is licensed to haul across state lines.

## How Much Does a Local Tow Cost in Portland?

Expect roughly $100 to $250 for a standard local tow, covering the hook-up fee and the first few miles. Extra miles past the included distance typically add a few dollars each.

Things that push a local price up:

- **Time of day.** Nights, weekends, and holidays often carry a surcharge.
- **Vehicle type.** AWD cars, EVs, lowered cars, and trucks usually need a flatbed, which costs more than a wheel-lift. See [flatbed vs wheel lift pricing in Portland](/towing-cost-portland-oregon-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/).
- **Location.** A car stuck on a steep stretch of Highway 26 near the Sunset Tunnel or in a parking structure takes more work than a flat street.
- **Weather.** Portland's wet season slows traffic and spikes demand. More in [towing cost in rainy weather](/towing-cost-portland-oregon-rainy-weather/).

For a fuller local breakdown, read [towing cost in Portland Oregon](/towing-cost-portland-oregon/).

## How Much Does Long Distance Towing Cost From Portland?

Long distance towing costs a hook-up fee plus a per-mile rate for every mile, and a typical Portland trip of 50 to 100 miles lands around $300 to $800 or more. Per-mile rates vary widely by company and vehicle, so treat any number as a ballpark.

Here's how the math usually works. Take the hook-up fee, add the loaded miles at the per-mile rate, then add any surcharges for after-hours, a flatbed, or tolls and fees. Some companies also charge for the deadhead, the empty miles the driver travels to reach you, if you're far from their yard.

On a 60-mile haul like Portland to Salem down I-5, the per-mile portion dominates the bill. Per-mile math is covered in [towing cost per mile in Portland](/towing-cost-in-portland-oregon-per-mile-rate/).

A long tow makes sense when:

- You're stranded far from home and the repair will be done there.
- The car is under a dealer warranty and the nearest authorized shop is far away.
- Your roadside coverage pays for the long tow but not a second one later.

Paying for two separate tows, one local to a shop and a second long haul afterward, costs more than one direct tow. Decide on the destination before the truck loads your car.

Many roadside plans cap towing at a set mileage, so check yours first. If you're covered, your cost may be zero up to the limit. If you're not, see [towing cost in Portland without roadside assistance](/towing-cost-in-portland-oregon-without-roadside-assistance/).

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged on a Portland Tow?

Ask for a total price in writing or by text before the truck hooks up, not just a per-mile rate. Then confirm what it includes.

Ask these four questions:

1. What is the total, all fees included?
2. Is that price for a flatbed or wheel-lift?
3. Does it change if the drop-off address changes?
4. How do you take payment?

Be careful if you're stuck on a freeway shoulder, such as I-5 or I-205. Unsolicited trucks that show up without being called are a red flag. Only get into the truck you or your insurer dispatched, and check the company name on the door.

If a bill doesn't match the quote, you have options. See [how to dispute a towing charge](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-if-overcharged/).

## Common Questions

**Q: How far is a local tow in Portland before it becomes long distance?**
A: Most companies treat roughly 10 to 15 miles as local. Past that, you're billed per mile, so ask each company where its cutoff falls.

**Q: Is it cheaper to tow my car myself with a rental trailer?**
A: Sometimes, for a long haul, but you need a vehicle rated to pull your car's weight and a way to load a dead car safely. For most breakdowns, a professional flatbed is safer and faster.

**Q: Does my car insurance cover a long distance tow?**
A: Only if you have roadside or towing coverage, and most policies cap the mileage or dollar amount. Read the limit before you call. See [how far insurance will tow your car](/how-far-will-insurance-tow-my-car-after-a-breakdown/).

**Q: Do tow trucks charge more at night or on weekends in Portland?**
A: Many do. After-hours and holiday surcharges are common, so ask for the all-in price upfront. Details in [emergency towing on weekends](/how-much-does-emergency-towing-cost-on-weekends/).

**Q: Can any tow company haul my car from Portland to Washington?**
A: No. Crossing state lines requires the right licensing and insurance, so confirm the company is authorized before you agree to a Portland to Vancouver WA haul.
