> **Quick Answer:** Allstate roadside assistance typically costs $15-25 per year but has longer wait times and restrictions. Calling a tow truck directly costs $100-200 but gets faster service and no coverage limits. Choose based on your immediate needs versus long-term savings.

## What To Do

1. **Check your current situation first**
   - Are you in immediate danger or safe location?
   - Do you already have Allstate roadside coverage active?
   - How urgent is your need for service?

2. **Use Allstate roadside if you have it and can wait**
   - Call 1-800-ALLSTATE (1-800-255-7828)
   - Have your policy number ready
   - Expect 30-90 minute response times in most areas, though rural calls can stretch past two hours
   - Limited to certain service types and distances
   - Coverage typically includes towing, battery jump-start, flat tire change, lockout service, and fuel delivery up to 2 gallons

3. **Call a local tow truck for faster service**
   - Search "tow truck near me" or call 411
   - Get price quote upfront before agreeing, and confirm whether the rate is flat or per-mile
   - Typical arrival time: 15-45 minutes in urban areas
   - No restrictions on distance or service type
   - Independent operators can tow to any shop you choose, not just the nearest facility

4. **Know Allstate's limitations**
   - Usually covers 4 service calls per year, after which you pay out of pocket at market rates
   - Towing limited to nearest qualified facility, often just 5-15 miles, which may not be your preferred mechanic
   - May not cover luxury vehicles, modified cars, or vehicles over a certain gross weight like heavy-duty trucks or RVs
   - Won't work if policy has lapsed, even by a day
   - Coverage does not always extend to rental cars or vehicles you borrow, so confirm before you assume

## What It Might Cost

**Allstate Roadside:**
- Annual fee: $15-25 added to your existing policy
- No per-incident cost within your annual call limit
- Overages charged at market rates, which can hit $150 or more for a single tow

**Direct Tow Truck:**
- Basic tow: $100-200 for a local haul, typically up to 5-10 miles
- Long distance: $3-5 per mile after the base charge, so a 50-mile tow can easily run $250-350
- Emergency/after-hours: 25-50% surcharge on nights, weekends, and holidays
- Payment required immediately, usually cash or card on the spot
- Some operators charge a separate hookup fee of $35-75 on top of mileage

**Where Allstate wins:** If you break down twice or more in a year, the annual fee pays for itself on the first call alone. The math is straightforward.

**Where going direct wins:** If you need a specific shop, need a long tow, or need a truck in under 30 minutes, paying out of pocket gives you full control.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I use Allstate roadside assistance if I'm driving someone else's car?**
A: Allstate roadside coverage is typically tied to the insured vehicle, not the driver. If you are in a vehicle not listed on your Allstate policy, you generally will not have coverage, so calling a local tow truck directly is your best option in that scenario.

**Q: What if Allstate says the wait is over 90 minutes, can I just call a tow truck and get reimbursed?**
A: Allstate does not have a standard reimbursement policy for self-arranged tows simply because wait times are long. Some policyholders have had partial reimbursements approved by escalating to a supervisor, but it is not guaranteed. Your best bet is to ask the dispatcher directly before you hang up and arrange your own service.

**Q: Does Allstate roadside assistance cover a dead battery replacement, or just a jump-start?**
A: Allstate will send a technician to jump-start your battery at no extra charge within your covered calls. If the battery needs to be replaced outright, the labor may be covered but you will pay for the battery itself at whatever rate the service provider charges, which typically runs $100-200 depending on the vehicle.

## Stay Safe

- Never stand behind your vehicle while waiting
- Turn on hazard lights and use road flares if available
- Stay inside vehicle on busy highways
- Move to passenger side if traffic is heavy
- Keep doors locked until verified help arrives
- Have backup plan if first option fails
