> **Quick Answer:** A standard tow in Phoenix during summer runs $75 to $150 for the first 5 miles, then $3 to $6 per mile after that. Flatbed rates and after-hours calls run higher, often $150 to $250 base. Heat-related demand surges in June through September, so expect premium pricing and longer wait times. Call your roadside assistance first, then a private tow company if coverage is maxed out.

## What To Do

1. **Get off the road completely.** Pull onto the shoulder as far right as possible, or into a parking lot if you can reach one. Phoenix surface streets and freeways like the I-10 and Loop 101 have high-speed traffic. A few extra feet between you and traffic lanes matters.

2. **Turn on your hazards immediately.** Do this before you do anything else, even before you call for help.

3. **Do not stay in the car if it is overheating badly or smoking.** Summer asphalt in Phoenix can exceed 150 degrees Fahrenheit. Standing outside is brutal, but a car fire is worse. If you see steam or [smoke coming from under the hood](/car-smoking-under-hood-safe-to-drive-or-tow/), get out and move away from the vehicle.

4. **Find shade if you exit the vehicle.** Sound wall, overpass, or a nearby building. Heat stroke can happen fast when temperatures are above 110 degrees. Bring your water bottle.

5. **Call your insurance roadside assistance first.** Check your policy before you dial a tow company. Many policies cover towing up to a set distance at no out-of-pocket cost. If you are not sure what your plan covers, the [GEICO roadside assistance towing limits](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-limits-how-many-miles/) page is a useful reference, and similar breakdowns exist for other major carriers.

6. **If you are uninsured or your coverage is exhausted, call a private tow company.** Get a quote over the phone before you agree to anything. Ask specifically: what is the hookup fee, what is the per-mile rate, and are there after-hours or heat-surge surcharges. Some Phoenix operators add a fuel surcharge in summer.

7. **Know where you want the car towed before the truck arrives.** Tow companies charge by the mile. Towing to the nearest shop is cheaper than towing across the Valley. Have a destination in mind.

8. **If you broke down on a freeway, call ADOT's Freeway Service Patrol.** In Maricopa County, Arizona Department of Transportation runs a free assist patrol on major freeways during peak hours. They can push you to safety or provide a free short tow to the nearest exit. The number is 511 or you can flag them down.

## What It Might Cost

| Scenario | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|
| Base hookup fee (private tow) | $75 to $125 |
| First 5 miles included | Usually yes |
| Per mile after that | $3 to $6 |
| Flatbed (required for AWD, low clearance) | Add $25 to $50 |
| After-hours call (midnight to 6 a.m.) | Add $50 to $100 |
| Cross-Valley tow (20+ miles) | $150 to $300 |
| ADOT Freeway Service Patrol | Free (limited hours, major freeways only) |

Phoenix towing prices are not regulated by the city, so rates vary. Companies operating near the I-17, US-60, and the Loop 202 know drivers are stranded and have limited options. Getting a quote before you agree protects you.

If your tow ends up costing more than expected, your insurance may reimburse part of it even if you did not use their dispatch line. Check with your carrier. The [car insurance deductible and towing costs](/car-insurance-deductible-applies-to-towing-cost/) article explains how reimbursement usually works.

## Stay Safe

- Stay hydrated. Keep at least one bottle of water in the car year-round if you drive in Phoenix.
- Do not sit in a parked car with the engine off in direct sun. Interior temps can hit 160 degrees within minutes.
- If you have children or pets in the vehicle, prioritize getting them out into shade first.
- Keep your phone charged above 20 percent before driving long stretches. A dead phone in Phoenix summer heat is a genuine emergency.
- If the car overheated, do not add cold water to a hot radiator. Wait at least 30 minutes after the engine cools before opening the cap.
- Watch for fast-moving traffic if you must exit the vehicle on a freeway shoulder. Face oncoming traffic so you can see and react.
