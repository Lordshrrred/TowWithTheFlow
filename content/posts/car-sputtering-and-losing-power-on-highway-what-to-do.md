> **Quick Answer:** Turn on your hazards, take your foot off the gas, and steer toward the right shoulder while you still have momentum. Don't brake hard. Stop as far from traffic as you can, stay buckled in or get out on the passenger side if it's unsafe, and call for a tow. A sputtering engine that cuts out on the highway is not something to restart and gamble on.

## What Do I Do Right Now If My Car Is Sputtering and Losing Power on the Highway?

Turn on your hazard lights immediately, ease off the accelerator, and move toward the right shoulder before the engine quits. Your power steering and brake booster may get heavier once the engine dies, but you still have both. Just firm up your grip.

Here's the order:

1. **Hazards on.** Do it first so drivers behind you read the slowdown.
2. **Lift off the gas gradually.** Slamming the brakes invites a rear-end hit.
3. **Signal and coast right.** Use your momentum. A car at 60 mph can roll a long way.
4. **Shift to neutral if the engine dies** so you can keep coasting without fighting it.
5. **Stop as far right as you can.** Turn your wheels away from the road, set the parking brake.

If you're stuck in a travel lane with no shoulder, keep the hazards on, stay belted, and call 911. Getting out into live traffic is the bigger risk. If you're in the left lane, [this guide on a breakdown in the left lane](/car-broke-down-in-left-lane-of-highway-what-to-do/) covers the crossing-traffic problem.

## Why Is My Car Sputtering and Losing Power at Highway Speed?

The most common causes are fuel starvation, a clogged fuel filter, a failing fuel pump, or a bad ignition component like a coil pack. Highway speed demands the most fuel flow, which is why a weak pump or clogged filter shows up there first.

What your car is doing tells you a lot:

- **Sputters when you press the gas, smooths out when you lift:** Classic fuel delivery problem. Suspect the fuel pump or filter.
- **Jerks and the check engine light flashes:** An active misfire. A flashing light means raw fuel is hitting the catalytic converter. Slow down and stop soon.
- **Loses power on hills or while passing:** Clogged fuel filter or failing pump. Also check whether the gas gauge is accurate.
- **Sputters, then dies with a battery light or dim dash:** Possible [alternator failure](/alternator-died-while-driving-symptoms-what-happens-next/). The engine runs off the battery until it drains.
- **Hesitates and smells like rotten eggs:** A clogged catalytic converter choking exhaust flow.

On modern cars, a failing crankshaft position sensor can also cause sudden sputtering and stalling with no warning.

## Should I Try to Restart the Engine After It Sputters Out?

You can try one restart once you're safely stopped on the shoulder, but don't count on it, and don't keep driving if it fires back up. A restart that works usually means the underlying fault is still there and will repeat at the worst moment.

If it restarts, treat it as a short window, not a fix. Drive directly to the nearest exit and a safe place, not the next town. If it won't restart after a couple of tries, stop cranking. Repeated cranking can flood the engine and drain the battery you'll need for hazards.

## When Should I Call a Tow Instead of Limping Home?

Call a tow any time the engine won't restart, the check engine light is flashing, or the sputtering comes back within minutes. Driving a car that's actively misfiring or starving for fuel can wreck the catalytic converter, which turns a few-hundred-dollar repair into a four-figure one.

Call a tow if:

- The engine dies and stays dead.
- The check engine light is flashing, not steady.
- You smell fuel, rotten eggs, or burning.
- Power loss gets worse with each mile.

Look at your coverage before you pay out of pocket. Tell the dispatcher you're on a highway shoulder. That moves you up the priority list.

## How Do I Stay Safe While Waiting on the Shoulder?

Stay in the car with your seatbelt on unless there's a fire risk, a smell of fuel, or a real chance of being struck. Most shoulder fatalities happen to people standing outside their vehicle.

If you have to exit, use the passenger-side door and stand well away from the road, behind a guardrail if there is one. Keep hazards on. If you carry reflective triangles, place them 50 to 100 feet behind the car, but only if you can do it without stepping into traffic. If you feel unsafe at any point, call 911. Most state DOTs also run a 511 service for road conditions and incident reports.

## Common Questions

**Q: Why does my car sputter when I accelerate but run fine at idle?**
A: Acceleration demands more fuel and air than idling does. A weak fuel pump, clogged filter, or dirty fuel injectors can keep up at idle but fall behind under load. A failing ignition coil can do the same.

**Q: Can bad gas cause my car to sputter and lose power?**
A: Yes. Water-contaminated or very old fuel can cause sputtering, hesitation, and stalling, often right after a fill-up. If it started after fueling, tell the shop, since they may need to drain the tank.

**Q: Is it safe to keep driving if my car sputters but doesn't die?**
A: Not for long. A steady or flashing check engine light with sputtering means something is misfiring or starving. Get off the highway and to a shop or safe stop, and don't push through a long drive.

**Q: How much does it cost to fix a car that sputters and loses power?**
A: It depends on the cause. A fuel filter or spark plugs are usually modest repairs, while a fuel pump or catalytic converter costs more. A scan for stored codes is the fastest way to narrow it down.

**Q: Should I turn off the AC if the car is losing power?**
A: Yes, shut off the AC and any heavy electrical load. It reduces strain on the engine and alternator and can help you reach the shoulder safely.
