> **Quick Answer:** Most roadside assistance plans cover a flat tire by sending someone to swap your flat for your spare. If you're riding on the rim with no usable spare, they'll tow you instead. What they don't cover is tire repair or replacement itself. The service is the labor and the tow. The tire cost comes out of your pocket.

## Does Roadside Assistance Actually Cover a Flat Tire, or Just Towing?

Roadside assistance covers the service call, not the tire. A tech comes out, mounts your spare, and you drive away. That's the covered part. If your spare is also flat, missing, or you've driven far enough on the rim that the tire is destroyed, the plan pivots to a tow to the nearest shop.

The phrase "flat tire on the rim" usually means one of two things: you drove on a flat until the tire separated from the wheel, or the tire is so far gone it can't hold air. In either case, a spare swap isn't possible. A tow is the only option, and that's covered.

## What Happens When There's No Spare?

You get towed. AAA, GEICO, Progressive, State Farm roadside, and most credit card plans all include towing as a fallback when a tire change isn't possible. The tow distance covered depends on your specific plan tier. AAA Classic covers 5 miles. AAA Plus covers 100 miles. Most insurance-based plans cover 15 to 25 miles.

If you need to go farther than your plan covers, you pay the per-mile overage. For more detail on what different plans actually pay for a tow, see [AAA vs roadside assistance through insurance](/aaa-vs-roadside-assistance-through-insurance-which-is-better/) to compare coverage levels before you call.

Check your card or policy app right now for your tow limit before the truck gets there. Surprises at billing time are worse than the flat itself.

## Will They Replace the Tire or Just Move the Car?

They move the car. No roadside assistance plan replaces or purchases a tire for you. The tech or tow driver gets you to a shop. What happens next is between you and the shop.

If the rim itself is bent or cracked from driving on a flat, that's also not covered by roadside assistance. Rim damage is a separate repair, typically $75 to $300 for a steel wheel and $200 to $800 for an alloy, depending on whether it can be straightened or needs replacing. Your auto insurance may cover rim damage if the flat was caused by a road hazard and you have the right endorsement, but that's a separate claim from your roadside assistance call.

Pull as far right as you can and call roadside assistance immediately. Don't try to change the tire yourself on a live lane or a narrow shoulder on an interstate. The risk isn't worth it.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will roadside assistance cover a flat tire if I caused the damage myself?**
A: Yes. Roadside assistance doesn't ask how you got the flat. It's a non-fault service. Whether you hit a pothole, ran over a nail, or drove on a low tire too long, the service call is covered.

**Q: Does roadside assistance cover a flat tire on a rental car?**
A: It depends on the source of your coverage. Your personal auto policy's roadside add-on often extends to rentals. AAA membership covers you, not the car, so yes. Credit card coverage varies. Check before you rely on it. For more on rentals specifically, see [roadside assistance for rental cars: who pays](/roadside-assistance-rental-car-who-pays/).

**Q: What if the rim is bent and the car can't be driven even with a new tire?**
A: The tow is still covered by your roadside plan. Rim repair or replacement is not. You'll need to talk to your auto insurer about whether a road hazard claim applies.

**Q: How long will I wait for roadside assistance for a flat tire?**
A: Typical wait times run 30 to 90 minutes depending on your location and provider. Rural areas take longer. If you're in a high-traffic metro, most providers get there in under an hour during normal hours.

**Q: Can I call roadside assistance if my tire isn't completely flat, just very low?**
A: Yes. Most plans cover tire inflation as a service. If the tire holds air after being filled, you drive away. If it doesn't, they swap your spare or tow you. Either way, make the call.
