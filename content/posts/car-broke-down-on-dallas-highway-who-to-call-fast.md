> **Quick Answer:** Call 911 if you're in danger, then call a tow company or roadside assistance service. In Dallas, TxDOT's Highway Emergency Response Operators (HERO) patrol I-20, I-30, I-35E, I-635 (LBJ Freeway), and US-75 during peak hours and will stop to help for free. If HERO doesn't reach you, call your insurance roadside line, AAA, or a local tow company. Get off the travel lanes immediately.

## What Do I Do the Second My Car Dies on a Dallas Highway?

Get the car as far right as possible, onto the shoulder or beyond the white line. Turn on your hazard lights before you do anything else. Dallas highways like I-35E through downtown and the LBJ Freeway (I-635) carry heavy traffic at highway speeds, and standing in a travel lane is genuinely life-threatening.

Once you're stopped safely:

1. Stay in the car if you're on a high-speed road with no barrier. Exit through the passenger side if you must get out, and stand behind the guardrail.
2. Call 911 if your car is blocking a lane, if you're injured, or if you're on a bridge like the I-30 Margaret Hunt Hill connector where there's no shoulder room.
3. Call TxDOT HERO by dialing 511 and asking for HERO, or flag one down. These trucks are free and they patrol Dallas metro highways daily.
4. Call your roadside assistance or a tow company if HERO hasn't arrived in 20 minutes.

Set out road flares or triangle reflectors behind the vehicle if you have them. If it's dark, a flare 100 feet behind the car on I-35E southbound near Oak Cliff can be the difference between a bad night and a fatal one.

## Who Exactly Do I Call for a Tow in Dallas?

Call your insurance company's roadside assistance line first, because it may be free under your policy. If you have AAA, that's your next call. If neither applies, you need a local tow company.

TxDOT contracts specific towers for each zone on Dallas freeways. If a HERO operator responds, they can request a contracted tow truck for your location at a state-regulated rate, which tends to be lower than an out-of-the-blue call to a random company. Ask the HERO operator to do this rather than calling a random truck off Google, especially on I-635 or US-75 near Plano, where predatory towing is a real problem.

If you're calling a tow company directly, expect to pay $75 to $125 for a local drop in Dallas. Longer hauls cost more. See the full breakdown in our [Dallas towing cost guide](/towing-cost-dallas-texas/) before you agree to a price.

## Is It Safe to Wait in My Car on a Dallas Freeway?

Yes, staying in the car is usually safer than standing on the shoulder, especially on I-20 west of I-35E or on the upper deck of I-345, where there's almost no shoulder clearance. Lock the doors, keep your seatbelt on, and keep hazards flashing.

The exception: if your car is on fire, smoking from the engine bay, or you smell fuel, get out immediately and move well away from the vehicle. A burning car is not worth staying in. If you see smoke, read [car smoking under hood on highway](/car-smoking-under-hood-on-highway-pull-over-or-keep-driving/) for the right call.

## How Long Will I Wait for Help on a Dallas Highway?

TxDOT HERO trucks cover the major corridors during peak hours and can reach you in 10 to 30 minutes on I-20, I-30, I-35E, I-635, and US-75. Off-peak hours or secondary roads like TX-183 or TX-114 through Las Colinas will take longer.

A private tow company in Dallas typically quotes 30 to 60 minutes depending on traffic. That window stretches during rush hour on the I-35E and I-30 interchange near downtown, which is one of the most congested spots in the metro. Be honest with yourself about how long you can safely wait on the shoulder, and call sooner rather than later.

If you have roadside assistance through your insurer, check whether they use a GPS dispatch app, as those often cut wait times significantly versus calling a 1-800 number.

## What If I Don't Have Roadside Assistance or Insurance?

You'll pay out of pocket for the tow, but you still have options. TxDOT HERO service is free regardless of your insurance status. They can push your car to a safer position, change a flat, or call a contracted tower.

If you need a tow with no insurance, rates in Dallas run $75 to $150 for a local flatbed. Avoid agreeing to a tow without getting a price in writing or verbally confirmed first. ## Common Questions

**Q: Does TxDOT HERO really come for free?**
A: Yes. TxDOT HERO trucks patrol Dallas freeways at no charge to the driver. They can help with flat tires, minor mechanical issues, and pushing disabled vehicles to a safe location. They cannot tow your car for you, but they can request a contracted tow on your behalf.

**Q: What number do I call for TxDOT roadside help in Dallas?**
A: Dial 511 from any phone in Texas and ask for HERO service. You can also call 911 and ask the dispatcher to contact TxDOT HERO for your location.

**Q: Can I leave my car on the Dallas highway shoulder overnight?**
A: No. TxDOT will have it towed, usually within a few hours on a major freeway. You'll pay the tow and storage fees. Get it moved to a parking lot or repair shop the same day.

**Q: What if my car breaks down in a Dallas toll lane or express lane?**
A: Call 911 immediately. Toll lanes on I-635 managed lanes and I-35E express lanes have very limited shoulder space. Do not exit the vehicle until police or HERO arrives and blocks traffic.

**Q: Will my insurance company cover the tow even if I called a company they didn't dispatch?**
A: It depends on your policy. Many policies reimburse a flat amount per tow. Call your insurer before authorizing the tow if you have time, so you know what's covered. [Comparing roadside plans](/state-farm-roadside-assistance-vs-aaa-which-is-better/) before a breakdown happens is the smarter move.
