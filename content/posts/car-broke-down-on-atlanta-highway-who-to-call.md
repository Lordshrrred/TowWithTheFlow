## Who Do I Actually Call First When My Car Breaks Down on an Atlanta Highway?

Call 911 if anyone is hurt or your car is blocking a lane. If you're safely on the shoulder, your next call is to roadside assistance, whether that's your insurance company's hotline, AAA, or a local tow company.

Georgia DOT runs a free program called HERO (Highway Emergency Response Operator). HERO trucks patrol metro Atlanta's major corridors, including I-285, I-75, I-85, and I-20, during peak hours. They can change a flat, push your car to the next exit, or call a tow on your behalf. You don't dial them directly. Either 911 dispatches them or you call 511, Georgia's traffic information line, and report your location. If a HERO truck is nearby, they'll come to you.

If HERO hasn't shown up in 15 minutes and you need a tow, call a private company. Give them your exact milepost marker or nearest exit. On I-285, for example, you'd say "southbound I-285 near the Ashford Dunwoody Road exit, milepost 29." That precision gets them to you faster than describing landmarks.

## Is It Safe to Stay in My Car on the Shoulder of I-285 or I-75?

No. Get out of the car and move away from traffic. This is the single most important thing you can do.

On high-speed Atlanta corridors like the I-75/I-85 Downtown Connector or the I-285 interchange at I-20 near the Fulton Industrial corridor, traffic moves fast and distracted drivers drift onto shoulders constantly. The shoulder is not a safe waiting zone. Exit through the passenger side if your driver's side is near traffic, move past the guardrail, and wait there. Keep your hazard lights on. At night, pop your trunk and leave it open so drivers see the silhouette. If you have road flares or LED triangles, place them 100 to 200 feet behind the car.

If you cannot exit the vehicle safely because of traffic on both sides, stay belted in with hazards running and call 911 immediately.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does Georgia have a free roadside assistance program on Atlanta highways?**
A: Yes. HERO (Highway Emergency Response Operator) trucks patrol I-285, I-75, I-85, and I-20 in metro Atlanta during peak hours at no charge. They handle flats, fuel delivery, and minor battery issues, and can radio for a tow if needed. Call 511 to report your location.

**Q: What information should I give the tow truck dispatcher when I call?**
A: Your highway number, direction of travel, the nearest exit name or milepost number, which shoulder you're on (inside or outside), and your vehicle's make, model, and color. Milepost markers are posted on small green signs along the guardrail.

**Q: Can I leave my car on the Atlanta highway shoulder overnight?**
A: Georgia State Patrol can authorize a tow of any vehicle left unattended on a highway shoulder, often within a few hours. If you cannot get a tow that day, place a note on your dash with your name and contact info, and call the Georgia State Patrol post for your county to notify them.

**Q: My car is smoking on the Atlanta highway. Should I pull over immediately?**
A: Yes, pull over right now. White smoke from the exhaust can mean a coolant leak into the combustion chamber. Blue or gray smoke means burning oil. Either one can lead to engine seizure if you keep driving. Get to the shoulder, shut the engine off, and do not open the hood until the engine cools. See [Smoking from Hood Stopped on Side of Road: What to Do Right Now](/smoking-from-hood-stopped-on-side-of-road-what-to-do/) for next steps.

**Q: What if my car broke down on Atlanta highway at night and I feel unsafe waiting?**
A: Lock yourself inside the car with hazards on and call 911. Georgia State Patrol responds to stranded motorist calls and will stay until help arrives. Do not accept help from strangers who stop uninvited on an Atlanta highway at night. Keep your phone charged and stay on the line with dispatch if you feel threatened.
