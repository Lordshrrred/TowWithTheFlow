> **Quick Answer:** A flatbed tow in Nashville with no insurance typically costs $100 to $250 for a local tow, plus roughly $4 to $8 per mile past the first few miles. Expect to pay at the scene by card or cash. Get the total quoted before the truck is dispatched, ask about hookup and after-hours fees, and make sure the driver confirms the price when they arrive.

## How Much Does a Flatbed Tow Cost in Nashville With No Insurance?

Plan on $100 to $250 for a local flatbed tow in Nashville, with most short in-town tows landing near the middle of that range. Having no insurance doesn't raise the rate by itself. It just means you pay the full amount out of pocket instead of through a policy or roadside plan.

The price usually breaks down like this:

- **Hookup or base fee:** $75 to $150, often covering the first few miles
- **Per-mile charge:** about $4 to $8 per mile after that
- **Add-ons:** after-hours, holiday, weather, winching, or tight-access fees

I'm giving ranges because rates vary by company and conditions, and no one can promise you an exact number without hearing your situation. For a deeper look at the numbers, see our breakdown of [Nashville flatbed rates](/towing-cost-nashville-tennessee-flatbed-rates/).

## Why Does a Flatbed Cost More Than a Hook Tow?

A flatbed costs more because the whole vehicle rides on the bed with all four wheels off the ground, which takes a bigger truck and more setup time. You're paying for protection, and sometimes that protection is required.

Insist on a flatbed if your car is:

- All-wheel drive or four-wheel drive (dragging two wheels can damage the drivetrain)
- Lowered, or has a damaged front or rear end
- Dealing with a transmission failure
- A newer or high-value vehicle

If you have a basic front-wheel-drive car with a simple dead battery, a wheel-lift truck may be fine and cheaper. Say what you drive when you call so they send the right truck the first time.

## What Do I Do Right Now If I Break Down on I-40 or I-65 With No Insurance?

Get the car as far onto the shoulder as you can, turn on your hazards, and call for a tow only after you're out of traffic danger. Nashville's interstates are brutal places to be stuck: the I-40 and I-65 split near downtown, the I-440 loop, and the I-24 stretch toward Murfreesboro all carry fast, heavy traffic with narrow shoulders in spots.

Here's the order to work in:

1. Stop, hazards on, wheels turned away from traffic.
2. If you can't get fully off the road, stay buckled in with hazards on and call 911.
3. If you're safely off the road, exit on the passenger side and stand well away from the vehicle.
4. Call two or three private towing companies and get a total price, not just a base rate.
5. Share your exact location using the nearest mile marker or exit number.

If you're on a Tennessee interstate and can't tell what's safe, TDOT's SmartWay program and the 511 line provide road conditions, and Tennessee Highway Patrol may dispatch help to disabled vehicles in a travel lane. Don't rely on that for a fast tow, though. ## Can I Save Money on a Flatbed Tow in Nashville?

Yes. The biggest savings come from choosing the destination wisely, because mileage is the cost you control. Towing to a shop two miles away costs far less than towing to your preferred mechanic across town in Antioch or Hermitage.

Other ways to cut the bill:

- Call during daytime hours if the car is safe where it is
- Tow to a nearby shop first, then move it later if needed
- Compare at least two quotes before agreeing
- Ask about a cash or card discount
- Check whether your car's manufacturer offers free roadside assistance, since some include it for the first few years

A dead battery or minor issue might be fixable on the spot with a jump instead of a tow. Ask the dispatcher whether a service call is cheaper before committing to a full haul. Compare with other numbers in our [Nashville towing cost overview](/towing-cost-nashville-tennessee/).

## Common Questions

**Q: Does a tow company in Nashville need proof of insurance before towing me?**
A: No. A tow company will haul a car without proof of insurance. They need to verify you own the vehicle or are authorized to move it, usually by registration or ID, and they'll collect payment on the spot.

**Q: What payment do Nashville tow trucks accept if I have no insurance?**
A: Most accept credit and debit cards, and many take cash. Some take mobile payment apps. Confirm the payment method when you call, because a driver who can't take your card can strand you with a bill you can't pay at the roadside.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow in Nashville cheaper at night?**
A: No, it's usually more expensive. Many companies add an after-hours, weekend, or holiday surcharge. If your car is safely parked and not in danger, waiting until morning can lower the price.

**Q: How long does a flatbed tow truck take to reach me in Nashville?**
A: Often 30 to 60 minutes, but it can run longer during rush hour on I-24, I-65, or in bad weather. Ask the dispatcher for an honest arrival estimate. If it's much longer than quoted, see what to do when a [tow truck is taking too long](/tow-truck-taking-too-long-what-are-my-options-roadside/).

**Q: Can I be towed to my own driveway instead of a shop?**
A: Yes. You can have the car taken to your home, a friend's house, or a shop of your choice. Mileage is what sets the price, so a nearby destination costs less.
