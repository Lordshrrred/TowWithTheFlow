> **Quick Answer:** A standard tow in Los Angeles runs $75 to $150 for a short local haul, typically the first 5 miles. After that, expect $3 to $7 per additional mile. Freeway breakdowns on the 405, 10, or 101 almost always cost more because of CHP protocols and mandatory dispatch fees. Flatbeds, after-hours calls, and rush-hour pickups push the total higher, sometimes past $250.

## What Does a Typical Tow Actually Cost in Los Angeles?

For a basic local tow within one neighborhood, $75 to $150 is the realistic range. That covers the hook-up fee and the first few miles to a nearby shop.

Break it down and you're looking at:

- **Hook-up/dispatch fee:** $50 to $100
- **Per-mile rate:** $3 to $7 per mile after the included mileage
- **After-hours surcharge:** $25 to $75 on top of the base rate
- **Flatbed upgrade:** Add $25 to $75 over a standard wheel-lift tow

A tow from Silver Lake to a shop in Glendale, about 8 miles, could run $120 to $175 depending on whether you call at noon or midnight. Cross-town tows, say from LAX-adjacent Inglewood up to the San Fernando Valley, are easily $200 or more. For more detail on [towing cost per mile in Los Angeles](/towing-cost-per-mile-los-angeles/), that breakdown gives you a clearer per-mile picture.

## Why Does a Freeway Breakdown Cost More on the 405 or 101?

On a California freeway, you don't always get to choose your tow truck. If the CHP dispatches a Freeway Service Patrol (FSP) truck or a rotation tow, that company's rates apply, not yours. FSP trucks handle quick fixes for free (fuel, tire changes), but the moment your car needs to be towed off the freeway, you're on a CHP-contracted tower's clock.

Freeway tows add costs through:

- **Rotation dispatch fees** set by the contracting agency
- **Storage clock that starts immediately** once your car hits their yard
- **Limited negotiating room** because you didn't choose the company

If you break down on the 405 near the Getty Center or on the 10 near the La Cienega interchange, know that your car may go to a specific impound yard, not the shop of your choice. That can mean a second tow later. On the 101 through Hollywood or the I-10/I-405 interchange at 5:30 p.m., a tow truck that normally reaches you in 30 minutes might take 75 minutes. That extended wait does not reduce your bill. ## What If I Don't Have Insurance or Roadside Assistance?

You'll pay the full out-of-pocket rate with no reimbursement. That's the straightforward reality.

Without coverage, your options are:

1. **Call a private tow company directly** and negotiate before they hook up. Always ask for the total cost, not just the per-mile rate.
2. **Use a comparison app** like Urgently or Honk to get competing quotes before committing.
3. **Check your credit card.** Many Visa Signature and Chase Sapphire cards include roadside dispatch. Read the fine print on reimbursement limits before you assume it's free.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does a flatbed tow truck cost in Los Angeles?**
A: A flatbed in LA typically runs $125 to $225 for a short local tow, about $25 to $75 more than a standard wheel-lift. AWD vehicles, lowered cars, and luxury cars almost always require a flatbed, so you usually don't have the option to skip it.

**Q: How long does it take for a tow truck to arrive in Los Angeles?**
A: During off-peak hours, 30 to 45 minutes is typical. During rush hour on major corridors like the 405 or 101, wait times of 60 to 90 minutes are common. CHP-dispatched trucks on the freeway sometimes arrive faster because they're already patrolling.

**Q: Can I negotiate the tow truck price in Los Angeles?**
A: On a private call, yes. Always ask for the total estimate before they hook up, not just the rate. Once the car is on the truck, you have less leverage. On a CHP rotation tow from a freeway, pricing is set by the contract and is not negotiable.

**Q: Does AAA cover towing in Los Angeles and how much will they pay?**
A: Yes. AAA Classic covers up to 5 miles per tow, Plus covers up to 100 miles, and Premier covers up to 200 miles. In LA, where shops can be far apart and traffic inflates distances, upgrading to Plus or Premier is worth it if you use the service even once.

**Q: What happens if I can't pay the tow truck driver in Los Angeles?**
A: In California, a tow operator can legally hold your vehicle until the bill is paid. If your car goes into storage, fees accumulate daily, typically $50 to $75 per day. Call your insurance company immediately and ask about direct billing to the tow company to avoid letting that clock run.
