> **Quick Answer:** Grinding brakes usually mean metal-on-metal contact from worn brake pads. Stop driving immediately to prevent rotor damage. Phoenix towing typically costs $75-150 for local distances, but continuing to drive will cost you hundreds more in brake repairs.

## What To Do

1. **Pull over safely** at the first opportunity. Use hazard lights.

2. **Stop driving the vehicle.** Grinding brakes can fail completely without warning.

3. **Check if the grinding happens when braking only** or while driving normally. Both scenarios require immediate professional attention.

4. **Call for a tow.** Don't risk it, even for short distances. You could lose braking entirely.

5. **Have the car towed to a brake specialist** or trusted mechanic, not just any shop.

6. **Avoid pumping the brakes** while waiting for the tow truck. This can worsen the damage.

## What Causes The Grinding

Grinding during braking means your brake pads are completely worn down. The metal backing plate is now scraping directly against your brake rotors. This destroys the rotors fast, turning a $150 brake pad job into a $400+ brake job with new rotors.

Rotors are not cheap to ignore. Once a rotor gets scored by metal-on-metal contact, it develops grooves and heat cracks. A rotor that has been driven on in this condition for even a few miles often cannot be resurfaced and must be fully replaced. That is why the repair estimate jumps so sharply so fast.

Grinding that happens while driving at normal speed, not just when you press the brake pedal, often points to a stuck brake caliper. A seized caliper keeps constant pressure on the rotor even when your foot is off the pedal. This generates serious heat, can warp a rotor in minutes, and in extreme cases can start a wheel fire. Do not assume this is a minor issue.

Sometimes grinding comes from a small rock or road debris caught between the pad and rotor. That scenario usually produces a rhythmic, repetitive scraping sound that changes with wheel speed. It can sometimes clear itself within a few rotations. But if the sound is constant and heavy, treat it as pad failure until a mechanic tells you otherwise. Do not gamble.

One more cause worth knowing: brake pads left in dry, dusty Arizona heat for extended periods can develop surface glazing. Glazed pads produce a harsh scraping sound but may still have material left on them. A mechanic can tell the difference in about two minutes, so get it checked either way.

## What It Might Cost

**Phoenix Towing Costs:**
- Local tow (under 10 miles): $75-120

**Brake Repair Estimates:**
- Brake pads only (if caught early): $150-250
- Pads plus rotors (likely scenario): $400-600
- Complete brake system work: $600-1200

One important note on repair costs: Phoenix heat accelerates brake wear. If you are doing a lot of stop-and-go driving in summer temperatures, your pads can wear 20-30% faster than manufacturer estimates suggest. Budget accordingly and do not skip the inspection interval your mechanic recommends.

If your vehicle has rear drum brakes rather than disc brakes on the rear axle, the grinding sound and repair costs differ slightly. Drum brake repairs typically run $200-350 per axle, and the grinding feel is often less sharp but still requires immediate attention.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive just a mile or two to get off the highway before calling a tow?**
A: Moving the car a very short distance to a safe shoulder or exit is reasonable, but keep your speed under 20 mph, leave a large gap in front of you, and stop the moment you reach a safe spot. Driving further than that risks scoring the rotors beyond repair or losing brake function entirely.

**Q: How do I know if it is the front or rear brakes grinding?**
A: Grinding felt through the brake pedal and steering wheel usually points to the front brakes. Grinding you feel more in the seat or hear from behind the car typically means rear brakes. Front brakes do the majority of your stopping work, so front brake failure is the more urgent situation.

**Q: Will my insurance or roadside assistance cover the tow for a brake failure?**
A: Many roadside assistance plans through AAA, insurance add-ons, or even credit card benefits cover towing for mechanical breakdowns, including brake failures. Check your plan before you call a private tow company, since covered tows can save you the full $75-180 out of pocket.

## Stay Safe

- Never drive with grinding brakes, even one block
- Keep extra distance from other vehicles if you must move the car to safety
- Test your emergency brake gently to ensure you have some stopping power
- If brakes fail completely while driving, pump the brake pedal, use emergency brake gradually, and downshift to slow down
- Call 911 if your brakes fail on a busy road or highway
