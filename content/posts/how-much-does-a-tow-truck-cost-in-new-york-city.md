> **Quick Answer:** A tow truck in New York City typically costs $75 to $175 for a short local tow of 5 to 10 miles. Cross-borough tows, flatbed service, or getting pulled off the Cross Bronx Expressway or the BQE during rush hour can push that to $250 or more. If the NYPD calls the tow, you're looking at a different fee schedule entirely.

## How Much Does a Tow Truck Actually Cost in NYC Right Now?

Expect to pay $75 to $175 for a standard local tow within the same borough. The base hook-up fee alone usually runs $50 to $75, then most private companies charge $3 to $7 per mile after that.

A tow from Astoria, Queens to a shop in Long Island City might run $90. The same truck hauling your car from the FDR Drive near the 34th Street exit down to a shop in Red Hook, Brooklyn can easily hit $150 to $200 once you factor in tolls and the driver's time sitting in Manhattan traffic.

Flatbed tow trucks, which are required for all-wheel-drive vehicles, lowered cars, and most modern luxury cars, cost more. Budget an extra $30 to $75 over a standard wheel-lift tow. If you need one and don't ask specifically, you may get a wheel-lift that damages your drivetrain.

For a deeper look at what Manhattan rates specifically look like, [this breakdown of Manhattan towing rates](/towing-cost-new-york-city-manhattan-rates/) covers the full picture.

## What Does the NYPD Tow Cost If My Car Gets Impounded?

If the NYPD dispatches a tow, the cost is set by the city, and it is not the same as hiring a private company. The city-contracted rate for a standard tow is currently around $185 for the tow itself, plus storage fees that start accruing immediately, typically $20 to $30 per day.

After 24 hours at an NYPD tow pound, you can expect to pay the tow fee plus at least one day of storage before you even get to the window. The tow pounds in Brooklyn (Erie Basin) and the Bronx (Pier 76 on the Hudson River side is now closed; the main Manhattan facility moved to a lot on the West Side) get overwhelmed fast on weekends.

You cannot negotiate an NYPD tow fee. Pay it, or your storage bill grows.

## Does the Cost Change Depending on Which Borough I'm In?

Yes, meaningfully. Manhattan is the most expensive borough for private tows because of traffic, bridge tolls, and parking for the truck itself. Staten Island tends to be cheaper for short local tows since distances are shorter and traffic is lighter, but getting a flatbed across the Goethals Bridge adds cost.

The Bronx is tricky. If you break down on the Cross Bronx Expressway (I-95) near the Bruckner interchange, you are in one of the most congested breakdown zones in the city. Response times are longer and some operators charge a congestion or highway fee on top of the standard rate, typically $25 to $50 extra.

Brooklyn and Queens are mid-range. A tow within Flushing or Bay Ridge usually stays under $125. But if you need to cross a bridge or a tunnel, the toll gets added to your bill.

If you were in an accident and need a tow, the cost calculation changes. See [what you'll pay for a tow after an accident in NYC](/towing-cost-new-york-city-after-accident/) before you agree to anything on scene.

Watch for these specific add-ons that inflate bills:

- **Fuel surcharge:** Legitimate, but should be small ($10 to $20), not $50.
- **After-hours fee:** Valid for 10 PM to 6 AM service, usually $25 to $50.
- **Dolly fee:** Charged when all four wheels need to be off the ground. On an AWD vehicle, this is legitimate. On a standard rear-wheel-drive car going two miles, question it.
- **Storage fee:** If the driver takes your car somewhere you did not agree to, and holds it there, you may owe storage before you can retrieve it.

Never hand over your keys without knowing where the car is going. Demand the destination address before you agree.

## Is AAA or Roadside Assistance Worth It for NYC Drivers?

If you drive regularly in New York City, roadside assistance coverage is worth having. AAA membership starts around $60 to $100 per year and covers tows up to a set mileage limit (usually 5 to 7 miles for the basic tier, more for Plus). In a city where a 5-mile tow costs $100 to $125 out of pocket, a single use pays for the membership.

Many auto insurance policies include roadside assistance as an add-on for $5 to $15 per month. Check your declarations page before paying for a tow out of pocket, you may already be covered and not know it.

## Common Questions

**Q: How much does it cost to tow a car from Manhattan to Brooklyn?**
A: Plan on $100 to $175. You'll pay the base hook-up fee, per-mile charge, and the toll for whichever bridge the driver uses. The driver will typically add the toll to your bill.

**Q: Can a tow truck driver charge whatever they want in NYC?**
A: No. New York State law requires towing companies to disclose their rates and give you an estimate before towing. If a driver skips this step, you can dispute the charge and file a complaint with the New York State Department of Transportation.

**Q: What happens if I can't pay the tow truck driver on the spot?**
A: Most drivers require payment before releasing your vehicle. Cash or card is standard. If you genuinely cannot pay, the driver may take the car to a storage facility and hold it until you settle the bill, at which point daily storage fees start stacking up.

**Q: How much does it cost to get a car out of an NYC tow pound?**
A: The city tow fee is around $185, plus storage that starts at roughly $20 to $30 per day. You also need to show proof of ownership, valid insurance, and a valid license to reclaim it. Go early in the morning; the lines are shorter.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a private tow company or wait for the NYPD to handle it on the highway?**
A: Private is usually faster and can be cheaper if it's a short tow. An NYPD-dispatched tow on a highway like the Van Wyck or the BQE goes to a city-contracted company at a fixed rate, which is around $185 plus storage. A private company you call yourself might do the same tow for $100 to $140 and take the car exactly where you want it.
