A short local tow of 5 to 10 miles runs $100 to $175 out of pocket. Longer hauls, flatbed service, or a breakdown on a major expressway can push that to $250 or more. ## How Much Will a Tow Actually Cost Me in Chicago With No Insurance?

A private tow in Chicago with no insurance or roadside plan will run you $100 to $175 for a typical short-distance haul in most neighborhoods. That covers the hookup fee ($75 to $125) plus a per-mile rate that usually lands between $3.50 and $6.50 depending on the company and time of day.

If you break down on the Dan Ryan (I-90/94), the Kennedy (I-90/94 northwest), or the Eisenhower (I-290) and the city's IDOT Highway Incident Response Team responds, they may move your car off the expressway. That initial assist is generally at no charge, but once your car is on the shoulder or at a nearby exit, you still need a private tow to get it home or to a shop. That secondary tow is fully on you.

Flatbed service costs more. If you drive an all-wheel-drive vehicle, a low-clearance car, or your wheels are locked, you need a flatbed and that adds $25 to $75 to the base price. See the full flatbed breakdown at [Towing Cost Chicago Illinois Flatbed Rates](/towing-cost-chicago-illinois-flatbed-rates/).

## What Do I Do First If My Car Breaks Down in Chicago With No Coverage?

Get off the roadway and get visible, then call a private tow company and lock in a price verbally before they hook your car.

Here is the sequence:

1. If you are on an expressway like I-55 near the Stevenson/I-94 interchange or on I-294 near O'Hare, activate your hazard lights immediately and coast to the right shoulder. If you cannot move, stay in the car with your seatbelt on until help arrives.
2. Call 911 if you are in a dangerous position (active lane, no shoulder, night on a high-speed section). Otherwise, skip to a tow call.
3. Search "tow truck Chicago" and call two or three companies to compare prices. Ask: what is your hookup fee, what is your per-mile rate, and is there an after-hours surcharge? Get the total quote before saying yes.
4. Tell them your exact cross street or highway exit. On the Kennedy, that might be "I-90 near the Ohio Street exit" or "I-94 southbound past Fullerton." Precision speeds up response.
5. Do not accept a tow from a company that pulls up uninvited without giving you a price first. Predatory towing near breakdown hotspots is a real problem in Chicago.

If you break down in a parking garage, a side street in Pilsen, or a commercial lot in River North, the process is the same but there is no immediate safety threat. Take your time, compare quotes.

## Will the City of Chicago Tow My Car and What Does That Cost?

If Chicago's Department of Streets and Sanitation or CPD orders your car towed (for blocking traffic, parking violations, or abandonment), you face a city impound fee on top of any towing charge. City-ordered tows in Chicago carry a tow fee set by the city, plus daily storage fees at the impound facility, and any outstanding tickets become a condition of release.

To avoid city impound, move your car off any street within a reasonable window. If you cannot move it immediately, place a note on the dash with a phone number and call a private tow within the hour. Do not leave a broken-down car unattended on a Chicago street overnight without arranging a tow.

## What If I Broke Down on the Expressway at Night in Chicago?

At night on Chicago expressways, your risk goes up and your options narrow. Get behind a guardrail if one is accessible and stand away from the car on the passenger side.

Illinois 511 (call or use the Illinois Department of Transportation traffic app) can give you real-time incident updates and confirm if a response team has been dispatched to your location. IDOT's Highway Incident Response Team patrols high-traffic corridors including I-90/94, I-290, I-55, and I-57 during peak and overnight hours. If they reach you before your tow truck, they can push your car to a safer spot and help coordinate a tow.

Night tows in Chicago typically carry a 15 to 25 percent after-hours surcharge. A tow that costs $130 at noon may run $155 to $165 at midnight. Weekend rates stack on top of that. ## Common Questions

**Q: How much is a tow truck in Chicago if I have no insurance or AAA?**
A: Budget $100 to $175 for a local tow of 5 to 10 miles. The hookup fee alone is usually $75 to $125, and mileage adds $3.50 to $6.50 per mile on top of that.

**Q: Can I get towed for free in Chicago if I break down on the expressway?**
A: IDOT's Highway Incident Response Team may move your car to a safe spot at no charge, but that is not a full tow to your destination. You still need to arrange and pay for a private tow from there.

**Q: What happens if I just leave my broken-down car on a Chicago street?**
A: CPD or Streets and Sanitation can order it towed to an impound lot, usually within a few hours on a busy street. You will owe the city tow fee, daily storage charges, and any outstanding parking tickets before they release the car.

**Q: Is it safe to wait inside my car for a tow truck on a Chicago highway?**
A: On a high-speed expressway like the Dan Ryan or the Kennedy, get out of the driver's side and stand behind a guardrail if possible. Staying inside a stopped car on an active highway shoulder is one of the most dangerous places to be. On a low-speed surface street, staying in the car with doors locked is usually fine.

**Q: Do tow truck prices in Chicago vary by neighborhood?**
A: Yes, practically speaking. Companies working the Loop, River North, or Near North Side often charge more due to traffic and parking difficulty. Tows from residential neighborhoods on the Far South Side or Northwest Side tend to run a bit cheaper. Always get a quote that includes your pickup address.
