## How Do I Get My Car Towed When It's Stuck in Snow in Denver?

Call a tow company that does recovery, not just hookup and haul, or use the roadside plan you already pay for. Check your insurance card, your AAA membership, and your carmaker's app first, because one of them may already cover this.

When you reach dispatch, say these things in this order:

1. "I'm stuck in snow." Recovery needs a winch or extra equipment.
2. Your exact location: cross streets, a mile marker, or a nearby landmark.
3. Whether the car is in a lane, on a shoulder, or off the road.
4. Whether it's front-wheel, rear-wheel, or all-wheel drive. This matters for how they pull it out.
5. If the car is lowered, has a bumper cover, or is a low-clearance sedan, say so.

No plan? Search "snow recovery" or "winch out" plus your neighborhood. Not every tow company will pull a car out of a snowbank.

## What Do I Do While I Wait for the Tow Truck?

Stay in the car with your seatbelt on unless it's unsafe, turn on your hazards, and run the engine only in short intervals with the tailpipe clear. A tailpipe packed with snow can push carbon monoxide into the cabin. Check it, dig it clear, and crack a window.

On I-25 near the Santa Fe Drive exit, I-70 through the Stapleton and Montbello stretch, or C-470 out by the foothills, a stopped car in a storm gets hit. Plows and drivers on packed snow can't stop fast. If you're in or next to a lane, stay buckled in the car. Standing outside on the shoulder is how people get hurt. If your vehicle is stopped in a travel lane or on a narrow shoulder and traffic is moving, call 911.

If you're in a residential spot in Capitol Hill, Park Hill, or Sloan's Lake, you can usually dig out yourself first. Clear snow from around all four tires and under the car, because snow packed against the undercarriage keeps the tires from gripping. Put sand, cat litter, or your floor mats under the drive wheels. Don't spin the tires. Spinning melts a polished ice rut and makes it worse.

While you wait, send your live location to someone you trust and keep your phone charged. Tow wait times spike during storms. ## Do I Need a Tow, or Can I Get Myself Out of the Snow?

You can get yourself out if the car is on flat ground, the snow is under about 6 inches, and you have traction material. If it's high-centered, in a ditch, or sinking deeper when you try, stop and call.

Try this, in order:

1. Dig out all four tires and the space under the car.
2. Turn off traction control. It cuts power when the wheels slip, which is exactly when you need power.
3. Start in second gear if you have a manual. Use light throttle in an automatic.
4. Rock the car gently: drive, reverse, drive, in small movements.

Give it three or four tries. If the wheels dig deeper, you're burning fuel and risking transmission damage for nothing. High-centered means the frame or belly pan is sitting on snow with the tires barely touching the ground. That needs a tow. Pushing it only digs you in further.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will AAA tow me out of a snowbank in Denver?**
A: Usually yes, as long as the car is within reach of a normal tow truck and not too far off the road. AAA often covers basic winching, but deep ditches or heavy recovery can fall outside the plan. No plan? See [stuck in snow in Denver with no AAA](/car-stuck-in-snow-denver-no-aaa-what-to-do/).

**Q: Is it safe to run my engine while stuck in snow?**
A: Only in short bursts with the tailpipe cleared. A blocked tailpipe can fill the cabin with carbon monoxide. Dig it out each time and crack a window.

**Q: Can I call the city or police to tow me out?**
A: Police won't pull you out of the snow for free. Call 911 only if you're blocking traffic, injured, or in danger. For everything else, call a private tow or your roadside plan.

**Q: Why won't some tow companies do a snow recovery?**
A: Recovery needs winch equipment and a crew willing to work in storm conditions. Many companies only haul cars that are already on firm ground. Ask first whether they do recovery.

**Q: How long will a tow truck take in a Denver snowstorm?**
A: Expect longer than usual during a storm, sometimes well over an hour, because everyone is calling at once. Our page on [Denver tow truck response times](/tow-truck-response-time-denver-colorado/) shows what's realistic.
