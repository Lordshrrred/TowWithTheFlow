> **Quick Answer:** Yes, State Farm roadside assistance covers towing, but only if you've added Emergency Road Service (ERS) to your policy. Coverage typically pays for a tow to the nearest qualified repair facility. What it does not do is write you a blank check: there are per-incident dollar limits, and some tows cost more than the plan covers. Know your limit before you call.

## Does State Farm Actually Pay for Towing, or Just Dispatch a Truck?

State Farm pays for the tow, not just the phone call. When you have Emergency Road Service added to your auto policy, State Farm arranges and covers the tow directly, up to your plan's reimbursement cap. You call their roadside number, they dispatch a provider, and the bill goes to them. If the total exceeds your limit, you pay the difference at the scene or when you pick up your car.

The key distinction: ERS is a separate add-on, not automatically bundled with comprehensive or collision coverage. If you never opted in, you have no roadside coverage through State Farm, even with a full-coverage policy.

## What Are the Actual Dollar Limits on a State Farm Tow?

State Farm's ERS benefit typically covers towing costs up to a set dollar amount per disablement, which as of recent policy terms is generally in the range of $75 to $100 per incident for basic coverage, though your specific limit depends on your state and the policy period you purchased. A local tow inside a city might run $75 to $125 for the first five to seven miles, which often fits inside that window. A flatbed tow from a highway or a longer-distance tow to a specialist shop can run $200 or more, and you will owe anything above your ceiling out of pocket.

If you need a long-distance tow, check [how much a long-distance tow truck costs per mile](/how-much-does-a-long-distance-tow-truck-cost-per-mile/) before you assume State Farm will cover all of it. It probably won't.

There is also a per-year usage limit on how many times you can call. State Farm typically allows a set number of ERS calls per year before they can flag your policy for review. For the specifics on that, see [State Farm roadside assistance: how many times per year can you use it](/state-farm-roadside-assistance-how-many-times-per-year/).

## What Else Does State Farm Roadside Assistance Cover Besides Towing?

Towing is just one benefit under ERS. The add-on also typically covers:

- Battery jump-start or battery delivery
- Flat tire change (using your spare)
- Lockout service if you've locked your keys inside
- Fuel delivery if you run dry
- Winching or extraction if your car is stuck (within a reasonable distance from a paved road)

Fuel delivery covers the service call but not the cost of the gas itself. Lockout service covers getting you back in, but if the lock mechanism is damaged, that repair is yours. If you're weighing whether it makes sense to call State Farm for a lockout versus a local locksmith, [this comparison breaks down which option is actually cheaper](/locked-keys-in-car-roadside-assistance-or-locksmith-which-is-cheaper/).

## Does State Farm Cover Towing After an Accident, or Only Breakdowns?

Both, with conditions. ERS covers towing after a mechanical breakdown. If you have comprehensive or collision coverage and the car needs a tow after an accident, that tow may be covered under those coverages instead, subject to your deductible. The two pathways are separate.

## How Do I Actually Use State Farm Roadside Assistance When I'm Stranded?

Call the number on your State Farm insurance card or log into the State Farm mobile app and request service through there. Have your policy number ready. State Farm will confirm you have ERS active, then dispatch a provider from their network to your location.

Stay in your car with your hazard lights on if you're on a highway or high-speed road. If you're broken down on the shoulder of an interstate, get as far from the travel lanes as possible before you wait. For more on staying safe in that situation, [how to stay safe broken down on a highway at night](/how-to-stay-safe-broken-down-on-highway-at-night/) covers exactly what to do while you wait for the truck.

Do not call a tow truck independently and then expect State Farm to reimburse you without pre-authorization. Some ERS plans do offer reimbursement if you arrange your own tow in an area where they can't dispatch, but you need to get that approval on the call before you hire anyone. Keep your receipt. Reimbursements without pre-authorization are often denied.

## Common Questions

**Q: How do I know if I have Emergency Road Service on my State Farm policy?**
A: Log into your State Farm account online or in the app and pull up your policy details. ERS shows as a line-item add-on. You can also call State Farm's main customer service line and ask an agent to confirm before you need it.

**Q: Will State Farm tow my car to any mechanic I choose, or just the nearest one?**
A: ERS covers a tow to the nearest qualified repair facility. If you want your car towed to a specific shop farther away, you may pay the difference in mileage beyond what the plan covers.

**Q: Does adding Emergency Road Service to my State Farm policy raise my rates significantly?**
A: ERS is one of the cheapest add-ons available, typically a few dollars a month. It rarely has a meaningful impact on your premium, but the exact cost depends on your state and existing policy.

**Q: Can I use State Farm roadside assistance for someone else's car I'm driving?**
A: ERS follows the car on your policy, not you as a driver. If you're driving a vehicle that isn't on your State Farm policy, that car is not covered by your ERS benefit.

**Q: What happens if State Farm can't find a tow truck in my area?**
A: In remote areas, State Farm may authorize you to arrange your own tow and submit for reimbursement. Get that authorization confirmed on the call and keep every receipt. Reimbursement without prior authorization is often denied.
