> **Quick Answer:** GEICO's roadside assistance towing benefit typically covers up to $100 per disablement incident, though some policies carry a higher limit. That covers most local tows but falls short on longer hauls. If your tow costs more, you pay the difference out of pocket. Check your declarations page or the GEICO app for the exact dollar cap on your specific policy before you call a truck.

## How Much Will GEICO Actually Pay for a Tow?

GEICO's standard roadside assistance add-on covers towing up to a dollar limit, most commonly $100 per incident. Some policy tiers push that to $150 or more, but $100 is the number you'll see most often. That dollar cap applies per breakdown event, not per year, but multiple uses in one policy term can trigger a review or non-renewal on some policies.

That $100 limit is enough to cover a hookup fee and a few miles of towing in many markets. A flatbed tow in most U.S. cities runs $75 to $125 for the first five miles, so you're close to the edge before the driver even shifts into gear. Once you're looking at 15, 20, or 30 miles to a shop, you'll almost certainly owe money at the end of that ride.

If you want to see how GEICO's reimbursement limit compares to what you're likely to actually owe, read through [GEICO Roadside Assistance Towing Reimbursement Limit](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-reimbursement-limit/) for a breakdown of what happens when your tow exceeds the cap.

## Does the Coverage Amount Change Based on How You Have Roadside Added?

Yes, and this matters. GEICO offers roadside assistance two ways: as an add-on to your auto policy, and bundled through certain insurance products. The add-on is the common one, and it carries that per-incident dollar cap. What you do not get with GEICO's own roadside product is unlimited mileage or a high flat-rate benefit like you'd see with a dedicated AAA membership. If you need a long-distance tow, that difference adds up fast. The [AAA vs. roadside assistance through insurance comparison](/aaa-vs-roadside-assistance-through-insurance-which-is-better/) lays out exactly where insurance-based coverage falls short for multi-mile situations.

## What If My Tow Costs More Than GEICO Covers?

You pay the difference directly to the tow company. GEICO either coordinates the tow and pays the driver up to your cap, or you pay the full bill and submit for reimbursement later, depending on how you initiated the call. If you used GEICO's app or dispatch service, they typically handle direct payment and bill you the overage. If you called a tow truck yourself first, you'll submit receipts for reimbursement and GEICO will cut a check up to your limit.

Save every receipt. Take a photo of the tow invoice before the driver leaves. Disputes over towing charges are common, and having documentation puts you in a stronger position. If you end up in a billing fight, the process for [disputing a towing charge after a roadside emergency](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-after-roadside-emergency/) walks through exactly how to handle it.

## Should I Use GEICO Roadside or Pay Out of Pocket?

Use GEICO roadside if your tow is short and within the dollar cap. Pay out of pocket if you want to protect your claim history. Roadside assistance claims through GEICO are technically separate from your collision or comprehensive coverage, but multiple roadside uses in a short period can flag your policy. If the tow is cheap enough that you can absorb it, paying directly avoids any policy risk. If you're facing a $250 tow and GEICO covers $100, it still makes sense to use the benefit and pay the $150 gap rather than eating the full amount. ## Common Questions

**Q: How do I find out my exact GEICO roadside towing limit?**
A: Log into your GEICO account or open the GEICO mobile app and pull up your policy declarations page. The roadside assistance benefit section lists your specific per-incident dollar cap. You can also call GEICO directly, but the app is faster when you're sitting on the side of the road.

**Q: Does GEICO roadside assistance have a mileage limit on towing?**
A: GEICO's roadside towing benefit is capped by dollar amount, not by miles. Once the tow bill hits your coverage ceiling, you owe the rest regardless of distance. There is no separate mileage cutoff built into the standard policy.

**Q: Will using GEICO roadside assistance raise my rates?**
A: A single use rarely causes a rate increase. Repeated claims in the same policy period are more likely to draw attention at renewal. GEICO treats roadside claims differently from at-fault accident claims, but it is still usage data on your file.

**Q: Can I call any tow truck or do I have to use GEICO's network?**
A: You can call GEICO dispatch and they'll send a contracted driver, or you can arrange your own tow and submit for reimbursement. Using their dispatch is simpler because they handle direct payment. If you go out of network, you front the cost and wait for reimbursement up to your limit.

**Q: Does GEICO roadside assistance cover towing after an accident, not just a breakdown?**
A: It depends on how the incident is classified. If the car is not drivable after a collision, GEICO may handle towing under your collision or comprehensive coverage instead of roadside assistance. Check with GEICO before dispatching so the claim goes under the right coverage and you're not double-dipping.
