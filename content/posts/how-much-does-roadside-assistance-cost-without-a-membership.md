> **Quick Answer:** Without a membership, expect to pay $50 to $125 for a jump start, $50 to $100 for a lockout, $75 to $125 for a tire change, and $75 to $200 or more for a local tow. Prices spike on nights, weekends, and holidays. If you have comprehensive or full-coverage auto insurance, your policy may already cover one or more of these services. Check before you call a private tow company.

## What Does Roadside Assistance Cost If You Just Call Someone Off the Street?

You are paying retail, and retail is not cheap. A basic jump start from a private roadside company runs $50 to $125. A lockout call lands between $50 and $100. A tire change, where the service tech swaps your flat for your spare, typically costs $60 to $100. A local tow of five miles or fewer usually runs $75 to $125 as a base hook-up fee, then $3 to $7 per mile after that.

These are not worst-case numbers. They are the going rate for a legitimate, licensed provider showing up in under an hour. If you call someone off a Google search with no reviews, the price can be anything.

## Does My Car Insurance Cover This Even If I Never Signed Up for Roadside Assistance?

Sometimes, yes. If you carry comprehensive coverage, many insurers include some form of towing or roadside help as a built-in benefit, not a separate add-on you had to opt into. This is worth a two-minute call to your insurance company before you hand over a credit card.

If you added roadside assistance as an optional rider, you are almost certainly covered for at least one service call per incident. [GEICO's roadside benefit](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-reimbursement-limit/), for example, caps reimbursement at a set dollar amount per tow, so even if you pay upfront, you may recover most of it. The same logic applies to [Progressive](/progressive-insurance-towing-coverage-limit-per-incident/) and other major carriers. Know your policy before you assume you are on your own.

## Is There a Cheaper Way to Pay Per Use Without a Full Membership?

Yes. Several services let you pay for a single dispatch without committing to an annual plan. Honk, Urgently, and Allstate's Motor Club (on a pay-per-use basis) connect you to a network provider and give you a fixed price upfront before anyone shows up. That locked-in quote is the main advantage over calling a random tow yard directly.

Your credit card may also cover you at no extra cost. Some Visa Signature, certain Chase Sapphire, and select American Express cards include roadside assistance as a cardholder benefit. It is not universal, but it is worth opening your card's app or calling the number on the back right now. [Credit card roadside coverage](/does-credit-card-roadside-assistance-cover-towing-cost/) varies a lot by card tier, so confirm the specifics before you assume it applies.

## How Much More Do You Pay Without a Membership Compared to Having One?

The math adds up fast. AAA Classic membership runs around $60 to $70 per year. A single tow without any coverage can cost $100 to $200 or more. One breakdown and you have already spent more than a year's worth of AAA dues. ## What Should I Watch Out For When Paying Out of Pocket?

Get a price confirmed before the truck moves. Predatory towing is real. Once a tow truck hooks your car and hauls it to a lot, you are at their mercy for release fees, storage charges, and anything else they want to add. In states like California and Texas, towing companies are required to provide a written estimate on request, but not every operator volunteers that information.

Ask these three questions before saying yes: What is the base hook-up fee? What is the per-mile rate? Where exactly will my car be taken? If they cannot answer all three clearly, call a different company. If you feel pressured or believe you were overcharged after the fact, [you can dispute the charge](/how-to-dispute-a-towing-charge-after-roadside-emergency/).

Also keep in mind: night calls, holiday weekends, and rural areas all carry premium pricing. A 2 a.m. tow on Labor Day weekend will cost more than the same tow on a Tuesday afternoon.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I call AAA just once without being a member?**
A: AAA does not offer single-use service to non-members. You would need to join first, which typically starts around $60 to $70 for the basic Classic level. In an emergency, you are better off using a pay-per-use app like Honk or checking your credit card benefits instead.

**Q: How much does a jump start cost without roadside assistance?**
A: A jump start from a private provider runs $50 to $125 depending on your location and time of day. If you have a portable jump starter in your trunk (a lithium-ion pack like the NOCO Boost Plus GB40), the cost is zero and you do not need to wait for anyone.

**Q: Will my insurance reimburse me if I pay for a tow out of pocket?**
A: Possibly. If your policy includes roadside assistance or towing coverage, you can often pay upfront and submit for reimbursement with a receipt. Call your insurer's claims line as soon as you are safe, and keep every piece of paperwork the tow company gives you.

**Q: Does it cost more to get roadside help on a highway versus a regular road?**
A: Dispatch and hook-up fees are usually the same, but highway calls sometimes take longer and may involve a provider with highway access authorization, which can add to the bill. The bigger cost variable is distance to the nearest service provider, not the road type itself.

**Q: Is roadside assistance through my car dealership worth it as a no-membership alternative?**
A: It depends on the terms. Dealer plans are often bundled into a new-car purchase and cover the first few years, but the per-incident coverage limits can be low. [Roadside assistance through a dealership](/roadside-assistance-through-car-dealership-worth-it/) is worth checking if you bought your car recently, since you may already be covered without knowing it.
