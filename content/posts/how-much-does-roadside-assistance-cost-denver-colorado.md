# How Much Does Roadside Assistance Cost in Denver Colorado

> **Quick Answer:** In Denver, pay-per-use roadside assistance runs $50 to $150 depending on the service. An annual AAA membership starts at $60-$75 per year and covers most calls. Your auto insurance may include roadside for $5-$15 added to your premium. If you need a tow on top of basic service, budget $75-$150 for local hauls under 10 miles.

Denver drivers deal with real variety when it comes to breakdowns. Summer afternoon thunderstorms strand cars on I-25. Winter black ice causes fender-benders on I-70 through the mountains. And the altitude alone can tax an already-weak battery. Knowing what roadside assistance actually costs before you need it makes a bad day a lot less expensive.

## What To Do

1. **Check what you already have.** Before you call anyone and pay out of pocket, look at your insurance card or call your insurance agent. Many policies include roadside coverage for a few dollars a month extra. Check your credit card benefits too. Cards like Chase Sapphire, Amex Gold, and many others include free towing or roadside as a cardholder benefit.

2. **Know the four main ways to get roadside help in Denver:**
   - **Your auto insurance add-on:** $5-$15/month added to your premium. Covers jump starts, lockouts, fuel delivery, and towing to the nearest shop. State Farm, GEICO, Allstate, and USAA all offer this.
   - **AAA membership:** Basic plan runs $60-$75/year for Colorado. Covers four service calls per year. Towing is free up to 5 miles on Classic, 100 miles on Plus ($98-$115/year).
   - **Pay-per-use apps:** Services like Urgent.ly or Honk connect you to local providers. Expect $75-$120 for a jump start or lockout, $100-$150 for a tow, billed to your card on the spot.
   - **Direct call to a Denver tow company:** No plan, no app, just a phone call. Hook fee of $65-$95 plus $3-$7 per mile. A 10-mile tow from downtown Denver to a shop in Lakewood or Aurora could run $95-$165.

3. **Identify your actual problem before you call.** A jump start costs less than a tow. If your car won't start, a dead battery is the first guess. If you see smoke or smell burning, that is a tow situation. If your car died while moving, see [Car Broke Down on Highway in Denver: Who to Call Right Now](/car-broke-down-on-highway-in-denver-who-to-call/) before you decide what to request.

4. **Call CDOT's Courtesy Patrol if you're on a metro Denver highway.** Colorado Department of Transportation runs free roving assistance trucks on I-25, I-70, I-225, and other major corridors during peak hours. They can push your car off the road, change a flat, or bring a small amount of fuel at no charge. Response time varies, but this costs you nothing.

5. **Get a quote before you agree to service.** Any reputable Denver tow or roadside company should give you a price upfront. If they won't quote over the phone, call someone else.

## Stay Safe

- Pull completely off the road and onto the shoulder. On I-25 or I-70, get as far right as possible.
- Turn on your hazard lights immediately, even in daylight.
- Stay inside the car with your seatbelt on if traffic is moving fast nearby. Getting out on a busy Denver highway shoulder is more dangerous than staying put.
- If your car is in a live lane and won't move, get out, move to the guardrail side, and call 911 first, then roadside help.
- At night, keep the interior dome light off so you don't attract attention or lose night vision if you need to watch for passing vehicles.
- Keep water in your car year-round. Denver summers hit 95-plus degrees and you could wait 30-60 minutes for help. See [Tow Truck Response Time Denver Colorado](/tow-truck-response-time-denver-colorado/) to set realistic expectations.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does my credit card actually cover roadside assistance or do I still have to pay?**
A: Some cards like Chase Sapphire Reserve cover roadside costs directly up to a set limit per incident, while others only offer a dispatch service where you still pay the provider out of pocket. Check your card's benefits guide or call the number on the back to confirm exactly what is covered before you rely on it.

**Q: Is CDOT Courtesy Patrol available 24 hours a day in Denver?**
A: No, Courtesy Patrol trucks run during peak commute hours on major metro corridors, not around the clock. If you break down late at night or on a weekend, you will likely need to call a paid roadside service or tow company.

**Q: Will roadside assistance cover me if I break down in the mountains west of Denver?**
A: Most plans will still dispatch help, but pay-per-use providers and direct tow companies often add a terrain surcharge and extra mileage fees in the foothills and mountain areas. Expect to pay 20-30% more than you would for the same service inside the metro, and confirm the total price before agreeing to anything.
