> **Quick Answer:** Portland tow companies typically charge $3 to $7 per mile on top of a hookup fee that runs $75 to $125. A short 5-mile tow across town lands somewhere between $90 and $160. Longer hauls from outer East Portland or near the I-205/I-84 interchange push that figure to $150 or more depending on truck type and time of day.

## What Does Per-Mile Towing Actually Cost in Portland Right Now?

Most Portland tow operators charge $3 to $7 per loaded mile, meaning the miles your car is on the hook. That rate does not include the hookup fee, which is billed the moment the driver attaches your vehicle. Expect $75 to $125 for that flat charge before a single mile rolls.

A rough breakdown by distance:

| Tow Distance | Estimated Total Cost |
|---|---|
| 1 to 5 miles | $90 to $160 |
| 6 to 10 miles | $120 to $195 |
| 11 to 20 miles | $155 to $265 |
| 20+ miles | $200 and up |

Flatbed rates run higher than wheel-lift. If your car is AWD, all-wheel drive low-clearance, or damaged from a collision, the driver may require a flatbed, which can add $20 to $40 to the base rate. For a full flatbed breakdown, see [towing cost in Portland Oregon flatbed](/towing-cost-portland-oregon-flatbed/).

## Does My Location in Portland Change What I'll Pay Per Mile?

Yes, your starting point matters, and so does where you're headed. If you break down on I-84 near the 122nd Avenue exit in outer East Portland and need a tow to a shop in Beaverton, you're looking at 15 to 18 miles. At $5 per mile plus a $100 hookup fee, that's $175 to $190 before any after-hours or weather surcharges.

A breakdown at the I-5/I-405 interchange downtown heading to a shop in Northwest Portland might be 3 to 4 miles. Same rate structure, much lower bill: $115 to $128.

Interstate 205 and the Barbur Boulevard corridor near the Terwilliger curves are common breakdown zones. Tows from those spots to shops in Lake Oswego or Tigard add extra mileage fast. Know your destination before you call so you can ask for a quote upfront.

## What Else Gets Added to the Per-Mile Rate?

Several things get stacked on top of the mileage charge, and dispatchers do not always volunteer this information.

- **After-hours fee:** Many Portland tow companies add $25 to $50 for calls after 10 p.m. or before 6 a.m.
- **Highway/freeway response surcharge:** Some operators charge extra for ODOT-required equipment on I-84, I-5, or I-205.
- **Winching fee:** If your car went off the road, into a ditch, or over a curb, add $75 to $150 for a winch-out before the tow even starts.

## How Do I Get an Accurate Quote Before the Truck Arrives?

Call at least two companies and give them your exact location and destination address. Say: "What is your hookup fee, your per-mile rate, and do you charge extra for after-hours?" Get those three numbers before you confirm the dispatch.

Portland has dozens of independent tow operators outside the big chains. Independent operators sometimes run lower per-mile rates, especially for short in-city hauls. The ODOT 511 line can help you reach a licensed operator if you are stranded on a state highway and not sure who to call.

If you have AAA or roadside coverage through your insurance, call that line first. Many plans cover a set number of tow miles per incident, and the per-mile math changes significantly if you only pay for miles beyond the covered distance.

## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average per-mile towing cost in Portland Oregon?**
A: The going rate is $3 to $7 per mile for the loaded portion of the tow, plus a hookup fee of $75 to $125. Most in-city tows in Portland total $90 to $200 depending on distance and truck type.

**Q: Do Portland tow companies charge more at night?**
A: Most do. After-hours surcharges of $25 to $50 are common for calls placed after 10 p.m. Always ask when you call dispatch.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive per mile in Portland?**
A: Yes. Flatbeds typically cost $20 to $40 more in base fees, and some operators charge a slightly higher per-mile rate. If your vehicle is AWD or damaged, you may not have a choice.

**Q: What if I break down on I-84 in Portland and cannot move my car?**
A: Turn on your hazards, stay in the vehicle if it is safe to do so, and call 911 or ODOT if you are blocking a lane. ODOT may dispatch a rotation tow through their freeway service. That tow is not free, but the rate is regulated.

**Q: How can I lower my towing cost per mile in Portland?**
A: Know your destination before you call, choose a nearby shop to reduce mileage, ask for an itemized quote upfront, and check whether your auto insurance or a membership program like AAA covers any portion of the bill. Even partial coverage cuts the out-of-pocket cost sharply.
