## How Much Does a Tow Actually Cost After Breaking Down on a Dallas Highway?

A standard tow from a Dallas highway runs $100 to $200 for most in-city hauls. The two components are a hook-up fee (what they charge just to show up and connect to your car) and a per-mile rate once your vehicle is loaded on the truck. In Dallas, hook-up fees average $75 to $125, and per-mile rates run $4 to $7. A breakdown on I-30 near the I-35E interchange to a shop near Uptown, roughly 8 miles, puts you around $115 to $180 total.

Flatbed trucks cost a little more than wheel-lift trucks because of the equipment and time involved. If your car is all-wheel drive or low to the ground, most reputable shops will insist on a flatbed anyway. For the full cost comparison on flatbed versus hook-and-chain options in Dallas, see [Towing Cost in Dallas Texas Hook and Chain vs Flatbed](/towing-cost-in-dallas-texas-hook-and-chain-vs-flatbed/).

## Does It Cost More If I Break Down on LBJ Freeway or I-635?

Yes, location adds cost in two ways: distance to the nearest lot or shop, and access difficulty. The LBJ Freeway (I-635) and the section of I-35E north of the High Five interchange are known for heavy traffic that slows truck response. Some companies add a congestion or difficulty fee, typically $25 to $50, for retrieval in high-traffic, highway-center-lane situations.

If you break down near the I-635 and US-75 interchange, which is one of the densest interchange points in the metro, give TxDOT's 511 service a call for real-time road info, and stay well off the travel lanes while you wait. The shoulder on those elevated sections is narrow. Police in Dallas can also call a contracted tow through the city's rotation program, which can sometimes get a truck to you faster than calling a private company cold.

## What If I Don't Have Insurance or Roadside Assistance Coverage?

You pay out of pocket and negotiate before the truck hooks up. Get the total price confirmed in writing (a text message works) before you agree to anything. Without insurance or AAA, you have no protection from inflated rates, and some operators take advantage of that.

## Is It Safe to Wait on the Shoulder of a Dallas Highway for a Tow?

Get as far off the road as physically possible and stay out of the car if you can do so safely. On I-20 or the Dallas North Tollway, the shoulder is often narrow and traffic moves fast. Turn on your hazard lights immediately, and if you have road flares or reflective triangles, set them at least 100 feet behind the vehicle.

If you are on an elevated section, like the I-30 canyon through downtown, or in a tunnel, do not stand behind the car. Get to the front of the vehicle, away from oncoming traffic, and stay against the barrier wall. Call 911 if you feel you are in immediate danger. TxDOT's Highway Emergency Response Operators (HERO) patrol major Dallas freeways and can push your car to a safe spot at no charge. They are not a tow service, but they can keep you safe until one arrives. ## Common Questions

**Q: What's the average tow truck response time on Dallas highways?**
A: During normal hours, expect 30 to 60 minutes on most Dallas freeways. On I-35E through downtown or I-635 during rush hour, that can stretch to 90 minutes. TxDOT HERO trucks can reach you faster in some corridors since they patrol continuously.

**Q: Can I choose which shop my car gets towed to if police call the tow?**
A: Yes. In Texas, you have the legal right to choose your own tow destination even if a police-dispatched truck is called. Tell the driver where you want the car to go before they hook up. Once it is loaded and moving, changing the drop point can trigger additional mileage charges.

**Q: Will my car get impounded if I leave it on the shoulder of a Dallas highway?**
A: Dallas police and TxDOT can have an abandoned vehicle tagged and removed after a short window, often as little as 24 hours on a high-volume freeway shoulder. Do not leave your car unattended on any highway shoulder and assume it will be there in the morning.

**Q: Does AAA cover highway tows in the Dallas metro area?**
A: Yes, AAA's Basic membership covers tows up to 5 miles. The Plus level covers up to 100 miles. For a highway breakdown in Dallas where your shop might be across town, the Plus tier is the one that actually helps. Anything beyond your covered mileage, you pay the per-mile rate on the overage.

**Q: Are after-hours tow rates higher in Dallas?**
A: Yes. Most Dallas towing companies add a surcharge for calls between roughly 10 PM and 6 AM, on weekends, and on major holidays. That premium runs $25 to $75 depending on the company.
