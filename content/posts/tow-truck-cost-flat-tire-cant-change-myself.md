> **Quick Answer:** A tow truck for a flat tire costs $75-200 depending on distance and time. Roadside tire change service runs $50-100. Check if your insurance, AAA, or car warranty covers roadside assistance first. Many services will change your tire on-site instead of towing.

## What To Do

1. **Check your coverage first**
   - Call your insurance company's roadside number
   - Check if you have AAA membership
   - Look for manufacturer roadside assistance (often covers 3-5 years)

2. **Call roadside tire service instead of towing**
   - Search "mobile tire service near me"
   - Many will change your tire for $50-100
   - Much cheaper than towing to a shop

3. **Get a towing quote if needed**
   - Ask for flat rate pricing upfront
   - Confirm mileage charges
   - Request drop-off at closest tire shop

4. **Consider your spare tire situation**
   - Full-size spare: any tire shop can mount it
   - Donut spare: drive directly to tire shop under 50 mph
   - No spare: you need a new tire or mobile service

Most people skip straight to calling a tow when a roadside tire change is both faster and cheaper. A mobile tech drives to you, swaps in your spare, and you're moving again in 20-40 minutes. A tow gets you to a shop, then you still wait for a tire. That's two waits, one higher bill, and a car you can't drive home until the shop finishes.

If you have a donut spare, treat it as a one-trip solution only. They're rated for 50 mph and typically 50-70 miles maximum. Don't run errands on it, don't take the highway if you can help it, and don't ignore the low-profile look. Donut spares sit at a smaller diameter than your drive wheels, which stresses your differential if you leave it on too long, especially on all-wheel-drive vehicles. Get to a shop the same day.

If the tire is damaged beyond a plug or patch, such as a sidewall blowout, a run-flat puncture outside the center tread, or any tire that's been driven on while flat, you need a full replacement. Running even 200 feet on a completely flat tire wrecks the sidewall and often the wheel rim. At that point, a mobile tech can still bring a replacement tire to you in some metro areas, but calling ahead and confirming they carry your size is important before you wait an hour for the wrong tire.

## What It Might Cost

**Towing charges:**
- Local tow (under 10 miles): $75-150
- Highway tow: $100-200
- After hours/weekends: add $25-50

**Roadside tire change:**
- Mobile tire service: $50-100
- AAA service call: $0 with membership
- Insurance roadside: often free or $25 deductible

**New tire costs:**
- Budget tires: $60-120 installed
- Mid-range: $100-200 installed
- High-end: $150-400 installed

A few cost details worth knowing. Towing companies charge a hook-up fee just for showing up, usually $50-75, and then a per-mile rate of $3-7 on top of that. A 15-mile tow can easily hit $150 before extras. After-hours calls on holidays can push that higher. Always ask for the total estimate before you agree, not just the per-mile rate.

If you have comprehensive or full-coverage auto insurance, check whether roadside assistance is bundled in before you pay out of pocket. Many policies include it for $5-10 per year added to your premium, meaning the call itself costs nothing beyond your deductible, which is often zero for roadside. Credit cards from Visa, Chase, and American Express also carry roadside benefits for cardholders that many people forget entirely.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can a mobile tire service come to me on the highway?**
A: Yes, most mobile tire services and roadside assistance providers will come to a highway shoulder. Give them your exact milepost number or nearest exit to speed up response. Stay in your vehicle with hazards on until they arrive.

**Q: Is it worth joining AAA just for a flat tire situation?**
A: Basic AAA membership runs about $60-80 per year and covers multiple service calls including towing up to 5 miles free and unlimited tire changes. If you drive frequently or have an older vehicle, it pays for itself after one or two calls.

**Q: What if I have no spare tire and no run-flat tires?**
A: Call a mobile tire service first and confirm they can bring your tire size to your location. If that is not available, you need a tow to the nearest shop. Do not attempt to drive on a flat, even slowly, as even a short distance can destroy the rim and add $150-400 in wheel damage to your bill.

## Stay Safe

- Pull completely off the road, hazards on
- Stay inside your vehicle if on a busy highway
- Don't attempt tire changes on dangerous shoulders
- Keep your phone charged for service calls
- Have your location ready (mile markers, exit numbers)
