A 50-mile tow can cost $225 to $475. After-hours, weekend, and freeway calls push the number up. Get a price confirmed before the truck moves your car.

## What You'll Pay: Local Towing in Dallas

Local means short haul. Most Dallas tow companies define that as anything under 10 miles, though some cap it at 5. You're paying two things: a hookup fee and a mileage rate.

Hookup fee: $50 to $100 in most parts of Dallas. This covers the truck showing up and attaching to your vehicle.

Mileage on top of that: $3 to $5 per mile for the first 10 miles.

So a 5-mile tow from a parking lot on Greenville Ave to a shop in East Dallas might cost $75 to $125 total. A 10-mile tow from Irving to Uptown could hit $125 to $150.

Dallas traffic is a factor. If the driver is sitting in I-35 or LBJ Freeway congestion to reach you, some companies add a wait or congestion surcharge. Ask before you commit.

## What You'll Pay: Long Distance Towing in Dallas

Long distance starts where local ends. Once you're past that 10-mile threshold, most Dallas tow operators charge $4 to $7 per mile. Premium flatbed service and specialty vehicle transport sit at the higher end.

Real-world examples:

- Dallas to Fort Worth (35 miles): $190 to $345
- Dallas to Waco (100 miles): $450 to $750
- Dallas to Houston (240 miles): $1,020 to $1,680
- Dallas to Austin (195 miles): $830 to $1,365

These are cash rates without insurance. If you have roadside coverage, your insurer may cap reimbursement at a flat dollar amount regardless of distance. ## What Drives the Price Up in Dallas Specifically

North Texas heat: Summer breakdowns mean high call volume. June through August, response times stretch and some operators bump rates during peak hours.

Freeway location: Towing from I-635, I-20, or the Dallas North Tollway involves more risk for the driver and usually a premium. Budget $50 to $100 extra if you're on a controlled-access road.

Toll roads: DFW has extensive tolling infrastructure. If the tow truck uses a toll route to reach you or deliver your car, that cost often passes to you. Confirm upfront.

Impound vs. private tow: If your car was impounded by Dallas PD or a parking enforcement contractor, rates are set by city permit and are non-negotiable. You'll pay the posted rate plus a storage fee for every day it sits.

## Stay Safe

- Pull as far off the road as possible. On Dallas freeways, get past the white line if you can.
- Turn on hazard lights immediately, even in daylight.
- Stay in your car on highway shoulders. Dallas traffic moves fast and drivers do not always move over.
- Never stand between your car and oncoming traffic while waiting.
- If you're in an unsafe location, call 911 first, then the tow company.
- At night, keep your interior dome light off so you can see approaching vehicles.

## Common Questions

**Q: Do Dallas tow companies charge extra if I break down on the highway?**
A: Yes, most add a surcharge of $50 to $100 for freeway or controlled-access road pickups on roads like I-635 or I-20, due to the added risk for the driver. Always confirm this fee when you call for a quote.

**Q: Will my roadside assistance plan cover the full cost of a long-distance tow from Dallas?**
A: Most roadside plans cap reimbursement at a flat dollar amount, often $50 to $100, regardless of how far the tow actually goes. For a haul over 50 miles, you will likely owe the difference out of pocket, so check your policy limit before authorizing the tow.

**Q: Is it cheaper to call a tow truck during the day in Dallas?**
A: Daytime weekday rates are generally the lowest, with after-hours and weekend calls adding $50 to $100 to your total. Summer months also see higher demand and occasional rate increases, so timing your call during business hours can save you money if your situation is not urgent.
