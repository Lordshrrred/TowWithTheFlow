> **Quick Answer:** Without insurance or a membership, you pay per service. A local tow typically runs $75 to $200 including the hookup fee and first few miles. Jump-starts, lockouts, tire changes, and fuel delivery usually run $50 to $150 each. Nights, weekends, highway locations, and heavy or all-wheel-drive vehicles push prices up. Always ask for a total quote before the truck leaves the dispatch lot.

## How Much Does a Tow Cost If I Have No Insurance or Membership?

Expect $75 to $200 for a local tow, and more for anything long, late, or complicated. The price is usually a hookup fee (often $50 to $125) plus a per-mile charge, commonly $3 to $7 per mile after the first few miles.

A 5-mile tow to a shop might land around $100 to $150. A 30-mile tow can reach $250 or more. Flatbed trucks cost more than hook-and-chain wheel-lift trucks, but flatbeds are the right choice for all-wheel-drive and low-clearance cars. See [towing a car with all wheel drive](/towing-a-car-with-all-wheel-drive-damage-risks/) before you agree to a wheel-lift.

City prices run higher. For examples, see what towing costs in [Austin](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-in-austin-texas/) or [Los Angeles](/how-much-does-a-tow-truck-cost-in-los-angeles-california/).

## What Do Jump-Starts, Lockouts, and Tire Changes Cost Without Coverage?

Most single-service calls cost $50 to $150, and the price depends on the service and the time of day. Typical ranges:

| Service | Typical Price |
|---|---|
| Jump-start | $50 to $100 |
| Lockout | $60 to $150 |
| Tire change (you have a good spare) | $60 to $125 |
| Fuel delivery | $60 to $120 plus the fuel |
| Winching or recovery from a ditch | $150 to $400+ |

Fuel delivery usually carries a service fee on top of the gas itself, and some companies cap how many gallons they'll bring.

A jump only works if the battery will take a charge. If the car clicks but won't crank again after the jump, you likely have a failed battery or alternator, and the next call is a tow. If you can't tell, our guide on [dead battery or something else](/car-wont-start-in-cold-weather-dead-battery-or-something-else/) walks through it.

## Why Is the Price Higher at Night, on Weekends, or on the Highway?

Because you are paying for urgency, and the extra can add 25% to 100% to the bill. After-hours surcharges, holiday rates, and weekend rates are standard. Highway recoveries cost more because they are riskier for the driver, and some states or counties assign highway tows to rotation companies with set rates.

More detail is in [how much emergency towing costs on weekends](/how-much-does-emergency-towing-cost-on-weekends/).

Heavy vehicles, lifted trucks, vans, and cars stuck in mud, snow, or a parking structure also cost more. Mention these details when you call so the quote is accurate.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged When I Have No Choice?

Get a total price in writing or by text before the driver hooks up your car. Ask these exact questions:

- What is the hookup fee and what does it include?
- What is the per-mile rate, and where does mileage start counting?
- Are there after-hours, fuel, or fuel-surcharge

## Can I Get Cheaper Help Than Paying Per Call?

Often yes, even without car insurance. Before you pay out of pocket, check these in order:

1. **Credit card benefits.** Many cards include roadside dispatch or reimbursement. See [whether your credit card covers roadside assistance](/does-my-credit-card-cover-roadside-assistance-towing/).
2. **Auto manufacturer roadside programs.** Many new and certified pre-owned vehicles include free roadside help for a set period. Check your owner's manual or the manufacturer's app.
3. **Renter or rideshare coverage.** If you are driving a rental, call the rental company first.
4. **A membership bought on the spot.** Some clubs let you join and use service the same day, but many have a waiting period or charge extra for a first-day call. Ask before assuming.

If you truly can't afford the tow, read [what to do when your car broke down and you have no money](/car-broke-down-no-money-for-tow-truck-what-are-my-options/).

Do not leave the car in an unsafe spot to save money. If you are on a shoulder or in a travel lane, call 911 first, turn on hazards, and get everyone out and away from traffic.

## Common Questions

**Q: What is the cheapest way to get a tow without insurance?**
A: Call two or three local companies and ask for an all-in price, including hookup, mileage, and any fees. Choose the shortest tow distance you can, such as the nearest reputable shop instead of your preferred shop across town.

**Q: Do I have to pay the tow driver in cash?**
A: Not always. Many companies take cards, but some small operators are cash only. Ask when you call so you aren't stuck with an unexpected payment problem.

**Q: Can I buy roadside assistance after my car already broke down?**
A: Sometimes you can join a motor club the same day, but many have waiting periods, and a breakdown that already happened is usually not covered. Ask directly before paying for a membership as a workaround.

**Q: Is a flatbed more expensive than a regular tow truck?**
A: Usually a little, often $25 to $75 more. A flatbed is the safer choice for all-wheel-drive, low-clearance, and damaged vehicles, so the extra cost is usually worth it.

**Q: Will the tow driver charge me if I cancel before they arrive?**
A: Many companies charge a dispatch or cancellation fee, especially if the truck is already on the way. Ask what the cancellation policy is when you call, and cancel as early as you can.
