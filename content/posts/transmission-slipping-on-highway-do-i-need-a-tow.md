> **Quick Answer:** Yes, pull over safely and call a tow. A slipping transmission on the highway means the clutch packs or bands inside are failing and driving further will turn a repair into a rebuild or replacement. If the slip is severe (engine revs shoot up but the car barely accelerates), do not drive it. If it's mild and only happened once, you may be able to limp a short distance, but the safest call is a flatbed.

## How Do I Know If My Transmission Is Actually Slipping or Just Shifting Hard?

Slipping feels like the engine revs climb normally but the car does not accelerate with them. You press the gas, the tachometer jumps to 3,500 RPM, and the speedometer barely moves. That gap between engine effort and forward motion is the slip. A hard shift feels different: the car jerks or clunks when it changes gears, but speed does follow the throttle. Both need a mechanic, but slipping is the more urgent of the two. Shuddering between gears, a burning smell from the vents, or the transmission warning light alongside those revving symptoms confirms you are dealing with a slip.

## Can I Drive a Slipping Transmission to the Nearest Exit or Should I Stop Immediately?

If the slip is constant and the car is struggling to maintain speed at 65 mph, stop now. Get to the right shoulder, activate your hazard lights, and stay in the car with your seatbelt on until traffic clears before you exit. Driving a severely slipping automatic transmission at highway speed overheats the transmission fluid fast. Burnt ATF (automatic transmission fluid) destroys friction material inside the clutch packs, and once that lining is gone it scores the metal drums underneath. What might have been a $1,500 solenoid or clutch pack job becomes a $3,500 to $5,000 rebuild. If the slip happened once and the car is driving normally now, you may have a mile or two of margin to reach a safe exit. Do not push it further than the nearest safe stopping point.

## What Does a Tow Cost for a Transmission Problem on the Highway?

Expect to pay $75 to $125 for a local tow under 5 miles, and $3 to $5 per mile beyond that if you are paying out of pocket without insurance or a roadside plan. Flatbed towing is the right call for most front-wheel-drive and all-wheel-drive vehicles with a bad transmission because wheel-lift towing can damage the drivetrain further when the transmission is already compromised. If you have AAA, roadside assistance through [State Farm](/state-farm-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-limit/) or [GEICO](/geico-roadside-assistance-towing-distance-limit/), or roadside coverage through your insurer, call them first. Many plans cover the tow at no cost up to a set mileage limit.

## What Should I Tell the Tow Truck Driver and the Shop?

Tell the driver you have a transmission problem and you need a flatbed, not a wheel-lift. Give them your exact milepost or highway marker. Most state highways post mile markers every tenth of a mile on green or white signs on the right shoulder. Note what the car

## Common Questions

**Q: My transmission slipped once on the highway and feels fine now. Is it safe to keep driving?**
A: One slip followed by normal behavior usually means low fluid, a faulty solenoid, or overheating. Check the transmission fluid level when the engine is warm and the car is in park. If it is low or smells burnt, do not drive it further. If the fluid looks normal, you can try driving to a nearby shop, but the problem will return and worsen quickly.

**Q: Will a transmission slip trigger the check engine light?**
A: Often yes. A slipping transmission can throw codes like P0720 (output speed sensor), P0740 (torque converter clutch solenoid), or P0730 (incorrect gear ratio). If your check engine light is flashing rather than staying steady, that is a more serious signal and you should pull over immediately. See [what a flashing check engine light means](/check-engine-light-flashing-should-i-pull-over-now/) for more detail.

**Q: Can I add transmission fluid on the side of the road to fix the slip?**
A: If the fluid is low, topping it off can stop a slip temporarily. Carry a bottle of the correct type (check your owner's manual: Dexron VI, Mercon SP, Toyota WS, and Honda ATF-DW1 are not interchangeable). But if the fluid is burnt or the level was not low, adding fluid will not help. Treat it as a temporary measure, not a fix.

**Q: Is it okay to tow my car with the transmission in neutral?**
A: For most vehicles, flatbed towing is safest. If a wheel-lift or dolly is the only option, the drive wheels need to be off the ground. For a front-wheel-drive car with a bad transmission, lift the front. For a rear-wheel-drive car, lift the rear. Never flat-tow (all four wheels on the ground) a vehicle with a damaged automatic transmission. The transmission is not receiving fluid pressure when the engine is off, and dragging it can destroy internal components.

**Q: How much does it cost to repair a slipping transmission versus replacing it?**
A: A solenoid replacement runs $150 to $400 parts and labor. A clutch pack rebuild inside the transmission is typically $1,200 to $2,500. A full rebuilt or remanufactured transmission replacement runs $2,500 to $5,000 or more depending on the vehicle. Catching it early, before you burn the friction material, is the difference between a solenoid job and a full replacement.
