> **Quick Answer:** Sometimes. Basic factory warranties like bumper-to-bumper and powertrain cover repairs, not towing. Most automakers bundle free roadside assistance with a new-car warranty, and that includes a tow, usually to the nearest dealer. Extended warranties and certified pre-owned plans often include towing too. Check your warranty booklet or owner's manual for the roadside section before you pay a driver.

## Does My Factory Warranty Cover Towing?

Usually yes, but not through the warranty itself. The tow comes from the roadside assistance program that automakers attach to the new-car warranty.

A bumper-to-bumper or powertrain warranty covers parts and labor to fix defects. It does not say "we pay for a tow." The separate roadside assistance benefit does, and it typically runs for the same period as the basic warranty or longer.

Here's what that benefit usually looks like:

- Tow to the nearest authorized dealer, or within a set mileage cap
- Jump-start, flat tire change, fuel delivery, and lockout help
- Available 24/7 through a number in your owner's manual or on your key fob card

The catch: coverage often depends on the breakdown being a mechanical failure. A tow after a collision, or from damage you caused, is generally not covered. That is a different claim, usually through your insurer. See [does Progressive cover towing after an accident](/does-progressive-cover-towing-after-accident/) for how that side works.

## What Does a Tow Cost If My Warranty Doesn't Cover It?

Expect $75 to $150 for a local tow of the first few miles, then roughly $3 to $7 for each additional mile. Rates run higher at night, on weekends, and in heavy traffic. Flatbeds cost more than hook-and-chain trucks.

All-wheel-drive and electric vehicles usually need a flatbed, which pushes the price up. Dragging an AWD car on two wheels can wreck the drivetrain. More on that in [towing a car with all wheel drive](/towing-a-car-with-all-wheel-drive-damage-risks/).

If you're stuck with an out-of-pocket bill, compare it against [what roadside assistance costs without insurance](/how-much-does-roadside-assistance-cost-without-insurance/) before you accept a quote.

## Which Warranty Types Actually Include Towing?

Warranties that include a tow are factory roadside programs, most certified pre-owned plans, and the better extended plans. A powertrain-only warranty alone does not.

| Coverage type | Towing included? |
|---|---|
| New-car bumper-to-bumper | Usually, via roadside assistance |
| Powertrain warranty alone | Usually no |
| Certified pre-owned (CPO) | Often yes, varies by brand |
| Manufacturer extended warranty | Often yes, check the contract |
| Third-party extended warranty | Varies widely, read the fine print |
| Used car dealer "30-day" warranty | Rarely |

Third-party extended warranties are the biggest gamble. Some include towing with a cap of $50 to $150 per event. Others reimburse only after the repair is approved. Look for "towing," "roadside," and "reimbursement" in the contract itself, not the sales brochure.

Your credit card may also help. See [whether your credit card covers roadside assistance towing](/does-my-credit-card-cover-roadside-assistance-towing/).

The order that protects your money:

1. Get safe first. Hazards on, move to the shoulder, exit the vehicle on the side away from traffic if you're on a highway.
2. Find the roadside number in your owner's manual, the warranty booklet, or the manufacturer's app.
3. Have your VIN, location, and mileage ready. The VIN is on the driver's side of the dashboard, visible through the windshield.
4. Ask where they will tow it and how many miles are covered.
5. Write down the claim number and the driver's company name.

If you already called a tow truck, don't assume you're out of luck. Some programs reimburse with receipts. The same logic applies to club memberships, covered in [does AAA pay back towing costs if I already called a tow truck](/does-aaa-pay-back-towing-costs-if-i-already-called-a-tow-truck/).

## What Won't a Warranty Tow Cover?

Most warranty tows exclude accidents, flat tires from road hazards on some plans, lockouts, and mileage beyond the cap. If the cause is your own neglect, the claim can be denied entirely.

Common denial reasons:

- Dead battery after the battery warranty expired
- Running out of fuel on some plans
- Breakdown caused by an aftermarket modification
- Towing past the covered distance to a shop you prefer
- Vehicle used for commercial work like rideshare or delivery

Mileage caps are the surprise most people hit. A 25-mile cap means you pay for every mile beyond it. Ask upfront, and if the nearest dealer is farther than the cap, ask about the nearest repair facility instead.

Rental cars follow different rules, so check [rental car insurance towing coverage](/does-rental-car-insurance-cover-towing-cost/) if that's your situation.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does a powertrain warranty cover towing if my engine fails?**
A: Not by itself. It pays to repair the engine or transmission, not to move the car. Towing usually comes from the manufacturer's roadside assistance, so call that line first.

**Q: Will my car warranty pay me back if I already paid for a tow?**
A: Sometimes. Many programs reimburse only if you kept receipts and the breakdown was a covered mechanical failure, and often only up to a set dollar amount. Call the roadside line and ask before you assume you're covered.

**Q: How long does factory roadside assistance last?**
A: It usually tracks the basic warranty term, commonly 3 to 5 years depending on the brand, and some automakers run it longer. Your owner's manual or warranty booklet lists the exact term and mileage limits.

**Q: Does an extended warranty cover towing to any shop I choose?**
A: Rarely. Most plans tow to the nearest authorized facility or the repair shop they approve, and cap the distance. A tow to a shop you pick yourself is often only partly reimbursed or not at all.

**Q: Is towing covered if my car dies right after I bought it used?**
A: Only if the sale came with a warranty or CPO plan that includes roadside. A standard "as-is" used car sale covers nothing. Check your paperwork, then your insurance, then your credit card.
