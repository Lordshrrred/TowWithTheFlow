> **Quick Answer:** The vehicle owner is responsible for the tow and storage fees in most cases, but only if the tow was legal. Property owners must follow state and local rules: posted signs, valid authorization, and a licensed tow company. If those rules were broken, the property owner or tow company can be held liable for the charges and any damage. Document everything before you pay.

## Who Is Responsible When My Car Is Towed From Private Property?

You are responsible for the fees if the tow was lawful, and the property owner or tow company is responsible if it wasn't. That split is the whole game.

A tow from private property is generally legal when the lot has clear signage about parking restrictions, the property owner or an authorized agent requested the tow, and the tow company holds the required local license. Rules differ by state and city, so what's required in one place may not apply in another.

Responsibility breaks down like this:

- **You:** Tow fee, daily storage, and any gate or release fees, when the tow was valid.
- **The property owner:** Liable if they had a car towed without proper signage, without a real violation, or in a way that breaks local law.
- **The tow company:** Liable for damage during the hook-up or transport, and for charges above what local law allows.

If you were parked in a visitor spot at your own apartment complex, a fire lane, or a spot that was never marked restricted, that matters. Write down exactly where you were parked.

## What Do I Do Right Now If My Car Was Towed From a Lot?

First confirm it was towed and not stolen, then document the scene before you do anything else. Call the non-emergency police line, because many cities require tow companies to report private-property tows within a short window, and police can tell you which company has your car.

Then:

1. **Photograph the signs.** Take wide shots showing the sign placement and close-ups of the text. Note whether signs are visible from where you parked, whether they list the tow company, and whether the lettering is legible.
2. **Photograph where you were parked.** Show lines, curbs, and any markings.
3. **Get the tow company's name and address** from the posted sign or police.
4. **Ask for an itemized bill** before paying anything.
5. **Inspect the car before you leave the yard.** Check bumpers, wheels, and undercarriage. If you spot damage, photograph it there and note it on the receipt. Our guide on [what happens if a tow truck damages your car](/what-happens-if-tow-truck-damages-my-car/) walks through the claim process.

Don't sign anything that waives your right to dispute. If the yard demands payment before releasing the car, pay with a credit card, not cash, and write "paid under protest" on the receipt. A card gives you a chargeback path if the tow turns out to be illegal.

## How Much Can a Tow Company Legally Charge for a Private Property Tow?

Many states and cities cap private property tow rates, and a tow company that exceeds the cap is charging you illegally. The cap, where one exists, usually covers the base hook-up fee, a per-day storage rate, and an after-hours release fee.

## Can I Fight a Private Property Tow I Think Was Illegal?

Yes. Pay first to get your car out, then dispute afterward, because storage fees grow every day and the yard has a lien on your vehicle.

Your options:

- **Small claims court.** Most states allow you to sue for the tow and storage fees, plus damage. Bring your photos, the itemized bill, and the receipt marked "paid under protest."
- **Tow hearing.** Some cities and counties offer an administrative hearing specifically for disputed tows, often with a short filing window. Ask the tow company or your city's consumer or transportation office whether one exists locally.
- **Credit card chargeback.** If you paid by card and the tow was clearly illegal, ask your issuer about a dispute.
- **Complaint to the licensing authority.** Tow companies are usually licensed by the state or city. A complaint can cost them their license.

If you can't afford the fees right now, read [what to do when you have no money for a tow truck](/car-broke-down-no-money-for-tow-truck-what-are-my-options/). Don't let a car sit in the yard hoping to sort it out later. Storage compounds, and unclaimed vehicles can be sold at lien auction.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can a property owner tow my car without warning?**
A: In many places, yes, if signs are properly posted. Some jurisdictions require specific signage, others require a warning period for residents. The sign requirements vary by city, so photograph what was posted.

**Q: Who pays if my car is damaged during a private property tow?**
A: The tow company is liable for damage it causes during hook-up, transport, or storage. Document damage at the yard before leaving and file a claim with the company's insurer.

**Q: Can I get my belongings out of a towed car?**
A: Often yes. Many places require yards to let you retrieve personal items, such as medication, child seats, and documents, without paying the full fee first. Ask, and stay polite but firm.

**Q: Can a tow company refuse to release my car if I can't pay right away?**
A: Generally yes, because they hold a lien on the vehicle. Many places do cap how long they can hold it before sale and require notice before auction. Contact them quickly about payment arrangements.

**Q: Does my car insurance cover a private property tow?**
A: Usually not, since it's a parking violation rather than a covered loss. Some roadside plans don't cover it either. Check your policy language rather than assuming.
