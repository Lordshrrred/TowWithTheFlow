> **Quick Answer:** If the transmission is slipping badly, meaning RPMs flare without the car accelerating, it drops out of gear, or it won't hold gear under load, tow it. Every mile you drive on a badly slipping transmission burns clutch material and can turn a repairable problem into a full rebuild. A mild, occasional slip with a shop under two miles away is the only case where driving is defensible.

## Can I Drive My Car to the Mechanic With a Badly Slipping Transmission?

No. If the slip is bad, tow it. Slipping means the clutch packs or bands inside the transmission are not gripping, and the friction from slipping burns off their lining and sends metal and debris through the fluid. Each mile makes the damage worse.

Here is how to judge "bad":

- **Tow it:** RPMs jump to 4,000 or more while the car barely speeds up, the car loses drive entirely at times, it won't climb a hill, it slams into gear, or you smell something burnt.
- **Maybe drive it, carefully:** one brief flare between shifts, then normal driving, and the shop is within a mile or two on flat roads.

If you're unsure which side you're on, you're on the tow side. A $100 tow beats turning a $400 to $1,500 repair into a $3,500 to $6,000 rebuild or replacement. For a closer look at the warning signs, see [Transmission Slipping While Driving: Should I Pull Over Now?](/transmission-slipping-while-driving-should-i-pull-over-now/)

## What Should I Do Right Now If It Starts Slipping on the Road?

Ease off the gas, get to a safe spot, and stop. Do not floor it to force the car through the slip, because that grinds the clutch material faster.

1. Turn on your hazards and move to the right shoulder or a parking lot.
2. Put the car in park and let it idle for a minute.
3. Check the dash for a transmission temperature or check engine light.
4. Pop the hood and check the fluid if your car has a dipstick (many newer cars do not, and you should not pry at a sealed unit).

Healthy transmission fluid is bright red or pink and smells slightly sweet. Dark brown or black fluid that smells burnt means the clutches are already cooking. Low fluid is the one case where a top-off might get you moving, but low fluid means a leak, so it's a temporary fix at best.

If you're on a freeway shoulder, get away from traffic and read [Car Died on Highway Shoulder: Is It Safe to Wait for a Tow Truck?](/car-died-on-highway-shoulder-safe-to-wait-for-tow-truck/) before you settle in to wait.

## Does the Type of Transmission Change the Answer?

Not the answer, but it changes what's likely wrong. Slipping usually means low or burnt fluid, worn clutch packs, or a failing torque converter in a traditional automatic. In a CVT (common in Nissan, Subaru, and Honda models), slipping can feel like a rubber-band surge or a whine, and it often means a worn belt or pulley. CVTs are the least forgiving of continued driving.

## Do I Need a Flatbed to Tow a Car With a Bad Transmission?

Yes, a flatbed is the safest choice, and you should ask for one by name. If the transmission can't hold gear, towing on two wheels (a wheel-lift) can spin internal parts without lubrication. That is especially true for front-wheel-drive cars with the rear wheels on the ground, and for all-wheel-drive vehicles in nearly every case.

Do not shift the car into neutral and let someone drag it behind a tow strap or tow it a long distance on its own wheels. Flatbed towing is typically priced by the hook-up fee plus a per-mile rate, and costs vary a lot by city and time of day. ## Common Questions

**Q: Is it safe to drive a car with a slipping transmission for a short distance?**
A: Only if the slip is mild and occasional and the shop is very close on flat roads. If the car flares RPMs hard, loses drive, or smells burnt, even a short drive can cause serious internal damage.

**Q: Will adding transmission fluid fix a slipping transmission?**
A: It can help if low fluid is the cause, but low fluid means there is a leak somewhere. Burnt, dark fluid means clutch damage has already started, and a top-off won't fix it.

**Q: How much does it cost to fix a slipping transmission?**
A: A fluid service or solenoid replacement can run a few hundred dollars. A rebuild typically runs $2,500 to $4,500, and a full replacement often runs $3,500 to $6,000 depending on the vehicle. Get a written estimate before approving work.

**Q: Why does my car rev high but not accelerate?**
A: That is the classic sign of slipping: the engine is spinning, but the transmission isn't passing the power to the wheels. Common causes are worn clutch packs, low fluid, or a failing torque converter.

**Q: Can I be stranded if I keep driving a slipping transmission?**
A: Yes. A slipping transmission can fail completely without warning, leaving you with no drive on a highway or an intersection. If that happens, see [Car Won't Go Into Gear Automatic Transmission Emergency](/car-wont-go-into-gear-automatic-transmission-emergency/) for what to do next.
