> **Quick Answer:** Pull far right onto the shoulder, turn on hazards, exit away from traffic, set up warning triangles behind your car, and work quickly. Houston's heavy traffic makes highway tire changes extremely dangerous. Call for roadside assistance when possible.

## What To Do

1. **Get off the road immediately.** Pull as far right as possible onto the shoulder. Avoid stopping on bridges, curves, or hills where visibility is poor. If your nearest exit is only a quarter mile away and the car can limp that far, it is worth losing the rim to get off the highway entirely. A destroyed rim costs far less than a highway collision.

2. **Make yourself visible.** Turn on hazard lights and interior dome light. If you have emergency flares or reflective triangles, place them 100-200 feet behind your vehicle. At night or in rain, add a second triangle at 300 feet. Drivers need extra distance to react on high-speed roads.

3. **Exit safely.** Get out on the passenger side, away from traffic. Keep everyone away from the traffic side of the vehicle. Passengers should move up the embankment or behind a guardrail if one is present, never stand between the car and the lane.

4. **Assess the situation.** On busy Houston highways like I-45, I-10, or the Beltway, consider calling for professional help instead of changing the tire yourself. If you are in a high-speed merge zone, near an overpass, or if it is raining hard enough to reduce visibility to under a few hundred feet, changing the tire yourself is not worth the risk.

5. **If you must change it yourself:**
   - Loosen lug nuts before jacking up the car. Break them loose while the weight of the car still holds the wheel steady.
   - Place the jack under the manufacturer's designated jack point, not under the rocker panel or you risk bending the pinch weld.
   - Place the jack on solid, level ground. On Houston's chip-seal shoulders, use a small board under the base if the surface is crumbling.
   - Raise the vehicle just enough to remove the flat tire, typically 2 to 3 inches of clearance.
   - Install the spare tire and hand-tighten lug nuts in a star pattern to seat the wheel evenly.
   - Lower the vehicle and fully tighten lug nuts in a star pattern. Torque to 80 to 100 foot-pounds if you have a torque wrench, or as tight as you can go with a standard lug wrench using body weight.

6. **Work quickly but safely.** Highway shoulders are not safe spaces. Complete the change as fast as possible without rushing safety steps. Studies from TxDOT show that stopped vehicles on highway shoulders are struck far more often than most drivers expect, even with hazards running.

7. **Get moving.** Pack up tools quickly and merge back into traffic carefully. Drive directly to a tire shop to replace or repair the damaged tire. Most compact spares are rated for no more than 50 mph and 50 to 70 miles of use. Do not take the spare onto I-10 at highway speeds.

## Houston-Specific Considerations

Houston's highways are notoriously busy with aggressive drivers and limited visibility during rain. The city's flat terrain helps with tire changes, but the constant traffic flow creates serious safety risks.

Summer heat makes tire failures more common and metal tools dangerously hot to handle. Asphalt surface temperatures in Houston regularly exceed 150 degrees Fahrenheit from June through September, which means kneeling on the shoulder without gloves or knee protection is genuinely painful and can cause burns. Keep work gloves in your emergency kit year-round.

Afternoon thunderstorms are another real hazard. Wet lug nuts are harder to break loose, jacks can shift on saturated ground, and passing drivers have significantly reduced stopping distance. If it is actively raining hard, call for help rather than attempting the change yourself.

Houston also has a high rate of secondary crashes, meaning collisions caused by drivers rubbernecking at a stopped vehicle. Position yourself as far from the lane as possible and never assume passing drivers can see you clearly.

## Common Questions

**Q: Is it safe to change a tire on the Houston highway shoulder at night?**
A: It is significantly more dangerous at night than during the day. If you must do it, place all your reflective triangles, turn on every light the car has, and use a headlamp so your hands are free. If you have any doubt, stay inside with your seatbelt on and call for roadside assistance.

**Q: How far can I drive on a flat before I have to stop?**
A: On a true flat with no air pressure, you should stop within a quarter mile or less. Driving further shreds the tire and can damage the rim and suspension. If you feel a blowout, slow down gradually and steer to the shoulder without braking hard.

**Q: Can a plugged tire be used long-term after a highway flat?**
A: A plug alone is a temporary fix. A proper patch-plug combination installed from inside the tire by a shop is a permanent repair for punctures in the tread area. Sidewall damage means the tire must be replaced, no repair is safe there.

- Call 511 for traffic updates before starting work
- Have someone call 911 if you feel unsafe
