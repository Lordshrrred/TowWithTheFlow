> **Quick Answer:** Who pays depends on your coverage. If you have roadside assistance through your insurer, AAA, or your car manufacturer, they typically cover a snow extraction or tow. Without that, you pay out of pocket: expect $75 to $150 for a local tow, more if you're in a ditch or on a rural road. Check your policy before you call a random tow truck.

## Who Actually Pays When Your Car Gets Stuck in Snow?

Your insurance company, your roadside assistance plan, or you pay, and which one depends entirely on what you're carrying. Roadside assistance add-ons from insurers like State Farm, GEICO, Liberty Mutual, and USAA typically cover snow extractions and tows, but the reimbursement cap varies by policy, often $50 to $100 per incident, which may not cover the full bill. AAA covers towing to the nearest approved facility regardless of cause, including snow. If you have no roadside plan at all, you're paying the tow company directly.

One thing people miss: standard comprehensive or collision coverage does NOT pay towing costs unless you also have roadside assistance or towing and labor coverage added on. Check your declarations page right now if you're not sure.

## How Much Does a Snow Tow Actually Cost?

A basic snow tow on a flat road runs $75 to $150 for the first 5 to 7 miles. If your car slid off the road into a ditch, costs jump. A winch-out pull from a ditch typically costs $150 to $300 depending on the depth, angle, and how long it takes. If you're on a rural highway and the nearest truck is 20 miles away, add a per-mile fee, usually $3 to $7 per mile beyond the base.

If you went off-road into deep snow, say sliding off a mountain pass or into a median on I-80 in a blizzard, you may need a heavy-duty wrecker rather than a standard flatbed. That can push the bill to $400 or more. Get a price confirmed before you authorize the tow.

For a full picture of what towing costs look like depending on distance, see [how much to tow a car 100 miles on the interstate](/how-much-to-tow-car-100-miles-interstate/).

## Does Roadside Assistance Cover Snow Extractions?

Most roadside assistance plans cover snow extractions, but not all of them cover winch-outs or off-road recoveries. Read the fine print. USAA roadside assistance, for example, covers towing and basic extraction, but has per-incident dollar limits that can leave you with a balance if the job is complicated. AAA Classic covers up to 5 miles of towing per call, while AAA Plus and Premier offer significantly longer distances.

If you're trying to figure out what your specific insurer covers right now, [Liberty Mutual's roadside assistance towing coverage limits](/liberty-mutual-roadside-assistance-towing-coverage-limits/) and [USAA's towing coverage details](/usaa-towing-coverage-how-much-does-it-pay/) are worth reading to understand what a typical policy actually pays.

Get your car visible and stay warm. Turn on your hazard lights immediately. If you're on a road shoulder, stay in the car with your seatbelt on until help arrives. ## Will My Insurance Rate Go Up If I File a Snow Tow Claim?

Filing a roadside assistance claim almost never affects your premium. Roadside assistance claims are generally not counted as at-fault incidents and do not trigger surcharges the way collision claims do. You can use it without worrying about your rate going up.

Where people get confused: if the snow incident involved an accident, and you file a collision or comprehensive claim to cover vehicle damage, that can affect your rate depending on your insurer and your state. The towing portion attached to that accident claim is a different story than a standalone roadside assistance call.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I get reimbursed for a snow tow if I pay out of pocket first?**
A: Yes, most insurers allow reimbursement if you pay a tow company directly and submit a receipt. Call your insurer's roadside line, get a claim number, and keep the tow company's invoice. Reimbursement is usually processed within 5 to 10 business days.

**Q: Does AAA cover getting pulled out of a ditch in snow?**
A: AAA covers extraction from snow and mud as part of their standard service, but heavily off-road situations or very deep ditches may require a specialized recovery truck that AAA dispatches separately. Classic members get up to 5 tow miles included; Plus and Premier members get 100 miles or more.

**Q: What if I'm stuck on a highway and blocking traffic?**
A: Call 911 if you're a traffic hazard. State police or highway patrol can arrange emergency towing and help manage traffic. You may not have a choice of tow company in that situation, and state-contracted tow rates can be higher, so document everything for insurance reimbursement.

**Q: Is a winch-out the same as a tow, or is it billed separately?**
A: It is often billed separately. A winch-out is the labor to pull your car free; a tow is transporting it. Some companies bundle them, others don't. Always ask for an itemized quote before you authorize work.

**Q: My car slid on ice and hit a curb before getting stuck. Does insurance cover that damage too?**
A: Curb damage from sliding on ice is typically covered under collision coverage, not comprehensive. You'd pay your collision deductible. The tow is a separate line item, usually handled under roadside assistance.
