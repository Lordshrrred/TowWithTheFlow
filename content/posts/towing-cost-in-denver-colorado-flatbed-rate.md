> **Quick Answer:** A flatbed tow in Denver typically costs $95 to $185 for the first 5 to 7 miles, with a per-mile rate of $4 to $7 after that. Hook-up fees alone run $75 to $110. AWD vehicles, lowered cars, and anything that can't roll always require a flatbed, which costs $25 to $50 more than a wheel-lift on the same route. Expect higher totals on I-70 or during snow.

## What Does a Flatbed Tow Actually Cost in Denver Right Now?

A flatbed tow for a standard sedan in Denver runs $95 to $185 for a local haul under 7 miles. The hook-up fee (also called a base rate or dispatch fee) accounts for most of that: typically $75 to $110 just to get the truck to you and load the car. Per-mile charges kick in after the first few miles at $4 to $7 per mile. A 15-mile tow from the I-25 and Colorado Boulevard interchange down to a shop in Englewood would realistically land between $145 and $210 before any extras.

AWD vehicles like a Subaru Outback or Ford F-150 with four-wheel drive must ride on a flatbed because spinning a driveshaft on a two-wheel lift can destroy the transfer case. Operators know this and price accordingly. If your car has all-wheel drive, don't let anyone load it on a wheel-lift to save money.

For a full breakdown of what flatbed compares to wheel-lift pricing, see [Towing Cost in Denver Colorado Flatbed vs Wheel Lift](/towing-cost-in-denver-colorado-flatbed-vs-wheel-lift/).

## Where in Denver Will My Flatbed Tow Cost More?

Highway breakdowns and hard-to-reach locations push the price up. If you're broken down on I-70 through the Mousetrap (the I-25 and I-70 interchange), the driver has to navigate active freeway lanes to load you, and many companies add a $25 to $50 highway access fee. Same goes for I-270 near Commerce City or anywhere along the T-REX corridor on I-25.

Breakdowns in the West Highlands or along Colfax in the heart of the city are generally no problem. But if you're stuck in a parking structure in LoDo or RiNo, some flatbed operators will refuse the job outright because their trucks physically can't clear the ceiling height. In that case you need a wheel-lift or a smaller rollback, which changes your cost picture.

Mountain routes are a separate category entirely. I-70 west of C-470 heading into the foothills toward Idaho Springs or the Eisenhower Tunnel zone carries serious surcharges. See [Towing Cost in Denver Colorado Mountains](/towing-cost-denver-colorado-mountains/) if that's your situation.

## Does My Insurance or Roadside Coverage Pay for a Flatbed?

Most roadside assistance policies cover a flatbed tow up to a set dollar limit or mileage cap, not a flat "yes, you're covered" answer. AAA Plus and AAA Premier cover up to 100 and 200 miles respectively per tow, flatbed included. State Farm's roadside add-on typically covers actual towing costs up to a dollar limit per occurrence, which may not fully cover a flatbed in Denver if your bill hits $180 or more.

GEICO roadside assistance has a known mileage restriction on covered tows. If you're on GEICO, confirm your limit before the truck loads your car, because once it's loaded, you own the bill. For a detailed look at coverage limits by insurer, [How Much Does Roadside Assistance Cost in Denver Colorado](/how-much-does-roadside-assistance-cost-denver-colorado/) covers what you're actually getting for your money.

One practical tip: tell the dispatcher upfront that you need a flatbed and give the reason (AWD, low-profile tires, inoperable wheels). Some policies cover flatbeds only when required by vehicle type, not just because you prefer it.

## How Do I Avoid Getting Overcharged for a Flatbed in Denver?

Get the per-mile rate and base fee quoted before the truck rolls. Any legitimate Denver towing company will give you both numbers over the phone. Ask specifically: "What is your hook-up fee and your per-mile rate for a flatbed?" If they won't answer, call another company.

Colorado does not cap towing rates by state law the way some states do. That means pricing varies significantly between operators. A 10-mile flatbed tow could cost $130 with one company and $240 with another. The difference is usually the per-mile rate and whether they're adding questionable fees.

If your car is being towed after an accident and you didn't choose the tow company, you still have the right to have your vehicle released to a tow company of your choice once it reaches the impound lot, though a gate fee typically applies.

## Common Questions

**Q: Why does a flatbed cost more than a regular tow truck in Denver?**
A: Flatbed trucks have a larger hydraulic deck, take longer to load, and require more driver skill to operate safely. The higher equipment cost and loading time both get priced into the rate. You're typically paying $25 to $50 more than a wheel-lift for the same distance.

**Q: Is a flatbed required for all-wheel drive cars in Colorado?**
A: Yes. Towing an AWD vehicle with a wheel-lift while the drivetrain is engaged causes serious transfer case damage. Reputable operators will require a flatbed for any AWD or 4WD vehicle, and your insurance should cover it as a required service.

**Q: How long does a flatbed tow truck take to arrive in Denver?**
A: Most areas inside I-270 see response times of 30 to 60 minutes under normal conditions. High-demand periods like rush hour on I-25 or during a winter storm can push that to 90 minutes or longer. Call as early as possible and give a precise location, not just a street name.

**Q: Can a flatbed tow truck fit in a parking garage in Denver?**
A: Most full-size flatbeds cannot. Standard flatbed trucks are 8 to 9 feet tall and most downtown Denver parking structures top out at 7 feet. If you're stuck in a garage, you need a wheel-lift truck or a smaller rollback. Make sure you tell the dispatcher you're in a structure.

**Q: What if I can't afford the flatbed tow bill on the spot?**
A: Most Denver towing companies require payment before releasing your vehicle, not after. If you're truly stuck, call your insurance company first to see if they'll pay directly. Some operators accept credit cards on-site. Community assistance programs and some credit unions offer short-term emergency loans, but there's no city-run fund specifically for towing costs in Denver.
