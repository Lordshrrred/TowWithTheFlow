Your alternator just quit. The battery warning light is blazing, your headlights are dimming, the radio cut out, and the car is starting to feel sluggish. You have minutes, not hours, before the engine dies completely. Here is exactly what to do.

> **Quick Answer:** Yes, you need a tow. A dead alternator means your battery is the only thing keeping the engine running, and it will drain completely within minutes to roughly 30 minutes depending on how charged it was. Do not try to limp it to a shop. Get off the road now, turn off every electrical load you can (AC, radio, lights if it is safe to do so), and call for a tow. The car cannot recharge itself and it will stall, possibly in traffic.

## What Are the Signs That My Alternator Actually Died While Driving?

The battery warning light is the clearest sign, but it is rarely the only one. When an alternator fails completely, your car switches to running entirely off battery reserve. You will usually see the battery light come on first, then notice the dashboard lights flickering or dimming, the AC blower slowing down, power windows moving sluggishly, and the radio cutting in and out. On many GM and Ford vehicles, the instrument cluster will visibly dim before anything else.

If you check your voltmeter (if your car has one on the dash), a healthy charging system reads 13.5 to 14.8 volts at idle. A dead alternator will show 12.4 volts or lower, and that number will keep dropping as the battery drains.

A failing alternator can also cause the engine to run rough as voltage drops, because the ignition system and fuel injectors are voltage-sensitive. If your engine starts stumbling along with the electrical symptoms, you are close to stalling.

## Do I Pull Over Immediately or Can I Drive a Few More Miles?

Pull over as soon as it is safe. Do not try to reach your destination or even a nearby exit if it means staying in moving traffic for more than a couple of minutes.

Here is the problem: a fully charged battery might keep a car running for 20 to 45 minutes with minimal electrical load, but most batteries are not sitting at full charge when an alternator dies. If your battery was already weak, you could stall in under 10 minutes. Stalling in traffic or on a highway at speed is far more dangerous than pulling onto a shoulder or into a parking lot right now.

Turn off your AC, heated seats, rear defroster, and radio immediately. If you are driving in daylight and it is safe to do so on a wide shoulder, you can turn the headlights off too. Every watt you cut buys you a little more time to reach a safe stopping point.

If you stall on a highway, read up on [what to do when broken down on a highway at night](/broken-down-on-highway-at-night-safety-tips-what-to-do/) because the safety steps there apply in daylight too.

## Should I Try to Jump-Start It or Will That Actually Help?

A jump start will not fix a dead alternator. It may temporarily restart the engine, but without a working alternator, the battery will drain again within minutes and you will stall a second time, possibly in a worse location.

This is a common mistake people make roadside. Someone jumps the car, it starts, the driver pulls back into traffic thinking they bought some time, and the car dies at a red light or on a freeway on-ramp two minutes later. Do not do this. If a jump gets the car started and you use that to move it 50 feet off the road to a safer spot, fine. But do not attempt to drive it anywhere meaningful.

The alternator is a belt-driven generator that keeps the battery topped off while the engine runs. Once it is dead, nothing you do roadside will fix it. The car needs to go to a shop on a flatbed or wheel-lift tow truck.

## What Will a Tow Cost and Does Insurance Cover It?

A local tow of 5 to 10 miles typically runs $75 to $150. If the shop you need is 20 or more miles away, expect to pay $3 to $7 per mile beyond the base drop, putting a 30-mile tow in the $150 to $250 range.

Whether your insurance covers it depends on your policy. Roadside assistance add-ons through carriers like Progressive, GEICO, and USAA often cover one tow per incident, sometimes up to a set dollar limit. Progressive's roadside add-on, for example, typically caps towing at a dollar amount per disablement event, which may or may not cover the full bill depending on distance. [Progressive's towing coverage limits](/progressive-insurance-covers-towing-how-much/) break down exactly what you can expect from that policy.

AAA membership covers towing up to 3, 7, or 100 miles depending on your membership tier (Classic, Plus, or Premier). If you have AAA Premier, a single tow of up to 100 miles is covered per incident.

If you have no coverage, call two or three local tow companies and get quotes before committing. Prices vary significantly by company and time of day.

## How Long Can I Realistically Wait on the Shoulder for a Tow?

Once you are safely off the road with hazard lights on, you can wait as long as needed. The hazard lights will drain the battery faster, but your priority is visibility, not battery preservation.

Average tow truck response times in suburban and urban areas run 30 to 60 minutes. Rural areas can be 90 minutes or more. Give the dispatcher your exact location: highway number, direction of travel, and the nearest mile marker or exit sign. That detail cuts response time significantly.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive my car to the mechanic if my alternator dies?**
A: No. Without a working alternator, your battery is the only power source, and it will drain completely and stall the engine, often within 15 to 30 minutes. Drive it only if you are moving it to a safe spot on the shoulder.

**Q: Will my car start again after the alternator dies?**
A: Only if the battery still has enough charge. Once the battery drains fully, the car will not start at all. A jump start may get it running briefly, but it will die again quickly without a working alternator to recharge the battery.

**Q: How much does an alternator replacement cost?**
A: Parts and labor typically run $400 to $900 at an independent shop, depending on the vehicle. Dealer labor rates push that higher. The alternator itself on most domestic sedans and trucks runs $150 to $350 for a remanufactured unit.

**Q: What if my car stalls in the middle of traffic before I can pull over?**
A: Keep the wheel straight, brake smoothly, and steer to the nearest shoulder or breakdown lane. Turn on hazards immediately. If you are on a highway and cannot safely exit the vehicle, stay inside with your seatbelt on and call 911.

**Q: Can a bad serpentine belt cause my alternator to stop working?**
A: Yes. The alternator is driven by the serpentine belt. If that belt snaps or slips off, the alternator stops generating power instantly. If your belt failed, you will also lose power steering on older hydraulic systems.
