> **Quick Answer:** Farmers Insurance roadside assistance does not publish a fixed mileage cap the way some competitors do. Coverage is typically tied to a dollar amount per tow, usually $75 to $100 depending on your policy and state. That dollar limit is what controls how far your car actually gets towed. Call 1-800-435-7764 to confirm your specific limit before the tow truck hooks up.

## What To Do

1. **Pull up your policy before you call dispatch.** Log into the Farmers app or your online account and find your Emergency Roadside Service endorsement. You need to know your per-occurrence dollar limit, not just whether you have coverage.

2. **Call Farmers roadside dispatch at 1-800-435-7764.** Tell them your location, what broke, and where you need the car delivered. They coordinate the tow truck. Do not call a tow company independently first or you risk a reimbursement process instead of direct payment, which takes longer.

3. **Ask the dispatcher exactly how far your coverage will take you.** Give them your destination address. They can tell you on the phone whether your dollar limit covers that distance. If it does not, you will owe the driver the difference in cash or card when the job is done.

4. **Do the math on distance vs. dollar limit.** Towing rates vary by region but typically run $4 to $7 per mile after a hook-up fee of $50 to $75. If your policy covers $100 total and the hook-up alone is $65, you are getting roughly five to seven miles of actual towing paid for. A 20-mile tow to a dealership could easily cost $150 to $185 out of pocket after your benefit is exhausted.

5. **Negotiate destination strategically.** If you have flexibility, tow to the nearest reputable shop rather than a dealer across town. Shorter tow, less out-of-pocket, faster service. You can arrange a second tow later once the car is stabilized.

6. **If you paid out of pocket, file for reimbursement immediately.** Keep the tow receipt. Farmers lets you file reimbursement for covered roadside events. The article on [Farmers Insurance towing reimbursement after a breakdown](/farmers-insurance-towing-reimbursement-after-breakdown/) walks through exactly how to get that money back.

7. **Check whether your roadside benefit resets per incident.** Farmers roadside is typically available per disablement, but there are annual limits on how many times you can use it. Review [how many times you can use Farmers Insurance towing benefits](/farmers-insurance-towing-benefit-how-many-times/) so you are not caught off guard later in the year.

## Stay Safe

- Get fully off the road before you do anything else. Engine trouble, flat tire, transmission failure: none of it matters if you are in a travel lane.
- Turn on hazard lights immediately. Keep them on until the tow truck arrives.
- Do not stand behind or in front of your vehicle on a highway shoulder. Stay behind the guardrail if one is present.
- If it is dark or visibility is poor, use a reflective triangle or flares if you have them.
- In extreme heat or cold, stay in the vehicle with doors locked unless there is a specific safety reason to get out.
- Do not accept a ride from an unknown driver who stops. Wait for the dispatched tow truck.

## Common Questions

**Q: Does Farmers roadside assistance cover the full tow or just part of it?**
A: Farmers covers towing up to your policy's dollar limit, typically $75 to $100 per incident. Anything beyond that amount is your responsibility, paid directly to the driver when the job is complete.

**Q: What happens if the tow costs more than my Farmers coverage limit?**
A: You pay the difference out of pocket to the tow truck driver at the time of service. Rates generally run $4 to $7 per mile plus a hook-up fee, so a longer tow to a dealership can easily exceed your limit by $75 to $125 or more.

**Q: Can I choose which shop my car gets towed to with Farmers roadside?**
A: Yes, you can direct the tow to a shop of your choosing, but the farther the destination, the more likely you will exceed your dollar limit. Choosing a closer shop reduces or eliminates any out-of-pocket overage.
