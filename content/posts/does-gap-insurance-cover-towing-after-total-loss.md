> **Quick Answer:** No. GAP insurance does not cover towing. It pays the difference between your car's actual cash value and what you still owe on your loan or lease when your insurer declares a total loss. The tow bill after a wreck or total loss event falls under your auto policy's collision, comprehensive, or roadside assistance coverage, not GAP.

## So What Does GAP Insurance Actually Cover?

GAP insurance covers one thing: the gap between your car's depreciated value and your remaining loan or lease balance. If your car is worth $18,000 at the time of the total loss but you owe $23,500, GAP pays the $5,500 difference so you are not stuck making payments on a car you no longer have. That is it. It does not touch medical bills, rental cars, or towing charges.

GAP is a financial product, not a roadside or repair product. It activates only after your primary insurer settles the total loss claim and cuts their payout check. By that point, your car has long since been towed.

## Who Actually Pays the Tow Bill After a Total Loss?

Your collision or comprehensive coverage pays the tow after a total loss, assuming you carry it. Those coverages include towing to the storage yard or repair shop as part of the claim. If the accident happened, collision applies. If a tree fell on your car or it was flooded, comprehensive applies.

If you only carry liability, you have a problem. Liability does not cover your own vehicle's tow or damage. You would be paying out of pocket, typically $100 to $200 for a local tow, more if the car needs to be transported across town to a tow yard.

Some drivers also have a separate roadside assistance add-on through their insurer, like Progressive's roadside package or GEICO's emergency road service, that covers the initial tow regardless of fault. That is worth checking before you call for a truck. For a detailed look at what those add-ons actually pay, see [does comprehensive insurance cover towing after an accident](/does-comprehensive-insurance-cover-towing-after-accident/).

## What If My Car Is at a Tow Yard While the Total Loss Claim Gets Settled?

Storage fees are where people get burned. Once your car sits in a tow yard, most yards charge $40 to $100 per day in storage. Total loss claims can take a week to two weeks to settle, sometimes longer if there is a GAP claim layered on top.

Here is the specific problem: your insurer typically covers reasonable towing and storage as part of the collision or comprehensive claim, but that coverage is not unlimited. Push your adjuster to move the vehicle quickly, or ask them to confirm in writing how many storage days they will cover. Do not assume the yard fees stop accruing while you wait.

## Does It Matter Whether My Car Was Totaled in a Crash or Due to a Mechanical Failure?

Yes, it matters for which coverage pays. A crash activates collision. A fire, flood, or other non-collision event activates comprehensive. Pure mechanical failure, like an engine seizing, is a different situation. Most standard policies do not cover towing for a mechanical breakdown unless you have a roadside assistance rider.

GAP does not care either way. It only cares that your insurer issued a total loss determination and paid actual cash value. The cause of the loss determines which part of your auto policy covers the tow. GAP sits entirely outside that conversation. ## Common Questions

**Q: Does GAP insurance reimburse me for anything other than the loan balance?**
A: No. GAP pays only the difference between your insurer's actual cash value settlement and your outstanding loan or lease balance. It does not reimburse deductibles, towing, storage, or rental cars.

**Q: Will GAP insurance cover my deductible after a total loss?**
A: Standard GAP does not cover your deductible. Some lenders sell a GAP product with a deductible waiver built in, but that is a separate feature you would have had to opt into at signing. Check your GAP contract specifically.

**Q: What if I do not have collision or comprehensive and my car gets totaled?**
A: You will pay the tow out of pocket. GAP also becomes useless in this situation because GAP only pays after your primary insurer settles the claim. No collision or comprehensive payout means no GAP payout either.

**Q: How long does a total loss claim take, and do storage fees keep adding up the whole time?**
A: Most total loss claims settle in one to three weeks. Storage fees do keep accumulating daily, and not all insurers cover the full amount. Tell your adjuster immediately if the car is in a paid storage lot so they can move it to their preferred facility or authorize pickup.

**Q: Does roadside assistance pay the tow even if the car ends up being a total loss?**
A: Yes. Roadside assistance does not know at the time of the tow whether the car will be totaled. It pays for the tow itself. The total loss determination happens later at the shop. Those are two separate transactions covered by two separate parts of your policy.
