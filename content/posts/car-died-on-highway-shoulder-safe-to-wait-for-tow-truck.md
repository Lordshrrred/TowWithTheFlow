> **Quick Answer:** Yes, you can wait on the highway shoulder for a tow, but only if you set up correctly. Get as far right as possible, turn on your hazard lights, and get everyone out of the car and behind the guardrail or up the embankment, away from traffic. Stay out of the vehicle until the tow truck arrives. The shoulder is one of the most dangerous places on a highway.

## Is It Safe to Stay Inside My Car While I Wait on the Shoulder?

No. Sitting inside a stopped vehicle on the highway shoulder puts you directly in the path of any driver who drifts right. A car gets rear-ended on a highway shoulder every few minutes across the country, and the people inside rarely have time to react. Get out, move away from the car, and stay behind any barrier you can find: a guardrail, a concrete barrier, or at minimum the far edge of the shoulder.

If there is no barrier and the shoulder is narrow, walk further up an embankment or into a grassy area off the road surface entirely. Cold or bad weather is uncomfortable. Getting hit by a car at highway speed is fatal. Discomfort wins.

One exception: if you break down on a bridge or overpass with no shoulder and no barrier, it may be safer to stay in the car with your seatbelt on than to stand in live traffic lanes. That is the rare situation where staying put is the lesser risk.

## What Do I Do the Moment My Car Dies on the Highway?

Steer to the right shoulder immediately, as far off the travel lanes as you can get. If your power steering is gone because the engine died, the wheel will feel stiff but it still turns. Use both hands and push hard.

Once stopped:

1. Shift into park and turn on your hazard lights right away.
2. If you have road flares or LED triangles, place them 200 to 300 feet behind the car on the shoulder side. That distance gives approaching drivers time to react.
3. Get all passengers out through the passenger-side door, never the driver's side into traffic.
4. Move everyone to the area behind a guardrail or well off the pavement.
5. Call 911 if you feel unsafe, then call a tow service or roadside assistance.

If your car stopped in a travel lane and you cannot get it to the shoulder, turn on your hazards, get out safely, and call 911 immediately. Do not try to push it in live traffic.

## How Long Will I Have to Wait, and What Should I Do During That Time?

Tow truck wait times vary a lot depending on where you are. On a busy metro interstate you might see a truck in 20 to 40 minutes. On a rural highway, it can run an hour or more. [Real wait time breakdowns by situation are covered here.](/how-long-does-it-take-for-a-tow-truck-to-arrive/)

While you wait:

- Stay away from the vehicle. Boredom and cold will tempt you back in. Resist it.
- Keep your phone battery in mind. Turn off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and screen brightness to preserve charge.
- If a highway patrol officer stops, let them stay behind you with their lights running. That patrol vehicle is protection.
- Know your exact location before you call for a tow: look for mile markers, highway numbers, or nearby exit signs. Tell the dispatcher the highway number, your direction of travel, and the nearest mile marker. That information cuts response time.

## Should I Try to Diagnose the Problem or Just Call a Tow?

Call the tow first, then look at the situation from a safe distance. Do not pop the hood and lean over the engine while you are next to a live highway. If the car is smoking, do not open the hood at all. [Smoke from the hood on a highway means you back away from the car immediately.](/car-smoking-from-hood-on-highway-what-to-do/)

A dead car on the highway shoulder could be a dead battery, a blown fuse, a failed alternator, a broken timing chain, or a seized engine. You are not going to fix most of those on the shoulder anyway, and attempting a repair puts you in a dangerous position for a long time. Get the car to a shop, then diagnose it. The one exception is a jump start: if someone with jumper cables safely pulls behind you on a wide shoulder, a quick jump attempt is reasonable. If it does not start in two tries, stop and wait for the tow.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I leave my car on the highway shoulder overnight if the tow truck can't come until morning?**
A: No. Most states allow highway patrol to have an abandoned vehicle on the shoulder towed within one to four hours, and a car left overnight is almost certain to be cited or impounded. More importantly, an unlit or poorly lit vehicle on a shoulder overnight is a serious collision hazard. [Here is what the law actually says about how long you can wait.](/how-long-can-you-wait-on-side-of-road-before-police-tow-your-car/)

**Q: My hazard lights are draining my battery. Should I turn them off?**
A: Keep them on. A dead battery is fixable. A driver who does not see your car and hits it is not. If your battery is so weak that hazards are a concern, that is a sign you need the tow sooner rather than later anyway.

**Q: What if I broke down on the highway and I have no cell service?**
A: Walk toward the nearest exit ramp or emergency call box, which are typically placed every mile on interstate highways. Blue emergency call box poles are often found along rural interstates and will connect you to a dispatcher. [There is a full guide on handling a breakdown with no cell signal here.](/car-broke-down-no-cell-service-on-highway-what-to-do/)

**Q: Will police tow my car for me if I call 911?**
A: If you call 911 for a safety situation, an officer will respond and can arrange a tow through their dispatch. That tow is usually from a rotation list and may be more expensive than calling your own tow service. You are not required to use their tow company, but in a genuine emergency getting the car moved matters more than shopping rates.

**Q: Is it safe to accept help from a stranger who pulls over?**
A: Use judgment. Staying in your car to talk through a cracked window is safer than getting out to talk. Accept a phone call offer if you have no signal. Decline offers to get in their vehicle. Most people who pull over are genuinely trying to help, but you do not know who they are, and keeping distance is reasonable.
