> **Quick Answer:** Towing from construction zones with police involvement costs $200-500+ due to traffic control requirements, safety protocols, and potential equipment needs. Police may be required for safety reasons, adding $100-200 in fees. Expect higher rates during peak hours and on major highways.

## What To Do

1. **Turn on hazard lights immediately** and try to move your vehicle to the safest possible location within the work zone barriers. Even rolling forward or backward 20-30 feet to reach a designated equipment staging area can make a major difference in how quickly help arrives.

2. **Call 911 first** if you're blocking traffic or in immediate danger. Construction zones require police coordination for safety. Dispatchers will alert both the tow and the zone's traffic control supervisor simultaneously, which cuts down on wait time.

3. **Stay in your vehicle if safe** or exit away from traffic if you must get out. Construction zones have limited safe areas. If there is a concrete barrier to your right, stand between it and your car, not on the open traffic side.

4. **Contact your insurance roadside assistance** before calling a private tow company. Many policies cover construction zone incidents, and using an insurer-dispatched truck means the operator already knows the zone surcharge structure and won't surprise you with added fees at the scene.

5. **Document the scene** with photos showing your vehicle's position relative to construction barriers and signage. This is especially useful if there is a dispute later about whether the breakdown was caused by road hazard damage from the construction surface.

6. **Expect extended wait times** as tow trucks need police escort or traffic control to safely reach you. In active daytime work zones on major interstates, waits of 60-90 minutes are common. Night shifts with fewer workers on site can actually move faster because there is less ongoing activity to coordinate around.

7. **Ask the tow operator about construction zone surcharges** upfront, as these fees vary significantly by location and complexity. Some states set a maximum allowable surcharge by regulation; others leave it entirely to the operator's discretion.

## What It Might Cost

**Base towing:** $150-300 for standard hookup and first few miles

**Police traffic control:** $100-200 additional fee in most jurisdictions

**Construction zone surcharge:** $50-150 for safety equipment and extended hookup time

**After-hours premium:** Add 25-50% if breakdown occurs at night

**Specialty equipment:** Extra $100-200 if barriers need to be moved or heavy-duty equipment is required

Total costs typically range $300-600, with major highway construction zones potentially reaching $700+ during peak traffic hours. If your vehicle is an SUV or pickup, expect the base hookup rate to sit at the higher end of that range because operators use flatbeds with more setup time involved.

Your [towing cost from interstate to dealership](/towing-cost-from-interstate-to-dealership/) may be covered partially by insurance, but construction zone fees often fall outside standard coverage. Check with your carrier about specific work zone provisions before you assume anything is included.

## Stay Safe

- Never attempt to change a tire or make repairs in an active construction zone
- Keep hazards on and stay visible to approaching traffic and construction workers
- Exit your vehicle only if absolutely necessary and move away from traffic flow
- Follow instructions from police officers and construction flaggers exactly
- Be prepared for lane closures that may delay the tow truck's arrival

Construction zones create unique challenges for towing operations. The combination of reduced lane space, active work crews, and heavy traffic means [tow truck operators need police coordination](/tow-truck-cost-accident-scene-police-report-required/) to safely access your vehicle. This coordination drives up costs but prevents serious accidents.

Emergency responders prioritize safety over speed in these situations. A breakdown that might take 30 minutes to resolve on a regular highway can easily stretch to 90 minutes in a construction zone while crews coordinate lane closures and traffic control. On major projects with multiple active lanes of work, the tow truck itself may need to travel in a flagged convoy with a pilot vehicle, which adds another coordination layer and another line item to your invoice.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will my roadside assistance plan actually cover the construction zone surcharges?**
A: Most basic roadside plans cover the base hookup and a set mileage allowance but exclude specialty surcharges like construction zone fees or police coordination costs. Call your provider before the tow begins and ask specifically whether work zone surcharges are reimbursable, so you are not caught off guard with a $150-200 gap to cover out of pocket.

**Q: Can I refuse to pay the construction zone surcharge if I think it is too high?**
A: You can dispute the amount, but the operator has a lien on your vehicle until payment is made in most states, so refusing on the spot usually means more problems. Get an itemized receipt showing every fee, then file a complaint with your state's towing regulation board or public utilities commission if the charges seem outside the legal limits for your area.

**Q: How long does a construction zone tow typically take from the time I call?**
A: Plan for 45-90 minutes in most active daytime zones. The tow company first has to coordinate with police or the zone's traffic control supervisor before they can enter, and that handoff alone adds 20-30 minutes compared to a standard highway breakdown response.
