> **Quick Answer:** Most states allow 24 to 72 hours before law enforcement can have your car towed as an abandoned vehicle. On active travel lanes or near an interchange, police can call a tow immediately, no waiting period required. If your car is on the shoulder, get it moved within a few hours. Do not leave it overnight and hope for the best.

## How Long Does the Law Actually Give Me Before My Car Gets Towed?

The standard window is 24 to 72 hours on a highway shoulder, depending on the state. That clock starts the moment a trooper tags your car with a warning notice, which is a paper or sticker placed on the windshield. Miss that window and the state police or highway authority will dispatch a tow at your expense, and you will also pay impound fees that typically run $40 to $100 per day on top of the tow charge.

The catch: that clock doesn't apply everywhere. If your car is partially blocking a lane, sitting near an on-ramp or off-ramp, or is a hazard in a construction zone, troopers can order an immediate tow without notice. Some states, like California on their Freeway Service Patrol corridors, have rigs that will push your car off the highway within minutes during peak hours whether you are ready or not.

## What Happens If I Just Leave My Car There and Come Back Later?

You will almost certainly come back to an empty shoulder and a tow bill. In most states, once law enforcement tags the vehicle, a tow company on the state rotation list is dispatched if you have not returned within the allowed window. That tow is non-negotiable, meaning you cannot call it off once the truck is en route. You will owe the tow fee plus storage from the moment the car hits the impound yard.

Tow fees on state contracts for highway clearance run $150 to $400 depending on the state and the distance to the nearest yard. Storage adds up fast. Three days in impound before you can get the car out could easily cost you more than the repair itself on an older vehicle.

If you broke down because of a mechanical failure like a [fuel pump failure on the highway](/car-fuel-pump-went-out-highway-breakdown-cost/), arrange a tow within the first hour. Do not treat the shoulder as free parking while you figure out logistics.

## Is It Safe to Stay With My Car or Should I Get Away From It?

Get off the highway. If you can get your car fully onto the shoulder, do it, then get everyone out of the vehicle and behind the guardrail or up the embankment, well away from traffic. Standing next to a disabled car on a 65 mph highway is one of the most dangerous places you can be. Drivers distracted by looking at your car drift toward the shoulder constantly. This is sometimes called the moth effect, and it kills people.

## What Do I Do in the First 30 Minutes to Avoid a Forced Tow?

Call for a tow or roadside assistance immediately, before you call anyone else. That first call starts the clock on your wait time and keeps you in control of where your car goes and who moves it. If you wait two hours to call, you may find a state-contracted tow already dispatched when your chosen tow company arrives.

Here is the order of operations:

1. Get the car as far onto the shoulder as possible, away from the travel lane.
2. Turn on hazard lights.
3. Get passengers behind the guardrail.
4. Call 911 if anyone is injured or if the car is blocking traffic.
5. Call your tow company or roadside assistance program.
6. Call 511, the national highway information line, if you want to check on state traffic services available in your area.

If your insurance includes roadside assistance, check your policy's tow distance limit before you call. Some policies only cover 5 to 15 miles, and a highway breakdown near a rural stretch could mean a long haul to the nearest shop. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can police tow my car immediately without a warning notice?**
A: Yes. If your car is blocking a lane, creating a hazard, or sitting in a no-stopping zone, police can order an immediate tow with no waiting period and no notice required.

**Q: Do I have to stay with the car while waiting for a tow on the highway?**
A: No, and you probably should not. Get off the roadway and behind the guardrail. Leave your hazards on and stay visible so the tow driver can find you, but do not stand in the traffic lane or lean against your car.

**Q: What if my car gets towed by the state before my tow truck arrives?**
A: You will owe the state-contracted tow company their fee. You can then arrange to move the car from the impound lot to a repair shop using your own tow company, but you will still pay both fees.

**Q: Will my insurance cover a state-forced highway tow?**
A: It depends on your policy. Roadside assistance add-ons typically cover a tow you initiate. A state-forced impound tow may not be covered. Call your insurer to confirm before you assume you are covered.

**Q: How do I find out which impound lot has my car if I come back and it is gone?**
A: Call the non-emergency line for the state police or highway patrol that covers that road. They log every tow they authorize and can tell you which yard has the vehicle and what fees have accrued.
