> **Quick Answer:** If you have a flat tire, no spare, and no roadside assistance membership, your best immediate moves are: get fully off the road, turn on hazard lights, then call a pay-per-use tow service or tire shop directly. Some areas have state-run highway patrol assist programs. If your car has a tire mobility kit (foam sealant and compressor), that may get you to a shop. Do not drive on a flat rim.

## What Are My Real Options When I Have a Flat Tire and No Spare?

You have four practical options: use a tire mobility kit if your car came with one, call a pay-per-use tow truck, call a mobile tire service, or contact your state's highway assistance program. None of these require a membership. They all cost money out of pocket, but they get the job done.

Many cars built after 2010, especially smaller sedans and crossovers, shipped without a spare tire at all. Instead, the trunk has a small compressor and a can of tire sealant, often labeled "Tire Mobility Kit" or branded as Fix-a-Flat style foam. If the puncture is small and in the tread (not the sidewall), the foam can seal it well enough to drive 50 miles or so at under 50 mph to reach a tire shop. Check your trunk or under the cargo floor before assuming you have nothing.

If the sidewall is blown or the rim is damaged, foam sealant will not help. You need a tow. See [how much emergency roadside assistance costs without a membership](/how-much-does-emergency-roadside-assistance-cost-without-membership/) before you call, so you are not surprised by the bill.

## How Do I Get a Tow Truck With No Roadside Assistance Membership?

Call a local tow company directly or use an app. You do not need AAA or any membership to get a tow. Pay-per-use dispatch apps like Urgent.ly or Honk connect you to a tow truck in your area within minutes, and you pay by credit card at the time of service. A local tow for a few miles typically runs $75 to $150, more on a highway or after hours.

Search "tow truck near me" on Google Maps. Call two or three shops and ask for the hookup fee plus per-mile rate before agreeing. Get the total in writing (a text message works) before the truck leaves their lot.

Your credit card may also include roadside assistance as a cardholder benefit. Visa, Mastercard, and some American Express cards offer this. Check the benefits number on the back of your card right now while you are waiting.

## Is It Safe to Wait on the Side of the Road for Help?

Get as far off the road as possible and stay outside the vehicle if you are on a high-speed highway. If you are on an interstate shoulder, exit the car through the passenger side (away from traffic) and stand behind the guardrail. A car stopped on a highway shoulder is a real hazard, especially at night or in low visibility.

Set out warning triangles or road flares behind the car if you have them. If not, leave your hazard lights running and keep your hood up so other drivers recognize you are disabled. Read [emergency triangle vs flares: which is safer on the highway](/emergency-triangle-vs-flares-which-is-safer-on-highway/) if you want to know which to use and how far back to place them.

If you are on a rural highway with no cell service, stay with the car and flag down a passing vehicle or a truck driver who can radio for help.

## What Should I Do If I Am on the Highway and the Flat Happened at Speed?

Pull over immediately but do it gradually. Do not slam the brakes. Grip the wheel firmly, ease off the gas, and steer toward the shoulder. A blown tire at highway speed can pull hard to one side, especially a front tire, so keep two hands on the wheel. Once stopped, do not attempt to change or repair anything while in a live travel lane.

If you cannot reach the shoulder (traffic is blocking you, or the flat happened near an exit ramp), turn on your hazards, slow down, and aim for the nearest exit or widened shoulder. Driving slowly on a flat rim for a short distance to reach a safer spot is better than stopping in a travel lane. You will destroy the tire and possibly the rim, but that is a repair bill, not a hospital bill.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive slowly on a flat tire to reach a gas station?**
A: Driving on a completely flat tire quickly destroys the tire, the rim, and sometimes the wheel well. If it is a very slow leak and the tire is not fully collapsed, you might nurse it a short distance. A fully flat tire: get off the road and call for help instead.

**Q: What is a tire mobility kit and does my car have one?**
A: It is a small air compressor and a can of chemical sealant that temporarily plugs small tread punctures. Check your trunk floor or under the cargo mat. Many cars from 2010 onward, including most Nissan, BMW, and Chevrolet models sold without a spare, include one. It will not fix a sidewall blowout.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover a flat tire tow?**
A: Only if you have roadside assistance added to your auto policy. Comprehensive or collision coverage does not cover towing for a flat. Call your insurer's claims line and ask specifically about roadside before assuming you are covered.

**Q: How long will a tow truck take to arrive if I call without a membership?**
A: Response times vary widely. In a metro area, expect 30 to 60 minutes on average. On a rural highway, it could be 90 minutes or more. Give the dispatcher your exact mile marker or nearest cross street, not just the highway name.

**Q: Can a mobile tire service come to me instead of towing?**
A: Yes. Mobile tire shops exist in most metro areas and many suburbs. Search "mobile tire repair near me." They carry common tire sizes and can mount and balance on the spot, which is often faster and cheaper than a tow plus shop visit. They typically cannot help with rim damage or sidewall blowouts.
