## What You'll Actually Pay for Flatbed Towing in Denver

Flatbeds cost more than wheel-lift tows. That is the tradeoff for having your car ride completely off the ground, no dragging, no drivetrain stress. If you have an AWD or 4WD vehicle, a low-clearance car, or a car with transmission damage, flatbed is often the only safe option.

Here is how Denver pricing typically breaks down:

- **Hook-up/base fee:** $75–$125
- **Per-mile rate:** $4–$7 per mile
- **After-hours surcharge:** $25–$75 added to your total
- **Snow or severe weather surcharge:** $50–$100, common from October through April
- **Long-distance haul (20+ miles):** Rates can jump to $6–$9 per mile from some carriers

A 5-mile flatbed tow from Capitol Hill to a shop in Englewood is going to run you roughly $110–$155. That same truck taking your car from Lakewood out to a dealership in Aurora, 20+ miles, will likely land at $195–$280. If you broke down on I-70 heading into the mountains, expect the high end of that range and possibly more. For mountain-specific pricing west of Denver, see [Towing Cost in Denver Colorado Mountains](/towing-cost-denver-colorado-mountains/).

## What To Do Right Now

1. **Get off the road.** If you are on I-25, I-70, or C-470, exit or coast to the shoulder as far right as possible. Turn on hazards immediately.
2. **Call multiple companies.** Do not take the first quote you get. Call two or three flatbed services and ask for a total, not just the per-mile rate.
3. **Ask specifically about flatbed availability.** Some dispatchers will quote you a price and send a wheel-lift instead. Say the words: "I need a flatbed, confirm one is available."
4. **Check your insurance or roadside plan first.** Some policies cover flatbed towing up to a set dollar amount or distance. A quick call before you pay out of pocket could save you $100+. If you have Progressive, check what they cover at [Progressive Roadside Assistance Covers How Much Towing](/progressive-roadside-assistance-covers-how-much-towing/).
5. **Confirm the destination address and total cost before the truck leaves.** Once your car is loaded, your leverage drops to zero.
6. **If you broke down on a highway or freeway,** stay outside the vehicle behind the guardrail while you wait. Do not stand between the car and traffic.

## Stay Safe

- Keep your hazard lights on from the moment you stop until the tow truck arrives.
- If it is dark or visibility is low, place road flares or reflective triangles 100–300 feet behind your car.
- Never stand directly behind or beside your vehicle on any road with moving traffic.
- If you are on the highway shoulder, get out the passenger side and stand behind a barrier if one is available. Review [Car Broke Down on Freeway: What to Do Right Now](/car-broke-down-on-freeway-what-to-do/) for the full safety checklist.
- Do not leave your car unattended without telling someone your location and the name of the tow company.

## Common Questions

**Q: Do I have to pay extra if my car needs a flatbed instead of a regular tow truck?**
A: Yes, flatbed towing costs more than wheel-lift towing because of the specialized equipment involved. In Denver, expect to pay roughly $20 to $50 more compared to a standard wheel-lift tow for the same distance.

**Q: Can I request a flatbed tow through my insurance roadside assistance, or do I have to find one myself?**
A: Most roadside assistance programs, including those through insurance policies and auto clubs, can dispatch a flatbed if you specifically request one. Always confirm flatbed availability when you call, since some dispatchers default to wheel-lift trucks unless you ask.

**Q: Why is my tow quote higher than what I expected based on the per-mile rate I was given?**
A: The per-mile rate is only part of the total. Hook-up fees, after-hours surcharges, and winter weather surcharges are added on top, which is why the final bill can look much higher than the mileage alone would suggest. Always ask for the full estimated total before the truck is dispatched.
