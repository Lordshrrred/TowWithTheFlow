> **Quick Answer:** Put your hazards on immediately. If your car can roll, shift to neutral and push it to the curb with help from bystanders. If it can't move, stay inside with your seatbelt on, call 911 to report the blockage, then call a tow truck. On a one way street, traffic behind you has nowhere to go, so getting help dispatched fast is the priority.

## What Do I Do in the First 60 Seconds When My Car Dies on a One Way Street?

Hazards on, then assess whether you can move the car. That is your entire first move. Press the hazard button before you do anything else. Then check your mirrors: how far back is traffic stacking, and is there any gap to the curb or a side street entrance you can coast into?

If the street is level or has a slight downgrade toward a clear spot, shift to neutral, get out carefully on the passenger side if traffic is moving beside you, and push. Even one or two bystanders can get a car rolling on flat pavement. You do not need to push far. Getting even half a car length into a bus stop cutout or driveway apron opens a lane.

If the car is stuck and immovable, get back inside, keep your seatbelt on, and call 911. Tell the dispatcher you are blocking a one way street and give cross streets. They can send police to direct traffic around you while you wait for the tow, and in most cities they will.

## Is It Safer to Stay in the Car or Stand Outside?

Stay inside the car with your seatbelt fastened. On a one way street with traffic stacked behind you, standing outside puts you between moving vehicles and your stopped car. Rear-end collisions into disabled vehicles are common, and being outside multiplies your injury risk significantly.

The exception: if you smell fuel or see smoke from the engine bay, get out and move to the sidewalk at least 50 feet away. A fire risk overrides the traffic risk. If you see smoke, check our guide on [car smoking under hood on the side of the road](/car-smoking-under-hood-on-side-of-road-what-to-do/) for what to do next.

If you have emergency triangles or road flares in your trunk and you can safely access them, place them 50 to 100 feet behind the car. On a narrow one way street, one triangle centered in the lane is enough to warn drivers before they close the gap. [Emergency triangles outperform flares](/emergency-triangle-vs-flares-which-is-safer-on-highway/) in urban settings because there is no ignition risk near stopped traffic.

## How Do I Get a Tow Truck to Me Fast on a One Way Street?

Call 911 first if you are actively blocking traffic, then immediately call a tow company or your roadside assistance provider. Give the dispatcher your exact cross streets, not just the street name. One way streets in dense neighborhoods often share names with parallel two way streets, and a tow driver who approaches from the wrong direction cannot reach you.

Useful details to give your tow dispatcher:

- The cross streets on both sides of where you are stopped
- Which direction traffic flows (so they can approach from behind)
- Whether any lanes are open beside you or if you are fully blocking
- Your car's make, model, and color

If you have AAA, your insurer's roadside app, or a service like OnStar, use it in parallel with the 911 call. Most urban tow companies can reach a city block within 30 to 45 minutes during normal hours, longer during rush hour. Police often arrive faster and can hold traffic while you wait.

## Will I Get a Ticket for Blocking a One Way Street?

Probably not if you call it in. Most cities do not ticket drivers for an unintentional mechanical breakdown, especially when you have reported it to police and have a tow on the way. The key is that you are not just sitting there without having called for help.

What can get you a ticket or a city-impound tow: leaving the car unattended without calling it in, blocking a fire hydrant, or letting it sit past the time frame a responding officer specifies. If a city tow truck removes your car before your private tow arrives, you will pay impound fees on top of the tow cost. That is worth avoiding by getting 911 on the line early and keeping the officer updated on your tow truck's ETA.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I push my car by myself on a one way street?**
A: Unlikely on anything but a steep downgrade. Most vehicles weigh 3,000 to 4,500 pounds. You need at least two people pushing from the rear while one steers. Ask bystanders directly: most will help in an emergency.

**Q: Should I turn my steering wheel to angle the car toward the curb while I push?**
A: Yes. Aim for the curb at a shallow angle so the car rolls into it naturally and stops. Do not aim straight for the curb or you will jump it. A 10 to 15 degree angle lets you guide the car into position without it rolling past the curb onto the sidewalk.

**Q: What do I tell 911 when I'm blocking a one way street?**
A: Give them your cross streets, confirm you are blocking traffic, state whether anyone is injured, and tell them a tow is already called or you need help getting one. They will prioritize accordingly. Stay on the line if they ask you to.

**Q: If police arrive before the tow truck, will they make me leave the car?**
A: Officers can order a city tow if the blockage is serious enough. Being cooperative and showing you already have a private tow en route usually prevents that. Give the officer your tow company's name and ETA so they know you are not stalling.

**Q: Does my insurance cover towing off a one way street in a city?**
A: Roadside assistance coverage under most policies covers the tow regardless of where you broke down. Check your policy's per-incident limit. If you carry [GEICO roadside assistance](/does-geico-cover-towing-after-breakdown/) or a similar add-on, the location type does not usually affect coverage as long as the vehicle is on a public road.
