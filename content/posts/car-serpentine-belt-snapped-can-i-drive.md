> **Quick Answer:** No, you cannot drive with a snapped serpentine belt. The belt powers your alternator, power steering pump, water pump, and AC compressor simultaneously. Without it, your battery will drain within minutes, your engine will overheat, and you will lose power steering. Pull over immediately, turn off the engine, and call for a tow.

## Can I Drive Even a Short Distance After My Serpentine Belt Snapped?

No. Even a half-mile drive risks serious engine damage. The moment the serpentine belt snaps, your water pump stops circulating coolant. Engines can overheat and suffer head gasket failure or a warped cylinder head within five to ten minutes of running without coolant flow. The repair bill for a blown head gasket runs $1,500 to $3,000 or more. A new serpentine belt and labor runs $75 to $200. The math is not close.

Your alternator also stops the second the belt snaps. The battery takes over powering your electronics, but most car batteries have enough reserve to run the ignition for only a few minutes under full electrical load. You will not make it to a shop.

## What Are the Signs That My Serpentine Belt Actually Snapped vs. Just Slipped?

A snapped belt is unmistakable. You will hear a loud bang or slapping noise, followed by silence where the belt used to be. Immediately after, you will notice the battery warning light illuminate, the power steering will feel heavy and stiff, and your temperature gauge will start climbing. If the belt just slipped or cracked, you may hear a loud squeal instead and the symptoms will be more gradual.

Pop the hood safely, after you have pulled completely off the road and turned off the engine. Look for the belt routing area around the front of the engine. If the belt is sitting coiled on the engine block or completely absent from the pulleys, it snapped. If you see a belt still on the pulleys but shredded or cracked through, it is failing and you still cannot drive it. Either way, you need a tow.

## What Happens to My Engine If I Ignore This and Keep Driving?

Your engine overheats first, typically within five minutes or less on a warm day. This can cause the head gasket to fail, the cylinder head to warp, or in a worst case, a seized engine. A seized engine means the car is done until you rebuild or replace the motor, which can cost more than the car is worth. For context, a water pump on most vehicles is driven entirely by the serpentine belt on accessory-driven systems, and without it spinning, heat builds fast. If you have been searching for information on what a seized engine costs, the [engine seized while driving towing cost and insurance](/engine-seized-while-driving-towing-cost-insurance/) breakdown is worth reading before you make a decision.

Your power steering loss is also a real safety issue, not just an inconvenience. On a highway at speed, a sudden loss of assisted steering is dangerous, especially in a turn or lane change.

## How Much Will the Repair Cost and Is a Tow Worth It?

The serpentine belt itself costs $25 to $75 in parts. Labor adds another $50 to $150 at most shops. Total out the door, figure $75 to $200. If your belt tensioner or an idler pulley failed and caused the belt to snap, add another $50 to $150 for those parts. A tow for a local breakdown typically runs $75 to $125 for the first few miles, depending on your location.

So yes, a $100 tow to avoid a $2,000 head gasket repair is an easy call. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive 1 mile to a gas station with a broken serpentine belt?**
A: No. Even one mile risks overheating your engine to the point of head gasket failure or worse. The water pump is not circulating coolant, and the engine can hit critical temperatures in under five minutes. Call a tow.

**Q: Will my car start after the serpentine belt snapped?**
A: It may start briefly because the battery still has a charge, but the alternator is not recharging it and the water pump is not running. Starting the engine in this state accelerates the damage. Do not start it.

**Q: How long does it take to replace a serpentine belt?**
A: Most shops can do it in 30 to 60 minutes. It is a straightforward job on most vehicles. Same-day service is common if you get there before early afternoon.

**Q: Can a serpentine belt snap without warning?**
A: Yes, though worn belts often show cracks, fraying, or glazing first. A failing tensioner or seized idler pulley can also cause a sudden snap. If your car made a squealing noise in the days before this happened, that was the warning you did not get to act on.

**Q: Does insurance cover towing for a snapped serpentine belt?**
A: Roadside assistance add-ons typically cover towing for mechanical breakdowns including a snapped belt. Standard liability-only policies do not. Check your declarations page or call your insurer while you wait for the tow truck.
