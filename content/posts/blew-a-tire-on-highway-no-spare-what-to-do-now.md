> **Quick Answer:** Pull as far right as you can, turn on your hazards, and stay in the car away from traffic. Without a spare, your options are a tow to the nearest tire shop, a mobile tire service that brings a tire to you, or run-flat foam as a very short-term fix. Do not try to drive on a blown tire. Call for help and wait it out safely.

## What Do I Do in the First 60 Seconds After a Blowout With No Spare?

Grip the wheel firmly, ease off the gas, and steer gradually to the right shoulder. Do not brake hard. A blown tire creates drag that will pull the car to one side, and sharp braking makes it worse. Once you're stopped, get as far from the travel lane as possible, ideally past the white fog line. Turn on your hazards immediately.

If you have road flares or reflective triangles, place them at least 100 feet behind your vehicle. If you're on an interstate with an outside barrier, exit through the passenger side and get behind the guardrail. The space between your car and passing traffic is the most dangerous place you can stand.

For more on staying safe in this exact situation, [how to stay safe broken down on a highway at night](/how-to-stay-safe-broken-down-on-highway-at-night/) covers the specifics you need whether it's dark or daylight.

## How Do I Get Help When I Have No Spare Tire?

Call a tow truck or a mobile tire service, those are your two real options. A tow truck brings your car to a tire shop. A mobile tire service brings a tire to you and mounts it on the spot, which is faster if you can get one to your location.

Start with your roadside assistance provider: AAA, your insurance company's roadside line, or the app tied to your credit card. If you don't have any of those, search "mobile tire service" plus the nearest city name. Many metro areas have services that will come to a highway shoulder within an hour.

If you're on a state highway, calling 511 connects you to your state's road condition and traveler information line, which can direct you to highway patrol or a service patrol truck. Many states run free service patrols on major interstates that can at minimum push you to a safe location.

Check whether your policy or membership covers this. [Does roadside assistance cover a flat tire on the rim](/does-roadside-assistance-cover-flat-tire-on-rim/) breaks down what most plans actually pay for in this scenario.

## Can I Use Tire Inflator Foam to Get Off the Highway?

Tire foam like Fix-a-Flat can get you moving again only if the tire has a small puncture and the sidewall is not damaged. After a blowout at highway speed, the sidewall is almost always shredded. Foam will not seal a sidewall failure. You'll just waste the can and still

## How Much Will a Tow Cost From the Highway to a Tire Shop?

A local tow of 5 to 15 miles typically runs $75 to $150 out of pocket if you're paying cash. Flatbed rates are higher than wheel-lift, and some states charge extra for highway service calls. If your roadside assistance plan covers towing, you may pay nothing or just a small dispatch fee.

If you're far from a tire shop, the cost goes up fast. Long interstate stretches can mean a 30 to 50 mile tow, which can run $200 to $350 without coverage. Know where your nearest town with a tire shop is before you commit to a direction.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive slowly on a blown tire to the nearest exit?**
A: No. Driving even a quarter mile on a blown tire destroys the rim and can cause you to lose control. The repair cost jumps from a tire swap to a tire plus a bent or cracked wheel. Call for help instead.

**Q: What if I have a run-flat tire, does that change anything?**
A: Yes. Run-flat tires, common on BMWs and some other models, are designed to be driven up to 50 miles at 50 mph after losing pressure. Check your owner's manual for the exact limit. You still need to get to a shop, but you can drive there rather than waiting for a tow.

**Q: How long will I wait for a tow truck on the highway?**
A: In most urban and suburban areas, 30 to 60 minutes. On rural stretches, it can be 90 minutes or more. Give the dispatcher your exact mile marker, not just the highway name, so they find you faster.

**Q: Will my car insurance cover the tow after a blowout?**
A: Only if you have roadside assistance or towing coverage added to your policy. Basic liability or collision coverage does not pay for a tow from a mechanical breakdown. Check your declarations page or call your insurer before you pay out of pocket.

**Q: Should I accept help from a stranger who pulls over?**
A: Use judgment. If someone in a marked highway service truck pulls over, that's legitimate help. If a random driver offers a ride, stay with your car, keep your doors locked, and wait for professional roadside assistance. Your car is easier to replace than your safety.
