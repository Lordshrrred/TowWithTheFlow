> **Quick Answer:** Call 911 first if you're in a dangerous spot, then your roadside assistance provider (AAA, your insurance, or a direct tow company). Get as far off the road as possible, turn on hazard lights, and stay in your car with your seatbelt on until help arrives. Do not change the tire yourself on a live freeway at night.

## Who Do I Call First for a Flat Tire on the Freeway at Night?

Call 911 if you're in an unsafe position, like partially blocking a lane, on a narrow shoulder, or near a blind curve. The dispatcher can send a highway patrol unit to block traffic while you wait. If you're safely off the road, skip 911 and call your roadside assistance provider directly: AAA, your insurance company's roadside line, or a tow service. Don't waste time trying to figure out who to call in the wrong order when you're sitting in the dark on a shoulder.

Check your insurance card or glove box paperwork right now. Most major carriers (State Farm, GEICO, USAA, Allstate) include a roadside number on the card itself. If you have AAA, open the app. If you have nothing, search "tow truck near me" and call the first local result with real reviews.

## Is It Safe to Change the Tire Myself on the Freeway?

No. At night, on a freeway, changing a tire yourself is genuinely dangerous. Freeway shoulders are narrow, traffic is fast, and drivers at night have reduced reaction time. A 2-inch margin between you and a passing semi at 70 mph is not a safe workspace. If you're on an urban section of freeway, like I-95 through a metro corridor, the shoulder may not exist at all.

The one exception: if you are completely off the road on a wide, flat shoulder with good visibility in both directions, solid ground under the jack points, and a functioning full-size spare (not a temporary donut), you can make a judgment call. But on most freeways at night, waiting for a professional is the safer move. Read through [tire blowout on highway: what to do](/tire-blowout-on-highway-what-to-do/) for a full breakdown of when it's worth attempting versus when to wait.

## How Do I Stay Safe While I Wait?

Get your car as far right as physically possible, ideally completely off the paved surface and onto the dirt or grass. Turn on your hazard lights the moment the tire goes flat. Do not get out and stand next to the car on the traffic side.

Once stopped, stay inside with your seatbelt fastened. A car that looks parked is still a target for distracted drivers, and being inside gives you some protection if someone clips you. If you have road flares or reflective triangles, place them 100 to 300 feet behind your car. Flares are visible from further away at night. ## What If I Have No Spare and No Roadside Coverage?

You still have options. Download a towing app like Urgently or Honk before you need one because they let you request a tow without a membership. Most tow companies operate 24 hours and will come to a freeway location. A local tow in most U.S. cities runs $75 to $150 for the first 5 miles. After that, expect $3 to $5 per mile.

If you have no spare, the tow truck will load your car and bring it to a tire shop. Most shops are closed at night, so the driver will drop it at a 24-hour location if one is nearby, or to a lot until morning. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can I drive on a flat tire to the next exit?**
A: Driving even a quarter mile on a flat destroys the tire and can damage the wheel rim, making a cheap fix much more expensive. If the next exit is visible, you might consider it at very low speed on a wide shoulder, but in most cases it is not worth the cost or the risk of losing control.

**Q: Will 911 send someone to change my tire?**
A: No. Highway patrol can show up to secure the scene and block traffic, but they do not change tires. You still need a tow truck or roadside assistance provider for the actual fix.

**Q: How long does a tow truck take at night?**
A: On a busy freeway near a city, expect 30 to 60 minutes. In rural areas or during high-demand periods, waits of 90 minutes or more are common. Give dispatch your exact mile marker and the direction you're traveling so they can find you faster.

**Q: What if I don't have a roadside assistance membership?**
A: Call a local tow company directly or use an app like Urgently or Honk. You pay out of pocket, typically $75 to $150 for a local tow, but it works the same as any roadside service. Some credit cards also include roadside dispatch, worth checking before you pay.

**Q: Is it okay to leave my car on the freeway shoulder overnight?**
A: Most state DOTs will ticket or tow an abandoned vehicle within 24 hours, sometimes sooner on high-traffic sections. If you can't get a tow truck that night, call your local highway patrol non-emergency line in the morning and let them know the vehicle is there and help is on the way.
