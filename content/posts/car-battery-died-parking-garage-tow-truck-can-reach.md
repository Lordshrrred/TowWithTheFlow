> **Quick Answer:** Most tow trucks can reach parking garages if there's 8+ feet of clearance and adequate maneuvering space. However, many garages have height restrictions that block standard tow trucks. Call your roadside service first to confirm access, then consider jump starting or mobile mechanics as alternatives.

## What To Do

1. **Check garage clearance first.** Look for height restriction signs at entrances. Standard tow trucks need 8-10 feet minimum clearance. Most urban parking garages post their clearance limit prominently at the entrance ramp, typically between 6 feet 2 inches and 7 feet. That rules out almost every standard wheel-lift or boom truck. Even if the entrance clears, interior ramps and turns may not. Measure or estimate before you call anyone, because that number is the first thing dispatch will ask you.

2. **Call your roadside assistance provider.** Give them the exact garage address and level. They'll know if their trucks can access your location. Mention the specific floor you're on, not just the garage name. A car stranded on the roof deck is a completely different job from one on level 2. Some fleets run low-profile rollback units with a 6-foot 6-inch working height specifically for garages, so your provider may have options a standard truck cannot offer.

3. **Try jump starting instead.** Ask nearby drivers, security guards, or parking attendants for a jump. This solves most dead battery problems without needing a tow. A dead battery is the single most common reason cars get stranded in garages, accounting for the majority of roadside calls in enclosed parking structures. Jump cables work fine if someone pulls their car next to yours, but many garage layouts make that tricky. A lithium jump pack that an attendant or passerby carries in their bag sidesteps that problem entirely.

4. **Request a mobile mechanic.** Many services send technicians with portable jump packs who can walk into tight spaces. A good lithium jump pack delivers 400 to 1000 peak amps, enough to start most gas engines and many diesels. The tech walks to your car, clamps on, and the car starts in under five minutes. No tow truck clearance issue at all. This is often the fastest resolution when the garage ceiling is too low.

5. **Move to street level if possible.** If your car starts with a jump, drive it to the street immediately for proper battery testing or replacement. A battery that died once in a garage will likely die again within days. Get it load-tested at any auto parts store, most do it free. If it fails the test, replace it before you park indoors again.

6. **Contact garage management.** Some facilities have maintenance staff with jump equipment or can provide temporary power access. Airport garages and large urban structures often keep a battery cart on-site for exactly this situation. Ask the attendant booth or call the management office number posted on the pay machine. You may get help in 15 minutes without spending a dime.

7. **Consider flatbed alternatives.** If towing is necessary, request a flatbed truck, which may have lower profile options for tight spaces. A standard flatbed sits around 48 to 52 inches off the ground and needs overhead room to tilt, so even a low-profile unit requires at minimum 7 feet 6 inches of clearance with the bed raised. If the garage is too tight for any truck, the only option is to push or roll the car to a ramp level or the street before the tow truck can hook up.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I push my car to a lower level so the tow truck can reach it?**
A: Yes, and this is often the most practical fix. Put the car in neutral, release the parking brake, and have two or three people push it toward the exit ramp. Make sure someone steers and that the path is clear. Once you reach street level or an accessible floor, the tow truck has no clearance problem.

**Q: Will my roadside assistance cover a mobile mechanic call inside a garage?**
A: Most major plans, including AAA and those bundled with car insurance, cover mobile battery service regardless of location. Call your provider and specify that a tow truck cannot access the garage. They will typically dispatch a mobile unit instead at no extra charge under your plan.

**Q: What if my car is in a private garage or gated structure and the tow company needs access?**
A: You or the property manager will need to let the driver in. Call garage management before the truck arrives and get them to unlock any gates or hold access doors open. Give the dispatch operator the management contact number so the driver can coordinate directly if you are not reachable.

## Stay Safe

- Keep your phone charged and have emergency numbers ready
- Stay with your vehicle until help arrives
- Lock your car if you need to leave the garage level
- Carry portable jump starter for future emergencies
- Never accept help from strangers in isolated garage areas
