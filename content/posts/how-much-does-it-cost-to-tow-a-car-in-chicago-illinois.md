## What Does a Tow Actually Cost in Chicago Right Now?

A local tow in Chicago typically runs $75 to $175 for the first 5 miles, with mileage charges of $3 to $5 per mile beyond that. Most breakdowns in the city land somewhere between $100 and $200 all-in. If you need a flatbed, which is required for all-wheel-drive vehicles and most modern sedans with low ground clearance, expect the higher end of that range.

Here's how the common scenarios break down:

| Situation | Typical Cost |
|---|---|
| Short local tow (under 5 miles) | $75–$150 |
| Standard tow (5–10 miles) | $125–$200 |
| Flatbed tow (any distance) | $150–$300 |
| After-hours or weekend | Add $50–$100 |
| Impound lot release (city tow) | $150–$250+ in fees |

These are private tow company rates. If the city of Chicago tows your car for a parking violation or accident clearance, that's a separate process with its own fee schedule through the Department of Streets and Sanitation.

## Does It Cost More to Get Towed From a Chicago Expressway?

Yes, expressway tows almost always cost more. The Illinois Tollway and IDOT both have contracted tow operators assigned to specific corridors. On the Dan Ryan (I-90/94), the Kennedy (I-90), the Eisenhower (I-290), or the Stevenson (I-55), you may not have a choice of which company responds, especially if IDOT dispatch sends one automatically after a police call.

These contracted towers are operating under state-set rate schedules, but the base rates can still exceed what a private call-ahead tow costs you. If you're stuck on the Kennedy near the Circle Interchange (where I-90/94 and I-290 meet), expect a wait and a bill in the $150 to $250 range depending on where your car needs to go. If you can safely call a private company before IDOT sends one, you may save money.

## How Much More Do I Pay After Hours or on Weekends in Chicago?

Most Chicago tow companies add $50 to $100 for calls between roughly 9 PM and 7 AM, and weekend surcharges are common too. If you break down on a Friday night in Wicker Park or after a Bears game near the Museum Campus, you're looking at peak-demand pricing on top of the base rate.

A few things that drive the price up fast: needing a winch-out if your car has gone off the road or into a median, needing dolly wheels for an AWD vehicle on a flatbed, or being in a location that requires the driver to navigate congested streets like Lower Wacker Drive. None of those extras are usually disclosed upfront unless you ask.

## What Should I Do Before I Agree to a Tow Price?

Get the total price confirmed over the phone before the truck rolls, and ask specifically: hookup fee, mileage rate, and any extras for your vehicle type. A reputable company will give you a number. One that won't is a red flag.

Two more things: know where you're sending the car before you call, because changing destinations mid-tow usually costs extra, and confirm the company is licensed in Illinois. The Illinois Commerce Commission regulates tow operators, and licensed companies are required to disclose rates. If you've had a bad experience or think you were overcharged, the ICC handles complaints.

## Common Questions

**Q: What is the average tow truck cost in Chicago?**
A: Most local tows in Chicago run $100 to $175 for distances under 5 miles. Flatbeds, after-hours calls, and expressway pickups push the price to $200 to $300 or more.

**Q: Can I negotiate the price with a tow company in Chicago?**
A: Sometimes. Private companies have more flexibility than contracted expressway towers. If you call ahead before a truck is dispatched, you have more leverage to get a flat rate quoted.

**Q: How much does the city charge if Chicago impounds my car?**
A: The city charges a towing fee plus daily storage fees. Towing fees are typically $150 to $250, and storage adds up fast if you don't retrieve the car within a day or two. Check the Chicago Department of Finance website for current rates.

**Q: Is a flatbed tow more expensive than a wheel-lift in Chicago?**
A: Yes, flatbeds generally run $30 to $75 more than a basic wheel-lift tow. If your car is all-wheel drive or has low clearance, the driver may require a flatbed regardless of what you request.

**Q: Does my car insurance cover towing in Chicago?**
A: Only if you have roadside assistance coverage added to your policy. Check your declarations page or call your insurer before you pay out of pocket. If you have State Farm, their towing coverage comes with dollar limits worth knowing in advance.
