> **Quick Answer:** You typically pay for snow ditch towing yourself unless you have roadside assistance coverage or comprehensive insurance. Costs range from $75-300 depending on location, equipment needed, and how stuck you are. Some insurance policies cover weather-related incidents.

## What To Do

1. **Stay in your vehicle** if it's safe and running. Turn on hazard lights immediately.

2. **Call for help** in this order:
   - Your roadside assistance provider (AAA, insurance company, credit card company)
   - Local towing company if no coverage
   - 911 if you're injured or in immediate danger

3. **Take photos** of your vehicle's position, any damage, and road conditions before the tow truck arrives. These photos matter if you file an insurance claim later or if there is a dispute about pre-existing damage.

4. **Ask about pricing upfront** when calling a tow company. Get the base rate plus any additional fees explained. Ask specifically whether the quote covers the full extraction or just the hookup, since companies sometimes charge separately for each stage.

5. **Stay with your vehicle** until help arrives unless it's unsafe. Keep your phone charged. If your battery is low, put the phone on airplane mode to stretch power while you wait.

## Who Pays for Snow Ditch Towing

**You pay out of pocket if:**
- No roadside assistance coverage
- Exceeded your annual coverage limit
- Policy excludes weather-related incidents

Many basic roadside plans cap towing benefits at two or three calls per year. If you already used them in October for a flat tire, you may be on your own in January when the ditch situation happens. Check your remaining calls before winter hits.

**Insurance may cover it if:**
- You have comprehensive coverage and the incident qualifies
- Your roadside assistance includes weather emergencies
- Another driver caused you to go off road

One important note: standard liability-only policies almost never cover your own vehicle recovery costs. Comprehensive is the tier where weather-related extraction has any realistic chance of being covered. Even then, weigh whether the claim is worth it. If your deductible is $500 and the tow costs $200, paying out of pocket protects your record.

**Roadside assistance typically covers:**
- AAA membership towing (usually 3-4 calls per year)
- Credit card roadside benefits
- Auto insurance roadside add-ons

Credit card roadside benefits are underused. Visa Signature, Mastercard World, and several other premium cards include towing up to a set dollar amount, often $50-100 per incident, with a limited number of calls per year. Check the benefits guide on your card before you assume you have no coverage.

## What It Might Cost

**Basic pull-out:** $75-150 for simple extraction with winch. This covers a vehicle that slid off a paved road into a shallow, accessible ditch with no obstacles.

**Moderate situation:** $150-250 for heavier equipment or longer distance. Expect this range if the truck needs to work from an awkward angle, the vehicle is buried past the wheel wells, or the terrain requires extra rigging.

**Complex recovery:** $250-500+ for specialized equipment, multiple vehicles, or dangerous conditions. Heavy SUVs and trucks cost more to extract than compact cars simply because of the weight involved. A recovery in a remote area with limited truck access can push costs even higher.

**Additional fees:**
- Mileage beyond included distance: $3-7 per mile
- After-hours service: 25-50% surcharge
- Waiting time: $50-100 per hour

During a major winter storm, local tow companies get overwhelmed fast. Wait times of two to four hours are common, and some companies charge waiting fees from the moment you call, not from when the truck arrives. Always ask how waiting time is billed.

## Common Questions

**Q: Will filing a tow claim raise my insurance rates?**
A: It depends on your insurer and how many claims you have filed recently. A single roadside assistance claim rarely triggers a rate increase, but comprehensive claims for vehicle recovery can count against you at renewal. If the tow costs less than your deductible, just pay it directly.

**Q: Can I refuse a tow and just wait for a friend to pull me out?**
A: Yes, you can wait for help from someone you know, and in a shallow ditch with solid footing that can work fine. Do not let anyone attempt a pull without a proper recovery strap rated for your vehicle's weight. A tow rope or bungee cord under load can snap and cause serious injury.

**Q: How long will I wait for a tow truck during a snowstorm?**
A: Response times during active winter storms typically run one to four hours, sometimes longer in rural areas. Call early, give your exact mile marker or GPS coordinates, and stay in the vehicle with hazards on so the driver can find you quickly.

## Stay Safe

- Never leave your vehicle in a blizzard or whiteout conditions
- Keep emergency supplies: blanket, water, flashlight, phone charger
- Don't attempt to dig out if traffic is nearby
- Run engine periodically for heat but crack a window to prevent carbon monoxide buildup
- Make yourself visible with hazard lights, flares, or bright clothing
